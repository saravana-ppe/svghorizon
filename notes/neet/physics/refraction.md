# Refraction and total internal reflection

## What refraction is

When light passes from one transparent medium into another, it changes speed. If it
enters at an angle, that change of speed bends the ray. This bending is refraction.

A stick held in water looks broken at the surface for exactly this reason.

## Snell's law

`n1 sin i = n2 sin r`

- Light going from a rarer to a denser medium bends **towards** the normal
- Light going from a denser to a rarer medium bends **away** from the normal
- A ray falling along the normal does not bend at all

## Total internal reflection

When light travels from a denser medium to a rarer one and the angle of incidence
crosses a certain value, no light escapes. All of it is reflected back inside. That
value is the **critical angle**.

`sin C = 1/n`

Two conditions must both be satisfied:

- light must travel from denser to rarer medium
- the angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle

### Where you see it

- **Optical fibres** carry light for kilometres by trapping it inside the core
- **A diamond sparkles** because its critical angle is only about 24°, so light
  bounces many times before leaving
- **A mirage** on a hot road is total internal reflection in layers of air

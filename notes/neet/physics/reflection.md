# Reflection of light

## What reflection means

Light travels in a straight line. When it falls on a surface and cannot pass through, it bounces back into the same medium. That bouncing back is called reflection.

A polished silver surface reflects almost all the light falling on it, which is why a mirror shows a clear image. A wall scatters light in every direction and shows none.

## The two laws of reflection

- The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection. Both angles are measured from the **normal**, not from the surface.
- The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in one plane.

These two laws hold for every reflecting surface, flat or curved, rough or smooth. In a rough surface each tiny part still obeys them, but the normals point in different directions, so the reflected rays scatter. This is **diffuse reflection**.

## Reflection at a plane mirror

An object placed in front of a plane mirror forms an image that is:

- virtual
- erect
- the same size as the object
- as far behind the mirror as the object is in front of it

The image is also laterally inverted, which is why the word AMBULANCE is painted in reverse on the front of the vehicle.

> If a mirror is rotated by an angle θ while the incident ray stays fixed, the reflected ray turns by 2θ. This result appears often in numerical questions.

## Spherical mirrors

A spherical mirror is a part of a hollow sphere whose one side is silvered. If the reflecting surface curves inwards it is a **concave** mirror. If it bulges outwards it is a **convex** mirror.

### Terms you must know

- **Pole (P)** — the centre of the reflecting surface
- **Centre of curvature (C)** — the centre of the sphere the mirror is part of
- **Radius of curvature (R)** — the distance PC
- **Principal focus (F)** — where rays parallel to the principal axis meet after reflection
- **Focal length (f)** — the distance PF, and `f = R / 2`

## The mirror formula

`1/v + 1/u = 1/f`

Here u is the object distance, v is the image distance and f is the focal length, all measured from the pole.

Magnification is given by:

`m = height of image / height of object = -v/u`

A negative magnification means the image is real and inverted. A positive value means it is virtual and erect.

### Sign convention

Distances are measured from the pole, taking the direction of the incident light as positive. So:

- distances measured against the incident light are negative
- heights above the principal axis are positive, below it negative
- for a concave mirror f is negative, for a convex mirror f is positive

## Image formation by a concave mirror

| Object position | Image position | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| At infinity | At F | Real, inverted, very small |
| Beyond C | Between F and C | Real, inverted, diminished |
| At C | At C | Real, inverted, same size |
| Between C and F | Beyond C | Real, inverted, enlarged |
| At F | At infinity | Real, inverted, very large |
| Between F and P | Behind the mirror | Virtual, erect, enlarged |

A convex mirror is simpler. Wherever the object is placed, the image is always virtual, erect and diminished, and lies between P and F. That wide field of view is why convex mirrors are used as rear view mirrors on vehicles.

## Previous year questions

**NEET 2022** — An object is placed 20 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 10 cm. Where is the image formed and what is its nature?

> At 20 cm in front of the mirror. Real, inverted and the same size as the object, since the object sits exactly at the centre of curvature.

**NEET 2019** — A ray of light strikes a plane mirror at an angle of 30° with the mirror surface. What is the angle of reflection?

> 60°. The angle given is with the surface, so the angle with the normal is 90° − 30° = 60°, and the angle of reflection equals it.

**NEET 2017** — Why does a convex mirror always produce a virtual image?

> Reflected rays from a convex mirror always diverge, so they never actually meet. They only appear to come from a point behind the mirror, which makes the image virtual.

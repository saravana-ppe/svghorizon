**NEET UG 2026 Biology – Notes from NCERT**

**NCERT chapters covered:** Class XII – Chapter 9 (Biotechnology: Principles and Processes), Chapter 10 (Biotechnology and its Applications)

**NEET syllabus topics in this unit:**

1. **Principles and process of biotechnology:** genetic engineering (recombinant DNA technology)
2. **Application of biotechnology in health and agriculture:** human insulin and vaccine production, gene therapy; genetically modified organisms – Bt crops; transgenic animals; biosafety issues – biopiracy and patents

> **NEET Focus: How this unit is tested**
> - Restriction enzymes: naming (EcoRI), palindromes, sticky ends, recognition sites in pBR322 and the gene they inactivate.
> - Vector features: *ori*, selectable marker, cloning site; insertional inactivation; blue–white selection.
> - Steps and enzymes: lysozyme / cellulase / chitinase, chilled ethanol, ligase, Taq polymerase, PCR steps.
> - Methods of introducing DNA: Ca<sup>2+</sup> + heat shock (42°C), micro-injection, biolistics, disarmed pathogens.
> - Bioreactors (stirred-tank), downstream processing.
> - Bt toxin genes and target pests (*cryIAc*, *cryIIAb*, *cryIAb*), RNAi against *Meloidogyne incognita*.
> - Insulin (A and B chains, C-peptide, Eli Lilly 1983), ADA deficiency gene therapy (1990), ELISA/PCR diagnosis.
> - Transgenic animals (α-1-antitrypsin, Rosie), GEAC, biopiracy (Basmati, turmeric, neem).
> - Assertion–reason and statement-based questions on the sequence of steps are common; about **3–4 questions** come from this unit every year.

## Quick Revision – One-liners

- EFB definition: integration of natural science and organisms, cells, parts thereof and molecular analogues for products and services.
- Two core techniques: genetic engineering and bioprocess engineering.
- First recombinant DNA: Cohen and Boyer, 1972; antibiotic-resistance gene from a *Salmonella typhimurium* plasmid, cloned in *E. coli*.
- Restriction enzymes = molecular scissors; ligase = molecular glue.
- First restriction endonuclease: *Hind* II (6 bp recognition sequence); 900+ enzymes from 230+ bacterial strains.
- EcoRI: *Escherichia coli* RY 13; recognition site GAATTC; cuts between G and A → sticky ends.
- Palindrome: same sequence on both strands when read in the same orientation.
- Gel electrophoresis: agarose (sea weed), DNA moves to the anode, ethidium bromide + UV → orange bands; recovery = elution.
- Vector essentials: *ori* (controls copy number), selectable marker, single cloning site.
- pBR322: *amp<sup>R</sup>*, *tet<sup>R</sup>*, *rop*; BamHI and SalI in *tet<sup>R</sup>*; PvuI and PstI in *amp<sup>R</sup>*.
- Insertional inactivation: recombinants lose the resistance/enzyme activity at the insert site.
- Blue colonies = non-recombinant; white/colourless = recombinant (β-galactosidase).
- *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* → Ti plasmid → T-DNA; disarmed retroviruses for animal cells.
- Competent cells: Ca<sup>2+</sup> → ice → heat shock 42°C → ice.
- Micro-injection (animal nucleus); biolistics – gold/tungsten (plants).
- Cell lysis: lysozyme (bacteria), cellulase (plant), chitinase (fungus); ribonuclease, protease; chilled ethanol; spooling.
- PCR: denaturation → annealing → extension; Taq polymerase from *Thermus aquaticus*; about a billion copies.
- Recombinant protein = protein from a gene expressed in a heterologous host.
- Continuous culture keeps cells in log phase.
- Bioreactors: 100–1000 L; stirred-tank most common; sparged → air bubbles for more oxygen transfer.
- Downstream processing = separation + purification (+ preservatives, clinical trials, quality control).
- Golden rice = vitamin A-enriched.
- Bt: protoxin → active in alkaline gut → pores in midgut cells → lysis.
- *cryIAc*, *cryIIAb* → cotton bollworm; *cryIAb* → corn borer.
- RNAi: *Meloidogyne incognita* in tobacco roots; sense + antisense RNA → dsRNA → silences the nematode mRNA.
- About 30 recombinant therapeutics approved worldwide; 12 marketed in India.
- Insulin: A and B chains, disulphide bridges; C-peptide removed; Eli Lilly 1983 (*E. coli*).
- Gene therapy: 1990, 4-year-old girl, ADA deficiency, lymphocytes + ADA cDNA via a retroviral vector; periodic infusion needed.
- Molecular diagnosis: PCR (HIV, cancer mutations), radioactive probes + autoradiography, ELISA (antigen–antibody).
- Over 95% of transgenic animals are mice.
- α-1-antitrypsin for emphysema; Rosie (1997) – human α-lactalbumin, 2.4 g/L.
- GEAC – validity of GM research and safety of GMOs for public services.
- Basmati patent 1997 (US company); India: 2,00,000 rice varieties, 27 Basmati varieties; turmeric and neem also targeted.
- Biopiracy = use of bio-resources without authorisation or compensation.

## Practice MCQs (NEET pattern)

1. The enzyme EcoRI was isolated from which strain of *E. coli*?
   - (a) K 12
   - (b) DH5α
   - (c) RY 13
   - (d) BL 21
2. In pBR322, insertion of foreign DNA at the BamHI site results in loss of resistance to:
   - (a) Ampicillin
   - (b) Tetracycline
   - (c) Kanamycin
   - (d) Chloramphenicol
3. In agarose gel electrophoresis, DNA fragments move towards the:
   - (a) Cathode, because DNA is positively charged
   - (b) Anode, because DNA is positively charged
   - (c) Cathode, because DNA is negatively charged
   - (d) Anode, because DNA is negatively charged
4. Which of the following is a palindromic sequence (5'→3' on the upper strand)?
   - (a) GAATTC
   - (b) GATACC
   - (c) GGATCA
   - (d) AATTGC
5. In the blue–white screening method, recombinant colonies are:
   - (a) Blue, because β-galactosidase is active
   - (b) Blue, because β-galactosidase is inactive
   - (c) Colourless, because β-galactosidase is active
   - (d) Colourless, because β-galactosidase is inactive
6. The correct sequence for making bacterial cells competent is:
   - (a) Heat shock at 42°C → Ca<sup>2+</sup> treatment → ice
   - (b) Ca<sup>2+</sup> treatment → ice → heat shock at 42°C → ice
   - (c) Ice → Ca<sup>2+</sup> treatment → heat shock at 72°C
   - (d) Lysozyme treatment → heat shock → ethanol
7. Which enzyme is used to break open fungal cells for DNA isolation?
   - (a) Lysozyme
   - (b) Cellulase
   - (c) Chitinase
   - (d) Protease
8. Taq polymerase is preferred in PCR because it:
   - (a) Is thermostable
   - (b) Does not need primers
   - (c) Works at low temperature
   - (d) Has proofreading activity absent in other polymerases
9. Sparged stirred-tank bioreactors differ from simple stirred-tank bioreactors in having:
   - (a) A foam control system
   - (b) Sterile air bubbles passed through the medium
   - (c) A pH control system
   - (d) Sampling ports
10. Bt toxin becomes active in the insect gut because of:
    - (a) Acidic pH
    - (b) Neutral pH
    - (c) Alkaline pH
    - (d) Enzymes secreted by the bacterium
11. The *cry* gene that controls corn borer is:
    - (a) *cryIAc*
    - (b) *cryIIAb*
    - (c) *cryIAb*
    - (d) *cryIIAc*
12. In RNAi-based protection of tobacco against *Meloidogyne incognita*, the silenced mRNA belongs to:
    - (a) The nematode
    - (b) Tobacco
    - (c) *Agrobacterium*
    - (d) The virus vector
13. In the production of human insulin by Eli Lilly:
    - (a) Pro-insulin was produced in yeast and C-peptide removed
    - (b) A and B chains were produced separately in *E. coli* and joined by disulphide bonds
    - (c) Insulin was extracted from pig pancreas
    - (d) A single gene coding for mature insulin was expressed in *Agrobacterium*
14. The first clinical gene therapy (1990) was given for:
    - (a) Cystic fibrosis
    - (b) Haemophilia
    - (c) Adenosine deaminase deficiency
    - (d) Sickle-cell anaemia
15. The first transgenic cow, Rosie, produced milk containing:
    - (a) Human α-1-antitrypsin
    - (b) Human insulin
    - (c) Human α-lactalbumin
    - (d) Human growth hormone

**Answers:** 1–c, 2–b, 3–d, 4–a, 5–d, 6–b, 7–c, 8–a, 9–b, 10–c, 11–c, 12–a, 13–b, 14–c, 15–c

## 1. Origin of Life

### 1.1 Origin of universe and earth
- **Universe** – about **13.8 billion years** old; huge clusters of **galaxies**.
- **Big Bang theory** – a singular huge explosion; universe expanded, temperature fell; **hydrogen and helium** formed; gases condensed under gravitation → galaxies.
- **Earth** formed about **4.5 billion years** ago in the solar system of the **Milky Way**.
- **Early earth:** **no atmosphere**; water vapour, **methane, CO<sub>2</sub> and ammonia** released from molten mass. **UV rays** split water into H<sub>2</sub> and O<sub>2</sub>; lighter H<sub>2</sub> escaped; O<sub>2</sub> combined with NH<sub>3</sub> and CH<sub>4</sub> → water, CO<sub>2</sub>, etc. **Ozone layer** formed. Water vapour fell as rain → **oceans**.
- **Life appeared about 500 million years after earth formed**, i.e., about **four billion years ago**.

### 1.2 Theories of origin of life

| Theory | Idea | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Panspermia** | Units of life (**spores**) transferred to different planets including earth (early Greek thinkers) | Still a favourite idea of some astronomers |
| **Spontaneous generation** | Life came from **decaying and rotting matter** like straw, mud | **Dismissed by Louis Pasteur** |
| **Chemical evolution (Oparin–Haldane)** | First life came from **pre-existing non-living organic molecules** (RNA, protein, etc.); life preceded by **chemical evolution** (organic molecules from inorganic constituents) | **Accepted by majority** |

- **Louis Pasteur** – in **pre-sterilised flasks**, life did not arise from **killed yeast**; in a flask **open to air**, new organisms arose → **life comes only from pre-existing life**.
- **Oparin (Russia) and Haldane (England)**: early earth conditions – **high temperature, volcanic storms, reducing atmosphere** with CH<sub>4</sub>, NH<sub>3</sub>, etc.

### 1.3 Miller's experiment (1953)
- **S.L. Miller** (American scientist) created early-earth conditions in the lab.
- **Electric discharge** in a closed flask containing **CH<sub>4</sub>, H<sub>2</sub>, NH<sub>3</sub> and water vapour at 800°C**.
- Observed formation of **amino acids**. Others observed **sugars, nitrogen bases, pigments and fats**.
- **Meteorite** analysis showed similar compounds → similar processes elsewhere in space.
- **Chemical evolution** more or less accepted.

### 1.4 Early life forms
- We have **no idea** how the first self-replicating metabolic capsule arose.
- **First non-cellular forms** of life: about **3 billion years ago** – giant molecules (RNA, protein, polysaccharides).
- **First cellular forms**: about **2000 million years ago**; probably **single cells**, all in **water**.
- **Abiogenesis** – first life arose slowly through evolutionary forces from non-living molecules – accepted by majority.

## 2. Evolution of Life Forms – A Theory

### 2.1 Theory of special creation (religious literature)
1. All living organisms were **created as such**.
2. **Diversity was always the same** since creation and will remain so.
3. **Earth is about 4000 years old**.
- All strongly challenged in the 19th century.

### 2.2 Darwin's theory
- **Charles Darwin** – sea voyage on **H.M.S. Beagle** round the world.
- Conclusions:
  - Existing life forms share similarities among themselves and with life forms that existed millions of years ago; many extinct.
  - **Gradual evolution** of life forms.
  - Populations have **built-in variation**; those better suited to natural conditions **outbreed** others.
  - **Fitness** = ultimately and only **reproductive fitness** – fitter individuals leave more progeny → **selected by nature** → **natural selection** (mechanism of evolution).
- **Alfred Wallace** (naturalist, **Malay Archipelago**) came to similar conclusions around the same time.
- All life forms share **common ancestors** present at different periods (epochs, periods, eras). Geological history correlates with biological history → earth is **billions of years old**.

## 3. Evidences for Evolution

### 3.1 Paleontological evidence (fossils)
- **Fossils** – remains of **hard parts** of life forms found in **rocks**.
- Rock **sediments** lie one over another; different-aged sediments contain fossils of different life forms.
- Some fossils resemble modern organisms; many represent **extinct** organisms (e.g., **dinosaurs**).
- Life forms varied over time and certain forms are restricted to certain geological time spans.
- Ages of fossils determined by **radioactive dating**.

### 3.2 Embryological evidence
- **Ernst Haeckel** proposed embryological support: features common to all vertebrate embryos but absent in adults. E.g., all vertebrate embryos including humans develop a **row of vestigial gill slits** behind the head, functional only in **fish**.
- **Disapproved by Karl Ernst von Baer** – embryos **never pass through the adult stages** of other animals.

### 3.3 Comparative anatomy and morphology

| Feature | Homologous organs | Analogous organs |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | **Similar anatomical structure** | **Different anatomical structure** |
| Function | **Different functions** | **Similar functions** |
| Evolution type | **Divergent evolution** | **Convergent evolution** |
| Indicates | **Common ancestry** | Similar habitat → similar adaptive features |
| Animal examples | **Forelimbs of whales, bats, cheetah and human** (humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals, phalanges); **vertebrate hearts and brains** | **Wings of butterfly and birds**; **eye of octopus and mammals**; **flippers of penguins and dolphins** |
| Plant examples | **Thorn of *Bougainvillea* and tendril of *Cucurbita*** | **Sweet potato (root modification) and potato (stem modification)** |

### 3.4 Molecular (biochemical) evidence
- **Similarities in proteins and genes** performing a given function among diverse organisms give clues to **common ancestry**.

### 3.5 Artificial selection
- Man has bred and domesticated plants and animals (e.g., **dog breeds**) within hundreds of years. If man could create new breeds, nature could have done the same over millions of years.

### 3.6 Industrial melanism (natural selection in action)
- **England**: collections from the **1850s (before industrialisation)** had **more white-winged moths**; in **1920 (after industrialisation)**, **more dark-winged (melanised) moths** in the same area.
- **Explanation:** predators spot a moth against a contrasting background.
  - Before industrialisation – tree trunks covered with **whitish lichens** → white moths camouflaged; dark moths picked by predators.
  - After industrialisation – trunks **darkened by smoke and soot** (lichens absent as they are **pollution indicators**) → dark moths camouflaged.
- In **rural areas** (no industrialisation), melanic moths remained low.
- **No variant is completely wiped out.**

### 3.7 Anthropogenic evolution
- Excess use of **herbicides, pesticides** → selection of **resistant varieties**; **antibiotic/drug resistance** in microbes and eukaryotic cells appears in **months or years**, not centuries.
- Evolution is **not a directed process** (not deterministic); it is a **stochastic process** based on **chance events** in nature and **chance mutations**.

## 4. Adaptive Radiation

- **Evolution of different species in a given geographical area starting from a point and radiating to other areas (habitats)**.

| Example | Details |
|---|---|
| **Darwin's finches** (Galapagos Islands) | Small black birds; many varieties on the same island; from **original seed-eating** forms, others with **altered beaks** evolved – **insectivorous and vegetarian** finches. One of the best examples |
| **Australian marsupials** | Many marsupials, each different, evolved from an **ancestral stock** within the Australian island continent |

- When **more than one adaptive radiation** occurs in an isolated area (different habitats) → **convergent evolution**.
- **Placental mammals in Australia** also show adaptive radiation; each appears "similar" to a corresponding marsupial, e.g., **placental wolf and Tasmanian wolf (marsupial)** → convergent evolution.

## 5. Biological Evolution

- Evolution by natural selection started when **cellular forms of life with differences in metabolic capability** originated.
- **Essence of Darwinian theory = natural selection.**
- Rate of appearance of new forms is linked to **life cycle/life span**. Bacteria: new variant population can appear **within days**; in fish or fowl, **millions of years**.
- Bacterial example: colony A has built-in variation; change in medium brings out part B that survives → B outgrows others → appears as a new species. **Fitness of B is better than A under new conditions.**
- **Fitness** is based on **inherited** characteristics → must have a **genetic basis**. Fitness is the end result of the ability to adapt and get selected.
- **Two key concepts of Darwinian theory:** **branching descent** and **natural selection**.

### 5.1 Lamarck
- French naturalist **Lamarck** – evolution driven by **use and disuse of organs** and **inheritance of acquired characters**.
- Example: **giraffes** elongated their necks to forage leaves on tall trees and passed this acquired character on. **Nobody believes this any more.**

### 5.2 Basis of natural selection (Darwin, influenced by Malthus)
- Possibly influenced by **Thomas Malthus**' work on populations.
- **Factual observations:**
  - **Natural resources are limited**
  - **Populations are stable in size** except for seasonal fluctuations
  - **Members of a population vary** (no two alike)
  - **Most variations are inherited**
  - Theoretically populations grow **exponentially**, but in reality sizes are limited → **competition for resources**
- **Darwin's insight:** heritable variations that make resource use better for a few enable them to reproduce and leave more progeny → over generations, population characteristics change → new forms appear.

## 6. Mechanism of Evolution

- **Hugo de Vries** – worked on **evening primrose** (*Oenothera*); proposed **mutations** – large differences arising **suddenly** in a population.
- He believed **mutation causes evolution**, not the minor heritable variations of Darwin.

| Darwin | de Vries |
|---|---|
| **Small, directional** variations | **Mutations – random and directionless** |
| Evolution is **gradual** | Mutation causes speciation in a **single step** = **saltation** |

- **Population genetics** later brought clarity → **modern synthetic theory**: variation (mutation + recombination + gene flow + genetic drift) + natural selection.

## 7. Hardy–Weinberg Principle

- **Allele frequencies in a population are stable and constant from generation to generation.**
- **Gene pool** (total genes and their alleles in a population) remains constant → **genetic equilibrium (Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium)**.
- **Sum of all allelic frequencies = 1.**
- For a gene with alleles **A (frequency p)** and **a (frequency q)**:
  - **p + q = 1**
  - Frequency of **AA = p<sup>2</sup>**, **aa = q<sup>2</sup>**, **Aa = 2pq**
  - **p<sup>2</sup> + 2pq + q<sup>2</sup> = 1** (binomial expansion of **(p + q)<sup>2</sup>**)
- When measured frequency differs from expected, the difference (direction) indicates the **extent of evolutionary change**. **Disturbance of equilibrium = evolution.**

### 7.1 Five factors affecting Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium
1. **Gene migration / gene flow** – migration of a section of a population changes gene frequencies in both original and new populations; repeated migration = **gene flow**.
2. **Genetic drift** – the same change in frequency occurring **by chance**. If the change is large enough, the new sample becomes a different species; the drifted population becomes **founders** → **founder effect**.
3. **Mutation**
4. **Genetic recombination**
5. **Natural selection**

> **Exam Tip (calculation):** If frequency of recessive homozygotes (aa) = 16% → q<sup>2</sup> = 0.16 → q = 0.4 → p = 0.6. Then AA = p<sup>2</sup> = 0.36 (36%), Aa = 2pq = 0.48 (48%).

### 7.2 Types of natural selection

| Type | What happens | Effect on distribution curve |
|---|---|---|
| **Stabilising** | **More individuals acquire the mean character value** | Peak gets **higher and narrower** at the mean |
| **Directional** | More individuals acquire a value **other than the mean** | Peak **shifts to one side** |
| **Disruptive** | More individuals acquire **peripheral values at both ends** | **Two peaks** at the extremes |

- **Natural selection** – heritable variations enabling better survival are enabled to reproduce and leave more progeny. Pre-existing advantageous mutations, when selected → new phenotypes → over generations → **speciation** (seen in microbial experiments).

## 8. A Brief Account of Evolution (Geological Time)

| Time (approx.) | Event |
|---|---|
| **2000 mya** | **First cellular forms** of life; some cells could release O<sub>2</sub> (like light reaction) |
| – | Single-celled → multicellular forms |
| **500 mya** | **Invertebrates** formed and active |
| **350 mya** | **Jawless fish** probably evolved; **fish with stout, strong fins** could move on land and back to water |
| **320 mya** | **Sea weeds and few plants** existed |
| – | **First organisms to invade land were plants**; widespread on land when animals invaded |
| – | **Lobefins** evolved into **first amphibians** (no specimens left; ancestors of frogs and salamanders) |
| – | Amphibians → **reptiles** (thick-shelled eggs that don't dry up); descendants: turtles, tortoises, crocodiles |
| Next 200 million years | Reptiles of different shapes and sizes **dominated**; giant ferns (pteridophytes) fell to form **coal deposits** |
| **200 mya** | Some land reptiles went back to water → fish-like reptiles (e.g., ***Ichthyosaurs***) |
| – | Land reptiles = **dinosaurs**; biggest ***Tyrannosaurus rex*** – about **20 feet** tall, dagger-like teeth |
| **65 mya** | **Dinosaurs suddenly disappeared** – reason unknown (climate change, or evolved into birds, or both) |
| – | **First mammals were shrew-like** (small fossils); viviparous; more intelligent |

- **Coelacanth** – a fish caught in **South Africa in 1938**, thought to be extinct; a **lobefin**.
- **Continental drift:** when South America joined North America, South American mammals (resembling horse, hippopotamus, bear, rabbit) were overridden by North American fauna. **Pouched mammals of Australia survived** due to lack of competition.
- Mammals living wholly in water: **whales, dolphins, seals, sea cows**.
- The most successful story: **evolution of man** with **language skills and self-consciousness**.

## 9. Origin and Evolution of Man

| Time | Ancestor | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **About 15 mya** | ***Dryopithecus*** and ***Ramapithecus*** | Primates; hairy; walked like gorillas and chimpanzees. ***Ramapithecus* – more man-like**; ***Dryopithecus* – more ape-like** |
| **3–4 mya** | Man-like primates (fossils in **Ethiopia and Tanzania**) | Walked upright in **eastern Africa**; not taller than **4 feet** |
| **2 mya** | **Australopithecines** | Lived in **East African grasslands**; hunted with **stone weapons** but essentially **ate fruit** |
| – | ***Homo habilis*** | **First human-like being (hominid)**; brain capacity **650–800 cc**; probably **did not eat meat** |
| **1.5 mya** | ***Homo erectus*** | Fossils discovered in **Java (1891)**; large brain about **900 cc**; probably **ate meat** |
| **1,00,000–40,000 years ago** | **Neanderthal man** | Brain **1400 cc**; lived in **near east and central Asia**; used **hides** to protect body; **buried their dead** |
| – | ***Homo sapiens*** | Arose in **Africa**; moved across continents; developed into distinct races |
| **75,000–10,000 years ago (ice age)** | **Modern *Homo sapiens*** | Arose |
| **About 18,000 years ago** | – | **Pre-historic cave art** – e.g., **Bhimbetka rock shelter, Raisen district, Madhya Pradesh** |
| **About 10,000 years ago** | – | **Agriculture** began; **human settlements** started |

- NCERT figure: the skull of a **baby chimpanzee** is more like an **adult human skull** than an adult chimpanzee skull.

> **PYQ Trend:** Brain capacities (*H. habilis* 650–800 cc; *H. erectus* 900 cc; Neanderthal 1400 cc), *Ramapithecus* more man-like, Neanderthals buried dead, *H. erectus* fossils from Java 1891, Bhimbetka cave art – frequently asked. Also Miller's experiment components and temperature.

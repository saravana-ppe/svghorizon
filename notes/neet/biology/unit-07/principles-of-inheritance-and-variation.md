## 1. Basic Terms

- **Genetics** – branch of biology dealing with **inheritance and variation** of characters from parents to offspring.
- **Inheritance** – process by which characters pass from parent to progeny; basis of **heredity**.
- **Variation** – degree by which progeny differ from parents.
- Humans knew from **8000–1000 B.C.** that sexual reproduction causes variation; used **artificial selection and domestication** (e.g., **Sahiwal cows** of Punjab).

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Gene (factor)** | Unit of inheritance; contains information to express a trait |
| **Alleles** | Genes coding for a pair of contrasting traits; slightly different forms of the same gene (e.g., T and t) |
| **Homozygous** | Identical alleles (TT or tt) |
| **Heterozygous** | Dissimilar alleles (Tt) |
| **Genotype** | Genetic constitution (TT, Tt, tt) |
| **Phenotype** | Observable/descriptive character (tall, dwarf) |
| **Dominant** | Factor expressed in the F<sub>1</sub> heterozygote (T) |
| **Recessive** | Factor not expressed in heterozygote (t) |
| **True-breeding line** | Shows stable trait inheritance after continuous self-pollination for several generations |
| **Monohybrid** | Heterozygous for one character (Tt) |

> **Note:** Use capital and small forms of the **same letter** for alleles (T and t), never T and d – otherwise you cannot tell whether they are alleles of the same gene.

## 2. Mendel's Experiments

- **Gregor Mendel** – hybridisation experiments on **garden pea** for **seven years (1856–1863)**.
- **First time** that **statistical analysis and mathematical logic** were applied to biology.
- **Large sampling size** gave credibility; confirmation over successive generations showed general rules.
- Selected **14 true-breeding pea varieties** as pairs differing in one character.

### 2.1 Seven pairs of contrasting traits (Table 4.1)

| No. | Character | Dominant / Recessive |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stem height | **Tall** / dwarf |
| 2 | Flower colour | **Violet** / white |
| 3 | Flower position | **Axial** / terminal |
| 4 | Pod shape | **Inflated** / constricted |
| 5 | Pod colour | **Green** / yellow |
| 6 | Seed shape | **Round** / wrinkled |
| 7 | Seed colour | **Yellow** / green |

> **Common Mistake:** Pod colour – **green is dominant**; seed colour – **yellow is dominant**. Don't mix them up.

## 3. Inheritance of One Gene – Monohybrid Cross

- **Tall (TT) × dwarf (tt)** → **F<sub>1</sub> all tall (Tt)**; no dwarf; no blending.
- **F<sub>1</sub> selfed** → **F<sub>2</sub>: 3/4 tall : 1/4 dwarf** – dwarf trait reappears unchanged.
- **Phenotypic ratio 3 : 1**; **genotypic ratio 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt**.
- Binomial expression: **(1/2 T + 1/2 t)<sup>2</sup> = 1/4 TT + 1/2 Tt + 1/4 tt**.
- Dwarf F<sub>2</sub> plants selfed → dwarf in F<sub>3</sub> and F<sub>4</sub> → dwarfs are homozygous (tt).
- **Punnett square** – developed by British geneticist **Reginald C. Punnett**; graphical representation to calculate probability of all genotypes in a cross.

### 3.1 Test cross
- Crossing an organism with **dominant phenotype** (unknown genotype) with the **recessive parent**.
- **TT × tt** → all dominant. **Tt × tt** → **1 dominant : 1 recessive**.
- Used to determine whether a dominant-looking individual is homozygous or heterozygous. (NCERT example: violet flower V dominant over white v.)

## 4. Mendel's Laws

### 4.1 First law – Law of Dominance
1. Characters are controlled by **discrete units called factors**.
2. **Factors occur in pairs**.
3. In a dissimilar pair, one member **dominates** (dominant) the other (recessive).
- Explains why only one parental character appears in F<sub>1</sub> and both in F<sub>2</sub>, and the 3:1 ratio.

### 4.2 Second law – Law of Segregation
- Alleles **do not blend**; both characters are recovered in F<sub>2</sub>.
- During gamete formation, the factors (alleles) of a pair **segregate** so that **a gamete receives only one of the two factors**.
- A **homozygous** parent produces all similar gametes; a **heterozygous** parent produces **two kinds of gametes in equal proportion**.

### 4.3 Third law – Law of Independent Assortment
- From the **dihybrid cross**: "**When two pairs of traits are combined in a hybrid, segregation of one pair of characters is independent of the other pair of characters.**"

## 5. Deviations from Mendelism

### 5.1 Incomplete dominance
- F<sub>1</sub> phenotype **in between** the two parents.
- Example: **flower colour in snapdragon (dog flower, *Antirrhinum* sp.)**.
  - Red (RR) × White (rr) → **F<sub>1</sub> Pink (Rr)**
  - F<sub>2</sub>: **1 Red (RR) : 2 Pink (Rr) : 1 White (rr)**
- **Phenotypic ratio = genotypic ratio = 1 : 2 : 1**.

### 5.2 Explanation of dominance (gene–enzyme concept)
- A modified allele may produce: (i) normal/less efficient enzyme, (ii) non-functional enzyme, or (iii) no enzyme.
- If (i), the modified allele is **equivalent** (same phenotype).
- If (ii) or (iii), phenotype depends only on the unmodified allele → unmodified (functioning) allele = **dominant**; modified allele = **recessive**.

### 5.3 Co-dominance – ABO blood groups
- **F<sub>1</sub> resembles both parents** – both alleles express.
- Controlled by **gene I** with **three alleles: I<sup>A</sup>, I<sup>B</sup> and i**.
- I<sup>A</sup> and I<sup>B</sup> produce slightly different forms of **sugar polymers** on the RBC plasma membrane; **i produces no sugar**.
- **I<sup>A</sup> and I<sup>B</sup> are completely dominant over i**; **I<sup>A</sup> and I<sup>B</sup> are co-dominant**.
- **Six genotypes, four phenotypes.**

| Genotype | Blood group |
|---|---|
| I<sup>A</sup>I<sup>A</sup>, I<sup>A</sup>i | **A** |
| I<sup>B</sup>I<sup>B</sup>, I<sup>B</sup>i | **B** |
| I<sup>A</sup>I<sup>B</sup> | **AB** (co-dominance) |
| ii | **O** |

### 5.4 Multiple alleles
- **More than two alleles** govern the same character (e.g., I<sup>A</sup>, I<sup>B</sup>, i).
- An individual can have only **two**; multiple alleles are found only in **population studies**.

### 5.5 One gene – different dominance for different phenotypes (starch in pea)
- Gene with alleles **B and b** controls **starch synthesis** in pea seeds.
  - **BB** – large starch grains, **round** seeds
  - **bb** – smaller starch grains, **wrinkled** seeds
  - **Bb** – **round** seeds (B dominant for seed shape) but **intermediate** starch grain size (incomplete dominance for grain size)
- **Dominance is not an autonomous feature of a gene**; it depends on the gene product and the phenotype examined.

> **PYQ Trend:** "Round vs wrinkled seed shape is dominant, but starch grain size shows incomplete dominance" – a classic NCERT-based concept question. Also: number of genotypes/phenotypes in ABO (6 and 4).

## 6. Inheritance of Two Genes – Dihybrid Cross

- **Round yellow (RRYY) × wrinkled green (rryy)** → **F<sub>1</sub> all round yellow (RrYy)**.
- Yellow dominant over green; round dominant over wrinkled.
- F<sub>1</sub> produces **four types of gametes** in equal frequency (**RY, Ry, rY, ry** – 1/4 each).
- **F<sub>2</sub> phenotypic ratio: 9 round yellow : 3 wrinkled yellow : 3 round green : 1 wrinkled green**.
- Derivation: (3 round : 1 wrinkled) × (3 yellow : 1 green) = 9 : 3 : 3 : 1.
- F<sub>2</sub> has **16 combinations**, **9 genotypes**, **4 phenotypes**.
- **Genotypic ratio: 1 RRYY : 2 RRYy : 1 RRyy : 2 RrYY : 4 RrYy : 2 Rryy : 1 rrYY : 2 rrYy : 1 rryy**.

> **Exam Tip:** Number of gametes from an individual heterozygous for n loci = **2<sup>n</sup>**. Heterozygous for 4 loci → **16** types of gametes. Dihybrid test cross (RrYy × rryy) → **1 : 1 : 1 : 1**.

## 7. Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance

### 7.1 Why Mendel's work was not recognised (published 1865, unrecognised till 1900)
1. **Communication was not easy**; work not widely publicised.
2. His concept of **discrete, non-blending factors** was not accepted as an explanation for the apparently **continuous variation** in nature.
3. Using **mathematics** to explain biology was new and unacceptable to many.
4. He could **not provide physical proof** of factors or what they were made of.

### 7.2 Rediscovery and chromosome theory
- **1900** – **de Vries, Correns and von Tschermak** independently rediscovered Mendel's results.
- Advances in microscopy → discovery of **chromosomes** (coloured bodies) that double and divide before each cell division. By **1902**, chromosome movement during meiosis was worked out.
- **Walter Sutton and Theodore Boveri** noted that **chromosome behaviour parallels gene behaviour**:

| Chromosomes | Genes |
|---|---|
| Occur in pairs | Occur in pairs |
| Segregate at gamete formation; only one of each pair goes to a gamete | Segregate at gamete formation; only one of each pair goes to a gamete |
| Independent pairs segregate independently | One pair segregates independently of another |

- The two alleles of a gene pair are on **homologous sites** of **homologous chromosomes**.
- **Sutton** united chromosomal segregation with Mendelian principles → **chromosomal theory of inheritance**.
- **Experimental verification** by **Thomas Hunt Morgan** and colleagues using ***Drosophila melanogaster***.

### 7.3 Why *Drosophila* was suitable
- Can be grown on **simple synthetic medium**.
- Completes life cycle in **about two weeks**.
- A single mating produces a **large number of progeny**.
- **Clear differentiation of sexes** – male and female easily distinguishable.
- Many hereditary variations visible under **low power microscopes**.

## 8. Linkage and Recombination

- **Morgan** carried out dihybrid crosses in *Drosophila* for **sex-linked genes** (on the X chromosome).
- Cross: **yellow-bodied, white-eyed females × brown-bodied, red-eyed males**; F<sub>1</sub> intercrossed → F<sub>2</sub> ratio **deviated significantly from 9:3:3:1**.
- When two genes are on the **same chromosome**, **parental combinations are much higher** than non-parental.
- **Linkage** – physical association of genes on a chromosome (term coined by Morgan).
- **Recombination** – generation of **non-parental gene combinations**.
- **Tightly linked** genes – very low recombination; **loosely linked** – higher recombination.

| Gene pair | Recombination |
|---|---|
| **White (w) and yellow (y)** | **1.3%** (very tightly linked) |
| **White (w) and miniature wing (m)** | **37.2%** (loosely linked) |

- **Alfred Sturtevant** (Morgan's student) used **recombination frequency as a measure of distance between genes** and **mapped** their positions → **genetic maps**. Used as the starting point in whole-genome sequencing (Human Genome Project).

> **Key Point:** Higher recombination frequency = genes farther apart. 1% recombination = 1 map unit (centimorgan). Linkage reduces the proportion of recombinants below 50%.

## 9. Polygenic Inheritance

- Traits not in distinct alternatives but **spread across a gradient** (e.g., **human height, skin colour**).
- Generally controlled by **three or more genes**; also influenced by **environment**.
- **Effect of each allele is additive**.
- Example (skin colour with genes A, B, C): **AABBCC – darkest**; **aabbcc – lightest**; 3 dominant + 3 recessive alleles – **intermediate**.

## 10. Pleiotropy

- **A single gene exhibiting multiple phenotypic expressions** = **pleiotropic gene**.
- Mechanism: effect of a gene on **metabolic pathways** contributing to different phenotypes.
- Example: **phenylketonuria** – mutation in the gene for **phenylalanine hydroxylase** (single gene) → **mental retardation** and **reduced hair and skin pigmentation**.

## 11. Sex Determination

### 11.1 Discovery
- **Henking (1891)** traced a nuclear structure through spermatogenesis in insects; **50% of sperms received it**, 50% did not. He called it the **X body** – later shown to be a chromosome → **X chromosome**.
- **Sex chromosomes** vs **autosomes** (rest of the chromosomes).

### 11.2 Types of sex determination

| Type | Female | Male | Heterogamety | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **XO type** | XX | **XO** (one X only) | **Male** | **Grasshopper** (many insects) |
| **XY type** | XX (homogametic) | **XY** (heterogametic) | **Male** | **Humans, *Drosophila*** |
| **ZW type** | **ZW** (heterogametic) | ZZ | **Female** | **Birds** |
| **Haplodiploidy** | Diploid (2n = 32) | **Haploid (n = 16)** | – | **Honey bee** |

### 11.3 Sex determination in humans (XY)
- **23 pairs**: **22 pairs autosomes** (same in both sexes) + one pair sex chromosomes.
- Female XX; male XY.
- Males produce **50% X-bearing and 50% Y-bearing sperms**; females produce only X-bearing ova.
- **Sex of the child is determined by the sperm** (father). Each pregnancy has **50% probability** of male or female.

### 11.4 Sex determination in honey bee (haplodiploid)
- **Fertilised egg (sperm + egg)** → **female** (queen or worker), **diploid, 32 chromosomes**.
- **Unfertilised egg** → **male (drone)** by **parthenogenesis**, **haploid, 16 chromosomes**.
- Special features: **males produce sperms by mitosis**; males **have no father** and **cannot have sons**, but **have a grandfather** and **can have grandsons**.

> **Exam Tip:** In birds, the **egg (ovum)** determines the sex of the chick, because the female is heterogametic (ZW).

## 12. Mutation

- Alteration of **DNA sequences** → changes in genotype and phenotype. Along with recombination, a source of **variation**.
- **Chromosomal aberrations** – loss (**deletion**) or gain (**insertion/duplication**) of a DNA segment; commonly seen in **cancer cells**.
- **Point mutation** – change in a **single base pair**; example: **sickle-cell anaemia**.
- **Frame-shift mutations** – deletion or insertion of base pairs.
- **Mutagens** – chemical and physical factors inducing mutations; e.g., **UV radiation**.

## 13. Genetic Disorders

### 13.1 Pedigree analysis
- Controlled crosses are not possible in humans → **study of family history** of a trait over generations = **pedigree analysis**.
- Strong tool to trace inheritance of a trait, abnormality or disease; tells whether a trait is **dominant or recessive**, **autosomal or sex-linked**.
- NCERT examples: **autosomal dominant – myotonic dystrophy**; **autosomal recessive – sickle-cell anaemia**.

### 13.2 Two categories

| Mendelian disorders | Chromosomal disorders |
|---|---|
| Alteration/mutation in a **single gene** | **Absence, excess or abnormal arrangement** of one or more chromosomes |
| Follow Mendelian inheritance; traced by pedigree | Studied by **karyotype** analysis |
| Haemophilia, cystic fibrosis, sickle-cell anaemia, colour blindness, phenylketonuria, thalassemia | Down's, Turner's, Klinefelter's syndromes |

## 14. Mendelian Disorders

| Disorder | Inheritance | Cause / features |
|---|---|---|
| **Colour blindness** | **Sex-linked (X) recessive** | Defect in **red or green cone** → failure to discriminate red and green. About **8% of males** and **0.4% of females**. Son of a carrier mother has **50% chance**. Daughter is colour blind only if mother is carrier **and** father is colour blind |
| **Haemophilia** | **Sex-linked (X) recessive** | A **single protein in the clotting cascade** is affected → a simple cut causes **non-stop bleeding**. Carrier female transmits to sons. Female haemophilic is **extremely rare** (mother must be at least carrier and father haemophilic). **Queen Victoria** was a carrier |
| **Sickle-cell anaemia** | **Autosomal recessive** | Alleles **Hb<sup>A</sup> and Hb<sup>S</sup>**; only **Hb<sup>S</sup>Hb<sup>S</sup>** is diseased; **Hb<sup>A</sup>Hb<sup>S</sup>** = carrier (**sickle-cell trait**). **Glutamic acid (Glu) replaced by valine (Val) at the 6th position of β-globin chain**, due to single base substitution at the 6th codon **GAG → GUG**. Mutant Hb **polymerises under low O<sub>2</sub> tension** → RBC changes from biconcave disc to **elongated sickle shape** |
| **Phenylketonuria** | **Autosomal recessive** (inborn error of metabolism) | Lacks enzyme converting **phenylalanine → tyrosine**; phenylalanine accumulates and converts to **phenylpyruvic acid**; accumulation in brain → **mental retardation**; excreted in urine (poor absorption by kidney) |
| **Thalassemia** | **Autosomal recessive** (blood disease) | Mutation or deletion → **reduced rate of synthesis of one of the globin chains (α or β)** → abnormal Hb → **anaemia** |

### 14.1 Thalassemia – types

| Type | Genes | Chromosome |
|---|---|---|
| **α-Thalassemia** | Two closely linked genes **HBA1 and HBA2** (four genes in total from both parents); more genes affected → less α-globin | **Chromosome 16** |
| **β-Thalassemia** | Single gene **HBB** (mutation of one or both) | **Chromosome 11** |

- **Thalassemia = quantitative problem** (too few globin molecules). **Sickle-cell anaemia = qualitative problem** (incorrectly functioning globin).

> **PYQ Trend:** Sickle-cell details (GAG → GUG, Glu → Val, 6th position of β chain), thalassemia chromosome numbers (α – 16, β – 11), quantitative vs qualitative difference, colour-blindness percentages and criss-cross inheritance of X-linked traits are all high-frequency questions.

## 15. Chromosomal Disorders

- **Aneuploidy** – gain or loss of chromosome(s) due to **failure of segregation of chromatids** during cell division.
  - **Trisomy** – one additional chromosome (2n + 1).
  - **Monosomy** – lack of one chromosome of a pair (2n − 1).
- **Polyploidy** – increase in a **whole set of chromosomes** due to **failure of cytokinesis** after telophase; common in **plants**.
- Normal human: **46 chromosomes (23 pairs)** – 22 pairs autosomes + 1 pair sex chromosomes.

| Syndrome | Karyotype | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Down's syndrome** | **Trisomy 21** (47 chromosomes) | First described by **Langdon Down (1866)**. **Short stature, small round head, furrowed (big wrinkled) tongue, partially open mouth**; **broad palm with characteristic palm crease**; flat back of head, broad flat face, many "loops" on finger tips, congenital heart disease; **retarded physical, psychomotor and mental development** |
| **Klinefelter's syndrome** | **47, XXY** (extra X) | **Overall masculine development** but also **feminine development – breasts (gynaecomastia)**; **tall stature**; **sterile** |
| **Turner's syndrome** | **45, XO** (one X missing) | Female; **sterile** – **rudimentary ovaries**; **lack of secondary sexual characters**; **short stature**, underdeveloped feminine character |

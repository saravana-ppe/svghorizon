**NEET UG 2026 Biology – Notes from NCERT**

**NCERT chapters covered:** Class XII – Chapter 4 (Principles of Inheritance and Variation), Chapter 5 (Molecular Basis of Inheritance), Chapter 6 (Evolution)

**NEET syllabus topics in this unit:**

1. **Heredity and variation:** Mendelian inheritance; deviations from Mendelism – incomplete dominance, co-dominance, multiple alleles and inheritance of blood groups, pleiotropy; elementary idea of polygenic inheritance; chromosome theory of inheritance; chromosomes and genes; sex determination – in humans, birds, honey bee; linkage and crossing over; sex-linked inheritance – haemophilia, colour blindness; Mendelian disorders in humans – thalassemia; chromosomal disorders in humans – Down's syndrome, Turner's and Klinefelter's syndromes
2. **Molecular basis of inheritance:** search for genetic material and DNA as genetic material; structure of DNA and RNA; DNA packaging; DNA replication; central dogma; transcription, genetic code, translation; gene expression and regulation – lac operon; genome and human genome project; DNA fingerprinting; protein biosynthesis
3. **Evolution:** origin of life; biological evolution and evidences for biological evolution (paleontology, comparative anatomy, embryology and molecular evidence); Darwin's contribution; modern synthetic theory of evolution; mechanism of evolution – variation (mutation and recombination) and natural selection with examples, types of natural selection; gene flow and genetic drift; Hardy–Weinberg's principle; adaptive radiation; human evolution

> **NEET Focus: How this unit is tested**
> - **Highest-weightage unit in NEET Biology.** Genetics problems (ratios, probability, blood group crosses, pedigree), molecular biology (experiments, enzymes, codons, lac operon) and evolution (homology/analogy, Hardy–Weinberg calculations, human evolution sequence).
> - Numerical questions: Chargaff's rule percentages, number of gametes (2<sup>n</sup>), Meselson–Stahl generations, DNA length calculations, Hardy–Weinberg frequencies.
> - Direct NCERT facts: Morgan's recombination values (1.3% and 37.2%), sickle-cell mutation (GAG → GUG, Glu → Val at 6th position), human genome features, RNA polymerase I/II/III products, brain capacities of human ancestors.

## Quick Revision – High-Yield One-liners

- Mendel: 1856–1863; 14 true-breeding varieties; 7 pairs of traits.
- Monohybrid F<sub>2</sub>: phenotype 3:1, genotype 1:2:1. Dihybrid F<sub>2</sub>: 9:3:3:1; 9 genotypes.
- Test cross (Tt × tt) → 1:1. Gametes = 2<sup>n</sup>.
- Incomplete dominance – snapdragon (1:2:1). Co-dominance – I<sup>A</sup>I<sup>B</sup>.
- ABO: 3 alleles, 6 genotypes, 4 phenotypes.
- Pea starch gene B/b – dominance for shape, incomplete dominance for grain size.
- Rediscovery 1900 – de Vries, Correns, von Tschermak. Chromosomal theory – Sutton and Boveri.
- Morgan – *Drosophila*; linkage; w–y 1.3%, w–m 37.2%. Sturtevant – gene mapping.
- Polygenic – skin colour, height. Pleiotropy – phenylketonuria.
- Henking 1891 – X body. XO – grasshopper; ZW – birds; honey bee 32/16.
- Colour blindness 8% males, 0.4% females. Haemophilia – Queen Victoria carrier.
- Sickle-cell: Glu → Val, 6th position β-chain; GAG → GUG.
- α-thalassemia – HBA1, HBA2 on chr 16; β-thalassemia – HBB on chr 11.
- Down's – trisomy 21 (Langdon Down, 1866); Klinefelter's – 47,XXY; Turner's – 45,XO.
- φ×174 – 5386 nucleotides; lambda – 48,502 bp; *E. coli* – 4.6 × 10<sup>6</sup> bp; human haploid – 3.3 × 10<sup>9</sup> bp.
- Meischer 1869 – nuclein. Watson–Crick 1953. Chargaff – A=T, G=C.
- Pitch 3.4 nm; 10 bp per turn; 0.34 nm between bp. A=T two H-bonds; G≡C three H-bonds.
- Mammalian DNA ≈ 2.2 m. Nucleosome ≈ 200 bp; histone octamer; lysine and arginine.
- Griffith 1928 (*S. pneumoniae*); Avery–MacLeod–McCarty 1933–44; Hershey–Chase 1952.
- Meselson–Stahl 1958 (<sup>15</sup>N, CsCl); Taylor 1958 (*Vicia faba*, radioactive thymidine).
- *E. coli* replicates in 18 min; about 2000 bp/s.
- RNA polymerase I – rRNA (28S, 18S, 5.8S); II – hnRNA; III – tRNA, 5S rRNA, snRNA.
- σ factor – initiation; ρ factor – termination.
- Capping – methyl guanosine triphosphate at 5'; tailing – 200–300 adenylates at 3'.
- Gamow – triplet; Khorana – synthetic RNA; Nirenberg – cell-free system; Ochoa – polynucleotide phosphorylase.
- 64 codons: 61 sense, 3 stop (UAA, UAG, UGA). AUG – Met + start.
- tRNA – clover leaf (2-D), inverted L (3-D). 23S rRNA – ribozyme.
- Lac operon – Jacob and Monod; i (repressor), z (β-gal), y (permease), a (transacetylase).
- HGP: 1990–2003; 3164.7 million bp; about 30,000 genes; dystrophin largest; <2% coding; chr 1 most genes (2968), Y fewest (231); 1.4 million SNPs.
- DNA fingerprinting – Alec Jeffreys; VNTR (mini-satellite), 0.1–20 kb; Southern blot.
- Universe 13.8 billion years; earth 4.5 billion years; life 4 billion years ago.
- Miller 1953: CH<sub>4</sub>, H<sub>2</sub>, NH<sub>3</sub>, water vapour, 800°C, electric discharge → amino acids.
- Homology – divergent; analogy – convergent.
- Industrial melanism – England, 1850s vs 1920.
- Darwin's finches – Galapagos; Australian marsupials – adaptive radiation.
- de Vries – evening primrose, saltation.
- Hardy–Weinberg: p<sup>2</sup> + 2pq + q<sup>2</sup> = 1; five disturbing factors.
- Coelacanth – 1938, South Africa. Dinosaurs extinct 65 mya. *T. rex* – 20 feet.
- Human evolution: *Dryopithecus*/*Ramapithecus* 15 mya → Australopithecines 2 mya → *H. habilis* (650–800 cc) → *H. erectus* 1.5 mya (900 cc) → Neanderthal (1400 cc) → *H. sapiens*.

## Practice Questions (NEET pattern)

1. A cross between Rr (pink) snapdragons gives which phenotypic ratio?
   - (a) 3 red : 1 white
   - (b) 9 : 3 : 3 : 1
   - (c) All pink
   - (d) 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white
2. How many types of gametes can an individual heterozygous for four loci produce?
   - (a) 4
   - (b) 8
   - (c) 32
   - (d) 16
3. A child has blood group O; the father is A and the mother is B. The genotypes of the parents are:
   - (a) I<sup>A</sup>I<sup>A</sup> and I<sup>B</sup>I<sup>B</sup>
   - (b) I<sup>A</sup>i and I<sup>B</sup>i
   - (c) I<sup>A</sup>I<sup>B</sup> and ii
   - (d) I<sup>A</sup>i and I<sup>B</sup>I<sup>B</sup>
4. In honey bee, drones are:
   - (a) Haploid, from unfertilised eggs
   - (b) Diploid, from fertilised eggs
   - (c) Triploid
   - (d) Diploid, from unfertilised eggs
5. Sickle-cell anaemia is caused by substitution of:
   - (a) Glutamic acid by valine at 6th position of β-chain
   - (b) Valine by glutamic acid at 6th position of α-chain
   - (c) Glutamic acid by lysine at 6th position of β-chain
   - (d) Valine by glutamic acid at 6th position of β-chain
6. Klinefelter's syndrome has the karyotype:
   - (a) 45, XO
   - (b) 47, XXX
   - (c) 47, XXY
   - (d) 47, +21
7. If a double-stranded DNA has 20% cytosine, the percentage of adenine is:
   - (a) 20%
   - (b) 40%
   - (c) 30%
   - (d) 60%
8. Hershey and Chase used radioactive sulphur to label:
   - (a) DNA of phage
   - (b) RNA of phage
   - (c) Bacterial DNA
   - (d) Protein coat of phage
9. In the Meselson–Stahl experiment, after 40 minutes in <sup>14</sup>N medium, the DNA was:
   - (a) All heavy
   - (b) All hybrid
   - (c) Half hybrid, half light
   - (d) All light
10. RNA polymerase III transcribes:
    - (a) rRNA 28S, 18S, 5.8S
    - (b) tRNA, 5S rRNA and snRNA
    - (c) hnRNA
    - (d) mRNA only
11. In the lac operon, the y gene codes for:
    - (a) β-galactosidase
    - (b) Transacetylase
    - (c) Permease
    - (d) Repressor
12. Which chromosome has the fewest genes in humans?
    - (a) Chromosome 1
    - (b) Chromosome 21
    - (c) X chromosome
    - (d) Y chromosome
13. The eye of octopus and the eye of mammals are an example of:
    - (a) Homologous organs; divergent evolution
    - (b) Adaptive radiation
    - (c) Vestigial organs
    - (d) Analogous organs; convergent evolution
14. In a population in Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, the frequency of the recessive phenotype is 0.09. The frequency of heterozygotes is:
    - (a) 0.21
    - (b) 0.42
    - (c) 0.49
    - (d) 0.09
15. The brain capacity of *Homo erectus* was about:
    - (a) 650–800 cc
    - (b) 1400 cc
    - (c) 900 cc
    - (d) 1650 cc

**Answers:** 1–d, 2–d, 3–b, 4–a, 5–a, 6–c, 7–c, 8–d, 9–c, 10–b, 11–c, 12–d, 13–d, 14–b, 15–c

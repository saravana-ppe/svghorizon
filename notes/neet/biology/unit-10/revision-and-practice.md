**NEET UG 2026 Biology – Notes from NCERT**

**NCERT chapters covered:** Class XII – Chapter 11 (Organisms and Populations), Chapter 12 (Ecosystem), Chapter 13 (Biodiversity and Conservation)

**NEET syllabus topics in this unit:**

1. **Organisms and environment:** population interactions – mutualism, competition, predation, parasitism; population attributes – growth, birth rate and death rate, age distribution
2. **Ecosystem:** patterns, components; productivity and decomposition; energy flow; pyramids of number, biomass, energy
3. **Biodiversity and its conservation:** concept of biodiversity; patterns of biodiversity; importance of biodiversity; loss of biodiversity; biodiversity conservation; hotspots, endangered organisms, extinction, Red Data Book, biosphere reserves, national parks, sanctuaries and sacred groves

> **NEET Focus: How this unit is tested**
> - Ecology contributes about **6–8 questions** every year – one of the highest-yield units, and largely factual/numerical.
> - Population: birth-rate/death-rate calculations, N<sub>t+1</sub> equation, exponential vs logistic equations, *r* values, age pyramids, J vs S curves.
> - Interactions: the +/–/0 table, examples (Pisaster, Balanus–Chthamalus, Abingdon tortoise, Monarch butterfly, *Calotropis*, fig–wasp, *Ophrys*, cuckoo, cattle egret, clown fish).
> - Ecosystem: GPP – R = NPP, 170 vs 55 billion tons, PAR (<50%) and 2–10% capture, steps of decomposition, 10% law, standing crop, inverted pyramids.
> - Biodiversity: Edward Wilson, Robert May (about 7 million), India 2.4% land / 8.1% diversity, species–area equation and Z values, Evil Quartet, examples of extinct species, hotspots (34; 3 in India), numbers of BR/NP/WLS, sacred groves, Rio 1992 and Johannesburg 2002.

## Quick Revision – One-liners

- Ecology studies organisms, populations, communities and biomes; Ramdeo Misra = Father of Ecology in India.
- Population attributes: birth rate, death rate, sex ratio, age distribution, density.
- Birth rate 8/20 = 0.4 per lotus per year; death rate 4/40 = 0.1 per fruitfly per week.
- Age pyramids: triangular = expanding; bell = stable; urn = declining.
- Density: per cent cover/biomass for banyan vs *Parthenium*; fish per trap = relative density; tiger census = pug marks and fecal pellets.
- N<sub>t+1</sub> = N<sub>t</sub> + [(B + I) – (D + E)]; immigration matters when a new habitat is being colonised.
- Exponential: dN/dt = rN; N<sub>t</sub> = N<sub>0</sub>e<sup>rt</sup>; J-shaped curve.
- r: Norway rat 0.015; flour beetle 0.12; India (1981) 0.0205.
- Logistic: dN/dt = rN(K – N)/K; S-shaped; Verhulst–Pearl; more realistic.
- Breed once: Pacific salmon, bamboo. Many small offspring: oysters, pelagic fishes.
- Mutualism +/+; competition –/–; predation and parasitism +/–; commensalism +/0; amensalism –/0.
- Prickly pear in Australia controlled by a cactus-feeding moth.
- *Pisaster* removal → more than 10 invertebrate species extinct within a year.
- Monarch butterfly – distasteful chemical from a poisonous weed eaten as a caterpillar.
- *Calotropis* – cardiac glycosides; about 25% of insects are phytophagous.
- Flamingoes and fishes compete for zooplankton in South American lakes.
- Abingdon tortoise extinct after goats were introduced (Galapagos).
- Connell: *Balanus* excludes *Chthamalus* (Scotland) – competitive release.
- Gause – competitive exclusion; MacArthur – 5 warbler species show resource partitioning.
- Liver fluke – snail and fish as intermediate hosts; *Cuscuta* – no chlorophyll or leaves.
- Brood parasitism: cuckoo (koel) lays eggs in the crow's nest.
- Commensalism: orchid on mango, barnacles on whale, cattle egret with cattle, clown fish with sea anemone.
- Mutualism: lichens, mycorrhizae, fig–wasp, *Ophrys* and bee (sexual deceit, pseudocopulation).
- Stratification: trees on top, shrubs, then herbs and grasses.
- GPP – R = NPP; secondary productivity = new organic matter by consumers.
- Biosphere NPP 170 billion t; oceans 55 billion t despite covering 70% of the surface.
- Decomposition: fragmentation → leaching → catabolism → humification → mineralisation.
- Lignin/chitin slow it; nitrogen and sugars speed it up; warm and moist favours it.
- PAR is less than 50% of incident radiation; plants capture 2–10% of PAR.
- GFC is the major conduit in aquatic ecosystems; DFC in terrestrial ones.
- 10% law; standing crop; dry weight is more accurate.
- Energy pyramid is always upright; biomass pyramid is inverted in the sea; number pyramid is inverted for a tree.
- Pyramid limitations: ignore multiple trophic levels, food webs and saprophytes.
- Carbon: gaseous cycle; phosphorus: sedimentary cycle.
- Biodiversity term popularised by Edward Wilson; genetic, species and ecological levels.
- *Rauwolfia vomitoria* – reserpine; India has 50,000+ rice strains and 1,000 mango varieties.
- About 1.5 million species described (IUCN 2004); Robert May estimates about 7 million.
- Animals >70%, of which insects >70%; plants ≤22%.
- India: 2.4% of land, 8.1% of diversity, one of 12 mega-diversity countries; about 45,000 plant species.
- Birds: Colombia 1,400; New York 105; Greenland 56; India 1,200+.
- log S = log C + Z log A; Z = 0.1–0.2 (regions), 0.6–1.2 (continents), 1.15 (frugivores).
- Humboldt – species–area; Tilman – diversity → stability and productivity; Ehrlich – rivet popper.
- IUCN 2004: 784 extinctions in 500 years (338 vertebrates, 359 invertebrates, 87 plants).
- Dodo – Mauritius; quagga – Africa; thylacine – Australia; Steller's sea cow – Russia.
- Threatened: birds 12%, mammals 23%, amphibians 32%, gymnosperms 31%.
- Sixth extinction is 100–1,000 times faster.
- Evil Quartet: habitat loss (most important), over-exploitation, alien invasions, co-extinctions.
- Nile perch → loss of 200+ cichlids; *Clarias gariepinus* threatens native catfishes.
- More than 25% of drugs come from plants; Amazon gives 20% of the oxygen.
- 34 hotspots (3 in India) cover less than 2% of land and could cut extinctions by about 30%.
- India: 14 biosphere reserves, 90 national parks, 448 wildlife sanctuaries.
- Sacred groves: Khasi–Jaintia (Meghalaya), Aravalli (Rajasthan), Western Ghats (Karnataka, Maharashtra), Sarguja–Chanda–Bastar.
- Earth Summit – Rio 1992; WSSD – Johannesburg 2002 (190 countries, target 2010).

## Practice MCQs (NEET pattern)

1. In a pond there were 20 lotus plants last year; 8 new plants were added through reproduction. The birth rate is:
   - (a) 0.2
   - (b) 2.5
   - (c) 8
   - (d) 0.4
2. The logistic growth equation is:
   - (a) dN/dt = rN (K – N)/K
   - (b) N<sub>t</sub> = N<sub>0</sub>e<sup>rt</sup>
   - (c) dN/dt = rN
   - (d) dN/dt = (b + d)N
3. An urn-shaped age pyramid indicates a population that is:
   - (a) Expanding
   - (b) Stable
   - (c) Growing exponentially
   - (d) Declining
4. The interaction in which one species is harmed and the other is unaffected is:
   - (a) Commensalism
   - (b) Competition
   - (c) Parasitism
   - (d) Amensalism
5. Removal of *Pisaster* from an intertidal area led to extinction of more than 10 invertebrate species because of:
   - (a) Predation
   - (b) Parasitism
   - (c) Interspecific competition
   - (d) Mutualism
6. *Balanus* excluding *Chthamalus* on the rocky coasts of Scotland was demonstrated by:
   - (a) Gause
   - (b) MacArthur
   - (c) Connell
   - (d) Tilman
7. The Mediterranean orchid *Ophrys* ensures pollination through:
   - (a) Nectar reward
   - (b) Sexual deceit
   - (c) Brood parasitism
   - (d) Wind
8. Which is the correct sequence in decomposition?
   - (a) Catabolism → leaching → fragmentation → humification
   - (b) Fragmentation → leaching → catabolism → humification → mineralisation
   - (c) Leaching → fragmentation → mineralisation → humification
   - (d) Humification → catabolism → fragmentation → leaching
9. Of the incident solar radiation, PAR forms:
   - (a) 100%
   - (b) 1%
   - (c) 2–10%
   - (d) Less than 50%
10. The pyramid that can never be inverted is the pyramid of:
    - (a) Number
    - (b) Biomass
    - (c) Energy
    - (d) Both biomass and energy
11. In a terrestrial ecosystem, the major part of energy flows through:
    - (a) Detritus food chain
    - (b) Grazing food chain
    - (c) Parasitic food chain
    - (d) Predator chain
12. In the species–area relationship, the value of Z for very large areas such as entire continents lies in the range:
    - (a) 0.1–0.2
    - (b) 0.2–0.4
    - (c) 0.6–1.2
    - (d) 1.5–2.0
13. The "rivet popper hypothesis" was proposed by:
    - (a) Edward Wilson
    - (b) David Tilman
    - (c) Paul Ehrlich
    - (d) Robert May
14. The most important cause of biodiversity loss is:
    - (a) Over-exploitation
    - (b) Alien species invasion
    - (c) Co-extinction
    - (d) Habitat loss and fragmentation
15. Which of the following is NOT one of the biodiversity hotspots covering India according to NCERT?
    - (a) Western Ghats and Sri Lanka
    - (b) Eastern Ghats
    - (c) Himalaya
    - (d) Indo-Burma
16. Sacred groves in Meghalaya are found in the:
    - (a) Khasi and Jaintia Hills
    - (b) Aravalli Hills
    - (c) Bastar area
    - (d) Western Ghats

**Answers:** 1–d, 2–a, 3–d, 4–d, 5–c, 6–c, 7–b, 8–b, 9–d, 10–c, 11–a, 12–c, 13–c, 14–d, 15–b, 16–a

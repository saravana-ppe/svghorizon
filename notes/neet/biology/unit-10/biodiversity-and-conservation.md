## 1. Biodiversity – Concept and Levels

- Numbers to remember: **>20,000 species of ants, 3,00,000 of beetles, 28,000 of fishes, nearly 20,000 of orchids**.
- **Biodiversity** – term popularised by the sociobiologist **Edward Wilson** – the **combined diversity at all levels of biological organisation**.

| Level | Meaning | NCERT example |
|---|---|---|
| **Genetic diversity** | Variation at the **genetic level within a single species** over its distributional range | ***Rauwolfia vomitoria*** in different **Himalayan ranges** – variation in potency and concentration of **reserpine**. India has **>50,000 genetically different strains of rice** and **1,000 varieties of mango** |
| **Species diversity** | Diversity at the **species level** | **Western Ghats have greater amphibian species diversity than the Eastern Ghats** |
| **Ecological diversity** | Diversity at the **ecosystem level** | India (deserts, rain forests, mangroves, coral reefs, wetlands, estuaries, alpine meadows) has greater ecosystem diversity than **Norway** |

### 1.1 How many species on Earth and in India?

- **IUCN (2004):** total described plant and animal species – **slightly more than 1.5 million**.
- Estimation method: **statistical comparison of temperate–tropical species richness** of an exhaustively studied insect group, then **extrapolation** to other groups.
- Extreme estimates: **20 to 50 million**; conservative, scientifically sound estimate by **Robert May: about 7 million**.

| Global fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Animals among recorded species | **>70%** |
| Plants (incl. algae, fungi, bryophytes, gymnosperms, angiosperms) | **no more than 22%** |
| Insects among animals | **>70%** (7 of every 10 animals are insects) |
| Fungi | **More species than fishes + amphibians + reptiles + mammals combined** |
| Prokaryotes | **No figures** – conventional taxonomy unsuitable; many not culturable; could run into **millions** |

- **India:** only **2.4% of the world's land area**, but **8.1% of global species diversity** → one of the **12 mega diversity countries**.
  - Nearly **45,000 species of plants** and **twice as many animals** recorded.
  - Applying May's estimate (only **22%** of species recorded so far): India probably has **>1,00,000 plant species** and **>3,00,000 animal species** yet to be discovered.
- "**Nature's biological library is burning** even before we catalogued the titles of all the books."

## 2. Patterns of Biodiversity

### 2.1 Latitudinal gradients

- **Species diversity decreases from the equator towards the poles**. **Tropics (23.5° N to 23.5° S)** harbour more species than temperate or polar areas.

| Place | Bird species |
|---|---|
| **Colombia** (near equator) | **about 1,400** |
| **New York (41° N)** | **105** |
| **Greenland (71° N)** | **56** |
| **India** | **>1,200** |

- A tropical forest (**Ecuador**) has up to **10 times** as many vascular plant species as an equal area of temperate forest (**Midwest USA**).
- **Amazonian rain forest** – **greatest biodiversity on earth**: **>40,000 plants, 3,000 fishes, 1,300 birds, 427 mammals, 427 amphibians, 378 reptiles, >1,25,000 invertebrates**; possibly **at least two million insect species** yet to be discovered.

> **Key Point: Why are the tropics more diverse? (three hypotheses)**
> 1. **Speciation is a function of time** – temperate regions had frequent **glaciations**; tropics remained **relatively undisturbed for millions of years** → long evolutionary time for diversification.
> 2. Tropical environments are **less seasonal, relatively more constant and predictable** → promote **niche specialisation** → greater diversity.
> 3. **More solar energy** in the tropics → **higher productivity** → indirectly greater diversity.

### 2.2 Species–area relationships

- **Alexander von Humboldt** (German naturalist and geographer), in South American jungles, observed that **within a region species richness increases with increasing explored area, but only up to a limit**.
- The relationship is a **rectangular hyperbola**; on a **logarithmic scale** it is a **straight line**:

> **Remember: Species–area equation**
> **log S = log C + Z log A**
> S = species richness; A = area; **Z = slope of the line (regression coefficient)**; C = Y-intercept.
> (Equivalent form: S = CA<sup>Z</sup>)

| Scale | Z value |
|---|---|
| Within a region (plants in Britain, birds in California, molluscs in New York state) | **0.1 to 0.2** – amazingly similar regardless of taxon or region |
| **Very large areas like entire continents** | **Much steeper: 0.6 to 1.2** |
| **Frugivorous birds and mammals in tropical forests of different continents** | **1.15** |

- **Steeper slope** means species richness increases much faster with area (areas are more distinct – less overlap in species).

## 3. Importance of Species Diversity to the Ecosystem

- Ecologists long believed **communities with more species tend to be more stable**.
- A **stable community**: (a) should not show **too much variation in productivity** year to year; (b) must be **resistant or resilient** to occasional disturbances; (c) must be **resistant to invasions by alien species**.
- **David Tilman's** long-term outdoor-plot experiments:
  - Plots with **more species showed less year-to-year variation in total biomass**.
  - **Increased diversity contributed to higher productivity**.

> **Remember: Rivet popper hypothesis – Paul Ehrlich (Stanford ecologist)**
> - **Airplane = ecosystem**; **rivets = species**.
> - Passengers popping rivets (species extinction) may not affect flight safety initially, but as more are removed, the plane becomes **dangerously weak**.
> - **Which rivet** is removed matters: rivets on the **wings** (**key species** driving major ecosystem functions) are more critical than rivets on seats or windows.

## 4. Loss of Biodiversity

- The biological wealth of our planet is declining rapidly, mainly due to **human activities**.
- Colonisation of **tropical Pacific Islands** by humans → extinction of **>2,000 species of native birds**.
- **IUCN Red List (2004)**: extinction of **784 species** in the last **500 years** – **338 vertebrates, 359 invertebrates, 87 plants**.
- **Recent extinctions:**

| Species | Place |
|---|---|
| **Dodo** | **Mauritius** |
| **Quagga** | **Africa** |
| **Thylacine** | **Australia** |
| **Steller's sea cow** | **Russia** |
| **Three subspecies of tiger – Bali, Javan, Caspian** | – |

- **Last twenty years: 27 species** disappeared.
- Extinctions are **not random** – **amphibians** appear more vulnerable.
- **>15,500 species** worldwide face the threat of extinction (**>650 from India**).

| Group | % facing threat of extinction |
|---|---|
| **Birds** | **12%** |
| **Mammals** | **23%** |
| **Amphibians** | **32%** |
| **Gymnosperms** | **31%** |

- **Five episodes of mass extinction** in > 3 billion years of life's history. The **"Sixth Extinction"** now in progress differs in **rate** – **100 to 1,000 times faster** than pre-human times, due to human activities.
- If trends continue, **nearly half of all species** might be wiped out within **100 years**.

### 4.1 Consequences of biodiversity loss in a region

1. **Decline in plant production**
2. **Lowered resistance to environmental perturbations** such as drought
3. **Increased variability** in certain ecosystem processes – plant productivity, water use, and pest and disease cycles

### 4.2 Causes – "The Evil Quartet"

| Cause | Key points and examples |
|---|---|
| **1. Habitat loss and fragmentation** | **Most important cause**. **Tropical rain forests** – once covered **>14%** of earth's land surface, now **no more than 6%**. **Amazon rain forest ("lungs of the planet")** cleared for **soya beans** and **grasslands for beef cattle**. Degradation by **pollution** also. **Fragmentation** badly affects **mammals and birds requiring large territories** and **migratory animals** |
| **2. Over-exploitation** | "Need" turns to "**greed**". **Steller's sea cow, passenger pigeon** – extinct due to over-exploitation in the last 500 years. Many **marine fish** populations over-harvested |
| **3. Alien species invasions** | **Nile perch** introduced into **Lake Victoria** (East Africa) → extinction of **>200 species of cichlid fish**. Invasive weeds: **carrot grass (*Parthenium*), *Lantana*, water hyacinth (*Eichhornia*)**. **African catfish *Clarias gariepinus*** illegally introduced for aquaculture – threat to **indigenous catfishes** |
| **4. Co-extinctions** | When a species becomes extinct, species associated with it **obligatorily** also become extinct – e.g., a **host fish** and its **unique assemblage of parasites**; **co-evolved plant–pollinator mutualism** |

> **PYQ Trend**
> "Which is the most important cause of biodiversity loss?" – **Habitat loss and fragmentation**. Match-the-column on Nile perch – cichlids (Lake Victoria), Steller's sea cow (over-exploitation), and invasive weeds is very common.

## 5. Biodiversity Conservation

### 5.1 Why conserve biodiversity?

| Argument | Meaning and examples |
|---|---|
| **Narrowly utilitarian** | **Direct economic benefits** – food (cereals, pulses, fruits), firewood, fibre, construction material, industrial products (**tannins, lubricants, dyes, resins, perfumes**), medicines. **>25% of drugs** sold worldwide are derived from **plants**; **25,000 plant species** contribute to traditional medicines. **Bioprospecting** – exploring molecular, genetic and species-level diversity for products of economic importance |
| **Broadly utilitarian** | **Ecosystem services** – the **Amazon forest** produces about **20% of the total oxygen** in earth's atmosphere through photosynthesis; **pollination** by bees, bumblebees, birds and bats; **aesthetic pleasures** (walking in woods, spring flowers, bulbul's song) |
| **Ethical** | Every species has an **intrinsic value**; we have a **moral duty** to care for their well-being and pass on our biological legacy in good order to future generations |

### 5.2 How do we conserve biodiversity?

| Feature | ***In situ*** (on site) | ***Ex situ*** (off site) |
|---|---|---|
| Approach | Protect the **whole ecosystem** – "**save the entire forest to save the tiger**" | Threatened animals and plants **taken out of their natural habitat** and placed in special settings |
| When used | General protection of biodiversity at all levels | When an organism is **endangered/threatened** and needs **urgent measures** |
| Examples | **Biodiversity hotspots, biosphere reserves, national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, sacred groves** | **Zoological parks, botanical gardens, wildlife safari parks**; **cryopreservation of gametes**, ***in vitro* fertilisation**, **tissue culture propagation**, **seed banks** |

### 5.3 Biodiversity hotspots

- Regions with **very high levels of species richness** and a **high degree of endemism** (species **confined to that region** and not found anywhere else).
- Initially **25** hotspots identified; **nine more** added → total **34** hotspots in the world.
- Hotspots are also regions of **accelerated habitat loss**.
- **Three hotspots cover India's high-biodiversity regions:**
  1. **Western Ghats and Sri Lanka**
  2. **Indo-Burma**
  3. **Himalaya**
- All hotspots together cover **less than 2% of the earth's land area**, but strict protection could reduce ongoing mass extinctions by **almost 30%**.

> **Note: Current count**
> Conservation International now lists **36** hotspots (Sundaland/Nicobar lies in India's region too), but **NEET follows NCERT – answer 34 and three for India** unless options clearly say otherwise.

### 5.4 Protected areas in India (NCERT figures)

| Type | Number (NCERT) |
|---|---|
| **Biosphere reserves** | **14** |
| **National parks** | **90** |
| **Wildlife sanctuaries** | **448** (summary says ">450") |

- Biosphere reserves have **zones**: a **core zone** (strictly protected, no human activity), a **buffer zone** (limited research/educational activity) and a **transition zone** (human settlements, sustainable use) – *standard concept beyond current NCERT text*.

### 5.5 Sacred groves

- **Tracts of forest set aside** in many cultures; **all trees and wildlife venerated and given total protection**.

| Region | Location |
|---|---|
| **Khasi and Jaintia Hills** | **Meghalaya** |
| **Aravalli Hills** | **Rajasthan** |
| **Western Ghat regions** | **Karnataka and Maharashtra** |
| **Sarguja, Chanda and Bastar areas** | **Madhya Pradesh** (NCERT wording) |

- In **Meghalaya**, sacred groves are the **last refuges for a large number of rare and threatened plants**.

### 5.6 Red Data Book and endangered organisms [Beyond current NCERT]

> **Note**
> The NEET 2026 syllabus names the "Red Data Book", which is no longer described in the NCERT reprint. The essentials below are standard and have been asked.

- **Red Data Book / Red List** – maintained by the **IUCN (International Union for Conservation of Nature and Natural Resources)**, headquartered in **Gland, Switzerland**; documents species facing risk of extinction.
- Main IUCN categories (in order of increasing threat among the "threatened" group):
  - **Vulnerable (VU)** → **Endangered (EN)** → **Critically Endangered (CR)**
  - Also: **Extinct (EX)**, **Extinct in the Wild (EW)**, **Near Threatened (NT)**, **Least Concern (LC)**, **Data Deficient (DD)**, **Not Evaluated (NE)**.
- **Endangered/threatened organisms** – NCERT defines them as organisms **facing a very high risk of extinction in the wild in the near future**.
- **Extinct in the wild** species may survive only in captivity (**zoological parks**) – a role of *ex situ* conservation.

### 5.7 International efforts

| Event | Year / place | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Convention on Biological Diversity ("The Earth Summit")** | **1992, Rio de Janeiro** | Called on all nations to take measures for **conservation of biodiversity and sustainable utilisation** of its benefits |
| **World Summit on Sustainable Development** | **2002, Johannesburg, South Africa** | **190 countries** pledged to achieve **by 2010** a **significant reduction in the current rate of biodiversity loss** |

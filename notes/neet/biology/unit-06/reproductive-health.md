## 1. Reproductive Health – Meaning and Strategies

- **WHO definition:** reproductive health means **total well-being in all aspects of reproduction – physical, emotional, behavioural and social**.
- **India** was among the first countries to initiate national programmes for reproductive health: **"family planning" in 1951**. Now called **Reproductive and Child Health Care (RCH) programmes**.

**Strategies:**
- Creating **awareness** through audio-visual and print media; role of parents, relatives, teachers and friends.
- **Sex education in schools** – to give right information and discourage myths and misconceptions.
- Information about reproductive organs, adolescence, safe and hygienic sexual practices, STDs, AIDS.
- Educating couples about **birth control options, care of pregnant mothers, post-natal care, breast feeding, equal opportunities for male and female child**.
- Awareness of problems of **uncontrolled population growth, sex abuse and sex-related crimes**.
- Strong **infrastructure, professional expertise and material support** for medical care in pregnancy, delivery, STDs, abortions, contraception, menstrual problems, infertility.
- **Statutory ban on amniocentesis for sex determination** (to check **female foeticide**); **massive child immunisation**.
- Research: **"Saheli"** – new oral contraceptive for females developed by **Central Drug Research Institute (CDRI), Lucknow**.

**Indicators of improved reproductive health:** better awareness; more medically assisted deliveries and better post-natal care → **decreased maternal and infant mortality rates**; more couples with small families; better detection and cure of STDs.

### 1.1 Amniocentesis
- Some **amniotic fluid** of the developing foetus is taken to analyse **fetal cells and dissolved substances**.
- Used to test for **genetic disorders** such as **Down syndrome, haemophilia, sickle-cell anaemia**, and to determine the survivability of the foetus.
- **Misuse for sex determination** (followed by MTP of female foetus) is illegal and **banned**.

## 2. Population Stabilisation and Birth Control

### 2.1 Population explosion
- World population: about **2 billion in 1900** → about **6 billion by 2000** → **7.2 billion in 2011**.
- India: about **350 million at independence** → close to **1 billion by 2000** → crossed **1.2 billion in May 2011**.
- **Reasons:** rapid decline in **death rate, maternal mortality rate (MMR) and infant mortality rate (IMR)**, and increase in the number of people in **reproducible age**.
- 2011 census: population growth rate **less than 2% (20/1000/year)**.
- **Measures:** motivating smaller families with contraceptives ("**Hum Do Hamare Do**"; "one child norm"); **raising marriageable age** – **females 18 years, males 21 years**; incentives for small families.

### 2.2 Ideal contraceptive
- **User-friendly, easily available, effective and reversible**, with **no or least side-effects**; should **not interfere with sexual drive, desire or the sexual act**.

## 3. Contraceptive Methods

### 3.1 Natural / traditional methods
- Principle: **avoid chances of ovum and sperm meeting**. No medicines/devices → side effects almost nil, but **failure rate high**.

| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| **Periodic abstinence** | Avoid coitus from **day 10 to 17** of the menstrual cycle (**fertile period**, when ovulation is expected) |
| **Withdrawal (coitus interruptus)** | Male withdraws penis just **before ejaculation** to avoid insemination |
| **Lactational amenorrhea** | No ovulation (and no cycle) during intense lactation after parturition; effective only up to **maximum six months** after parturition, while the mother **fully breast-feeds** |

### 3.2 Barrier methods
- Physically prevent ovum and sperm from meeting.

| Method | Details |
|---|---|
| **Condoms** (male and female) | Thin **rubber/latex sheath** covering the penis (male) or vagina and cervix (female) before coitus. **"Nirodh"** – popular male condom brand. **Additional benefit: protection from STIs and AIDS**. Disposable, self-inserted, privacy |
| **Diaphragms, cervical caps and vaults** | Rubber barriers inserted into the female tract to **cover the cervix**; **reusable** |
| **Spermicidal creams, jellies and foams** | Used **along with barriers** to increase contraceptive efficiency |

### 3.3 Intra Uterine Devices (IUDs)
- Inserted by doctors or expert nurses into the **uterus through the vagina**.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Non-medicated IUDs** | **Lippes loop** |
| **Copper-releasing IUDs** | **CuT, Cu7, Multiload 375** |
| **Hormone-releasing IUDs** | **Progestasert, LNG-20** |

- **Mode of action:**
  - IUDs **increase phagocytosis of sperms** within the uterus.
  - **Cu ions** suppress **sperm motility and fertilising capacity**.
  - **Hormone-releasing IUDs** also make the **uterus unsuitable for implantation** and the **cervix hostile to sperms**.
- **Ideal for females who want to delay pregnancy and/or space children**. One of the **most widely accepted methods in India**.

### 3.4 Oral contraceptives (pills)
- Small doses of **progestogens** or **progestogen–estrogen combinations**.
- Taken daily for **21 days**, starting preferably within the **first five days** of the menstrual cycle; after a **gap of 7 days** (menstruation occurs), repeated.
- **Mode of action:** **inhibit ovulation and implantation**; **alter the quality of cervical mucus** to prevent/retard sperm entry.
- Very effective, fewer side effects, well accepted.
- **Saheli** – **non-steroidal** preparation; **"once a week" pill**; very few side effects, high contraceptive value (developed by CDRI, Lucknow).

### 3.5 Injectables and implants
- **Progestogens alone or with estrogen** as **injections** or **implants under the skin**.
- Mode of action similar to pills; **effective periods much longer**.

### 3.6 Emergency contraception
- **Progestogens or progestogen–estrogen combinations or IUDs within 72 hours of coitus** – very effective to avoid possible pregnancy due to **rape or casual unprotected intercourse**.

### 3.7 Surgical methods (sterilisation)
- **Terminal method** to prevent further pregnancies; blocks **gamete transport**.

| Procedure | Sex | What is done |
|---|---|---|
| **Vasectomy** | Male | Small part of **vas deferens removed or tied up** through a small incision on the **scrotum** |
| **Tubectomy** | Female | Small part of the **fallopian tube removed or tied up** through a small incision in the **abdomen or through vagina** |

- **Highly effective but reversibility is very poor.**
- (Surgical methods block gamete transport; they **do not prevent gamete formation**. Removal of gonads is not a contraceptive option because it stops hormone production.)

### 3.8 Cautions
- Contraceptive choice should be made **with qualified medical professionals**.
- Contraceptives are **not regular requirements** for reproductive health – they are used against a natural event.
- Possible ill-effects: **nausea, abdominal pain, breakthrough bleeding, irregular menstrual bleeding, even breast cancer**.

> **PYQ Trend:** Match IUD types (Lippes loop – non-medicated; CuT, Cu7, Multiload 375 – copper; Progestasert, LNG-20 – hormonal); Saheli (non-steroidal, weekly, CDRI Lucknow); lactational amenorrhea up to 6 months; emergency contraception within 72 hours; mode of action of pills.

## 4. Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP)

- **Intentional or voluntary termination of pregnancy before full term** = **MTP or induced abortion**.
- About **45–50 million MTPs per year** worldwide = **1/5th of total conceived pregnancies** per year.
- Debated due to emotional, ethical, religious and social issues.
- **India legalised MTP in 1971** with strict conditions (to check misuse and female foeticide).

### 4.1 MTP (Amendment) Act, 2017
- Aim: reduce **illegal abortion** and consequent **maternal mortality and morbidity**.

| Duration of pregnancy | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Within first 12 weeks** | Opinion of **one registered medical practitioner** |
| **More than 12 weeks but fewer than 24 weeks** | **Two registered medical practitioners** must be of the opinion, in good faith, that grounds exist |

**Grounds:**
1. Continuation would involve **risk to the life of the pregnant woman** or **grave injury to physical or mental health**; or
2. **Substantial risk** that the child, if born, would suffer from **physical or mental abnormalities** so as to be seriously handicapped.

### 4.2 Why MTP?
- To get rid of **unwanted pregnancies** (casual unprotected intercourse, contraceptive failure, rape).
- When continuation of pregnancy could be **harmful or fatal** to mother, foetus or both.

### 4.3 Safety and concerns
- MTPs are relatively **safe during the first trimester (up to 12 weeks)**. **Second trimester abortions are much riskier**.
- Disturbing trends: majority of MTPs performed **illegally by unqualified quacks** (unsafe, could be fatal); **misuse of amniocentesis** for sex determination followed by MTP of female foetus.
- Solution: counselling, avoiding unprotected coitus, more health care facilities.

## 5. Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)

- Infections transmitted through sexual intercourse = **STIs / venereal diseases (VD) / reproductive tract infections (RTI)**.
- Common STIs: **gonorrhoea, syphilis, genital herpes, chlamydiasis, genital warts, trichomoniasis, hepatitis-B, HIV (AIDS)**. HIV is the most dangerous.
- **Hepatitis-B and HIV** can also spread by **sharing injection needles, surgical instruments, blood transfusion, and from infected mother to foetus**.
- **Not completely curable: hepatitis-B, genital herpes and HIV.** Others are **completely curable** if detected early and treated properly.
- Early symptoms: **itching, fluid discharge, slight pain, swellings** in the genital region.
- **Infected females may often be asymptomatic** → remain undetected.
- Social stigma deters timely treatment.
- **Complications:** **pelvic inflammatory diseases (PID), abortions, still births, ectopic pregnancies, infertility, even cancer of the reproductive tract**.
- Incidence is very high in the **15–24 years** age group.

**Prevention:**
1. **Avoid sex with unknown/multiple partners.**
2. **Always use condoms** during coitus.
3. In case of doubt, go to a qualified doctor for **early detection** and complete treatment.

> **Common Mistake:** "All STDs are completely curable" is FALSE. Hepatitis-B, genital herpes and HIV are not completely curable. (NCERT Exercise Q12.)

## 6. Infertility and Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ART)

- **Infertility** – inability to conceive or produce children **even after 2 years of unprotected sexual cohabitation**.
- Causes: **physical, congenital, diseases, drugs, immunological or psychological**.
- In India the female is often blamed, but **more often than not the problem lies in the male partner**.
- Infertility clinics help diagnosis and corrective treatment; where correction is impossible, **ART** is used.

| Technique | Description |
|---|---|
| **IVF–ET (In Vitro Fertilisation – Embryo Transfer)** | "**Test tube baby**" programme. Ova from wife/donor and sperms from husband/donor are induced to form a zygote under **simulated conditions in the laboratory**; followed by embryo transfer |
| **ZIFT (Zygote Intra Fallopian Transfer)** | **Zygote or early embryo (up to 8 blastomeres)** transferred into the **fallopian tube** |
| **IUT (Intra Uterine Transfer)** | **Embryos with more than 8 blastomeres** transferred into the **uterus** |
| **In vivo fertilisation + transfer** | Embryos formed by fusion of gametes **within the female** can also be transferred to females who cannot conceive |
| **GIFT (Gamete Intra Fallopian Transfer)** | **Ovum from a donor** transferred into the **fallopian tube** of another female who **cannot produce ova** but can provide a suitable environment for fertilisation and development |
| **ICSI (Intra Cytoplasmic Sperm Injection)** | **A sperm is directly injected into the ovum** in the laboratory to form an embryo |
| **AI (Artificial Insemination)** | For male inability to inseminate or **very low sperm count**: semen from husband or healthy donor is artificially introduced into the **vagina** or **uterus (IUI – intra-uterine insemination)** |

- These techniques need **high precision, specialised professionals and expensive instruments** → available in few centres; affordable to few.
- Emotional, religious and social factors are deterrents.
- **Legal adoption** is one of the best options for couples wanting parenthood (many orphaned/destitute children in India).

> **Exam Tip:** ZIFT – zygote/embryo up to 8 blastomeres into **fallopian tube**. IUT – embryo more than 8 blastomeres into **uterus**. GIFT – **gamete (ovum)** into fallopian tube. ICSI – sperm **injected into ovum**. IUI – **semen** into uterus. "In ET, embryos are always transferred into the uterus" is FALSE (ZIFT goes into the fallopian tube).

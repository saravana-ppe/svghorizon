## 1. Flower – The Site of Sexual Reproduction

- All flowering plants show sexual reproduction. Flowers are **morphological and embryological marvels** and the **sites of sexual reproduction**.
- Flowers have aesthetic, ornamental, social, religious and cultural value; cultivation of flowers = **floriculture**.
- **Androecium** (whorl of stamens) = male reproductive organ; **gynoecium** (pistil) = female reproductive organ.
- Much before the flower appears, the **decision to flower** is taken; **hormonal and structural changes** lead to differentiation of the **floral primordium** → inflorescence → floral buds → flowers.

## 2. Stamen, Microsporangium and Pollen Grain

### 2.1 Stamen and anther
- Stamen = long slender stalk **filament** + terminal, generally bilobed **anther**.
- Proximal end of filament attached to the **thalamus or petal**.
- A typical angiosperm anther is **bilobed**, each lobe with **two theca** → **dithecous**. A longitudinal groove often separates the theca.
- In transverse section, anther is **four-sided (tetragonal)** with **four microsporangia** at the corners, **two in each lobe** → **tetrasporangiate**.
- Microsporangia develop into **pollen sacs**, extending the length of the anther, packed with pollen grains.

### 2.2 Structure of microsporangium
- Near-circular in outline; surrounded by **four wall layers** (outside to inside):

| Wall layer | Function |
|---|---|
| **Epidermis** | Protection; helps in **dehiscence** of anther |
| **Endothecium** | Protection; helps in dehiscence |
| **Middle layers** | Protection; helps in dehiscence |
| **Tapetum** (innermost) | **Nourishes the developing pollen grains**. Cells have **dense cytoplasm** and generally **more than one nucleus** (binucleate/multinucleate) |

- **Sporogenous tissue** – group of compactly arranged homogeneous cells in the **centre** of each young microsporangium.

### 2.3 Microsporogenesis
- Cells of sporogenous tissue undergo **meiosis** → **microspore tetrads** (haploid).
- Each sporogenous cell is a potential **pollen mother cell (PMC) / microspore mother cell**.
- **Microsporogenesis** = formation of **microspores from a PMC through meiosis**.
- As anthers mature and **dehydrate**, microspores dissociate and develop into **pollen grains**.
- Several thousand microspores per microsporangium; released on **dehiscence** of the anther.

**Sequence:** sporogenous tissue → pollen mother cell → microspore tetrad → pollen grain → male gametes

### 2.4 Pollen grain (male gametophyte)
- Pollen grains represent the **male gametophytes**.
- Generally **spherical**, about **25–50 micrometres** in diameter.
- **Two-layered wall:**

| Layer | Composition / features |
|---|---|
| **Exine** (hard outer layer) | Made of **sporopollenin** – one of the **most resistant organic materials** known; withstands **high temperatures, strong acids and alkali**; **no enzyme degrades it**. Has apertures called **germ pores** where **sporopollenin is absent**. Pollen are **well preserved as fossils** due to sporopollenin. Shows fascinating patterns |
| **Intine** (inner wall) | Thin, continuous layer of **cellulose and pectin** |

- Cytoplasm surrounded by plasma membrane.
- **Mature pollen grain has two cells:**

| Cell | Features |
|---|---|
| **Vegetative cell** | **Bigger**; abundant food reserve; **large, irregularly shaped nucleus** |
| **Generative cell** | **Small**; floats in the cytoplasm of the vegetative cell; **spindle-shaped** with dense cytoplasm and a nucleus |

- In **over 60% of angiosperms**, pollen grains are shed at the **2-celled stage**. In the rest, the generative cell divides mitotically into **two male gametes** before shedding (**3-celled stage**).

### 2.5 Pollen – allergy, nutrition, viability
- Pollen of many species cause **severe allergies and bronchial afflictions** (asthma, bronchitis). ***Parthenium* (carrot grass)** came into India as a **contaminant with imported wheat** and causes pollen allergy.
- Pollen are **rich in nutrients** – pollen tablets and syrups used as **food supplements**; claimed to increase performance of athletes and race horses.
- **Viability** depends on temperature and humidity:
  - **Rice and wheat** – lose viability within **30 minutes** of release
  - Some members of **Rosaceae, Leguminosae and Solanaceae** – viable for **months**
- Pollen can be stored for years in **liquid nitrogen (−196°C)** → **pollen banks** (like seed banks) for crop breeding.

> **PYQ Trend:** Sporopollenin (exine, resistant, fossils), germ pores lack sporopollenin, tapetum nourishes pollen and is multinucleate, 60% shed at 2-celled stage – these are among the most frequently asked facts in this chapter.

## 3. The Pistil, Megasporangium (Ovule) and Embryo Sac

### 3.1 Pistil
- Gynoecium may be a single pistil (**monocarpellary**) or more than one (**multicarpellary**).
  - Fused pistils = **syncarpous** (e.g., ***Papaver***)
  - Free pistils = **apocarpous** (e.g., ***Michelia***)
- Each pistil has **stigma** (landing platform for pollen), **style** (elongated slender part) and **ovary** (basal bulged part).
- Inside the ovary is the **ovarian cavity (locule)**; the **placenta** is inside it. **Ovules (megasporangia)** arise from the placenta.
- Number of ovules per ovary: **one** (wheat, paddy, mango) to **many** (papaya, watermelon, orchids).

### 3.2 Structure of ovule (anatropous ovule)

| Part | Description |
|---|---|
| **Funicle** | Stalk attaching ovule to placenta |
| **Hilum** | Region where body of ovule fuses with funicle (junction) |
| **Integuments** | One or two protective envelopes encircling the nucellus |
| **Micropyle** | Small opening at the tip where integuments are absent |
| **Chalaza** | Basal part of ovule, **opposite the micropylar end** |
| **Nucellus** | Mass of cells within the integuments; **abundant reserve food** |
| **Embryo sac (female gametophyte)** | Located in the nucellus; an ovule generally has **a single embryo sac** formed from a megaspore |

### 3.3 Megasporogenesis
- **Megasporogenesis** = formation of megaspores from the **megaspore mother cell (MMC)**.
- Ovules generally differentiate a **single MMC** in the **micropylar region** of the nucellus. It is a large cell with dense cytoplasm and a prominent nucleus.
- MMC undergoes **meiosis** → **four megaspores**.

### 3.4 Female gametophyte (embryo sac) development
- In most flowering plants, **one megaspore is functional**; the other **three degenerate**.
- Only the functional megaspore develops into the embryo sac → **monosporic development**.
- Nucleus of functional megaspore divides **mitotically** → **2-nucleate** (nuclei move to opposite poles) → **4-nucleate** → **8-nucleate** embryo sac.
- These mitotic divisions are **strictly free nuclear** (no immediate cell wall formation).
- After the 8-nucleate stage, **cell walls** are laid down → typical embryo sac.

### 3.5 Mature embryo sac – 7-celled, 8-nucleate

| Location | Cells | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Micropylar end** | **Egg apparatus** = **2 synergids + 1 egg cell** | Synergids have special cellular thickenings at the micropylar tip = **filiform apparatus**, which **guides pollen tubes into the synergid** |
| **Chalazal end** | **3 antipodals** | – |
| **Centre** | **Large central cell** with **2 polar nuclei** (below the egg apparatus) | – |

- **Total: 7 cells, 8 nuclei.**

### 3.6 Ploidy of structures (frequently asked)

| Structure | Ploidy |
|---|---|
| Nucellus, integuments, MMC, PMC | **2n** |
| Megaspore, microspore, pollen grain, embryo sac cells, egg, synergids, antipodals, male gametes | **n** |
| Polar nuclei (each) | **n** (together 2n in central cell) |
| Zygote, embryo | **2n** |
| Primary endosperm nucleus, endosperm | **3n** |
| Seed coat (from integuments), pericarp (from ovary wall) | **2n** |

## 4. Pollination

- Male and female gametes are **non-motile**; they must be brought together.
- **Pollination** = transfer of pollen grains (from the anther) to the **stigma** of a pistil.

### 4.1 Kinds of pollination

| Type | Definition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Autogamy** | Pollen from anther to stigma of the **same flower** | Complete autogamy is rare in open flowers; needs synchrony in pollen release and stigma receptivity and close positioning |
| **Geitonogamy** | Pollen from anther to stigma of **another flower of the same plant** | **Functionally cross-pollination** (involves an agent), but **genetically similar to autogamy** |
| **Xenogamy** | Pollen from anther to stigma of a **different plant** | **Only type bringing genetically different pollen** to the stigma |

**Chasmogamous and cleistogamous flowers:**
- Plants like ***Viola* (common pansy), *Oxalis* and *Commelina*** produce two types of flowers:
  - **Chasmogamous** – open, with exposed anthers and stigma
  - **Cleistogamous** – **do not open at all**; anthers and stigma lie close; anthers dehisce in the bud → **invariably autogamous**; **assured seed set** even without pollinators

### 4.2 Agents of pollination
- **Abiotic:** wind and water. **Biotic:** animals. **Majority of plants use biotic agents.**
- Abiotic pollination is a chance factor → flowers produce **enormous amounts of pollen** compared to ovules.

**Wind pollination (anemophily):**
- **More common** among abiotic pollinations.
- Pollen **light and non-sticky**.
- **Well-exposed stamens**; **large, often feathery stigma**.
- Often **a single ovule** per ovary and **numerous flowers packed** in an inflorescence.
- Example: **corn cob** – the "tassels" are **stigma and style** waving in the wind. Common in **grasses**.

**Water pollination (hydrophily):**
- **Quite rare**; limited to about **30 genera, mostly monocots**.
- Examples: ***Vallisneria*** and ***Hydrilla*** (fresh water); marine **sea-grasses** like ***Zostera***.
- **Not all aquatic plants** use water: **water hyacinth and water lily** flowers emerge above water and are pollinated by **insects or wind**.
- ***Vallisneria*** – female flower reaches the **surface** by a long stalk; male flowers/pollen released on the surface and carried passively by currents.
- **Sea-grasses** – female flowers **submerged**; pollen released **inside water**; pollen **long and ribbon-like**.
- In most water-pollinated species, pollen is protected from wetting by a **mucilaginous covering**.
- **Wind and water pollinated flowers are not colourful and do not produce nectar.**

**Animal pollination (zoophily):**
- Agents: **bees, butterflies, flies, beetles, wasps, ants, moths, birds (sunbirds, humming birds), bats**. Also **lemurs, arboreal rodents, gecko lizard and garden lizard**.
- **Insects, particularly bees**, are the dominant biotic agents.
- Insect-pollinated flowers: **large, colourful, fragrant, rich in nectar**. Small flowers are clustered into inflorescences.
- Flowers pollinated by **flies and beetles** secrete **foul odours**.
- **Floral rewards:** **nectar and pollen**. Pollen is generally **sticky** in animal-pollinated flowers.
- Some rewards are **safe places to lay eggs**:
  - ***Amorphophallus*** – **tallest flower** (about **6 feet**)
  - **Moth and *Yucca*** – obligate mutualism; neither can complete its life cycle without the other. Moth lays eggs in the locule of the ovary; flower is pollinated by the moth; larvae emerge as seeds develop.
- **Pollen/nectar robbers** – floral visitors that consume pollen or nectar without pollinating.

## 5. Outbreeding Devices

- Continued self-pollination → **inbreeding depression**. Most flowers are hermaphrodite, so plants have evolved devices to **discourage self-pollination and encourage cross-pollination**:

| Device | How it works | Prevents |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-synchronisation** of pollen release and stigma receptivity | Pollen released before stigma is receptive, or vice versa | Autogamy |
| **Different positions** of anther and stigma | Pollen cannot contact stigma of the same flower | Autogamy |
| **Self-incompatibility** | **Genetic mechanism**; prevents self-pollen (same flower or other flowers of same plant) from fertilising ovules by **inhibiting pollen germination or pollen tube growth** in the pistil | Autogamy and geitonogamy |
| **Unisexual flowers – monoecious** (male and female flowers on the same plant, e.g., **castor, maize**) | – | **Prevents autogamy but NOT geitonogamy** |
| **Unisexual flowers – dioecious** (male and female flowers on different plants, e.g., **papaya**) | – | **Prevents both autogamy and geitonogamy** |

> **PYQ Trend:** "Which prevents both autogamy and geitonogamy?" – **dioecy (papaya)**. "Castor and maize" – monoecious; prevents autogamy only.

## 6. Pollen–Pistil Interaction

- Pollination does not guarantee the **right type** of pollen. The **pistil can recognise** compatible vs incompatible pollen.
  - **Compatible** → pistil accepts and promotes post-pollination events.
  - **Incompatible** → pistil rejects by preventing **pollen germination on stigma** or **pollen tube growth in style**.
- This recognition is a **continuous dialogue** mediated by **chemical components** of pollen interacting with those of the pistil.

**Events after compatible pollination:**
1. Pollen grain **germinates** on the stigma → **pollen tube** emerges through **one of the germ pores**.
2. Contents of pollen grain move into the pollen tube.
3. Pollen tube grows through the tissues of the **stigma and style** and reaches the **ovary**.
4. In 2-celled pollen, the **generative cell divides into two male gametes** during pollen tube growth in the stigma. In 3-celled pollen, the tube carries two male gametes from the beginning.
5. Pollen tube enters the ovule through the **micropyle** and then enters **one of the synergids** through the **filiform apparatus** (which guides entry).

- **Pollen–pistil interaction** = all events from pollen deposition on the stigma until pollen tubes enter the ovule. A **dynamic process** of recognition followed by promotion or inhibition.
- Knowledge helps plant breeders manipulate interactions, even in incompatible pollinations, to get desired hybrids.
- **Pollen germination** can be studied on a slide with **about 10% sugar solution** (pea, chickpea, *Crotalaria*, balsam, *Vinca*) – tubes seen in 15–30 minutes.

## 7. Artificial Hybridisation – Emasculation and Bagging

- **Artificial hybridisation** – major approach of crop improvement; crossing different species/genera to combine desirable characters.
- Ensure only desired pollen is used and the stigma is protected from contamination.

| Step | Description |
|---|---|
| **Emasculation** | If the female parent has **bisexual flowers**, **remove anthers from the flower bud before the anther dehisces** using forceps |
| **Bagging** | Cover emasculated flowers with a bag (generally **butter paper**) to prevent contamination with unwanted pollen |
| **Pollination** | When the stigma becomes receptive, dust mature pollen from the male parent on it |
| **Rebagging** | Flowers are rebagged; fruits allowed to develop |

- If the female parent has **unisexual flowers**, **no emasculation** is needed; female flower buds are bagged before opening.

## 8. Double Fertilisation

1. Pollen tube releases **two male gametes** into the cytoplasm of the **synergid**.
2. **Syngamy:** one male gamete fuses with the **egg cell nucleus** → **diploid zygote**.
3. **Triple fusion:** the other male gamete fuses with the **two polar nuclei** in the central cell → **triploid primary endosperm nucleus (PEN)**. (Fusion of **three haploid nuclei**.)
4. Since **two types of fusion** (syngamy and triple fusion) occur in an embryo sac → **double fertilisation** – **unique to flowering plants**.
- The central cell becomes the **primary endosperm cell (PEC)** → develops into **endosperm**; zygote → **embryo**.

## 9. Post-fertilisation: Structures and Events

Post-fertilisation events = **endosperm and embryo development**, maturation of **ovules into seeds** and **ovary into fruit**.

### 9.1 Endosperm
- **Endosperm development precedes embryo development** (to provide assured nutrition).
- PEC divides repeatedly → **triploid endosperm tissue** filled with reserve food for the developing embryo.
- **Most common type:** PEN undergoes successive **nuclear divisions** → free nuclei = **free-nuclear endosperm**; later cell walls form → **cellular endosperm**.
- **Coconut:** **tender coconut water = free-nuclear endosperm** (thousands of nuclei); surrounding **white kernel = cellular endosperm**.
- Endosperm may be **completely consumed** by the embryo before seed maturation (e.g., **pea, groundnut, beans**) or **persist** in the mature seed (e.g., **castor, coconut**) and be used during germination.

### 9.2 Embryo
- Develops at the **micropylar end** where the zygote is.
- **Most zygotes divide only after a certain amount of endosperm is formed** – adaptation for assured nutrition.
- Early stages of embryogeny are similar in monocots and dicots.
- **Stages:** zygote → **proembryo** → **globular** → **heart-shaped** → **mature embryo**.

**Dicot embryo:**
- **Embryonal axis + two cotyledons**.
- **Epicotyl** – portion of embryonal axis **above** the cotyledons; ends in the **plumule** (stem tip).
- **Hypocotyl** – cylindrical portion **below** the cotyledons; ends in the **radicle** (root tip), covered by a **root cap**.

**Monocot (grass) embryo:**
- **One cotyledon** = **scutellum**, situated **laterally** on the embryonal axis.
- Lower end: radicle and root cap enclosed in an undifferentiated sheath = **coleorrhiza**.
- **Epicotyl** – portion above the scutellum attachment; has **shoot apex and a few leaf primordia** enclosed in a hollow foliar structure = **coleoptile**.

### 9.3 Seed
- **Final product of sexual reproduction**; often described as a **fertilised ovule**. Formed inside fruits.
- Seed = **seed coat(s) + cotyledon(s) + embryo axis**.
- Cotyledons – simple, generally **thick and swollen** due to food storage (e.g., legumes).

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-albuminous (ex-albuminous)** | **No residual endosperm** (completely consumed) | **Pea, groundnut** |
| **Albuminous** | **Retain part of endosperm** | **Wheat, maize, barley, castor** |
| **Perisperm** | **Residual, persistent nucellus** | **Black pepper, beet** |

- **Integuments** harden into tough protective **seed coats**. **Micropyle** remains as a small pore – allows entry of **oxygen and water** during germination.
- Mature seed becomes dry – **10–15% moisture by mass**; metabolic activity slows. The embryo may enter **dormancy**, or germinate under favourable conditions (moisture, oxygen, suitable temperature).

### 9.4 Fruit
- Ovules mature into seeds and the **ovary develops into a fruit** simultaneously.
- **Ovary wall → pericarp** (fruit wall). Fruits may be **fleshy** (guava, orange, mango) or **dry** (groundnut, mustard).
- **True fruits** – develop **only from the ovary** (most fruits).
- **False fruits** – **thalamus also contributes** to fruit formation, e.g., **apple, strawberry, cashew**.
- **Parthenocarpic fruits** – develop **without fertilisation**; **seedless**, e.g., **banana**. Can be **induced by growth hormones**.

### 9.5 Advantages / significance of seeds
- Pollination and fertilisation are **independent of water** → seed formation is more dependable.
- Better **adaptive strategies for dispersal** → colonisation of new habitats.
- **Food reserves** nourish seedlings until they photosynthesise.
- **Hard seed coat** protects the young embryo.
- Products of sexual reproduction → **new genetic combinations** (variations).
- **Basis of agriculture** – dehydration and dormancy allow **storage** of seeds for food and next season's crop.

### 9.6 Seed viability records
- **Oldest:** ***Lupinus arcticus*** (lupine) from **Arctic Tundra** – germinated and flowered after an estimated **10,000 years** of dormancy.
- **Date palm, *Phoenix dactylifera*** – about **2000 years** old viable seed from **King Herod's palace near the Dead Sea**.
- Fruits with very large numbers of tiny seeds: **orchids**, parasitic ***Orobanche*** and ***Striga***; tiny seed of ***Ficus***.

## 10. Apomixis and Polyembryony

### 10.1 Apomixis
- Production of **seeds without fertilisation**; found in some **Asteraceae and grasses**.
- A form of **asexual reproduction that mimics sexual reproduction**.
- Ways:
  - Diploid egg cell formed **without reduction division**, develops into embryo without fertilisation.
  - More often (as in many ***Citrus* and mango** varieties), **nucellar cells** surrounding the embryo sac divide, protrude into the embryo sac and develop into embryos.
- Apomictic embryos are **genetically identical to the parent** – **clones**.

### 10.2 Polyembryony
- **Occurrence of more than one embryo in a seed**, e.g., **orange (*Citrus*)** – squeezing seeds shows many embryos of different sizes.

### 10.3 Importance of apomixis in hybrid seed industry
- Hybrid seeds must be produced **every year**; if seeds from hybrids are sown, progeny **segregate** and lose hybrid characters.
- Hybrid seed production is costly.
- If hybrids are made **apomictic**, there is **no segregation** → farmers can use hybrid seeds year after year.
- Active research to understand the **genetics of apomixis** and **transfer apomictic genes** into hybrids.

> **Exam Tip:** Apomixis = seed without fertilisation. Parthenocarpy = fruit without fertilisation (seedless). Polyembryony = more than one embryo per seed. Nucellar embryos are diploid and genetically identical to the mother plant.

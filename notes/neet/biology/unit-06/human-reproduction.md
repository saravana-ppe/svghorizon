## 1. Introduction

- Humans are **sexually reproducing and viviparous**.
- Reproductive events: **gametogenesis** → **insemination** (transfer of sperms into female genital tract) → **fertilisation** → **zygote** → **blastocyst** → **implantation** → **gestation** (embryonic development) → **parturition** (delivery).
- These events occur after **puberty**.
- **Sperm formation continues even in old men**, but **ovum formation ceases in women around 50 years**.

## 2. Male Reproductive System

- Located in the **pelvis region**: a pair of **testes**, **accessory ducts**, **glands** and **external genitalia**.

### 2.1 Testes
- Situated **outside the abdominal cavity** in a pouch called the **scrotum**, which keeps testes **2–2.5°C lower** than normal internal body temperature – necessary for **spermatogenesis**.
- Each adult testis: **oval**, **4–5 cm long, 2–3 cm wide**; covered by a dense covering.
- Each testis has about **250 compartments** called **testicular lobules**.
- Each lobule contains **one to three highly coiled seminiferous tubules** in which sperms are produced.

### 2.2 Seminiferous tubule and interstitial spaces

| Cell | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Spermatogonia (male germ cells)** | Lining inside the seminiferous tubule | Undergo meiotic divisions → **sperm formation** |
| **Sertoli cells** | Lining inside the seminiferous tubule | **Provide nutrition to the germ cells** |
| **Leydig cells (interstitial cells)** | **Interstitial spaces** outside the tubules (with small blood vessels) | **Synthesise and secrete androgens** (testicular hormones) |
| Immunologically competent cells | Interstitial spaces | Immunity |

### 2.3 Male accessory ducts
**Seminiferous tubules → rete testis → vasa efferentia → epididymis → vas deferens → ejaculatory duct → urethra → urethral meatus**
- **Epididymis** – along the **posterior surface** of each testis.
- **Vas deferens** – ascends to the abdomen and **loops over the urinary bladder**; receives a duct from the **seminal vesicle** and opens into the urethra as the **ejaculatory duct**.
- Ducts **store and transport** sperms.
- **Urethra** – from the urinary bladder, through the penis, to its external opening, the **urethral meatus**.

### 2.4 External genitalia
- **Penis** – made of special tissue that helps in **erection** to facilitate **insemination**.
- Enlarged end = **glans penis**, covered by a loose fold of skin = **foreskin**.

### 2.5 Male accessory glands
- **Paired seminal vesicles, a prostate and paired bulbourethral glands**.
- Secretions constitute the **seminal plasma**, rich in **fructose, calcium and certain enzymes**.
- **Bulbourethral glands** also help in **lubrication** of the penis.

## 3. Female Reproductive System

- In the **pelvic region**: a pair of **ovaries**, a pair of **oviducts**, **uterus**, **cervix**, **vagina** and **external genitalia**; plus a pair of **mammary glands**.

### 3.1 Ovaries
- **Primary female sex organs** – produce the **ovum** and **ovarian (steroid) hormones**.
- One on each side of the lower abdomen; each about **2–4 cm** long; connected to the pelvic wall and uterus by **ligaments**.
- Covered by a thin **epithelium** enclosing the **ovarian stroma**, which has a peripheral **cortex** and an inner **medulla**.

### 3.2 Oviducts (fallopian tubes)
- Each about **10–12 cm** long; from the periphery of each ovary to the uterus.

| Part | Features |
|---|---|
| **Infundibulum** | **Funnel-shaped**, closest to the ovary; edges have finger-like projections called **fimbriae** that **collect the ovum** after ovulation |
| **Ampulla** | **Wider part** of the oviduct (**site of fertilisation**) |
| **Isthmus** | Last part; **narrow lumen**; joins the uterus |

### 3.3 Uterus
- Single; also called **womb**; shaped like an **inverted pear**; supported by ligaments.
- Opens into vagina through a narrow **cervix**. Cavity of cervix = **cervical canal**; cervical canal + vagina = **birth canal**.

| Layer of uterine wall | Nature | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Perimetrium** | External thin membranous | – |
| **Myometrium** | Middle thick layer of **smooth muscle** | **Strong contractions during delivery** |
| **Endometrium** | Inner **glandular** layer lining the uterine cavity | **Cyclical changes during menstrual cycle** |

### 3.4 Female external genitalia
- **Mons pubis** – cushion of fatty tissue covered by skin and pubic hair.
- **Labia majora** – fleshy folds extending down from mons pubis, surrounding the vaginal opening.
- **Labia minora** – paired folds under the labia majora.
- **Hymen** – membrane often partially covering the vaginal opening. It can be torn in many ways (fall, jolt, tampon, sports like horse riding, cycling); may persist after coitus. **Presence or absence of hymen is not a reliable indicator of virginity or sexual experience.**
- **Clitoris** – tiny finger-like structure at the upper junction of the two labia minora, above the urethral opening.

### 3.5 Mammary glands
- Functional mammary glands are characteristic of **all female mammals**.
- Paired (breasts); glandular tissue + variable fat.
- Each breast has **15–20 mammary lobes** containing clusters of cells called **alveoli**; alveolar cells **secrete milk**, stored in alveolar lumens.
- **Pathway of milk:** **alveoli → mammary tubules → mammary duct → mammary ampulla → lactiferous duct → nipple**.

## 4. Gametogenesis

### 4.1 Spermatogenesis
- Begins at **puberty** in the testis.
1. **Spermatogonia** (diploid, **46 chromosomes**) on the inner wall of seminiferous tubules multiply by **mitosis**.
2. Some spermatogonia = **primary spermatocytes** (2n) periodically undergo **meiosis**.
3. **Meiosis I** (reduction division) → **two equal haploid secondary spermatocytes** (**23 chromosomes** each).
4. **Meiosis II** → **four equal haploid spermatids** (23 chromosomes each).
5. **Spermiogenesis** – transformation of **spermatids into spermatozoa (sperms)**.
6. Sperm heads become embedded in **Sertoli cells**; finally released from the seminiferous tubules by **spermiation**.

**Hormonal control of spermatogenesis:**
- At puberty, **GnRH** (hypothalamic hormone) increases significantly.
- GnRH acts on the **anterior pituitary** → secretion of **LH and FSH** (gonadotropins).
- **LH acts on Leydig cells** → synthesis and secretion of **androgens** → androgens stimulate **spermatogenesis**.
- **FSH acts on Sertoli cells** → secretion of factors that help **spermiogenesis**.

### 4.2 Structure of sperm
- Microscopic; **head, neck, middle piece and tail**; enveloped by a plasma membrane.

| Part | Contents / function |
|---|---|
| **Head** | **Elongated haploid nucleus**; anterior portion covered by a cap-like **acrosome** filled with **enzymes that help fertilisation** |
| **Middle piece** | **Numerous mitochondria** – produce energy for tail movement (**sperm motility**) |
| **Tail** | Motility |

- A human male ejaculates about **200–300 million sperms** per coitus. For normal fertility: at least **60% must have normal shape and size**, and at least **40% must show vigorous motility**.
- Secretions of **epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicle and prostate** are essential for **maturation and motility** of sperms.
- **Semen = seminal plasma + sperms**.
- Functions of male accessory ducts and glands are maintained by **androgens**.

### 4.3 Oogenesis
- Formation of a mature female gamete; **markedly different** from spermatogenesis.
1. Initiated during **embryonic development** – a couple of million **oogonia** (gamete mother cells) are formed in each **fetal ovary**; **no more oogonia are added after birth**.
2. Oogonia enter **prophase I** of meiosis and get **temporarily arrested** = **primary oocytes**.
3. Primary oocyte surrounded by a layer of **granulosa cells** = **primary follicle**.
4. Many follicles degenerate from birth to puberty → at puberty only **60,000–80,000 primary follicles** remain in **each ovary**.
5. Primary follicle + more granulosa layers + a new **theca** = **secondary follicle**.
6. Secondary follicle → **tertiary follicle**, characterised by a fluid-filled cavity = **antrum**. Theca organises into **theca interna** and **theca externa**.
7. **At the tertiary follicle stage**, the primary oocyte grows and **completes meiosis I** – an **unequal division** → large haploid **secondary oocyte** (retains most of the nutrient-rich cytoplasm) + tiny **first polar body**.
8. Tertiary follicle → **mature Graafian follicle**. Secondary oocyte forms a new membrane = **zona pellucida**.
9. Graafian follicle **ruptures** to release the **secondary oocyte (ovum)** = **ovulation**.
10. **Meiosis II** is completed only when a **sperm enters** – unequal → **second polar body** + haploid **ovum (ootid)**.

### 4.4 Spermatogenesis vs oogenesis

| Feature | Spermatogenesis | Oogenesis |
|---|---|---|
| Starts | At **puberty** | During **embryonic (fetal) life** |
| Location | Seminiferous tubules of testis | Ovary |
| Continuity | Continuous; even in old men | Ceases around 50 years (menopause) |
| Products per mother cell | **4 equal sperms** | **1 ovum + polar bodies** (unequal divisions) |
| Arrest | No arrest | Arrested at **prophase I** (primary oocyte) until puberty; meiosis II completed only after sperm entry |
| Cytoplasm | Equal distribution | Unequal; ovum retains most cytoplasm |

> **PYQ Trend:** Primary oocyte is arrested in prophase I; meiosis I completed in the tertiary follicle; ovum released at secondary oocyte stage; meiosis II completed after sperm entry. Also: 60,000–80,000 follicles per ovary at puberty; 250 lobules per testis.

## 5. Menstrual Cycle

- Reproductive cycle in **female primates** (monkeys, apes, humans).
- **Menarche** – first menstruation at puberty.
- Repeated at an average interval of **28/29 days**. **One ovum** released in the **middle** of each cycle.

| Phase | Days (approx.) | Ovarian events | Uterine events | Hormones |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Menstrual phase** | **1–5** (lasts 3–5 days) | – | **Breakdown of endometrial lining** and its blood vessels → menstrual flow | Low progesterone (corpus luteum degenerated) |
| **Follicular (proliferative) phase** | After menstruation to about day 13 | **Primary follicles grow into a mature Graafian follicle** | **Endometrium regenerates by proliferation** | **LH and FSH increase gradually**; growing follicles secrete **estrogens** |
| **Ovulatory phase** | About **day 14** | **Rupture of Graafian follicle → ovulation** | – | **LH and FSH peak**; rapid LH secretion = **LH surge** induces ovulation |
| **Luteal (secretory) phase** | Day 15–28 | Remaining parts of Graafian follicle → **corpus luteum** | Endometrium maintained, ready for implantation | Corpus luteum secretes large amounts of **progesterone** (essential for **maintenance of endometrium**) |

- **Menstruation occurs only if the ovum is not fertilised.** In the absence of fertilisation, the **corpus luteum degenerates** → endometrium disintegrates → menstruation → new cycle.
- **Lack of menstruation** may indicate **pregnancy**, but can also be due to stress, poor health, etc.
- **During pregnancy, all events of the menstrual cycle stop** – no menstruation.
- **Menopause** – menstrual cycles cease around **50 years**.
- Cyclic menstruation indicates the normal reproductive phase between **menarche and menopause**.

> **Remember – Menstrual hygiene (NCERT box):** Bathe and clean regularly; use sanitary napkins or clean homemade pads; change every **4–5 hours**; wrap used napkins in paper and dispose properly (not in toilet drains or open); wash hands with soap after handling.

## 6. Fertilisation and Implantation

### 6.1 Fertilisation
- During **coitus**, semen is released into the **vagina** = **insemination**.
- Sperms swim through the **cervix** → **uterus** → **ampullary region of the fallopian tube**.
- The ovum is also transported to the **ampullary region** → **fertilisation takes place in the ampulla**.
- Fertilisation occurs only if ovum and sperms reach the ampulla **simultaneously** – so not all copulations lead to pregnancy.
- **Fertilisation** – fusion of a sperm with an ovum.
- Sperm contacts the **zona pellucida** and induces **changes in the membrane that block entry of additional sperms** → ensures **only one sperm** fertilises (prevents polyspermy).
- **Acrosome secretions** help the sperm enter the ovum cytoplasm through zona pellucida and plasma membrane.
- Sperm entry induces **completion of meiosis II** of the secondary oocyte → **second polar body** + haploid **ovum (ootid)**.
- Haploid nuclei of sperm and ovum fuse → **diploid zygote (46 chromosomes)**.

### 6.2 Sex determination
- Female: **XX**; male: **XY**.
- All ova carry **X**. Sperms: **50% carry X**, **50% carry Y**.
- X-sperm + ovum → **XX (female)**; Y-sperm + ovum → **XY (male)**.
- **The sex of the baby is determined by the father, not the mother** – so blaming women for giving birth to daughters is scientifically wrong.

### 6.3 Cleavage and implantation
1. **Cleavage** – mitotic divisions start as the zygote moves through the **isthmus** towards the uterus → 2, 4, 8, 16 daughter cells = **blastomeres**.
2. Embryo with **8 to 16 blastomeres** = **morula**.
3. Morula continues to divide → **blastocyst** as it moves into the uterus.
4. Blastocyst: outer layer = **trophoblast**; inner group of cells attached to trophoblast = **inner cell mass**.
5. **Trophoblast attaches to the endometrium**; inner cell mass differentiates into the **embryo**.
6. Uterine cells divide rapidly and cover the blastocyst → blastocyst becomes **embedded in the endometrium** = **implantation** → leads to **pregnancy**.

## 7. Pregnancy and Embryonic Development

### 7.1 Placenta
- After implantation, finger-like projections called **chorionic villi** appear on the trophoblast, surrounded by uterine tissue and maternal blood.
- Chorionic villi + uterine tissue become **interdigitated** → **placenta** – structural and functional unit between foetus and mother.
- **Functions:**
  - Supply of **oxygen and nutrients** to the embryo
  - Removal of **CO<sub>2</sub> and excretory wastes**
  - Connected to the embryo through the **umbilical cord** (transport of substances)
  - **Endocrine tissue** – produces **hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin), hPL (human placental lactogen), estrogens, progestogens**
- **Relaxin** is secreted by the **ovary** in the later phase of pregnancy.
- **hCG, hPL and relaxin are produced only during pregnancy.**
- During pregnancy, levels of **estrogens, progestogens, cortisol, prolactin, thyroxine** increase several-fold – supporting fetal growth, maternal metabolic changes and maintenance of pregnancy.

### 7.2 Germ layers and stem cells
- Immediately after implantation, the **inner cell mass** differentiates into **ectoderm** (outer) and **endoderm** (inner); **mesoderm** appears between them. These three layers form all adult tissues.
- The inner cell mass contains **stem cells** with potency to give rise to **all tissues and organs**.

### 7.3 Month-wise embryonic development

| Time | Development |
|---|---|
| **After 1 month** | **Heart** is formed (heart sound can be heard with a stethoscope – first sign of growing foetus) |
| **End of 2nd month** | Foetus develops **limbs and digits** |
| **End of 12 weeks (first trimester)** | **Most major organ systems** formed; limbs and **external genital organs well developed** |
| **5th month** | **First movements** of foetus; **hair on head** appears |
| **End of 24 weeks (second trimester)** | Body covered with **fine hair**; **eyelids separate**; **eyelashes** formed |
| **End of 9 months** | Foetus fully developed, ready for delivery |

## 8. Parturition and Lactation

### 8.1 Parturition
- **Gestation period** – average duration of human pregnancy, about **9 months**.
- **Parturition** – delivery of the foetus (childbirth) by vigorous contraction of the uterus.
- Induced by a complex **neuroendocrine mechanism**:
  1. Signals originate from the **fully developed foetus and the placenta** → mild uterine contractions = **foetal ejection reflex**.
  2. This triggers release of **oxytocin** from the **maternal pituitary**.
  3. Oxytocin acts on uterine muscle → **stronger contractions** → further oxytocin secretion (**positive feedback**).
  4. Stronger and stronger contractions → expulsion of the baby through the **birth canal**.
- Soon after, the **placenta** is also expelled.
- Hormones involved in induction of parturition (NCERT summary): **cortisol, estrogens and oxytocin**. Doctors inject **oxytocin** to induce delivery.

### 8.2 Lactation
- Mammary glands **differentiate during pregnancy** and start producing milk towards the end of pregnancy = **lactation**.
- **Colostrum** – milk produced in the **initial few days**; contains several **antibodies** essential to develop **resistance** in new-born babies.
- **Breast-feeding** during the initial period is recommended for a healthy baby.

## 1. Microbes – Introduction

- Microbes are present everywhere – soil, water, air, inside bodies of animals and plants; even in **extreme sites** like **geysers (thermal vents) at up to 100°C**, deep soil, under thick snow, highly acidic environments.
- Diverse: **protozoa, bacteria, fungi, microscopic plant and animal viruses, viroids and prions** (proteinaceous infectious agents).
- Bacteria and many fungi can be grown on **nutritive media** to form **colonies** visible to the naked eye.
- Not all microbes are harmful; many are **useful** to humans.

## 3. Microbes in Industrial Products

- Industrial scale production needs growing microbes in very large vessels called **fermentors**.

- **Yeast *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*** (**brewer's yeast** – same as baker's yeast) ferments **malted cereals and fruit juices** to produce **ethanol**.

### 3.2 Antibiotics
- **Anti** = against; **bio** = life → "against life" of disease-causing organisms, but "**pro life**" for humans.
- **Antibiotics** – chemical substances produced by some microbes that **kill or retard the growth of other (disease-causing) microbes**.
- **Penicillin** – **first antibiotic**; a **chance discovery** by **Alexander Fleming**:
  - Working on ***Staphylococci***, he saw a **mould** in an unwashed culture plate around which *Staphylococci* could not grow.
  - The chemical was named **penicillin**, after the mould ***Penicillium notatum***.
  - Full potential as an antibiotic established later by **Ernest Chain and Howard Florey**.
  - Used extensively to treat **American soldiers wounded in World War II**.
  - **Fleming, Chain and Florey – Nobel Prize 1945**.
- Antibiotics have greatly improved treatment of **plague, whooping cough (kali khansi), diphtheria (gal ghotu) and leprosy (kusht rog)**.

## 4. Microbes in Sewage Treatment

- **Sewage** – **municipal waste water**; major component is **human excreta**; contains large amounts of **organic matter and microbes** (many pathogenic).
- Cannot be discharged directly into rivers; treated in **sewage treatment plants (STPs)** by **heterotrophic microbes** naturally present in sewage.

### 4.1 Primary treatment (physical)
- **Physical removal of particles** – large and small – by **filtration and sedimentation**.
- **Floating debris** removed by **sequential filtration**.
- **Grit** (soil and small pebbles) removed by **sedimentation**.
- All settled solids = **primary sludge**; supernatant = **effluent** → taken for secondary treatment.

### 4.2 Secondary treatment (biological)
1. Primary effluent passed into large **aeration tanks**; **constantly agitated mechanically and air pumped in**.
2. Vigorous growth of useful **aerobic microbes into flocs** (**masses of bacteria associated with fungal filaments** forming mesh-like structures).
3. Microbes **consume the major part of organic matter** → **significantly reduces BOD**.
4. Once BOD is reduced, effluent passed into a **settling tank**; bacterial flocs **sediment** = **activated sludge**.
5. **A small part of activated sludge is pumped back into the aeration tank** as **inoculum**.
6. **Major part of sludge** pumped into large tanks called **anaerobic sludge digesters** → **anaerobic bacteria digest** the bacteria and fungi in the sludge → produce **methane, hydrogen sulphide and CO<sub>2</sub>** = **biogas** (inflammable; energy source).
7. Effluent from secondary treatment released into **rivers and streams**.

### 4.3 BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand)
- **BOD** = amount of **oxygen that would be consumed if all the organic matter in one litre of water were oxidised by bacteria**.
- BOD test measures the **rate of oxygen uptake** by micro-organisms → indirectly a **measure of organic matter** in water.
- **Greater the BOD, more is the polluting potential.**

> **Exam Tip (NCERT Exercise Q11):** BOD values A = 20 mg/L, B = 8 mg/L, C = 400 mg/L. Most polluted = **C (untreated sewage, 400)**; **A (20) = secondary effluent**; **B (8) = river water** (relatively clean).

### 4.4 Importance and government initiatives
- Microbial sewage treatment has been practised for **more than a century**; **no man-made technology has rivalled it**.
- Sewage production has increased with urbanisation, but STPs have not kept pace → untreated sewage pollutes rivers.
- **Ministry of Environment and Forests** – **Ganga Action Plan** and **Yamuna Action Plan** – build STPs so only treated sewage enters rivers.

## 5. Microbes in Production of Biogas

- **Biogas** – mixture of gases, **predominantly methane**, produced by microbial activity; used as **fuel**.
- In dough, cheese and beverages the main gas is **CO<sub>2</sub>**. But certain bacteria growing **anaerobically on cellulosic material** produce large amounts of **methane along with CO<sub>2</sub> and H<sub>2</sub>** → **methanogens**, e.g., ***Methanobacterium***.
- Methanogens are found in:
  - **Anaerobic sludge** during sewage treatment
  - **Rumen** (part of stomach) of **cattle** – help **break down cellulose** and play an important role in cattle nutrition (humans cannot digest cellulose)
- Cattle excreta (**dung, "gobar"**) is rich in these bacteria → **gobar gas**.

### 5.1 Biogas plant
- **Concrete tank (10–15 feet deep)** in which bio-wastes are collected and a **slurry of dung** is fed.
- A **floating cover** over the slurry keeps **rising** as gas is produced.
- **Outlet** connected to a pipe supplying biogas to nearby houses.
- **Spent slurry** removed through another outlet and used as **fertiliser**.
- Built mostly in **rural areas** (cattle dung available); used for **cooking and lighting**.
- Technology developed in India mainly by **IARI (Indian Agricultural Research Institute)** and **KVIC (Khadi and Village Industries Commission)**.

## 6. Microbes as Biocontrol Agents

- **Biocontrol** – use of **biological methods** for controlling **plant diseases and pests**.
- Chemical insecticides, pesticides and weedicides are **toxic** and pollute soil, ground water, fruits, vegetables.

### 6.1 Organic farming philosophy
- Relies on **natural predation** rather than chemicals.
- Key belief: **biodiversity furthers health**; more variety → more sustainable.
- Pests are **not eradicated** but kept at **manageable levels** by checks and balances.
- **Eradication of pests is undesirable** – beneficial predatory and parasitic insects that depend on them would not survive.
- Holistic approach – understand the **webs of interaction** between organisms; know pests, predators, their life cycles, feeding patterns, habitats.

### 6.2 Biocontrol agents

| Agent | Controls | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Ladybird** (red and black beetle) | **Aphids** | Predator |
| **Dragonflies** | **Mosquitoes** | Predator |
| ***Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt)** | **Butterfly caterpillars** | Available in sachets as **dried spores**, mixed with water and sprayed on vulnerable plants like **brassicas and fruit trees**; eaten by insect larvae; **toxin released in the larval gut** → larvae killed; **other insects unharmed**. **Bt toxin genes** introduced into plants → **Bt-cotton** (pest resistant) |
| ***Trichoderma*** | Several **plant pathogens** | **Free-living fungi**, very common in **root ecosystems**; effective biocontrol agents |
| **Baculoviruses** (genus ***Nucleopolyhedrovirus***) | **Insects and other arthropods** | **Species-specific, narrow-spectrum** insecticidal applications; **no negative impacts** on plants, mammals, birds, fish or non-target insects; desirable in **integrated pest management (IPM)** programmes and ecologically sensitive areas |

## 7. Microbes as Biofertilisers

- Overuse of **chemical fertilisers** contributes to pollution → pressure to switch to **organic farming** and **biofertilisers**.
- **Biofertilisers** – organisms that **enrich the nutrient quality of the soil**. Main sources: **bacteria, fungi and cyanobacteria**.

| Biofertiliser | Organism | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Symbiotic N<sub>2</sub>-fixing bacteria** | ***Rhizobium*** | Forms **root nodules** on **leguminous plants**; fixes atmospheric nitrogen into organic forms |
| **Free-living N<sub>2</sub>-fixing bacteria** | ***Azospirillum*** and ***Azotobacter*** | Fix nitrogen while **free-living in soil** |
| **Mycorrhiza** (fungus–root symbiosis) | Many members of genus ***Glomus*** | Fungal symbiont **absorbs phosphorus** from soil and passes it to the plant. Plants also get **resistance to root-borne pathogens, tolerance to salinity and drought**, overall increase in growth. (Fungus gets food/carbohydrates from the plant) |
| **Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae)** | ***Anabaena, Nostoc, Oscillatoria*** | **Autotrophic**; many **fix atmospheric nitrogen**; important biofertiliser in **paddy fields**; also **add organic matter** to soil |

- Many biofertilisers are available commercially; farmers use them to replenish soil nutrients and reduce chemical fertiliser use.

> **Remember – microbial helpers in agriculture:** Bt – caterpillars; *Trichoderma* – fungal plant pathogens; baculoviruses (*Nucleopolyhedrovirus*) – insects, species-specific; *Rhizobium* – legumes; *Azotobacter*, *Azospirillum* – free-living N<sub>2</sub> fixers; *Glomus* – phosphorus (mycorrhiza); *Anabaena*, *Nostoc*, *Oscillatoria* – paddy fields.

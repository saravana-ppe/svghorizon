**NEET UG 2026 Biology – Notes from NCERT**

**NCERT chapters covered:** Class XII – Chapter 7 (Human Health and Disease), Chapter 8 (Microbes in Human Welfare)

**NEET syllabus topics in this unit:**

1. **Health and disease:** pathogens; parasites causing human diseases (malaria, filariasis, ascariasis, typhoid, pneumonia, common cold, amoebiasis, ringworm, dengue, chikungunya); basic concepts of immunology – vaccines; cancer, HIV and AIDS; adolescence, drug and alcohol abuse; tobacco abuse
2. **Microbes in human welfare:** in household food processing, industrial production, sewage treatment, energy generation, and as biocontrol agents and biofertilisers

> **NEET Focus: How this unit is tested**
> - Pathogen–disease–symptom–transmission matching (e.g., *Salmonella typhi* – Widal test; *Wuchereria* – elephantiasis; *Microsporum* – ringworm; *Entamoeba* – amoebiasis with houseflies as mechanical carriers).
> - Malaria life cycle (sporozoites, haemozoin, female *Anopheles*, two hosts).
> - Immunity: innate barriers, antibody H<sub>2</sub>L<sub>2</sub>, IgE in allergy, IgA in colostrum, primary vs secondary lymphoid organs, MALT (50%), active vs passive immunity.
> - HIV: retrovirus, reverse transcriptase, macrophages as "HIV factory", helper T-cells decline, ELISA.
> - Drugs: source plant and effect (heroin – depressant; cocaine – dopamine transport; cannabinoids – cardiovascular); nicotine and CO.
> - Microbes: organism–product matching (*Aspergillus niger* – citric acid; *Trichoderma polysporum* – cyclosporin A; *Monascus purpureus* – statins; *Propionibacterium sharmanii* – Swiss cheese), BOD, activated sludge, biogas, Bt, *Trichoderma*, baculoviruses, biofertilisers.

## Quick Revision – High-Yield One-liners

- Health – complete physical, mental and social well-being.
- William Harvey – blood circulation (disproved humor theory).
- *Salmonella typhi* – typhoid – Widal test – Typhoid Mary (Mary Mallon).
- Pneumonia – *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae* – alveoli filled with fluid.
- Common cold – rhino viruses – nose and respiratory passage, not lungs – 3–7 days.
- Malaria – *P. vivax, P. malariae, P. falciparum*; malignant = *P. falciparum*; haemozoin → fever every 3–4 days.
- Sporozoites (infective to humans) stored in mosquito salivary glands.
- Amoebiasis – *Entamoeba histolytica* – large intestine – houseflies are mechanical carriers.
- Ascariasis – *Ascaris*; filariasis – *Wuchereria bancrofti, W. malayi*.
- Ringworm – *Microsporum, Trichophyton, Epidermophyton*.
- *Gambusia* fish eat mosquito larvae. Dengue and chikungunya – *Aedes*.
- Smallpox eradicated by vaccination.
- Innate barriers: physical, physiological, cellular, cytokine (interferons).
- Antibody – H<sub>2</sub>L<sub>2</sub>; IgA, IgM, IgE, IgG.
- B-cells – humoral; T-cells – CMI, graft rejection.
- Colostrum – IgA (passive immunity). Tetanus antitoxin, snake anti-venom – passive immunisation.
- Hepatitis B vaccine – from yeast (recombinant).
- Allergy – IgE; histamine, serotonin from mast cells.
- Rheumatoid arthritis – autoimmune.
- Primary lymphoid – bone marrow, thymus; secondary – spleen, lymph nodes, tonsils, Peyer's patches, appendix. MALT = 50% of lymphoid tissue.
- AIDS first reported 1981; HIV – retrovirus, RNA genome, reverse transcriptase.
- Macrophages – HIV factory; helper T-cells decline; ELISA diagnosis; NACO.
- Incubation 5–10 years.
- Cancer: loss of contact inhibition; metastasis; proto-oncogenes (c-onc); α-interferon as biological response modifier.
- CT – X-rays, 3-D image; MRI – magnetic fields, non-ionising radiation.
- Heroin – diacetylmorphine – *Papaver somniferum* – depressant.
- Cannabinoids – *Cannabis sativa* – cardiovascular effects.
- Cocaine – *Erythroxylum coca* – dopamine transport – stimulant, hallucinations.
- Nicotine → adrenaline, noradrenaline → BP and heart rate ↑. Smoking → CO ↑.
- Adolescence – 12–18 years.
- LAB – curd; increases vitamin B<sub>12</sub>.
- Swiss cheese – *Propionibacterium sharmanii* (CO<sub>2</sub> holes); Roquefort – fungus.
- Wine, beer – no distillation; whisky, brandy, rum – distillation.
- Penicillin – Fleming, *Penicillium notatum*; Chain and Florey; Nobel 1945.
- Citric acid – *Aspergillus niger*; acetic – *Acetobacter aceti*; butyric – *Clostridium butylicum*; lactic – *Lactobacillus*.
- Streptokinase – *Streptococcus* – clot buster. Cyclosporin A – *Trichoderma polysporum*. Statins – *Monascus purpureus*.
- Lipases – detergents; pectinases, proteases – juice clarification.
- Flocs = bacteria + fungal filaments. Activated sludge. Anaerobic sludge digester → biogas (CH<sub>4</sub>, H<sub>2</sub>S, CO<sub>2</sub>).
- BOD – O<sub>2</sub> to oxidise organic matter in 1 litre of water.
- Ganga Action Plan, Yamuna Action Plan.
- Methanogens – *Methanobacterium*; rumen; gobar gas; biogas tank 10–15 feet deep; IARI and KVIC.
- Ladybird – aphids; dragonflies – mosquitoes; Bt – caterpillars; *Trichoderma* – plant pathogens; baculoviruses – *Nucleopolyhedrovirus*.
- *Rhizobium* – symbiotic N<sub>2</sub>; *Azospirillum*, *Azotobacter* – free-living; *Glomus* – mycorrhiza (P); *Anabaena, Nostoc, Oscillatoria* – paddy.

## Practice Questions (NEET pattern)

1. Widal test is used for the diagnosis of:
   - (a) Malaria
   - (b) Typhoid
   - (c) Pneumonia
   - (d) Amoebiasis
2. The chill and high fever in malaria are due to release of:
   - (a) Haemozoin
   - (b) Histamine
   - (c) Interferon
   - (d) Serotonin
3. Which of the following infects the nose and respiratory passage but not the lungs?
   - (a) *Streptococcus pneumoniae*
   - (b) Rhino virus
   - (c) *Haemophilus influenzae*
   - (d) *Mycobacterium*
4. Houseflies act as mechanical carriers for:
   - (a) *Ascaris*
   - (b) *Wuchereria*
   - (c) *Entamoeba histolytica*
   - (d) *Plasmodium*
5. Interferons are part of which barrier of innate immunity?
   - (a) Physical
   - (b) Physiological
   - (c) Cellular
   - (d) Cytokine
6. The antibody responsible for allergic reactions is:
   - (a) IgA
   - (b) IgG
   - (c) IgE
   - (d) IgM
7. Which is a primary lymphoid organ?
   - (a) Spleen
   - (b) Lymph node
   - (c) Thymus
   - (d) Peyer's patches
8. In HIV infection, the cells that act as "HIV factory" are:
   - (a) B-lymphocytes
   - (b) Macrophages
   - (c) Helper T-lymphocytes
   - (d) Neutrophils
9. Cocaine interferes with the transport of:
   - (a) Acetylcholine
   - (b) Dopamine
   - (c) Serotonin
   - (d) GABA
10. The large holes in Swiss cheese are due to CO<sub>2</sub> produced by:
    - (a) *Lactobacillus*
    - (b) *Aspergillus niger*
    - (c) *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*
    - (d) *Propionibacterium sharmanii*
11. Cyclosporin A, an immunosuppressant, is obtained from:
    - (a) *Monascus purpureus*
    - (b) *Trichoderma polysporum*
    - (c) *Penicillium notatum*
    - (d) *Streptococcus*
12. Activated sludge in sewage treatment is:
    - (a) Primary sludge
    - (b) Sediment of bacterial flocs after secondary treatment
    - (c) Grit
    - (d) Biogas
13. Baculoviruses used as biocontrol agents mostly belong to the genus:
    - (a) *Nucleopolyhedrovirus*
    - (b) *Bacillus*
    - (c) *Trichoderma*
    - (d) *Glomus*
14. Mycorrhizal fungi of the genus *Glomus* mainly help plants absorb:
    - (a) Nitrogen
    - (b) Potassium
    - (c) Phosphorus
    - (d) Calcium

**Answers:** 1–b, 2–a, 3–b, 4–c, 5–d, 6–c, 7–c, 8–b, 9–b, 10–d, 11–b, 12–b, 13–a, 14–c

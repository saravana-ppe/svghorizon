## 1. Concept of Health

- **Old view:** health = balance of certain "**humors**" (early Greeks like **Hippocrates** and **Indian Ayurveda**). Persons with "**black bile**" were thought to have hot personality and fevers.
- Disproved by:
  - **William Harvey** – discovered **blood circulation** using **experimental method**.
  - **Thermometer** showed **normal body temperature** in persons with black bile.
- Modern view: **mind influences the immune system** through the **neural and endocrine systems**; the immune system maintains health.
- Health is affected by:
  1. **Genetic disorders** – deficiencies a child is born with or inherits
  2. **Infections**
  3. **Life style** – food and water, rest and exercise, habits
- **Definition:** Health is **a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being** – not merely the absence of disease or physical fitness.
- Healthy people are more **efficient at work** → productivity, **economic prosperity**, **longevity**, reduced infant and maternal mortality.
- Maintaining health: **balanced diet, personal hygiene, regular exercise, yoga**; awareness about diseases, **vaccination**, proper **waste disposal**, **vector control**, hygiene of food and water.
- **Disease** – functioning of one or more organs/systems adversely affected, with signs and symptoms.
  - **Infectious diseases** – easily transmitted from one person to another (e.g., AIDS – fatal).
  - **Non-infectious diseases** – e.g., **cancer** (major cause of death among these).

## 2. Common Diseases in Humans

- **Pathogens** – disease-causing organisms: **bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoans, helminths**.
- Most **parasites** are pathogens (they harm the host by living in or on it).
- Pathogens must **adapt** to the host environment (e.g., gut pathogens must survive **low stomach pH** and digestive enzymes).

### 2.1 Master table of diseases

| Disease | Pathogen (type) | Transmission | Symptoms / key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Typhoid** | ***Salmonella typhi*** (bacterium) | **Contaminated food and water** → enters **small intestine** → migrates to other organs through **blood** | **Sustained high fever (39°–40°C)**, weakness, stomach pain, **constipation**, headache, loss of appetite; **intestinal perforation** and death in severe cases. Confirmed by **Widal test**. **Mary Mallon ("Typhoid Mary")** – a cook and **carrier** who spread typhoid for years |
| **Pneumonia** | ***Streptococcus pneumoniae*** and ***Haemophilus influenzae*** (bacteria) | **Inhaling droplets/aerosols** from infected person; **sharing glasses and utensils** | Infects **alveoli** – filled with fluid → severe respiratory problems. Fever, chills, cough, headache; in severe cases **lips and finger nails turn grey to bluish** |
| **Common cold** | **Rhino viruses** (virus) | **Droplets from cough/sneeze** inhaled directly, or through **contaminated objects** (pens, books, cups, doorknobs, keyboard, mouse) | Infects **nose and respiratory passage but NOT lungs**. Nasal congestion and discharge, sore throat, hoarseness, cough, headache, tiredness; lasts **3–7 days** |
| **Malaria** | ***Plasmodium*** (protozoan) – ***P. vivax, P. malariae, P. falciparum*** | Bite of **infected female *Anopheles*** mosquito | **Chill and high fever recurring every 3–4 days** (due to **haemozoin**). **Malignant malaria (*P. falciparum*)** – most serious, can be fatal |
| **Amoebiasis (amoebic dysentery)** | ***Entamoeba histolytica*** (protozoan) in **large intestine** | **Faecal contamination of food and water**; **houseflies as mechanical carriers** | **Constipation, abdominal pain and cramps**, stools with **excess mucous and blood clots** |
| **Ascariasis** | ***Ascaris*** – common round worm (helminth), **intestinal parasite** | Eggs excreted in faeces contaminate soil, water, plants; infection through **contaminated water, vegetables, fruits** | **Internal bleeding, muscular pain, fever, anaemia, blockage of intestinal passage** |
| **Filariasis (elephantiasis)** | ***Wuchereria*** (***W. bancrofti, W. malayi***) – filarial worms (helminth) | Bite of **female mosquito** vectors (*Culex*) | Slowly developing **chronic inflammation** of organs where they live for years, usually **lymphatic vessels of lower limbs**; **genital organs** also affected → gross deformities |
| **Ringworm** | Fungi: ***Microsporum, Trichophyton, Epidermophyton*** | From **soil**, or using **towels, clothes, comb** of infected persons | **Dry, scaly lesions** on skin, nails, scalp with **intense itching**. **Heat and moisture** help growth – thrive in **skin folds** (groin, between toes) |
| **Dengue** [Beyond current NCERT text – named in NEET syllabus] | **Dengue virus** (flavivirus) | Bite of ***Aedes*** mosquito (*A. aegypti*), mainly **day-biting** | High fever, severe headache, **pain behind the eyes**, **joint and muscle pain** ("break-bone fever"), rash; **platelet count** may fall, with bleeding in severe cases |
| **Chikungunya** [Beyond current NCERT text – named in NEET syllabus] | **Chikungunya virus** (alphavirus) | Bite of ***Aedes*** mosquito | Fever with **severe, often prolonged joint pain**, rash, headache |

- **Other bacterial diseases** (NCERT): **dysentery, plague, diphtheria**.
- NCERT notes recent widespread incidence of **vector-borne (*Aedes* mosquitoes) diseases – dengue and chikungunya** in many parts of India.

"Lips and nails turn bluish" – pneumonia. Genera of ringworm fungi and *Wuchereria* species are also frequently asked.

## 3. Life Cycle of *Plasmodium* (Malaria)

1. *Plasmodium* enters the human body as **sporozoites (infectious form)** through the bite of an infected **female *Anopheles*** mosquito.
2. Parasites **multiply within liver cells** (asexual reproduction in liver).
3. Then they attack **red blood cells**, multiply (asexual reproduction in RBCs) and cause their **rupture**.
4. Rupture of RBCs releases a toxic substance, **haemozoin**, responsible for **chill and high fever recurring every 3–4 days**.
5. Some parasites in RBCs develop into **gametocytes** (sexual stages).
6. When a female *Anopheles* bites an infected person, gametocytes enter the mosquito; **fertilisation and development** take place in the **mosquito's stomach/gut**.
7. Parasites multiply to form **sporozoites**, which are stored in the **salivary glands** of the mosquito.
8. When the mosquito bites a human, sporozoites are introduced → cycle repeats.

- *Plasmodium* needs **two hosts – human and mosquito**. The **female *Anopheles*** is both a host and the **vector** (transmitting agent).

> **Remember:** Sexual reproduction of *Plasmodium* occurs in the **mosquito**; asexual in **human** (liver cells and RBCs). Infective stage to human = **sporozoite**; infective stage to mosquito = **gametocyte**.

## 4. Prevention and Control of Infectious Diseases

| Type | Measures |
|---|---|
| **Personal hygiene** | Keep body clean; consume **clean drinking water, food, vegetables, fruits** |
| **Public hygiene** | **Proper disposal of waste and excreta**; periodic cleaning and **disinfection of water reservoirs**, pools, cesspools, tanks; standard hygiene in **public catering** |
| Especially for food/water-borne diseases | Typhoid, amoebiasis, ascariasis |
| **Air-borne diseases** (pneumonia, common cold) | Above measures + **avoid close contact** with infected persons or their belongings |
| **Vector-borne diseases** (malaria, filariasis, dengue, chikungunya) | **Control or eliminate vectors** and breeding places: avoid **stagnant water**; clean **household coolers**; **mosquito nets**; introduce fish like ***Gambusia*** (feeds on mosquito larvae); spray **insecticides** in ditches, drains and swamps; **wire mesh** on doors and windows |

- **Vaccines and immunisation** have **completely eradicated smallpox**; **polio, diphtheria, pneumonia and tetanus** have been controlled to a large extent.
- **Biotechnology** is making newer, safer vaccines available.
- **Antibiotics** and other drugs help treat infectious diseases.

## 5. Immunity

- **Immunity** – overall ability of the host to fight disease-causing organisms, conferred by the **immune system**.
- Two types: **innate immunity** and **acquired immunity**.

### 5.1 Innate immunity
- **Non-specific** defence; **present at birth**.
- Provides **barriers** to the entry of foreign agents. **Four types of barriers:**

| Barrier | Examples |
|---|---|
| **(i) Physical** | **Skin** (main barrier); **mucus coating** of epithelium lining respiratory, gastrointestinal and urogenital tracts (traps microbes) |
| **(ii) Physiological** | **Acid in stomach, saliva in mouth, tears from eyes** – prevent microbial growth |
| **(iii) Cellular** | Leukocytes: **polymorpho-nuclear leukocytes (PMNL – neutrophils), monocytes, natural killer cells** (a type of lymphocyte) in blood; **macrophages** in tissues – **phagocytose and destroy** microbes |
| **(iv) Cytokine** | Virus-infected cells secrete **interferons**, which **protect non-infected cells** from further viral infection |

### 5.2 Acquired immunity
- **Pathogen-specific**; characterised by **memory**.
- **First encounter** → **primary response** (low intensity).
- **Subsequent encounter** with the same pathogen → highly intensified **secondary or anamnestic response** (due to memory).
- Carried out by two types of lymphocytes: **B-lymphocytes and T-lymphocytes**.

| Type of acquired response | Mediated by | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Humoral immune response (antibody-mediated)** | **B-lymphocytes** | Produce an army of proteins called **antibodies** into the blood. **T-cells do not secrete antibodies but help B-cells produce them** |
| **Cell-mediated immunity (CMI)** | **T-lymphocytes** | Responsible for **graft rejection**; body differentiates "**self**" and "**non-self**" |

### 5.3 Structure of antibody
- Each antibody has **four peptide chains**: **two small light chains** and **two longer heavy chains** → represented as **H<sub>2</sub>L<sub>2</sub>**.
- Types: **IgA, IgM, IgE, IgG**, etc.
- Found in blood → **humoral** response.

### 5.4 Organ transplantation and graft rejection
- When organs like **heart, eye, liver, kidney** fail, **transplantation** may be the only remedy.
- Grafts from any source (animal, other primate, any human) would be **rejected** sooner or later.
- **Tissue matching and blood group matching** are essential; patient takes **immuno-suppressants all his/her life**.
- **Cell-mediated immune response is responsible for graft rejection.**

### 5.5 Active and passive immunity

| Feature | Active immunity | Passive immunity |
|---|---|---|
| How | Host exposed to **antigens** (living or dead microbes or other proteins) → **antibodies produced in the host** | **Ready-made antibodies** given directly |
| Speed | **Slow**; takes time for full response | **Immediate** |
| Examples | **Immunisation** (injecting microbes deliberately) or **natural infection** | **Colostrum** – yellowish fluid in initial days of lactation with abundant **IgA** to protect the infant; **antibodies from mother to foetus through placenta**; **antitoxin** for tetanus; **anti-venom** for snakebites |

### 5.6 Vaccination and immunisation
- Principle: **memory** of the immune system.
- **Vaccine** – preparation of **antigenic proteins** of the pathogen or **inactivated/weakened pathogen** introduced into the body.
- Antibodies produced **neutralise** pathogenic agents during actual infection.
- Vaccines also generate **memory B and T-cells** → quick recognition on subsequent exposure → **massive antibody production**.
- **Passive immunisation** – for quick response to deadly microbes:
  - **Tetanus** – inject **preformed antibodies or antitoxin** (antibodies to the toxin).
  - **Snakebite** – injection contains **preformed antibodies against snake venom**.
- **Recombinant DNA technology** – antigenic polypeptides produced in **bacteria or yeast** → large-scale vaccine production. Example: **hepatitis B vaccine produced from yeast**.

### 5.7 Allergies
- **Allergy** – **exaggerated response** of the immune system to certain antigens in the environment.
- **Allergens** – substances causing allergy: **mites in dust, pollens, animal dander**.
- Antibodies produced: **IgE type**.
- Symptoms: **sneezing, watery eyes, running nose, difficulty in breathing**.
- Caused by release of **histamine and serotonin** from **mast cells**.
- Diagnosis: patient exposed to or injected with **very small doses of possible allergens**; reactions studied.
- Treatment: **anti-histamine, adrenalin and steroids** quickly reduce symptoms.
- Modern life style has lowered immunity; more children in **metro cities** suffer from allergies and asthma – possibly due to the **protected environment** early in life.

### 5.8 Auto-immunity
- Memory-based acquired immunity evolved in **higher vertebrates**, based on the ability to differentiate foreign organisms from **self-cells**.
- Sometimes, due to genetic and unknown reasons, the body **attacks self-cells** → **auto-immune disease**.
- Example: **rheumatoid arthritis**. (Unit 5: **myasthenia gravis** is also autoimmune.)

## 6. Immune System in the Body

- Consists of **lymphoid organs, tissues, cells and soluble molecules** (antibodies).
- **Unique** – recognises foreign antigens, responds to them and **remembers** them.
- Also involved in **allergic reactions, auto-immune diseases and organ transplantation**.

### 6.1 Lymphoid organs

| Type | Organs | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary lymphoid organs** | **Bone marrow and thymus** | Immature lymphocytes **differentiate into antigen-sensitive lymphocytes** |
| **Secondary lymphoid organs** | **Spleen, lymph nodes, tonsils, Peyer's patches of small intestine, appendix** | Sites for **interaction of lymphocytes with antigen**; lymphocytes proliferate to become **effector cells** |

| Organ | Features |
|---|---|
| **Bone marrow** | **Main lymphoid organ** where **all blood cells including lymphocytes are produced** |
| **Thymus** | **Lobed** organ near the heart, **beneath the breastbone**; **large at birth**, reduces with age – **very small by puberty** |
| Bone marrow + thymus | Provide micro-environments for development and maturation of **T-lymphocytes** |
| **Spleen** | Large **bean-shaped**; mainly **lymphocytes and phagocytes**; **filters blood** by trapping blood-borne microorganisms; large **reservoir of erythrocytes** |
| **Lymph nodes** | Small solid structures along the lymphatic system; **trap microorganisms/antigens** in lymph and tissue fluid; activate lymphocytes → immune response |
| **MALT (mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue)** | Lymphoid tissue in the lining of **respiratory, digestive and urogenital tracts**; about **50% of the lymphoid tissue** in the human body |

> **Exam Tip:** Abbreviations asked in NCERT exercises: **MALT** – Mucosa-Associated Lymphoid Tissue; **CMI** – Cell-Mediated Immunity; **AIDS** – Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome; **NACO** – National AIDS Control Organisation; **HIV** – Human Immunodeficiency Virus; **ELISA** – Enzyme Linked Immuno-Sorbent Assay.

## 7. AIDS (Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome)

### 7.1 Meaning and history
- **Acquired** – deficiency acquired during lifetime (**not congenital**); **Immuno Deficiency** – deficiency of immune system; **Syndrome** – group of symptoms.
- First reported in **1981**; in about 25 years killed **more than 25 million** people worldwide.

### 7.2 Causative agent
- **Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)** – a **retrovirus**, having an **envelope enclosing the RNA genome**.

### 7.3 Transmission
- (a) **Sexual contact** with infected person
- (b) **Transfusion of contaminated blood** and blood products
- (c) **Sharing infected needles** (intravenous drug abusers)
- (d) **From infected mother to child through placenta**
- **High-risk groups:** people with **multiple sexual partners**, **intravenous drug addicts**, those needing **repeated blood transfusions**, **children of HIV-infected mothers**.
- **HIV/AIDS does NOT spread by mere touch or physical contact** – only through **body fluids**. Infected persons should **not be isolated** from family and society.
- **Time-lag** between infection and symptoms: a few months to many years (**usually 5–10 years**).

### 7.4 Mechanism (replication of retrovirus)
1. Virus enters the body → enters **macrophages**.
2. **RNA genome replicates to form viral DNA** with the enzyme **reverse transcriptase**.
3. Viral DNA **incorporates into host cell's DNA** and directs infected cells to produce **virus particles**.
4. **Macrophages continue producing virus** → act as an **"HIV factory"**.
5. Simultaneously, HIV enters **helper T-lymphocytes (T<sub>H</sub>)**, replicates and produces progeny viruses.
6. Progeny viruses released in blood attack other helper T-cells → **progressive decrease in the number of helper T-lymphocytes**.
7. Person suffers bouts of **fever, diarrhoea and weight loss**.
8. Due to fall in helper T-cells → **opportunistic infections** by bacteria (especially ***Mycobacterium***), viruses, fungi and parasites like ***Toxoplasma***.
9. Patient becomes so **immuno-deficient** that he/she cannot protect against these infections.

### 7.5 Diagnosis and treatment
- Widely used diagnostic test: **ELISA (enzyme linked immuno-sorbent assay)**.
- Treatment with **anti-retroviral drugs** is only **partially effective** – can **prolong life** but **cannot prevent death**.

### 7.6 Prevention
- **No cure → prevention is the best option.** "**Don't die of ignorance.**"
- HIV spreads mostly due to **conscious behaviour patterns** (unlike pneumonia or typhoid).
- **NACO (National AIDS Control Organisation)** and NGOs educate people; **WHO** programmes.
- Steps:
  - Making **blood from blood banks safe** from HIV
  - Using only **disposable needles and syringes**
  - **Free distribution of condoms**
  - **Controlling drug abuse**
  - Advocating **safe sex**
  - **Regular check-ups** for HIV in susceptible populations
- HIV/AIDS should not be hidden; infected people need **help and sympathy**, not rejection.

## 8. Cancer

### 8.1 Nature of cancer
- One of the most dreaded diseases; **more than a million Indians** suffer from cancer.
- In normal cells, growth and differentiation are **highly controlled**; in cancer cells these **regulatory mechanisms break down**.
- **Contact inhibition** – normal cells stop uncontrolled growth on contact with other cells. **Cancer cells lose contact inhibition** → divide continuously → **tumours**.

| Benign tumour | Malignant tumour |
|---|---|
| **Remains confined** to original location | Mass of proliferating **neoplastic/tumour cells** |
| Does not spread | **Grows rapidly, invades and damages** surrounding normal tissues; **starves normal cells** by competing for nutrients |
| Little damage | Cells sloughed off reach distant sites through **blood** and start new tumours = **metastasis** (**most feared property**) |

### 8.2 Causes of cancer – carcinogens
- **Carcinogens** – physical, chemical or biological agents that induce neoplastic transformation.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Physical** | **Ionising radiations – X-rays and gamma rays**; **non-ionising radiation – UV** → DNA damage |
| **Chemical** | Chemical carcinogens in **tobacco smoke** – major cause of **lung cancer** |
| **Biological** | **Oncogenic viruses** with **viral oncogenes** |
| **Genetic** | **Cellular oncogenes (c-onc) or proto-oncogenes** in normal cells – when **activated** under certain conditions → oncogenic transformation |

### 8.3 Detection and diagnosis
- **Early detection** allows successful treatment in many cases.
- **Biopsy** – a piece of suspected tissue cut into thin sections, stained and examined under microscope (**histopathological studies**) by a pathologist.
- **Blood and bone marrow tests** – for increased cell counts in **leukaemias**.
- **Radiography (X-rays), CT (computed tomography) and MRI (magnetic resonance imaging)** – for cancers of **internal organs**.
  - **CT** – uses **X-rays** to generate a **three-dimensional image**.
  - **MRI** – uses **strong magnetic fields and non-ionising radiations** to detect pathological and physiological changes.
- **Antibodies against cancer-specific antigens** used for detection.
- **Molecular biology** techniques detect **genes for inherited susceptibility** → such individuals can avoid particular carcinogens (e.g., tobacco smoke for lung cancer).

### 8.4 Treatment
- **Surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy and immunotherapy**.
- **Radiotherapy** – tumour cells irradiated lethally, protecting surrounding normal tissue.
- **Chemotherapy** – drugs kill cancer cells; some are tumour-specific; side effects like **hair loss, anaemia**.
- Most cancers: **combination of surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy**.
- Tumour cells **avoid detection and destruction by the immune system** → patients are given **biological response modifiers** such as **α-interferon**, which **activates the immune system** to destroy the tumour.

## 9. Drugs and Alcohol Abuse

- Commonly abused drugs: **opioids, cannabinoids and coca alkaloids** – mostly from **flowering plants**; some from **fungi**.

### 9.1 Commonly abused drugs

| Drug | Source | Action / effect | Mode of intake |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Opioids – heroin ("smack")** | **Diacetylmorphine** – obtained by **acetylation of morphine**, extracted from the **latex of poppy plant *Papaver somniferum*** | Bind to **opioid receptors** in **CNS and gastrointestinal tract**; heroin is a **depressant** – **slows down body functions**. White, odourless, bitter crystalline compound | **Snorting and injection** |
| **Cannabinoids** – **marijuana, hashish, charas, ganja** | **Inflorescences** (flower tops), leaves and **resin** of ***Cannabis sativa*** | Interact with **cannabinoid receptors** principally in the **brain**; known for effects on the **cardiovascular system**. Also abused by some **sportspersons** | **Inhalation and oral ingestion** |
| **Coca alkaloid – cocaine ("coke", "crack")** | **Coca plant *Erythroxylum coca*** (native to **South America**) | **Interferes with transport of the neurotransmitter dopamine**; potent **stimulant** of CNS → **euphoria and increased energy**; excessive dose → **hallucinations** | Usually **snorted** |
| Hallucinogenic plants | ***Atropa belladonna*** and ***Datura*** | Hallucinations | – |
| Medicinal drugs abused | **Barbiturates, amphetamines, benzodiazepines** | Normally used for **depression and insomnia** | – |

- **Morphine** – very effective **sedative and painkiller**; useful after surgery.
- **Drug abuse** = taking drugs for a purpose other than medicinal use, or in amounts/frequency that impair physical, physiological or psychological functions.

### 9.2 Tobacco
- Used for **more than 400 years**; **smoked, chewed or used as snuff**.
- Contains many chemicals including **nicotine (an alkaloid)**.
- **Nicotine stimulates the adrenal gland** to release **adrenaline and nor-adrenaline** → **raise blood pressure and heart rate**.
- **Smoking** is associated with increased **cancers of lung, urinary bladder and throat**, **bronchitis, emphysema, coronary heart disease, gastric ulcer**.
- **Tobacco chewing** → increased risk of **cancer of the oral cavity**.
- **Smoking increases carbon monoxide (CO) in blood** and **reduces haem-bound oxygen** → **oxygen deficiency** in the body.
- **Smoking paves the way to hard drugs.** Statutory warning on cigarette packets.

> **PYQ Trend:** Heroin = diacetylmorphine (depressant, from *Papaver somniferum*); cocaine = dopamine transport interference (*Erythroxylum coca*); cannabinoids = cardiovascular effects (*Cannabis sativa*); nicotine → adrenaline → BP ↑; smoking → CO ↑ and haem-bound O<sub>2</sub> ↓. These are asked almost every year in some form.

## 10. Adolescence and Drug/Alcohol Abuse

- **Adolescence** – both "**a period**" and "**a process**" during which a child matures in attitudes and beliefs for effective participation in society; about **12–18 years**; **bridge between childhood and adulthood**; a **very vulnerable** phase.
- **Causes of drug/alcohol use among youth:**
  - **Curiosity, need for adventure and excitement, experimentation**
  - Using drugs to **escape facing problems**
  - **Stress** from pressure to excel in **academics/examinations**
  - Perception that it is "**cool**" or progressive – promoted by TV, movies, newspapers, internet
  - **Unstable or unsupportive family structures**
  - **Peer pressure**

### 10.1 Addiction and dependence

| Addiction | Dependence |
|---|---|
| **Psychological attachment** to certain effects (euphoria, temporary well-being) | Tendency of the body to show a characteristic, unpleasant **withdrawal syndrome** if regular dose is **abruptly discontinued** |
| Repeated use increases **tolerance level of receptors** → respond only to **higher doses** → greater intake | Withdrawal symptoms: **anxiety, shakiness, nausea, sweating** (relieved when use resumes); may be **severe or life-threatening** – need medical supervision |
| **Even one use can be a fore-runner to addiction** | Leads patient to **ignore social norms** to get funds → social adjustment problems |

### 10.2 Effects of drug/alcohol abuse
- **Immediate:** **reckless behaviour, vandalism, violence**.
- **Excessive doses** → **coma and death** due to **respiratory failure, heart failure or cerebral haemorrhage**.
- **Combination of drugs or drugs with alcohol** → overdosing and death.
- **Warning signs in youth:** drop in academic performance, unexplained absence from school, lack of interest in personal hygiene, withdrawal, isolation, depression, fatigue, aggressive and rebellious behaviour, deteriorating relationships, loss of interest in hobbies, change in sleeping and eating habits, fluctuations in weight and appetite.
- May turn to **stealing**; causes **mental and financial distress** to family.
- **Intravenous drug users** are more likely to get **AIDS and Hepatitis B** (sharing needles) – both chronic and ultimately fatal.
- **Alcohol in adolescence** → heavy drinking in adulthood.
- **Chronic use** damages **nervous system and liver (cirrhosis)**.
- Drugs and alcohol during **pregnancy** adversely affect the **foetus**.

### 10.3 Misuse of drugs in sports
- Sportspersons misuse **narcotic analgesics, anabolic steroids, diuretics and certain hormones** to increase muscle strength, bulk and aggressiveness.

| Side effects of anabolic steroids in females | Side effects in males |
|---|---|
| **Masculinisation**, increased aggressiveness, mood swings, depression, **abnormal menstrual cycles**, **excessive facial and body hair**, **enlargement of clitoris**, **deepening of voice** | **Acne**, increased aggressiveness, mood swings, depression, **reduction in size of testicles**, **decreased sperm production**, kidney and liver dysfunction, **breast enlargement**, **premature baldness**, **enlargement of prostate gland** |

- In adolescents: severe facial and body **acne** and **premature closure of growth centres of long bones** → **stunted growth**.
- Effects may be **permanent** with prolonged use.

### 10.4 Prevention and control
- "**Prevention is better than cure**"; parents and teachers have special responsibility. Parenting with **high nurturance and consistent discipline** lowers risk.
1. **Avoid undue peer pressure** – respect the child's choice and personality; don't push beyond threshold limits.
2. **Education and counselling** – to face problems and stresses, accept disappointments and failures; channel energy into **sports, reading, music, yoga**, extracurricular activities.
3. **Seeking help from parents and peers** – vent anxiety and guilt; get guidance.
4. **Looking for danger signs** – alert parents, teachers and friends should identify warning signs and inform.
5. **Seeking professional and medical help** – psychologists, psychiatrists, **de-addiction and rehabilitation** programmes.

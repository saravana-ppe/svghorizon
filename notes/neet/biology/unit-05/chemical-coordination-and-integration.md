## 1. Endocrine Glands and Hormones

- Neural coordination is **fast but short-lived**, and nerves do not reach all cells → hormones give continuous regulation.
- **Endocrine glands** – **lack ducts** (**ductless glands**); secretions = **hormones**.
- **Modern definition:** **Hormones are non-nutrient chemicals which act as intercellular messengers and are produced in trace amounts.**
- Invertebrates have simple endocrine systems; vertebrates have many hormones.
- **Organised endocrine bodies:** pituitary, pineal, thyroid, adrenal, pancreas, parathyroid, thymus and gonads (testis/ovary).
- **Other hormone-producing organs:** gastrointestinal tract, liver, kidney, heart.

## 2. Hypothalamus

- **Basal part of diencephalon** (forebrain); regulates a wide spectrum of body functions.
- Contains groups of **neurosecretory cells called nuclei** that produce hormones regulating pituitary hormone synthesis and secretion.

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Releasing hormones** (stimulate pituitary) | **GnRH (gonadotrophin releasing hormone)** – stimulates pituitary synthesis and release of gonadotrophins |
| **Inhibiting hormones** (inhibit pituitary) | **Somatostatin** – inhibits release of **growth hormone** |

- Hypothalamic hormones pass through **axons**, are released from nerve endings, and reach the pituitary through a **portal circulatory system** → regulate the **anterior pituitary**.
- The **posterior pituitary** is under **direct neural regulation** of the hypothalamus.

## 3. Pituitary Gland

- Located in a bony cavity called **sella tursica**; attached to the hypothalamus by a **stalk**.
- Divisions:
  - **Adenohypophysis** = **pars distalis** (anterior pituitary) + **pars intermedia**
  - **Neurohypophysis** = **pars nervosa** (posterior pituitary)
- In humans, pars intermedia is **almost merged with pars distalis**.

| Part | Hormone | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Pars distalis (anterior pituitary)** | **Growth hormone (GH)** | Growth |
| | **Prolactin (PRL)** | Growth of **mammary glands** and **formation of milk** |
| | **TSH (thyroid stimulating hormone)** | Synthesis and secretion of **thyroid hormones** |
| | **ACTH (adrenocorticotrophic hormone)** | Synthesis and secretion of **glucocorticoids** from **adrenal cortex** |
| | **LH (luteinizing hormone)** | Males: **androgens** from testis. Females: **induces ovulation** of fully mature (**Graafian**) follicles; **maintains corpus luteum** |
| | **FSH (follicle stimulating hormone)** | Males: with androgens, regulates **spermatogenesis**. Females: **growth and development of ovarian follicles** |
| **Pars intermedia** | **MSH (melanocyte stimulating hormone)** | Acts on **melanocytes**; regulates **skin pigmentation** |
| **Pars nervosa (posterior pituitary)** – **stores and releases** (made by hypothalamus, transported axonally) | **Oxytocin** | Contraction of **smooth muscles**; vigorous **uterine contraction at childbirth**; **milk ejection** |
| | **Vasopressin (ADH)** | Acts on kidney; **resorption of water and electrolytes by distal tubules** → reduces water loss (**antidiuretic**) |

- **LH and FSH** = **gonadotrophins**.
- Pars distalis produces **six trophic hormones**; pars intermedia **one**; pars nervosa **two**.

### 3.1 Pituitary disorders

| Condition | Cause | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Gigantism** | **Over-secretion of GH** (in childhood) | Abnormal growth of body |
| **Pituitary dwarfism** | **Low secretion of GH** | Stunted growth |
| **Acromegaly** | **Excess GH in adults**, especially middle age | Severe **disfigurement (especially face)**; serious complications, premature death if unchecked; hard to diagnose early |
| **Diabetes insipidus** | Impaired synthesis/release of **ADH** | Diminished water conservation → **water loss and dehydration** |

## 4. Pineal Gland

- Located on the **dorsal side of forebrain**.
- Secretes **melatonin**.
- Regulates the **24-hour (diurnal) rhythm** – **sleep–wake cycle, body temperature**.
- Also influences **metabolism, pigmentation, menstrual cycle** and **defence capability**.

## 5. Thyroid Gland

- **Two lobes** on either side of the **trachea**, connected by a thin flap of connective tissue called **isthmus**.
- Composed of **follicles** and **stromal tissues**. Each follicle = **follicular cells** enclosing a cavity.
- Follicular cells synthesise **tetraiodothyronine (thyroxine, T<sub>4</sub>)** and **triiodothyronine (T<sub>3</sub>)**.
- **Iodine** is essential for normal hormone synthesis.

### 5.1 Functions of thyroid hormones
- Regulate **basal metabolic rate (BMR)**.
- Support **red blood cell formation**.
- Control metabolism of **carbohydrates, proteins and fats**.
- Maintain **water and electrolyte balance**.
- **Thyrocalcitonin (TCT)** – **protein hormone** that **regulates (lowers) blood calcium levels**.

### 5.2 Thyroid disorders

| Condition | Cause | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Hypothyroidism and goitre** | **Iodine deficiency** in diet | **Enlargement of thyroid gland** (goitre) |
| **Cretinism** | **Hypothyroidism during pregnancy** | Defective development of baby: **stunted growth, mental retardation, low IQ, abnormal skin, deaf-mutism** |
| Hypothyroidism in adult women | – | **Irregular menstrual cycle** |
| **Hyperthyroidism** | **Cancer** of thyroid or **nodules** | Abnormally high hormone levels; adversely affects physiology |
| **Exophthalmic goitre (Graves' disease)** | Form of **hyperthyroidism** | **Enlarged thyroid, protrusion of eyeballs, increased BMR, weight loss** |

## 6. Parathyroid Gland

- **Four** parathyroid glands on the **back side of the thyroid**, one pair in each lobe.
- Secrete the **peptide hormone parathyroid hormone (PTH)**; secretion regulated by **circulating Ca<sup>2+</sup> levels**.
- **PTH increases blood Ca<sup>2+</sup>** (**hypercalcemic hormone**):
  - Stimulates **bone resorption** (dissolution/demineralisation)
  - Stimulates **reabsorption of Ca<sup>2+</sup> by renal tubules**
  - Increases **Ca<sup>2+</sup> absorption from digested food**
- Along with **TCT**, maintains **calcium balance**.

> **Remember:** PTH raises blood calcium (hypercalcemic); TCT (thyrocalcitonin) lowers it. Low Ca<sup>++</sup> in body fluid → **tetany**.

## 7. Thymus

- **Lobular** structure between the lungs, **behind the sternum** on the ventral side of the **aorta**.
- Major role in development of the **immune system**.
- Secretes peptide hormones **thymosins**:
  - **Differentiation of T-lymphocytes** → **cell-mediated immunity**
  - Promote **production of antibodies** → **humoral immunity**
- **Degenerates in old individuals** → less thymosin → **weak immune responses** in old age.

## 8. Adrenal Gland

- **One pair**, one **above each kidney**.
- Two tissues: central **adrenal medulla** and outer **adrenal cortex**.

### 8.1 Adrenal medulla
- Secretes **adrenaline (epinephrine)** and **noradrenaline (norepinephrine)** = **catecholamines**.
- Rapidly secreted in **stress and emergency** → **emergency hormones / hormones of fight or flight**.
- Effects:
  - Increase **alertness, pupillary dilation, piloerection** (raising of hairs), **sweating**
  - Increase **heart beat, strength of heart contraction and rate of respiration**
  - Stimulate **glycogen breakdown** → increased blood glucose
  - Stimulate breakdown of **lipids and proteins**

### 8.2 Adrenal cortex
- Three layers (inner to outer): **zona reticularis** (inner), **zona fasciculata** (middle), **zona glomerulosa** (outer).
- Secretes **corticoids**:

| Type | Main hormone | Functions |
|---|---|---|
| **Glucocorticoids** (carbohydrate metabolism) | **Cortisol** | Stimulate **gluconeogenesis, lipolysis and proteolysis**; inhibit cellular uptake and use of amino acids; maintain **cardiovascular system and kidney functions**; **anti-inflammatory**; **suppress immune response**; stimulate **RBC production** |
| **Mineralocorticoids** (water and electrolytes) | **Aldosterone** | Acts on **renal tubules**: **reabsorption of Na<sup>+</sup> and water**; **excretion of K<sup>+</sup> and phosphate** → maintains electrolytes, body fluid volume, osmotic pressure and **blood pressure** |
| **Androgenic steroids** (small amounts) | – | Growth of **axial, pubic and facial hair** during puberty |

- **Addison's disease** – **underproduction of adrenal cortex hormones** → altered carbohydrate metabolism → **acute weakness and fatigue**.

## 9. Pancreas

- **Composite gland** – both **exocrine and endocrine**.
- Endocrine part = **Islets of Langerhans**: about **1 to 2 million** islets, only **1–2% of pancreatic tissue**.

| Cell | Hormone | Action | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **α-cells** | **Glucagon** (peptide) | Acts mainly on **liver cells (hepatocytes)**: stimulates **glycogenolysis** and **gluconeogenesis**; reduces cellular glucose uptake and use | **Hyperglycemia** → **hyperglycemic hormone** |
| **β-cells** | **Insulin** (peptide) | Acts mainly on **hepatocytes and adipocytes**: enhances **cellular glucose uptake and utilisation**; stimulates **glycogenesis** (glucose → glycogen) | **Hypoglycemia** → lowers blood glucose |

- Glucose homeostasis is maintained jointly by **insulin and glucagon**.
- **Diabetes mellitus** – **prolonged hyperglycemia**; loss of **glucose through urine** and formation of harmful **ketone bodies**. Treated with **insulin therapy**. (Due to insulin deficiency and/or insulin resistance.)

## 10. Gonads

### 10.1 Testis
- A pair in the **scrotal sac** (outside abdomen). Dual function: **primary sex organ** and **endocrine gland**.
- Composed of **seminiferous tubules** and **stromal/interstitial tissue**.
- **Leydig cells (interstitial cells)** in intertubular spaces produce **androgens, mainly testosterone**.
- **Functions of androgens:**
  - Development, maturation and function of **male accessory sex organs** (epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, prostate, urethra)
  - **Muscular growth, facial and axillary hair, aggressiveness, low pitch of voice**
  - Major stimulatory role in **spermatogenesis**
  - Act on CNS → **male sexual behaviour (libido)**
  - **Anabolic** effects on protein and carbohydrate metabolism

### 10.2 Ovary
- A pair in the abdomen; primary female sex organ; produces **one ovum** per menstrual cycle.
- Composed of **ovarian follicles** and **stromal tissue**.
- Produces steroid hormones **estrogen** and **progesterone**.
- **Estrogen** – mainly from **growing ovarian follicles**:
  - Growth and activity of **female secondary sex organs**
  - Development of growing ovarian follicles
  - **Female secondary sex characters** (e.g., high-pitched voice)
  - **Mammary gland development**
  - Regulates **female sexual behaviour**
- **Progesterone** – mainly from the **corpus luteum** (formed from the ruptured follicle after ovulation):
  - **Supports pregnancy**
  - Acts on mammary glands → formation of **alveoli** (milk-storing sacs) and **milk secretion**

## 11. Hormones of Heart, Kidney and Gastrointestinal Tract

| Source | Hormone | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Atrial wall of heart** | **Atrial natriuretic factor (ANF)** (peptide) | When BP increases, causes **dilation of blood vessels** → **reduces blood pressure** |
| **Juxtaglomerular cells of kidney** | **Erythropoietin** (peptide) | Stimulates **erythropoiesis (RBC formation)** |
| **GI tract** | **Gastrin** | Acts on **gastric glands**: secretion of **HCl and pepsinogen** |
| | **Secretin** | Acts on **exocrine pancreas**: secretion of **water and bicarbonate ions** |
| | **Cholecystokinin (CCK)** | Acts on **pancreas and gall bladder**: secretion of **pancreatic enzymes and bile juice** |
| | **Gastric inhibitory peptide (GIP)** | **Inhibits gastric secretion and motility** |
| Non-endocrine tissues | **Growth factors** | Normal growth of tissues, repair and regeneration |

## 12. Mechanism of Hormone Action

- Hormones act by binding to specific proteins called **hormone receptors**, present **only in target tissues**.
  - **Membrane-bound receptors** – on the cell membrane of target cells
  - **Intracellular receptors** – inside the cell, mostly **nuclear receptors**
- Binding → **hormone–receptor complex**. Each receptor is **specific** to one hormone.

### 12.1 Classification by chemical nature

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Peptide, polypeptide, protein hormones** | **Insulin, glucagon, pituitary hormones, hypothalamic hormones** |
| **Steroids** | **Cortisol, testosterone, estradiol, progesterone** |
| **Iodothyronines** | **Thyroid hormones** |
| **Amino-acid derivatives** | **Epinephrine** |

### 12.2 Two mechanisms

| Membrane-bound receptor (e.g., protein hormones like FSH) | Intracellular receptor (e.g., steroid hormones, iodothyronines) |
|---|---|
| Hormone **does not enter** the target cell | Hormone enters the cell |
| Generates **second messengers** (e.g., **cyclic AMP, IP<sub>3</sub>, Ca<sup>++</sup>**) | Hormone–receptor complex interacts with the **genome** |
| Second messengers regulate **cellular metabolism** | Regulates **gene expression or chromosome function** |
| Biochemical responses → physiological functions | Cumulative biochemical actions → physiological and developmental effects |

### 12.3 Hormones – quick classification by effect (NCERT exercise pattern)

| Category | Hormone |
|---|---|
| **Hyperglycemic** | **Glucagon** (also cortisol, adrenaline raise blood glucose) |
| **Hypoglycemic** | **Insulin** |
| **Hypercalcemic** | **PTH** |
| **Hypocalcemic** | **Thyrocalcitonin (TCT)** |
| **Gonadotrophic** | **LH, FSH** |
| **Progestational** | **Progesterone** |
| **Blood pressure lowering** | **ANF** |
| **Androgens / estrogens** | **Testosterone / estradiol** |
| **Emergency hormone** | **Adrenaline** |
| **Antidiuretic** | **Vasopressin (ADH)** |

> **PYQ Trend:** Hormone–gland–disorder matching (GH – acromegaly; ADH – diabetes insipidus; thyroxine – cretinism, goitre, Graves'; cortex – Addison's; insulin – diabetes mellitus), second messengers (cAMP, IP<sub>3</sub>, Ca<sup>++</sup>), and "steroid hormones act via intracellular receptors" are asked very often.

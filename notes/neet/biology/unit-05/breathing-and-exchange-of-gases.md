## 1. Respiratory Organs in Animals (recall)

- **Breathing** = exchange of O<sub>2</sub> from the atmosphere with CO<sub>2</sub> produced by cells (commonly called respiration).
- Mechanisms vary with **habitat and level of organisation**.

| Animal group | Respiratory organ / method |
|---|---|
| Sponges, coelenterates, flatworms (lower invertebrates) | **Simple diffusion over entire body surface** |
| Earthworms | **Moist cuticle** |
| Insects | **Tracheal tubes** (network of tubes) |
| Most aquatic arthropods and molluscs | **Gills** (branchial respiration) |
| Terrestrial forms | **Lungs** (pulmonary respiration) |
| Fishes | Gills |
| Amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals | Lungs |
| Frogs (amphibians) | Also **moist skin** (cutaneous respiration) |

## 2. Human Respiratory System

### 2.1 Pathway of air
**External nostrils** (above upper lip) → **nasal passage** → **nasal chamber** → **pharynx** (common passage for food and air) → **larynx** → **trachea** → **primary bronchi** → secondary bronchi → tertiary bronchi → bronchioles → **terminal bronchioles** → **alveoli**

### 2.2 Key structures
- **Larynx** – cartilaginous box; helps in **sound production** → "**sound box**".
- **Epiglottis** – thin elastic **cartilaginous flap** that covers the **glottis** during **swallowing**, preventing entry of food into the larynx.
- **Trachea** – straight tube extending to mid-thoracic cavity; divides at the level of the **5th thoracic vertebra** into **right and left primary bronchi**.
- **Trachea, primary, secondary, tertiary bronchi and initial bronchioles** are supported by **incomplete cartilaginous rings**.
- **Alveoli** – very thin, irregular-walled, **vascularised bag-like** structures arising from each terminal bronchiole.
- **Lungs** = branching network of bronchi, bronchioles and alveoli. Two lungs covered by a **double-layered pleura** with **pleural fluid** between them, which **reduces friction** on the lung surface.
  - **Outer pleural membrane** – in contact with the **thoracic lining**
  - **Inner pleural membrane** – in contact with the **lung surface**

### 2.3 Conducting and respiratory parts

| Part | Extent | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Conducting part** | External nostrils up to **terminal bronchioles** | Transports air to alveoli; **clears foreign particles**, **humidifies**, brings air to **body temperature** |
| **Respiratory (exchange) part** | **Alveoli and their ducts** | Site of actual **diffusion of O<sub>2</sub> and CO<sub>2</sub>** between blood and air |

### 2.4 Thoracic chamber
- **Anatomically air-tight** chamber.
- Formed: **dorsally** by vertebral column, **ventrally** by sternum, **laterally** by ribs, **lower side** by dome-shaped **diaphragm**.
- Any change in thoracic volume is reflected in the lung (pulmonary) volume – essential because we **cannot directly alter pulmonary volume**.

### 2.5 Steps of respiration
1. **Breathing (pulmonary ventilation)** – atmospheric air drawn in; CO<sub>2</sub>-rich alveolar air released.
2. **Diffusion of gases** (O<sub>2</sub> and CO<sub>2</sub>) across the **alveolar membrane**.
3. **Transport of gases** by blood.
4. **Diffusion of O<sub>2</sub> and CO<sub>2</sub>** between **blood and tissues**.
5. **Utilisation of O<sub>2</sub>** by cells for catabolic reactions and release of CO<sub>2</sub> (**cellular respiration**).

## 3. Mechanism of Breathing

- Two stages: **inspiration** (air drawn in) and **expiration** (alveolar air released).
- Air moves by creating a **pressure gradient** between lungs and atmosphere.
  - **Inspiration** – intra-pulmonary pressure **less than** atmospheric pressure (negative pressure in lungs).
  - **Expiration** – intra-pulmonary pressure **higher than** atmospheric pressure.
- Muscles: **diaphragm** and **external and internal intercostals** (between ribs).

| Inspiration | Expiration |
|---|---|
| **Diaphragm contracts** → increases thoracic volume in the **antero-posterior axis** | Diaphragm **relaxes** and returns to normal position |
| **External intercostal muscles contract** → lift ribs and sternum → increases volume in the **dorso-ventral axis** | Intercostal muscles relax; sternum returns |
| Thoracic volume ↑ → pulmonary volume ↑ → intra-pulmonary pressure ↓ below atmospheric | Thoracic and pulmonary volume ↓ → intra-pulmonary pressure slightly ↑ above atmospheric |
| Air rushes **in** | Air expelled **out** |

- Strength of inspiration and expiration can be increased with **additional abdominal muscles** (forced breathing).
- A healthy human breathes **12–16 times per minute**.
- Volumes are measured using a **spirometer** (clinical assessment of pulmonary function).

## 4. Respiratory Volumes and Capacities

| Volume / capacity | Definition | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Tidal Volume (TV)** | Air inspired or expired during **normal** respiration | About **500 mL** (6000–8000 mL per minute) |
| **Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV)** | Additional air inspired by **forcible inspiration** | **2500–3000 mL** |
| **Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV)** | Additional air expired by **forcible expiration** | **1000–1100 mL** |
| **Residual Volume (RV)** | Air **remaining in lungs even after forcible expiration** | **1100–1200 mL** |
| **Inspiratory Capacity (IC)** | Total air inspired after a normal expiration | **TV + IRV** |
| **Expiratory Capacity (EC)** | Total air expired after a normal inspiration | **TV + ERV** |
| **Functional Residual Capacity (FRC)** | Air remaining in lungs after a **normal expiration** | **ERV + RV** |
| **Vital Capacity (VC)** | Maximum air breathed in after a forced expiration (or out after a forced inspiration) | **ERV + TV + IRV** |
| **Total Lung Capacity (TLC)** | Total air in lungs at the end of a forced inspiration | **RV + ERV + TV + IRV** = **VC + RV** |

> **Exam Tip:** Using average values (TV 500, IRV 2500–3000, ERV 1000–1100, RV 1100–1200): VC ≈ 4000–4600 mL; TLC ≈ 5100–5800 mL; FRC ≈ 2100–2300 mL. Air in lungs after normal breathing = FRC. Tidal volume per hour ≈ 500 × 12–16 × 60 = 3,60,000–4,80,000 mL.

## 5. Exchange of Gases

- **Alveoli** are the primary sites of exchange; exchange also occurs between **blood and tissues**.
- By **simple diffusion**, based on **pressure/concentration gradient**. Also affected by **solubility** of gases and **thickness of membranes**.
- **Partial pressure** – pressure contributed by an individual gas in a mixture (pO<sub>2</sub>, pCO<sub>2</sub>).

### 5.1 Table 14.1 – Partial pressures (mm Hg)

| Gas | Atmospheric air | Alveoli | Deoxygenated blood | Oxygenated blood | Tissues |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **O<sub>2</sub>** | **159** | **104** | **40** | **95** | **40** |
| **CO<sub>2</sub>** | **0.3** | **40** | **45** | **40** | **45** |

- O<sub>2</sub> gradient: alveoli → blood → tissues. CO<sub>2</sub> gradient: tissues → blood → alveoli.
- **Solubility of CO<sub>2</sub> is 20–25 times higher than O<sub>2</sub>** → more CO<sub>2</sub> diffuses per unit difference in partial pressure.

### 5.2 Diffusion membrane – three layers
1. **Thin squamous epithelium of alveoli**
2. **Endothelium of alveolar capillaries**
3. **Basement substance** between them (thin basement membrane supporting the squamous epithelium + basement membrane around the single-layered capillary endothelium)
- Total thickness **much less than a millimetre**.

> **PYQ Trend:** Partial pressure table values (especially pO<sub>2</sub> alveoli 104, deoxygenated blood 40, oxygenated 95; pCO<sub>2</sub> 40/45) and "CO<sub>2</sub> is 20–25 times more soluble" are high-frequency questions.

## 6. Transport of Gases

| Gas | Form | Percentage |
|---|---|---|
| O<sub>2</sub> | Bound to haemoglobin in **RBCs** (oxyhaemoglobin) | **97%** |
| O<sub>2</sub> | Dissolved in **plasma** | **3%** |
| CO<sub>2</sub> | As **bicarbonate** | **70%** |
| CO<sub>2</sub> | By RBCs as **carbamino-haemoglobin** | **20–25%** |
| CO<sub>2</sub> | Dissolved in plasma | **7%** |

### 6.1 Transport of oxygen
- **Haemoglobin** – red, **iron-containing** pigment in RBCs; binds O<sub>2</sub> **reversibly** → **oxyhaemoglobin**.
- Each haemoglobin molecule carries a **maximum of four O<sub>2</sub> molecules**.
- Binding depends primarily on **pO<sub>2</sub>**; also affected by **pCO<sub>2</sub>, H<sup>+</sup> concentration and temperature**.
- **Oxygen dissociation curve** – **sigmoid** curve of % saturation of Hb vs pO<sub>2</sub>.

| Site | Conditions | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Alveoli** | High pO<sub>2</sub>, low pCO<sub>2</sub>, low H<sup>+</sup>, lower temperature | **Formation of oxyhaemoglobin** |
| **Tissues** | Low pO<sub>2</sub>, high pCO<sub>2</sub>, high H<sup>+</sup>, higher temperature | **Dissociation of O<sub>2</sub>** from oxyhaemoglobin |

- Every **100 mL of oxygenated blood delivers about 5 mL of O<sub>2</sub>** to tissues under normal conditions.

### 6.2 Transport of carbon dioxide
- **Carbamino-haemoglobin (20–25%)** – binding depends on **pCO<sub>2</sub>**; **pO<sub>2</sub>** is a major factor affecting it.
  - Tissues (high pCO<sub>2</sub>, low pO<sub>2</sub>) → **more binding**
  - Alveoli (low pCO<sub>2</sub>, high pO<sub>2</sub>) → **dissociation**; CO<sub>2</sub> released
- **Carbonic anhydrase** – very high concentration in **RBCs**, minute quantity in plasma. Catalyses in both directions:
  - **CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O ⇌ H<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub> ⇌ HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>−</sup> + H<sup>+</sup>**
  - At tissues (high pCO<sub>2</sub>) → forms HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>−</sup> and H<sup>+</sup>.
  - At alveoli (low pCO<sub>2</sub>) → reverse; CO<sub>2</sub> and H<sub>2</sub>O formed and CO<sub>2</sub> released.
- Every **100 mL of deoxygenated blood delivers about 4 mL of CO<sub>2</sub>** to the alveoli.

## 7. Regulation of Respiration

| Centre / receptor | Location | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Respiratory rhythm centre** | **Medulla** | **Primarily responsible** for regulating respiratory rhythm |
| **Pneumotaxic centre** | **Pons** | **Moderates** the rhythm centre; neural signal **reduces the duration of inspiration** and thereby alters respiratory rate |
| **Chemosensitive area** | **Adjacent to the rhythm centre** (medulla) | Highly sensitive to **CO<sub>2</sub> and H<sup>+</sup>**; their increase activates it → signals rhythm centre to eliminate them |
| **Receptors** | **Aortic arch and carotid artery** | Recognise changes in **CO<sub>2</sub> and H<sup>+</sup>**; send signals to rhythm centre |

- **Role of oxygen in regulating respiratory rhythm is quite insignificant.**

## 8. Disorders of the Respiratory System

| Disorder | Description |
|---|---|
| **Asthma** | Difficulty in breathing causing **wheezing** due to **inflammation of bronchi and bronchioles** |
| **Emphysema** | **Chronic** disorder in which **alveolar walls are damaged** → respiratory surface decreased. Major cause: **cigarette smoking** |
| **Occupational respiratory disorders** | In industries involving **grinding or stone-breaking**, excess dust overwhelms body defence; long exposure → inflammation → **fibrosis** (proliferation of fibrous tissue) → serious lung damage. Workers should wear **protective masks** |

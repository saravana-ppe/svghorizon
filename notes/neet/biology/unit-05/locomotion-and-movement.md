## 1. Movement and Locomotion

- Movement is a significant feature of living beings: protoplasmic streaming (*Amoeba*), cilia, flagella, tentacles.
- **Locomotion** – **voluntary movements** resulting in **change of place** (walking, running, climbing, flying, swimming).
- Same structures may serve both: *Paramoecium* cilia – food movement through cytopharynx and locomotion; *Hydra* tentacles – prey capture and locomotion.
- **All locomotions are movements, but all movements are not locomotions.**
- Locomotion is for food, shelter, mate, breeding grounds, favourable climate or escape from predators.

## 2. Types of Movement

| Type | Mechanism | Examples in human body |
|---|---|---|
| **Amoeboid** | **Pseudopodia** formed by streaming of protoplasm; **microfilaments** (cytoskeleton) involved | **Macrophages and leucocytes** |
| **Ciliary** | Coordinated movement of cilia in internal tubular organs lined by **ciliated epithelium** | **Trachea** (removes dust and foreign particles); **passage of ova** through female reproductive tract |
| **Flagellar** | Flagella | **Swimming of spermatozoa**; water current in canal system of **sponges**; locomotion of ***Euglena*** |
| **Muscular** | Contractile property of muscles | Movement of **limbs, jaws, tongue**; locomotion |

- Locomotion requires coordinated activity of **muscular, skeletal and neural systems**.

## 3. Muscle

- **Specialised tissue of mesodermal origin**.
- About **40–50% of body weight** of a human adult.
- Properties: **excitability, contractility, extensibility and elasticity**.

| Feature | Skeletal | Visceral (smooth) | Cardiac |
|---|---|---|---|
| Location | Associated with **skeletal components** | **Inner walls of hollow visceral organs** – alimentary canal, reproductive tract | **Heart** |
| Appearance | **Striated** | **Non-striated (smooth)** | **Striated**; cells in **branching pattern** |
| Control | **Voluntary** | **Involuntary** | **Involuntary** (not directly controlled by nervous system) |
| Function | Locomotion, changes of posture | Transport of food through gut, gametes through genital tract | Pumping blood |

## 4. Structure of Skeletal Muscle

- Each muscle is made of **muscle bundles (fascicles)** held together by a collagenous connective tissue layer called **fascia**.
- Each bundle contains **muscle fibres (muscle cells)**.
- Each fibre is lined by plasma membrane **sarcolemma**, enclosing **sarcoplasm**.
- **Muscle fibre is a syncytium** – sarcoplasm contains **many nuclei**.
- **Sarcoplasmic reticulum** (ER of muscle) = **store house of calcium ions**.
- Many parallel filaments in sarcoplasm = **myofilaments or myofibrils**.

### 4.1 Bands and lines of the myofibril

| Structure | Description |
|---|---|
| **I-band (Isotropic, light band)** | Contains **actin (thin filaments)** |
| **A-band (Anisotropic, dark band)** | Contains **myosin (thick filaments)** |
| **Z-line** | Elastic fibre in the **centre of each I-band**, bisecting it; **thin filaments firmly attached** to it |
| **M-line** | Thin fibrous membrane in the **middle of the A-band** holding thick filaments together |
| **H-zone** | **Central part of thick filament not overlapped by thin filaments** (in resting state) |
| **Sarcomere** | Portion of myofibril **between two successive Z-lines**; **functional unit of contraction** |

- Striated appearance is due to the distribution of **actin and myosin**.
- Actin filaments are thinner (**thin filaments**); myosin filaments thicker (**thick filaments**).

### 4.2 Contractile proteins

**Actin (thin) filament:**
- Two **F (filamentous) actins** helically wound to each other.
- Each F-actin is a polymer of monomeric **G (globular) actins**.
- Two filaments of **tropomyosin** run close to the F-actins throughout the length.
- **Troponin** (complex protein) distributed at regular intervals on tropomyosin.
- In the **resting state**, a subunit of troponin **masks the active binding sites for myosin** on actin.

**Myosin (thick) filament:**
- Polymerised protein made of many monomers called **meromyosins**.
- Each meromyosin has:
  - **Heavy meromyosin (HMM)** – **globular head with a short arm**
  - **Light meromyosin (LMM)** – **tail**
- HMM (head + short arm) projects outwards at regular distance and angle = **cross arm**.
- **Globular head is an active ATPase enzyme**; has **binding sites for ATP** and **active sites for actin**.

## 5. Mechanism of Muscle Contraction – Sliding Filament Theory

**Sliding filament theory:** contraction of a muscle fibre takes place by the **sliding of the thin filaments over the thick filaments**.

1. **Signal from CNS via a motor neuron**. A motor neuron + the muscle fibres connected to it = **motor unit**.
2. Junction between motor neuron and sarcolemma = **neuromuscular junction or motor-end plate**.
3. Neural signal releases the neurotransmitter **acetylcholine** → generates an **action potential in the sarcolemma**.
4. Action potential spreads through the fibre → **release of Ca<sup>++</sup>** from sarcoplasmic reticulum into sarcoplasm.
5. **Ca<sup>++</sup> binds to a subunit of troponin** on actin → **removes masking of active sites** for myosin.
6. Using energy from **ATP hydrolysis**, the **myosin head binds to exposed active sites on actin** → **cross bridge** forms.
7. Cross bridge **pulls actin filaments towards the centre of the A-band**; Z-lines pulled inwards → **sarcomere shortens (contraction)**.
8. Myosin releases **ADP and Pi** and goes back to relaxed state.
9. **A new ATP binds** → cross bridge is **broken**.
10. ATP hydrolysed again by myosin head → cycle of cross-bridge formation and breakage repeats → further sliding.
11. Process continues until **Ca<sup>++</sup> is pumped back** into sarcoplasmic cisternae → actin masked again → Z-lines return → **relaxation**.

### 5.1 Changes during contraction

| Structure | During contraction |
|---|---|
| **I-band** | **Reduced** (shortens) |
| **A-band** | **Retains its length** (no change) |
| **H-zone** | Reduces / disappears |
| **Sarcomere** | Shortens |

- **Fatigue** – repeated activation leads to accumulation of **lactic acid** due to **anaerobic breakdown of glycogen**.

### 5.2 Red and white muscle fibres

| Feature | Red fibres | White fibres |
|---|---|---|
| **Myoglobin** (red O<sub>2</sub>-storing pigment) | **High** | **Very less** (pale/whitish) |
| Mitochondria | **Plenty** | Few |
| Sarcoplasmic reticulum | – | **High** |
| Energy | **Aerobic** (aerobic muscles) | **Anaerobic** |

> **PYQ Trend:** "During contraction, which band remains unchanged?" (A-band). "Ca<sup>++</sup> binds to?" (troponin). "Site of Ca<sup>++</sup> storage?" (sarcoplasmic reticulum). "ATPase activity?" (myosin head). "Neurotransmitter at neuromuscular junction?" (acetylcholine).

## 6. Skeletal System

- Framework of **bones and a few cartilages**.
- **Bone** – very hard matrix due to **calcium salts**; **cartilage** – slightly pliable matrix due to **chondroitin salts**.
- **206 bones** and a few cartilages in humans.
- Two divisions: **axial (80 bones)** and **appendicular (126 bones)**.

### 6.1 Axial skeleton (80 bones)

**Skull (22 bones)**
- **Cranial bones – 8** → form the cranium protecting the brain.
- **Facial bones – 14** → front part of skull.
- **Hyoid** – single **U-shaped** bone at the base of the buccal cavity.
- **Ear ossicles** – three in each middle ear: **malleus, incus, stapes**.
- Skull articulates with vertebral column by **two occipital condyles** → **dicondylic skull**.

**Vertebral column (26 vertebrae)**
- **Dorsally placed**; extends from the base of the skull; main framework of trunk.
- Each vertebra has a central **neural canal** through which the **spinal cord** passes.
- First vertebra = **atlas**; articulates with occipital condyles.

| Region | Number |
|---|---|
| Cervical | **7** |
| Thoracic | **12** |
| Lumbar | **5** |
| Sacral | **1 (fused)** |
| Coccygeal | **1 (fused)** |

- **Seven cervical vertebrae in almost all mammals** including humans.
- Functions: protects spinal cord, supports head, attachment for ribs and back musculature.

**Sternum** – flat bone on the **ventral midline of thorax**.

**Ribs (12 pairs)**
- Thin flat bones connected **dorsally to vertebral column** and **ventrally to sternum**.
- Two articulation surfaces on dorsal end → **bicephalic**.

| Type | Pairs | Attachment |
|---|---|---|
| **True ribs** | **1st–7th** | Dorsally to thoracic vertebrae; ventrally to sternum by **hyaline cartilage** |
| **Vertebrochondral (false) ribs** | **8th, 9th, 10th** | Do not articulate directly with sternum; join the **7th rib** with hyaline cartilage |
| **Floating ribs** | **11th and 12th** | **Not connected ventrally** |

- **Rib cage** = thoracic vertebrae + ribs + sternum.

### 6.2 Appendicular skeleton
- Limb bones + girdles. **Each limb has 30 bones**.

| Fore limb (hand) | Number | Hind limb (leg) | Number |
|---|---|---|---|
| Humerus | 1 | **Femur** (thigh bone – **longest bone**) | 1 |
| Radius and ulna | 2 | Tibia and fibula | 2 |
| **Carpals** (wrist) | **8** | **Tarsals** (ankle) | **7** |
| **Metacarpals** (palm) | **5** | **Metatarsals** | **5** |
| **Phalanges** (digits) | **14** | **Phalanges** | **14** |
| – | – | **Patella** (knee cap; cup-shaped; covers knee ventrally) | 1 |
| **Total** | **30** | **Total** | **30** |

**Girdles** (articulate limbs with axial skeleton; each has two halves):

| Pectoral girdle (each half) | Pelvic girdle |
|---|---|
| **Clavicle** (collar bone) – long slender bone with **two curvatures** | Two **coxal bones**, each formed by fusion of **ilium, ischium and pubis** |
| **Scapula** – large triangular flat bone in the dorsal thorax **between the 2nd and 7th ribs** | **Acetabulum** – cavity at the point of fusion; **thigh bone (femur) articulates** here |
| **Spine** of scapula projects as a flat expanded process = **acromion**; **clavicle articulates** with it | Two halves meet ventrally at the **pubic symphysis** (containing **fibrous cartilage**) |
| **Glenoid cavity** – depression below the acromion; articulates with **head of humerus** → **shoulder joint** | – |

## 7. Joints

- Points of contact between bones, or between bones and cartilages. Joint acts as a **fulcrum** for muscle force.

| Type | Movement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Fibrous** | **No movement** | Flat **skull bones** fused by dense fibrous connective tissue as **sutures** (cranium) |
| **Cartilaginous** | **Limited movement** | Joint between **adjacent vertebrae** |
| **Synovial** | **Considerable movement**; **fluid-filled synovial cavity** between articulating surfaces | See below |

**Types of synovial joints:**

| Synovial joint | Example |
|---|---|
| **Ball and socket** | Between **humerus and pectoral girdle** (shoulder); also femur and acetabulum (hip) |
| **Hinge** | **Knee joint** (also between phalanges) |
| **Pivot** | Between **atlas and axis** |
| **Gliding** | Between **carpals** |
| **Saddle** | Between **carpal and metacarpal of thumb** |

## 8. Disorders of Muscular and Skeletal System

| Disorder | Description |
|---|---|
| **Myasthenia gravis** | **Autoimmune** disorder affecting the **neuromuscular junction** → fatigue, weakening and **paralysis of skeletal muscle** |
| **Muscular dystrophy** | **Progressive degeneration of skeletal muscle**, mostly due to **genetic disorder** |
| **Tetany** | **Rapid spasms (wild contractions)** in muscle due to **low Ca<sup>++</sup>** in body fluid |
| **Arthritis** | **Inflammation of joints** |
| **Osteoporosis** | **Age-related**; **decreased bone mass**, increased fractures; common cause: **decreased estrogen** |
| **Gout** | Inflammation of joints due to **accumulation of uric acid crystals** |

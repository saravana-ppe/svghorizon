## 1. Blood

- Simple organisms (sponges, coelenterates) circulate water from surroundings through body cavities. Complex organisms use body fluids – **blood** (most common) and **lymph**.
- **Blood** – a **special connective tissue** with a fluid matrix (**plasma**) and **formed elements**.

### 1.1 Plasma (about 55% of blood)
- **Straw-coloured, viscous** fluid.
- **90–92% water**; **6–8% proteins**.

| Plasma protein | Function |
|---|---|
| **Fibrinogen** | **Clotting / coagulation** of blood |
| **Globulins** | **Defence mechanisms** of the body |
| **Albumins** | **Osmotic balance** |

- Also contains minerals (Na<sup>+</sup>, Ca<sup>++</sup>, Mg<sup>++</sup>, HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>−</sup>, Cl<sup>−</sup>), glucose, amino acids, lipids (always in transit).
- **Clotting factors** present in **inactive** form.
- **Serum = plasma without the clotting factors.**

### 1.2 Formed elements (about 45% of blood)

**Erythrocytes (RBCs)**
- **Most abundant** blood cells: **5 to 5.5 million per mm<sup>3</sup>** in a healthy adult man.
- Formed in **red bone marrow** in adults.
- **No nucleus** in most mammals; **biconcave**.
- Contain **haemoglobin** (red, iron-containing complex protein): **12–16 g per 100 mL** of blood.
- Average lifespan **120 days**; destroyed in the **spleen ("graveyard of RBCs")**.

**Leucocytes (WBCs)**
- **Colourless** (no haemoglobin); **nucleated**; **6000–8000 per mm<sup>3</sup>**; generally short-lived.

| Category | Type | % of WBCs | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Granulocytes** | **Neutrophils** | **60–65%** (most abundant) | **Phagocytic** – destroy foreign organisms |
| | **Eosinophils** | **2–3%** | **Resist infections**; associated with **allergic reactions** |
| | **Basophils** | **0.5–1%** (least) | Secrete **histamine, serotonin, heparin**; involved in **inflammatory reactions** |
| **Agranulocytes** | **Lymphocytes** | **20–25%** | **B and T** types; responsible for **immune responses** |
| | **Monocytes** | **6–8%** | **Phagocytic** |

**Platelets (thrombocytes)**
- **Cell fragments** produced from **megakaryocytes** (special bone marrow cells).
- **1,50,000–3,50,000 per mm<sup>3</sup>**.
- Release substances involved in **coagulation**. Reduced number → **clotting disorders** and excessive blood loss.

> **Remember – WBC order (most to least):** "**N**ever **L**et **M**onkeys **E**at **B**ananas" – Neutrophils > Lymphocytes > Monocytes > Eosinophils > Basophils.

## 2. Blood Groups

### 2.1 ABO grouping
- Based on presence or absence of two **surface antigens A and B** on RBCs. Plasma has **natural antibodies**.
- **Antigens** – chemicals that induce an immune response. **Antibodies** – proteins produced in response to antigens.

| Blood group | Antigens on RBCs | Antibodies in plasma | Can receive from (donor's group) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | A | anti-B | A, O |
| **B** | B | anti-A | B, O |
| **AB** | A, B | **nil** | **AB, A, B, O** |
| **O** | **nil** | **anti-A, anti-B** | **O** |

- **O = universal donor**; **AB = universal recipient**.
- Mismatched transfusion causes **clumping (destruction of RBCs)**.

### 2.2 Rh grouping
- **Rh antigen** – similar to one in **Rhesus monkeys**; present on RBCs of nearly **80% of humans** → **Rh positive (Rh+ve)**; absent → **Rh negative (Rh−ve)**.
- An Rh−ve person exposed to Rh+ve blood forms **anti-Rh antibodies** → Rh must also be matched before transfusion.

**Erythroblastosis foetalis (Rh incompatibility):**
- **Rh−ve mother** with **Rh+ve foetus**.
- **First pregnancy:** foetal Rh antigens do not reach mother's blood (bloods separated by **placenta**). But during **delivery of the first child**, small amounts of foetal Rh+ve blood may enter maternal blood → mother starts making **anti-Rh antibodies**.
- **Subsequent pregnancies:** maternal anti-Rh antibodies leak into foetal blood and **destroy foetal RBCs** → fatal, or severe **anaemia and jaundice** in the baby.
- **Prevention:** administer **anti-Rh antibodies to the mother immediately after the delivery of the first child**.

## 3. Coagulation of Blood

- Mechanism to **prevent excessive blood loss** after injury.
- **Clot (coagulum)** – network of threads called **fibrins** in which dead and damaged formed elements are trapped.
- **Cascade:**
  1. Injury → **platelets release factors**; tissues at the site also release factors → activate coagulation.
  2. Series of linked enzymic reactions (**cascade process**) involving inactive plasma factors → forms **thrombokinase** (enzyme complex).
  3. Thrombokinase converts **prothrombin → thrombin** (active).
  4. Thrombin converts **fibrinogen → fibrin** (network of threads).
- **Calcium ions play a very important role** in clotting.

## 4. Lymph (Tissue Fluid)

- As blood passes through capillaries, water and small water-soluble substances move out into spaces between cells, leaving larger proteins and most formed elements behind → **interstitial fluid / tissue fluid**.
- Has the **same mineral distribution as plasma**.
- Exchange of nutrients and gases between blood and cells always occurs **through this fluid**.
- **Lymphatic system** – elaborate network of vessels that collects this fluid and drains it back to **major veins**. Fluid in it = **lymph**.
- **Lymph** – **colourless**; contains specialised **lymphocytes** (immune responses).
- Carrier of **nutrients, hormones**, etc.
- **Fats are absorbed through lymph in the lacteals** of intestinal villi.

| Feature | Blood | Lymph |
|---|---|---|
| Colour | Red | Colourless |
| RBCs | Present | Absent |
| Proteins | More (large proteins) | Fewer |
| Flow | Through blood vessels | Through lymph vessels, drains into veins |

## 5. Circulatory Pathways

| Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Open** | Blood pumped by heart passes through large vessels into open spaces/body cavities called **sinuses** | **Arthropods, molluscs** |
| **Closed** | Blood always circulates through a closed network of vessels; **more advantageous** (flow precisely regulated) | **Annelids, chordates** |

### 5.1 Heart in vertebrates

| Animal | Chambers | Circulation |
|---|---|---|
| **Fishes** | **2** (1 atrium, 1 ventricle); pumps **deoxygenated** blood to gills | **Single circulation** |
| **Amphibians and reptiles (except crocodiles)** | **3** (2 atria, 1 ventricle); oxygenated (left atrium) and deoxygenated (right atrium) blood **mix in the single ventricle** | **Incomplete double circulation** |
| **Crocodiles, birds, mammals** | **4** (2 atria, 2 ventricles); no mixing | **Double circulation** |

## 6. Human Heart

### 6.1 Structure
- **Mesodermally derived**; in the **thoracic cavity** between the two lungs, **slightly tilted to the left**; size of a **clenched fist**.
- Protected by a **double-walled pericardium** enclosing **pericardial fluid**.
- **Four chambers:** two small upper **atria**, two larger lower **ventricles**.

| Septum | Separates | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| **Inter-atrial septum** | Right and left atria | **Thin, muscular** |
| **Inter-ventricular septum** | Left and right ventricles | **Thick-walled** |
| **Atrio-ventricular septum** | Atrium and ventricle of the same side | **Thick fibrous tissue** (with an opening) |

| Valve | Location |
|---|---|
| **Tricuspid valve** (three muscular flaps/cusps) | Between **right atrium and right ventricle** |
| **Bicuspid / mitral valve** | Between **left atrium and left ventricle** |
| **Semilunar valves** | Openings of right ventricle into **pulmonary artery** and left ventricle into **aorta** |

- Valves allow blood flow in **one direction only** (atria → ventricles → pulmonary artery/aorta); prevent backflow.
- Entire heart is made of **cardiac muscle**; **ventricle walls much thicker** than atria.

### 6.2 Nodal tissue (conducting system)

| Component | Location |
|---|---|
| **Sino-atrial node (SAN)** | **Right upper corner of the right atrium** |
| **Atrio-ventricular node (AVN)** | **Lower left corner of the right atrium**, close to the atrio-ventricular septum |
| **AV bundle (bundle of His)** | From AVN, passes through atrio-ventricular septa, emerges on top of the inter-ventricular septum and divides into **right and left bundles** |
| **Purkinje fibres** | Minute fibres from the bundle branches, throughout the **ventricular musculature** |

- Nodal musculature is **autoexcitable** – generates action potentials without external stimuli.
- **SAN generates the maximum number of action potentials (70–75 per minute)** → initiates and maintains rhythmic contraction → **pacemaker**.
- Heart beats **70–75 times per minute** (average **72**).

## 7. Cardiac Cycle

1. **Joint diastole** – all four chambers relaxed. Tricuspid and bicuspid valves **open**; blood flows from **pulmonary veins and vena cava** through atria into ventricles. **Semilunar valves closed**.
2. **Atrial systole** – SAN generates an action potential; both atria contract simultaneously → **increases blood flow into ventricles by about 30%**.
3. Action potential conducted by **AVN and AV bundle**; **bundle of His** transmits it through the entire ventricular musculature.
4. **Ventricular systole** – ventricles contract; atria relax (atrial diastole coincides). Ventricular pressure rises → **tricuspid and bicuspid valves close** (due to attempted backflow).
5. Pressure rises further → **semilunar valves open** → blood flows into pulmonary artery (right) and aorta (left).
6. **Ventricular diastole** – ventricles relax; pressure falls → **semilunar valves close** (prevent backflow).
7. Pressure declines further → tricuspid and bicuspid valves **pushed open** by blood in atria → blood again moves into ventricles → joint diastole.

### 7.1 Numerical values

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Heart rate | **72 beats per minute** |
| Duration of one cardiac cycle | **0.8 seconds** |
| **Stroke volume** (blood pumped by each ventricle per cycle) | About **70 mL** |
| **Cardiac output** = stroke volume × heart rate | About **5000 mL (5 L) per minute** |

- Body can alter stroke volume and heart rate → cardiac output. **Athletes** have much higher cardiac output.

### 7.2 Heart sounds

| Sound | Associated with |
|---|---|
| **First heart sound – "lub"** | **Closure of tricuspid and bicuspid (AV) valves** |
| **Second heart sound – "dub"** | **Closure of semilunar valves** |

- Heard through a **stethoscope**; of clinical diagnostic significance.

## 8. Electrocardiogram (ECG)

- **ECG** – graphical representation of the **electrical activity** of the heart during a cardiac cycle; obtained by an **electrocardiograph**.
- Standard ECG: **three electrical leads** – one to **each wrist** and to the **left ankle**. For detailed evaluation, multiple leads on the chest.

| Wave | Represents |
|---|---|
| **P-wave** | **Depolarisation of the atria** → atrial contraction |
| **QRS complex** | **Depolarisation of the ventricles** → initiates ventricular contraction; contraction starts shortly after Q = **beginning of systole** |
| **T-wave** | **Repolarisation of the ventricles** (return from excited to normal state); **end of T-wave = end of systole** |

- **Counting QRS complexes** in a given time gives the **heart rate**.
- Deviation from normal shape indicates abnormality – great clinical significance.

## 9. Double Circulation

### 9.1 Blood vessels
- Arteries and veins have **three layers**:
  1. **Tunica intima** – inner lining of **squamous endothelium**
  2. **Tunica media** – middle layer of **smooth muscle and elastic fibres** (**thinner in veins**)
  3. **Tunica externa** – outer layer of **fibrous connective tissue with collagen fibres**

### 9.2 Pathways

| Circulation | Route |
|---|---|
| **Pulmonary circulation** | **Right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs** (oxygenated) **→ pulmonary veins → left atrium** |
| **Systemic circulation** | **Left ventricle → aorta → arteries → arterioles → capillaries → tissues** → venules → veins → **vena cava → right atrium** |

- Systemic circulation supplies nutrients, O<sub>2</sub> to tissues and removes CO<sub>2</sub> and harmful substances.
- **Hepatic portal system** – **hepatic portal vein** carries blood from **intestine to liver** before it enters systemic circulation.
- **Coronary system** – special blood vessels exclusively for circulation to and from the **cardiac musculature**.

> **Common Mistake:** The pulmonary artery carries **deoxygenated** blood; pulmonary veins carry **oxygenated** blood. Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry blood to the heart – not defined by oxygen content.

## 10. Regulation of Cardiac Activity

- Normal heart activity is **auto-regulated** by **nodal tissue** → heart is **myogenic**.
- A special neural centre in the **medulla oblongata** moderates cardiac function through the **autonomic nervous system (ANS)**.

| Signal | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Sympathetic nerves** | **Increase** heart rate, strength of ventricular contraction and **cardiac output** |
| **Parasympathetic nerves** | **Decrease** heart rate, speed of conduction of action potential and cardiac output |
| **Adrenal medullary hormones** | **Increase** cardiac output |

## 11. Disorders of the Circulatory System

| Disorder | Description |
|---|---|
| **Hypertension (high blood pressure)** | BP higher than normal **120/80 mm Hg** (120 = **systolic/pumping**; 80 = **diastolic/resting**). Repeated readings of **140/90 or higher** indicate hypertension. Leads to heart diseases; affects **brain and kidney** |
| **Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) / atherosclerosis** | Affects vessels supplying the **heart muscle**; caused by deposits of **calcium, fat, cholesterol and fibrous tissue** → narrower lumen |
| **Angina (angina pectoris)** | **Acute chest pain** when **not enough oxygen reaches the heart muscle**; more common in middle-aged and elderly; due to conditions affecting blood flow |
| **Heart failure** | Heart **not pumping blood effectively** enough for body's needs. Also called **congestive heart failure** (lung congestion is a main symptom). **Not the same as cardiac arrest** (heart stops beating) or **heart attack** (heart muscle suddenly damaged by inadequate blood supply) |

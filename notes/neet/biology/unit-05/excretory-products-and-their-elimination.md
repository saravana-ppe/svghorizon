## 1. Modes of Excretion

- Animals accumulate **ammonia, urea, uric acid, CO<sub>2</sub>, water and ions** (Na<sup>+</sup>, K<sup>+</sup>, Cl<sup>−</sup>, phosphate, sulphate).
- Major nitrogenous wastes: **ammonia, urea and uric acid**.

| Mode | Waste | Toxicity / water need | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ammonotelism** | Ammonia | **Most toxic**; needs **large amount of water** | **Many bony fishes, aquatic amphibians, aquatic insects** |
| **Ureotelism** | Urea | Less toxic | **Mammals, many terrestrial amphibians, marine fishes** |
| **Uricotelism** | Uric acid (pellet or paste) | **Least toxic**; **minimum water loss** | **Reptiles, birds, land snails, insects** |

- **Ammonia** is readily soluble; excreted by **diffusion across body surfaces or gill surfaces** (in fish) as **ammonium ions**. **Kidneys play no significant role** in its removal.
- **Terrestrial adaptation** → production of less toxic urea and uric acid to **conserve water**.
- In ureotelic animals, ammonia is converted into **urea in the liver**, released into blood, filtered and excreted by kidneys. Some urea may be retained in the **kidney matrix** to maintain desired **osmolarity**.

### 1.1 Excretory structures in animals

| Structure | Animals | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Protonephridia / flame cells** | **Platyhelminthes (e.g., *Planaria*)**, rotifers, some annelids, and the cephalochordate ***Amphioxus*** | Mainly **osmoregulation** (ionic and fluid volume regulation) |
| **Nephridia** | **Earthworms** and other annelids | Remove nitrogenous wastes; fluid and ionic balance |
| **Malpighian tubules** | Most **insects including cockroaches** | Remove nitrogenous wastes; osmoregulation |
| **Antennal glands / green glands** | **Crustaceans like prawns** | Excretion |
| **Kidneys** | Vertebrates | Complex tubular organs |

> **Exam Tip:** "A chordate with flame cells" = ***Amphioxus*** (cephalochordate). This is a direct NCERT exercise question.

## 2. Human Excretory System

- **A pair of kidneys, one pair of ureters, a urinary bladder and a urethra**.

### 2.1 Kidney – external features
- **Reddish brown, bean-shaped**, between the levels of the **last thoracic and third lumbar vertebra**, close to the dorsal inner wall of the abdominal cavity.
- Adult kidney: **10–12 cm long, 5–7 cm wide, 2–3 cm thick**, average weight **120–170 g**.
- **Hilum** – notch on the inner concave surface through which **ureter, blood vessels and nerves** enter.
- **Renal pelvis** – broad funnel-shaped space inner to the hilum, with projections called **calyces**.
- Outer layer: tough **capsule**.

### 2.2 Kidney – internal structure
- Two zones: outer **cortex**, inner **medulla**.
- Medulla divided into conical **medullary pyramids** projecting into the **calyces**.
- **Columns of Bertini** – cortex extending between medullary pyramids as **renal columns**.

### 2.3 Nephron – the functional unit
- Each kidney has **nearly one million nephrons**.
- Two parts: **glomerulus** and **renal tubule**.

| Part | Description |
|---|---|
| **Glomerulus** | **Tuft of capillaries** formed by the **afferent arteriole** (fine branch of renal artery); blood leaves by the **efferent arteriole** |
| **Bowman's capsule** | **Double-walled cup** enclosing the glomerulus |
| **Malpighian body / renal corpuscle** | **Glomerulus + Bowman's capsule** |
| **Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)** | Highly coiled network after Bowman's capsule |
| **Henle's loop** | **Hairpin-shaped**; descending and ascending limbs |
| **Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)** | Highly coiled; continues from ascending limb |
| **Collecting duct** | Straight tube into which DCTs of many nephrons open; many converge and open into the **renal pelvis** through medullary pyramids in the calyces |

- **Malpighian corpuscle, PCT and DCT** are in the **cortex**; **loop of Henle dips into the medulla**.

| Type of nephron | Loop of Henle | Vasa recta |
|---|---|---|
| **Cortical nephrons** (majority) | **Short**; extends very little into medulla | **Absent or highly reduced** |
| **Juxta medullary nephrons** | **Very long**; runs deep into medulla | Present |

- **Peritubular capillaries** – fine capillary network formed by the efferent arteriole around the renal tubule.
- **Vasa recta** – minute vessel of this network running **parallel to Henle's loop**, forming a **U shape**.

## 3. Urine Formation

Three main processes: **glomerular filtration, reabsorption and secretion**.

### 3.1 Glomerular filtration
- About **1100–1200 mL of blood** filtered by the kidneys **per minute** = roughly **1/5th of the blood pumped by each ventricle** per minute.
- Driven by **glomerular capillary blood pressure**.
- Filtration through **3 layers**:
  1. **Endothelium** of glomerular blood vessels
  2. **Epithelium of Bowman's capsule**
  3. **Basement membrane** between them
- Epithelial cells of Bowman's capsule = **podocytes**, arranged to leave minute spaces = **filtration slits or slit pores**.
- Almost all plasma constituents **except proteins** pass into Bowman's capsule → **ultrafiltration**.
- **Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR)** – amount of filtrate formed by kidneys per minute ≈ **125 mL/minute = 180 litres per day**.

### 3.2 Juxta Glomerular Apparatus (JGA)
- Special sensitive region formed by cellular modifications in the **DCT and afferent arteriole** at their point of contact.
- A **fall in GFR** activates **JG cells to release renin** → stimulates glomerular blood flow → GFR back to normal (**autoregulation**).

### 3.3 Reabsorption
- Filtrate formed per day (**180 L**) vs urine released (**1.5 L**) → nearly **99% of filtrate is reabsorbed**.
- **Active reabsorption:** **glucose, amino acids, Na<sup>+</sup>**.
- **Passive reabsorption:** **nitrogenous wastes**; **water** (passive in initial segments).

### 3.4 Tubular secretion
- Tubular cells secrete **H<sup>+</sup>, K<sup>+</sup> and ammonia** into the filtrate.
- Helps maintain **ionic and acid–base balance** of body fluids.

## 4. Functions of the Tubules

| Segment | Function |
|---|---|
| **PCT** | Lined by **simple cuboidal brush border epithelium** (increases surface area). **Nearly all essential nutrients, 70–80% of electrolytes and water** reabsorbed. Maintains pH and ionic balance by **secreting H<sup>+</sup> and ammonia** and **absorbing HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>−</sup>** |
| **Descending limb of Henle's loop** | **Permeable to water**, almost **impermeable to electrolytes** → **concentrates** the filtrate as it moves down |
| **Ascending limb of Henle's loop** | **Impermeable to water**; allows transport of **electrolytes** actively or passively → filtrate gets **diluted** as it moves up. **Reabsorption is minimum**. Maintains **high osmolarity of medullary interstitial fluid** |
| **DCT** | **Conditional reabsorption of Na<sup>+</sup> and water**; reabsorbs HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>−</sup>; **selective secretion of H<sup>+</sup>, K<sup>+</sup> and NH<sub>3</sub>** → maintains pH and Na–K balance |
| **Collecting duct** | Extends from cortex to inner medulla. **Large amounts of water reabsorbed** → **concentrated urine**. Allows passage of **small amounts of urea into medullary interstitium** (keeps osmolarity). Secretes **H<sup>+</sup> and K<sup>+</sup>** (pH and ionic balance) |

> **PYQ Trend:** Descending limb – permeable to water only; ascending limb – impermeable to water, permeable to electrolytes. PCT – maximum reabsorption, brush border. These are the most frequently asked nephron facts.

## 5. Counter Current Mechanism (Concentration of Filtrate)

- Mammals can produce **concentrated urine**. **Henle's loop and vasa recta** play a significant role.
- Filtrate flows in **opposite directions** in the two limbs of Henle's loop → **counter current**. Blood in the two limbs of **vasa recta** also flows in a counter current pattern.
- Proximity and counter current maintain an **increasing osmolarity** towards the inner medulla: **300 mOsmol L<sup>−1</sup> in cortex → about 1200 mOsmol L<sup>−1</sup> in inner medulla**.
- This gradient is mainly caused by **NaCl and urea**:
  - **NaCl** is transported by the **ascending limb of Henle's loop** → exchanged with the **descending limb of vasa recta** → returned to the interstitium by the **ascending portion of vasa recta**.
  - Small amounts of **urea** enter the **thin segment of the ascending limb** → transported back to the interstitium by the **collecting tubule**.
- The interstitial gradient allows easy passage of **water from the collecting tubule** → concentrates urine.
- Human kidneys can produce urine nearly **four times more concentrated** than the initial filtrate.

## 6. Regulation of Kidney Function

Hormonal feedback mechanisms involving the **hypothalamus, JGA and, to some extent, the heart**.

### 6.1 ADH (antidiuretic hormone / vasopressin)
- **Osmoreceptors** are activated by changes in blood volume, body fluid volume and ionic concentration.
- **Excessive loss of fluid** → osmoreceptors stimulate the **hypothalamus** to release **ADH** from the **neurohypophysis**.
- ADH facilitates **water reabsorption from later parts of the tubule** → **prevents diuresis**.
- **Increase in body fluid volume** → switches off osmoreceptors → suppresses ADH (feedback).
- ADH also **constricts blood vessels** → raises BP → increases glomerular blood flow and **GFR**.
- **Diabetes insipidus** – impaired synthesis or release of ADH → kidney cannot conserve water → **water loss and dehydration**.

### 6.2 Renin–angiotensin mechanism
1. **Fall in glomerular blood flow / BP / GFR** → **JG cells release renin**.
2. Renin converts **angiotensinogen** (in blood) → **angiotensin I** → **angiotensin II**.
3. **Angiotensin II** – powerful **vasoconstrictor** → increases glomerular BP and **GFR**.
4. Angiotensin II also activates the **adrenal cortex** to release **aldosterone**.
5. **Aldosterone** → reabsorption of **Na<sup>+</sup> and water** from the **distal parts of the tubule** → increases BP and GFR.

### 6.3 Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF)
- **Increased blood flow to the atria** → release of **ANF**.
- ANF causes **vasodilation** → **decreases blood pressure**.
- ANF acts as a **check on the renin–angiotensin mechanism**.

## 7. Micturition

- Urine is stored in the **urinary bladder** until a **voluntary signal from the CNS**.
- Stretching of the filled bladder → **stretch receptors** send signals to CNS → CNS sends motor messages → **contraction of smooth muscles of the bladder** and simultaneous **relaxation of the urethral sphincter** → urine released.
- **Micturition** = release of urine; the neural mechanism = **micturition reflex**.

| Urine characteristic | Value |
|---|---|
| Volume per day (adult) | **1 to 1.5 litres** |
| Colour | Light yellow, watery |
| pH | **Slightly acidic, about 6.0** |
| Urea excreted per day | **25–30 g** |

- **Glycosuria** (glucose in urine) and **ketonuria** (ketone bodies) indicate **diabetes mellitus**.

## 8. Role of Other Organs in Excretion

| Organ | Role |
|---|---|
| **Lungs** | Remove large amounts of **CO<sub>2</sub> (about 200 mL/minute)** and significant water |
| **Liver** (largest gland) | Secretes bile containing **bilirubin, biliverdin, cholesterol, degraded steroid hormones, vitamins and drugs** → pass out with digestive wastes |
| **Skin – sweat glands** | Sweat: watery fluid with **NaCl, small amounts of urea, lactic acid**; primary function is **cooling** |
| **Skin – sebaceous glands** | Eliminate **sterols, hydrocarbons and waxes** through **sebum**; protective oily covering |
| **Saliva** | Small amounts of nitrogenous wastes |

## 9. Disorders of the Excretory System

| Disorder / procedure | Description |
|---|---|
| **Uremia** | **Accumulation of urea in blood** due to kidney malfunction; highly harmful; may lead to **kidney failure** |
| **Haemodialysis (artificial kidney)** | Blood drained from a convenient **artery**, **anticoagulant heparin** added, pumped into a dialysing unit with a **coiled cellophane tube** surrounded by **dialysing fluid** (same composition as plasma **except nitrogenous wastes**). Nitrogenous wastes move out by concentration gradient. Cleared blood pumped back through a **vein** after adding **anti-heparin** |
| **Kidney transplantation** | **Ultimate method** for acute renal failure; donor preferably a **close relative** to minimise rejection |
| **Renal calculi** | **Stone** or insoluble mass of **crystallised salts (oxalates, etc.)** in the kidney |
| **Glomerulonephritis (nephritis)** | **Inflammation of glomeruli** of kidney |

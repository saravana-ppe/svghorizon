## 1. Neural System – Introduction

- **Coordination** – process through which two or more organs interact and complement each other's functions (e.g., during exercise, respiration, heart rate and blood flow increase).
- The **neural system** gives **point-to-point** connections for **quick** coordination; the **endocrine system** gives chemical integration through **hormones**.
- Neural system is made of **neurons** – cells that detect, receive and transmit stimuli.
- *Hydra* – **network of neurons**; insects – brain + ganglia; vertebrates – more developed.

## 2. Human Neural System

| Division | Components / function |
|---|---|
| **Central neural system (CNS)** | **Brain and spinal cord**; site of **information processing and control** |
| **Peripheral neural system (PNS)** | All nerves associated with the CNS |

**Nerve fibres of PNS:**
- **Afferent fibres** – transmit impulses **from tissues/organs to CNS**.
- **Efferent fibres** – transmit regulatory impulses **from CNS to tissues/organs**.

**Divisions of PNS:**
- **Somatic neural system** – relays impulses from CNS to **skeletal muscles**.
- **Autonomic neural system** – transmits impulses from CNS to **involuntary organs and smooth muscles**.
  - **Sympathetic** neural system
  - **Parasympathetic** neural system
- **Visceral nervous system** – part of the PNS comprising the whole complex of **nerves, fibres, ganglia and plexuses** by which impulses travel from the **CNS to the viscera and from the viscera to the CNS**.

## 3. Neuron – Structural and Functional Unit

### 3.1 Structure
- Three parts: **cell body, dendrites and axon**.
- **Cell body** – cytoplasm with typical organelles and granular bodies called **Nissl's granules**.
- **Dendrites** – short, repeatedly branched fibres; also contain Nissl's granules; transmit impulses **towards the cell body**.
- **Axon** – long fibre with branched distal end; each branch ends as a bulb-like **synaptic knob** containing **synaptic vesicles** with **neurotransmitters**. Axons transmit impulses **away from the cell body** to a synapse or neuromuscular junction.

### 3.2 Types of neurons

| Type | Structure | Found in |
|---|---|---|
| **Multipolar** | One axon, two or more dendrites | **Cerebral cortex** |
| **Bipolar** | One axon, one dendrite | **Retina of eye** |
| **Unipolar** | Cell body with one axon only | Usually **embryonic stage** |

### 3.3 Myelinated vs non-myelinated axons

| Feature | Myelinated | Non-myelinated |
|---|---|---|
| Sheath | **Schwann cells form myelin sheath** around axon | Enclosed by a Schwann cell that **does not form a myelin sheath** |
| **Nodes of Ranvier** | Present – **gaps between adjacent myelin sheaths** | Absent |
| Found in | **Spinal and cranial nerves** | **Autonomous and somatic neural systems** |

## 4. Generation and Conduction of Nerve Impulse

### 4.1 Resting potential (polarised state)
- Neurons are **excitable** because their membranes are **polarised**.
- Ion channels are **selectively permeable**.
- At rest, the axonal membrane is **more permeable to K<sup>+</sup>** and **nearly impermeable to Na<sup>+</sup>**; also impermeable to **negatively charged proteins** in axoplasm.
- **Inside axon:** high K<sup>+</sup>, negatively charged proteins, low Na<sup>+</sup>.
- **Outside axon:** low K<sup>+</sup>, high Na<sup>+</sup>.
- Gradients are maintained by the **sodium–potassium pump**: **3 Na<sup>+</sup> out for 2 K<sup>+</sup> in** (active transport).
- Result: **outer surface positive, inner surface negative** → **polarised**.
- Potential difference across resting membrane = **resting potential**.

### 4.2 Action potential (depolarisation)
1. Stimulus at site A → membrane becomes **freely permeable to Na<sup>+</sup>** → rapid **influx of Na<sup>+</sup>**.
2. Polarity **reversed**: outer surface negative, inner surface positive → **depolarised**.
3. Potential difference at site A = **action potential = nerve impulse**.
4. At site B (just ahead), outer surface still positive and inner negative. **Current flows on the inner surface from A to B**, and **on the outer surface from B to A** → polarity reversed at B → action potential at B.
5. Sequence repeats along the axon → **impulse conducted**.

### 4.3 Repolarisation
- Rise in Na<sup>+</sup> permeability is **extremely short-lived**; quickly followed by a **rise in K<sup>+</sup> permeability**.
- **K<sup>+</sup> diffuses outside** → restores resting potential → fibre becomes responsive again.

## 5. Transmission of Impulses – Synapse

- **Synapse** – formed by membranes of a **pre-synaptic neuron** and a **post-synaptic neuron**, which may or may not be separated by a **synaptic cleft**.

| Feature | Electrical synapse | Chemical synapse |
|---|---|---|
| Membranes | **Very close proximity** | Separated by fluid-filled **synaptic cleft** |
| Transmission | Current flows **directly** from one neuron to the other (like conduction along an axon) | By **neurotransmitters** |
| Speed | **Always faster** | Slower |
| Occurrence | **Rare** in our system | Common |

**Transmission at a chemical synapse:**
1. Impulse arrives at axon terminal.
2. **Synaptic vesicles** move to the membrane, **fuse** with plasma membrane and **release neurotransmitters** into the cleft.
3. Neurotransmitters **bind to specific receptors** on the post-synaptic membrane.
4. Binding **opens ion channels** → entry of ions → new potential in post-synaptic neuron.
5. New potential may be **excitatory or inhibitory**.

## 6. Central Neural System – Brain

- Brain = central information processing organ; "**command and control system**".
- Controls: voluntary movements, balance, vital involuntary organs (lungs, heart, kidneys), **thermoregulation, hunger and thirst, circadian (24-hour) rhythms**, endocrine glands, behaviour; processes vision, hearing, speech, memory, intelligence, emotions, thoughts.
- Protected by the **skull**; covered by **cranial meninges**:
  - **Dura mater** – outer layer
  - **Arachnoid** – very thin middle layer
  - **Pia mater** – inner layer (in contact with brain tissue)
- Three major parts: **forebrain, midbrain, hindbrain**.

### 6.1 Forebrain

| Part | Features and functions |
|---|---|
| **Cerebrum** | **Major part** of the human brain. A deep cleft divides it into **left and right cerebral hemispheres**, connected by a tract of nerve fibres called **corpus callosum** |
| **Cerebral cortex** | Layer of cells covering the hemispheres; thrown into prominent folds; **grey matter** (neuron cell bodies concentrated). Contains **motor areas, sensory areas** and **association areas** (neither clearly sensory nor motor) responsible for **intersensory associations, memory and communication** |
| **White matter** | Inner part of cerebral hemisphere; **myelinated fibre tracts** give opaque white appearance |
| **Thalamus** | Cerebrum wraps around it; **major coordinating centre for sensory and motor signalling** |
| **Hypothalamus** | Lies at the **base of the thalamus**. Centres controlling **body temperature, urge for eating and drinking**. Contains **neurosecretory cells** secreting **hypothalamic hormones** |
| **Limbic system (limbic lobe)** | Inner parts of cerebral hemispheres + deep structures like **amygdala, hippocampus**. With hypothalamus, regulates **sexual behaviour, emotional reactions (excitement, pleasure, rage, fear) and motivation**; also olfaction and autonomic responses |

### 6.2 Midbrain
- Located between the **thalamus/hypothalamus** of forebrain and **pons** of hindbrain.
- **Cerebral aqueduct** – canal passing through the midbrain.
- Dorsal portion has four round swellings (lobes) = **corpora quadrigemina**.
- Receives and integrates **visual, tactile and auditory inputs**.

### 6.3 Hindbrain

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **Pons** | **Fibre tracts** that **interconnect different regions** of the brain (also contains pneumotaxic centre – see Breathing and Exchange of Gases) |
| **Cerebellum** | **Very convoluted surface** to provide space for many more neurons; integrates information from **semicircular canals** of ear and auditory system (balance) |
| **Medulla oblongata** | Connected to the **spinal cord**; centres controlling **respiration, cardiovascular reflexes and gastric secretions** |

- **Brain stem** = **midbrain + pons + medulla oblongata**; connects brain and spinal cord.

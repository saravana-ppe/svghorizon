**NEET UG 2026 Biology – Notes from NCERT**

**NCERT chapters covered:** Class XI – Chapter 8 (Cell: The Unit of Life), Chapter 9 (Biomolecules), Chapter 10 (Cell Cycle and Cell Division)

**NEET syllabus topics in this unit:**

1. Cell theory and cell as the basic unit of life; structure of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells; plant cell and animal cell; cell envelope, cell membrane, cell wall; cell organelles – structure and function: endomembrane system (ER, Golgi bodies, lysosomes, vacuoles), mitochondria, ribosomes, plastids, microbodies; cytoskeleton, cilia, flagella, centrioles (ultrastructure and function); nucleus – nuclear membrane, chromatin, nucleolus
2. Chemical constituents of living cells: biomolecules – structure and function of proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, nucleic acids; enzymes – types, properties, enzyme action, classification and nomenclature
3. Cell division: cell cycle, mitosis, meiosis and their significance

> **NEET Focus: How this unit is tested**
> - One of the highest-weightage units of Class XI. Questions are conceptual and statement-based ("Which of the following statements is correct?").
> - Cell: ribosome sizes (70S/80S and subunits), 9+2 and 9+0 (triplet) arrangement, double-membraned organelles, endomembrane system members, fluid mosaic model, mesosomes, plasmids, centromere positions.
> - Biomolecules: acid-soluble vs acid-insoluble pool, most abundant protein, homopolymer vs heteropolymer, starch–iodine, enzyme classes with examples, cofactors (prosthetic group, coenzyme, metal ion), competitive inhibition (malonate).
> - Cell cycle: DNA content (C) vs chromosome number (n) at each phase, sub-stages of prophase I and their events, G<sub>0</sub>, mitosis vs meiosis.

## Quick Revision – High-Yield One-liners

- Cell theory: Schleiden (1838) + Schwann (1839); *Omnis cellula-e cellula* – Virchow (1855).
- Nucleus discovered – Robert Brown (1831); chromatin named – Flemming.
- Smallest cell – Mycoplasma (0.3 µm). Largest single cell – ostrich egg.
- PPLO = Pleuro Pneumonia Like Organisms.
- Plasmids – antibiotic resistance.
- Mesosome – infoldings of plasma membrane; wall formation, DNA replication, respiration, secretion.
- Chromatophores – cyanobacteria.
- Bacterial flagellum – filament, hook, basal body.
- Pili and fimbriae – not for motility.
- Inclusion bodies – phosphate, cyanophycean, glycogen granules; not membrane-bound.
- Gas vacuoles – blue-green, purple and green photosynthetic bacteria.
- Fluid mosaic model – Singer and Nicolson (1972). RBC membrane – 52% protein, 40% lipid.
- Na<sup>+</sup>/K<sup>+</sup> pump – active transport.
- Middle lamella – calcium pectate.
- Endomembrane system – ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vacuoles.
- SER – lipid and steroid hormone synthesis.
- Golgi – Camillo Golgi (1898); glycoproteins and glycolipids.
- Tonoplast – vacuole membrane; vacuole up to 90% of plant cell volume.
- Cristae – inner mitochondrial membrane; power house.
- Amyloplast – starch; elaioplast – oils; aleuroplast – proteins.
- Ribosomes – George Palade (1953); 80S (60S + 40S), 70S (50S + 30S).
- Cilia/flagella – 9+2; centriole – 9 triplets, cartwheel, hub.
- Perinuclear space – 10–50 nm.
- Nucleolus – rRNA synthesis.
- Human DNA – about 2 m in 46 chromosomes.
- Collagen – most abundant animal protein. RuBisCO – most abundant protein in biosphere.
- Haemoglobin – 2α + 2β.
- Inulin – fructose polymer. Starch–iodine blue.
- Carbonic anhydrase – accelerates rate 10 million times.
- Malonate – competitive inhibitor of succinic dehydrogenase.
- Haem – prosthetic group of peroxidase and catalase. Zinc – carboxypeptidase. NAD/NADP – niacin.
- Interphase > 95% of cell cycle. G<sub>0</sub> – quiescent (heart cells).
- Centriole duplicates in S phase.
- Syncytium – coconut liquid endosperm.
- Diplotene may last months or years in vertebrate oocytes.

## Practice Questions (NEET pattern)

1. Which of the following is NOT a function of mesosomes?
   - (a) Cell wall formation
   - (b) DNA replication
   - (c) Protein synthesis
   - (d) Respiration
2. Which organelle is NOT a part of the endomembrane system?
   - (a) Golgi apparatus
   - (b) Lysosome
   - (c) Vacuole
   - (d) Mitochondrion
3. The 70S ribosome is made of subunits:
   - (a) 60S + 40S
   - (b) 40S + 30S
   - (c) 50S + 30S
   - (d) 50S + 20S
4. The arrangement of microtubules in a centriole is:
   - (a) 9 triplets with a central hub
   - (b) 9+2 doublets
   - (c) 9 doublets without central tubules
   - (d) 9+1 triplets
5. Chromosomes with centromere close to one end, forming one very short and one very long arm, are:
   - (a) Metacentric
   - (b) Acrocentric
   - (c) Sub-metacentric
   - (d) Telocentric
6. The most abundant protein in the whole biosphere is:
   - (a) Collagen
   - (b) Haemoglobin
   - (c) Insulin
   - (d) RuBisCO
7. Which pair is correctly matched?
   - (a) Lyases – hydrolysis of ester bonds
   - (b) Isomerases – transfer of groups
   - (c) Ligases – linking two compounds
   - (d) Transferases – removal of groups leaving double bonds
8. Haem in catalase acts as a:
   - (a) Coenzyme
   - (b) Prosthetic group
   - (c) Metal ion activator
   - (d) Competitive inhibitor
9. Malonate inhibits succinic dehydrogenase because it:
   - (a) Resembles the substrate and competes for the active site
   - (b) Denatures the enzyme
   - (c) Binds to the coenzyme
   - (d) Removes the metal ion
10. If a cell has 2C DNA content at G<sub>1</sub>, its DNA content at G<sub>2</sub> will be:
    - (a) 1C
    - (b) 2C
    - (c) 8C
    - (d) 4C
11. Crossing over takes place during:
    - (a) Leptotene
    - (b) Pachytene
    - (c) Zygotene
    - (d) Diakinesis
12. In anaphase I of meiosis:
    - (a) Sister chromatids separate
    - (b) Centromeres split
    - (c) Homologous chromosomes separate
    - (d) Chiasmata form

**Answers:** 1–c, 2–d, 3–c, 4–a, 5–b, 6–d, 7–c, 8–b, 9–a, 10–d, 11–b, 12–c

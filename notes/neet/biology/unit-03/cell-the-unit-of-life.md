## 1. What is a Cell?

- All organisms are composed of cells. **Unicellular** – single cell; **multicellular** – many cells.
- Unicellular organisms are capable of **(i) independent existence** and **(ii) performing the essential functions of life**.
- Anything less than a complete cell does not ensure independent living. Hence, **cell is the fundamental structural and functional unit of all living organisms**.
- **Antonie Von Leeuwenhoek** – first saw and described a **live cell**.
- **Robert Brown** – discovered the **nucleus** (1831).
- The invention and improvement of the microscope, leading to the **electron microscope**, revealed all structural details of the cell.

## 2. Cell Theory

| Scientist | Year | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Matthias Schleiden** (German botanist) | 1838 | Observed that **all plants are composed of different kinds of cells** which form the tissues of the plant |
| **Theodore Schwann** (German zoologist) | 1839 | Studied animal cells; reported a thin outer layer – today's **plasma membrane**. Concluded that the **cell wall is a unique character of plant cells**. Proposed that bodies of animals and plants are composed of **cells and products of cells** |
| **Schleiden and Schwann** | – | Together formulated the **cell theory** – but it did **not explain how new cells are formed** |
| **Rudolf Virchow** | 1855 | Explained that cells divide and new cells are formed from **pre-existing cells** – ***Omnis cellula-e cellula***. Gave the cell theory its final shape |

**Cell theory as understood today:**
1. All living organisms are composed of **cells and products of cells**.
2. All cells arise from **pre-existing cells**.

> **Common Mistake:** Robert Brown discovered the **nucleus**, not the cell. (Robert Hooke first used the word "cell".) NCERT Exercise Q1 tests exactly this.

## 3. An Overview of the Cell

- **Onion cell** (typical plant cell) – distinct **cell wall** as outer boundary; cell membrane just inside it.
- **Human cheek cell** – outer **membrane** is the delimiting structure.
- **Nucleus** – dense membrane-bound structure containing **chromosomes**, which contain **DNA**.
- **Eukaryotic cells** – have **membrane-bound nuclei**. **Prokaryotic cells** – lack a membrane-bound nucleus.
- **Cytoplasm** – semi-fluid matrix occupying the cell volume in both; the **main arena of cellular activities**.
- Eukaryotic cells have membrane-bound **organelles**: ER, Golgi complex, lysosomes, mitochondria, microbodies, vacuoles. **Prokaryotes lack membrane-bound organelles**.
- **Ribosomes** are **non-membrane-bound** organelles found in **all cells**. Within the cell, they are found in the cytoplasm, **within chloroplasts and mitochondria**, and **on rough ER**.
- **Centrosome** – non-membrane-bound organelle in **animal cells**; helps in **cell division**.

### 3.1 Cell size and shape

| Cell | Size |
|---|---|
| **Mycoplasma** – smallest cells | About **0.3 µm** in length |
| Bacteria | **3 to 5 µm** |
| Human RBC | About **7.0 µm** in diameter |
| Largest isolated single cell | **Egg of an ostrich** |
| Some of the longest cells | **Nerve cells** |
| Typical eukaryotic cell | **10–20 µm** |
| PPLO | About **0.1 µm** |
| Viruses | **0.02–0.2 µm** |

- Shapes vary with function: disc-like, polygonal, columnar, cuboid, thread-like or irregular. Examples in NCERT figure: **columnar epithelial cells** (long and narrow), **WBC** (amoeboid), **RBC** (round and biconcave), **nerve cell** (branched and long), **tracheid** (elongated), **mesophyll cells** (round and oval).

> **Note:** The NCERT text prints "0.3 mm" for Mycoplasma; the correct unit is **0.3 µm** (micrometre). NEET uses the correct value.

## 4. Prokaryotic Cells

### 4.1 General features
- Represented by **bacteria, blue-green algae, mycoplasma and PPLO** (Pleuro Pneumonia Like Organisms).
- Generally **smaller** and **multiply more rapidly** than eukaryotic cells.
- Four basic shapes of bacteria: **bacillus** (rod), **coccus** (spherical), **vibrio** (comma), **spirillum** (spiral).
- All have a **cell wall** surrounding the cell membrane **except mycoplasma**.
- **No well-defined nucleus**; genetic material is **naked** (not enveloped by a nuclear membrane).
- **Genomic DNA** – single chromosome, **circular DNA**.
- **Plasmids** – small circular DNA **outside the genomic DNA**. Confer unique phenotypic characters such as **resistance to antibiotics**. Used to **monitor bacterial transformation** with foreign DNA.
- No organelles like those of eukaryotes, **except ribosomes**.
- Unique **inclusions**.
- **Mesosome** – a specialised differentiated form of cell membrane, **characteristic of prokaryotes**; essentially **infoldings of the cell membrane**.

### 4.2 Cell envelope and its modifications
- Most prokaryotic (bacterial) cells have a chemically complex **cell envelope** – a tightly bound **three-layered** structure:
  1. **Glycocalyx** (outermost)
  2. **Cell wall**
  3. **Plasma membrane**
- Each layer has a distinct function, but they act together as a **single protective unit**.
- Based on differences in cell envelope and response to the staining procedure developed by **Gram**:
  - **Gram positive** – take up the Gram stain
  - **Gram negative** – do not take up the stain
- **Glycocalyx** differs in composition and thickness:
  - **Slime layer** – loose sheath (in some)
  - **Capsule** – thick and tough (in others)
- **Cell wall** – determines shape; strong structural support to prevent the bacterium from **bursting or collapsing**.
- **Plasma membrane** – **selectively permeable**; interacts with the outside world; structurally similar to that of eukaryotes.

### 4.3 Mesosome – functions
- Formed by extensions of the plasma membrane into the cell, as **vesicles, tubules and lamellae**.
- Functions:
  - **Cell wall formation**
  - **DNA replication and distribution to daughter cells**
  - **Respiration**
  - **Secretion processes**
  - Increase **surface area** of plasma membrane and **enzymatic content**
- **Chromatophores** – membranous extensions into the cytoplasm in some prokaryotes like **cyanobacteria**; contain **pigments**.

### 4.4 Surface structures
- **Flagella** – thin filamentous extensions from the cell wall (in motile bacteria). Bacteria vary in the number and arrangement of flagella.
  - Bacterial flagellum has **three parts: filament, hook and basal body**. **Filament** is the **longest** portion, extending from cell surface to outside.
- **Pili** – elongated **tubular** structures made of a **special protein**. **Not involved in motility**.
- **Fimbriae** – small **bristle-like fibres** sprouting out of the cell. Help attach bacteria to **rocks in streams** and to **host tissues**. **Not involved in motility**.

### 4.5 Ribosomes and inclusion bodies
- In prokaryotes, ribosomes are **associated with the plasma membrane**.
- Size about **15 nm by 20 nm**.
- Two subunits – **50S and 30S** – together form the **70S** prokaryotic ribosome.
- Ribosomes are the **site of protein synthesis**.
- **Polyribosome (polysome)** – several ribosomes attached to a **single mRNA**; they translate the mRNA into proteins.
- **Inclusion bodies** – reserve material stored in the cytoplasm; **not bound by any membrane**; lie free in cytoplasm. Examples: **phosphate granules, cyanophycean granules, glycogen granules**.
- **Gas vacuoles** – found in **blue-green, purple and green photosynthetic bacteria**.

> **PYQ Trend:** Mesosome functions, plasmids (antibiotic resistance), the three-layered cell envelope, and "pili and fimbriae are not involved in motility" are repeatedly asked. Also: 70S = 50S + 30S (Svedberg units are **not additive**).

## 5. Eukaryotic Cells

- Include **all protists, plants, animals and fungi**.
- **Extensive compartmentalisation** of cytoplasm through membrane-bound organelles.
- Organised **nucleus with a nuclear envelope**.
- Variety of complex **locomotory and cytoskeletal structures**.
- Genetic material organised into **chromosomes**.

### 5.1 Plant cell vs animal cell

| Feature | Plant cell | Animal cell |
|---|---|---|
| Cell wall | Present | Absent |
| Plastids | Present | Absent |
| Vacuole | **Large central vacuole** | Small or absent |
| Centrioles | **Absent in almost all plant cells** | Present |
| Other structures shown in NCERT figure | Plasmodesmata, middle lamella | Microvilli |

### 5.2 Prokaryotic vs eukaryotic cells

| Feature | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | No well-defined nucleus; naked DNA (nucleoid) | Organised nucleus with nuclear envelope |
| DNA | Single circular chromosome; plasmids often present | Linear DNA in multiple chromosomes, with histones |
| Membrane-bound organelles | Absent | Present |
| Ribosomes | 70S (50S + 30S) | 80S (60S + 40S) in cytoplasm; 70S in mitochondria and chloroplasts |
| Mesosome | Present | Absent |
| Size | Generally smaller | Larger |
| Examples | Bacteria, cyanobacteria, mycoplasma, PPLO | Protists, fungi, plants, animals |

## 6. Cell Membrane

### 6.1 Composition
- Detailed structure studied after the **electron microscope** (1950s). Chemical studies on human **RBC membranes** helped deduce the structure.
- Mainly **lipids and proteins**.
- Major lipids are **phospholipids** arranged in a **bilayer** – **polar heads outwards**, **hydrophobic tails inwards**. This protects the non-polar tails of saturated hydrocarbons from the aqueous environment.
- Also contains **cholesterol**, **proteins** and **carbohydrates**.
- Ratio of protein to lipid varies in different cells. **Human RBC membrane: about 52% protein and 40% lipids.**
- Membrane proteins (by ease of extraction):
  - **Peripheral proteins** – lie on the **surface**
  - **Integral proteins** – **partially or totally buried** in the membrane

### 6.2 Fluid mosaic model
- Proposed by **Singer and Nicolson (1972)**; widely accepted.
- The **quasi-fluid nature of lipid** enables **lateral movement of proteins** within the bilayer. This ability to move is measured as **fluidity**.
- Fluidity is important for **cell growth, formation of intercellular junctions, secretion, endocytosis, cell division**.

### 6.3 Transport across the membrane
- Membrane is **selectively permeable**.

| Type | Energy | Direction | Details |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Passive transport** | No energy needed | Along concentration gradient (high → low) | Neutral solutes by **simple diffusion**; water by **osmosis** |
| Facilitated (carrier-mediated) | No energy | Along gradient | **Polar molecules** cannot pass through the non-polar lipid bilayer; need a **carrier protein** |
| **Active transport** | **ATP used** | **Against** concentration gradient (low → high) | Example: **Na<sup>+</sup>/K<sup>+</sup> pump** |

## 7. Cell Wall

- Non-living rigid outer covering of the plasma membrane in **fungi and plants**.
- Functions: gives **shape**; protects from **mechanical damage and infection**; helps in **cell-to-cell interaction**; **barrier to undesirable macromolecules**.

| Organism | Cell wall composition |
|---|---|
| **Algae** | Cellulose, **galactans, mannans** and minerals like **calcium carbonate** |
| Other plants | **Cellulose, hemicellulose, pectins and proteins** |
| Fungi (from Unit 1) | Chitin and polysaccharides |

- **Primary wall** – cell wall of a young plant cell; **capable of growth**, which diminishes as the cell matures.
- **Secondary wall** – formed on the **inner side** (towards the membrane).
- **Middle lamella** – layer mainly of **calcium pectate**; glues neighbouring cells together.
- **Plasmodesmata** – traverse the cell wall and middle lamella; **connect the cytoplasm of neighbouring cells**.

## 8. Endomembrane System

- Includes **ER, Golgi complex, lysosomes and vacuoles** – their functions are **coordinated**.
- **Mitochondria, chloroplasts and peroxisomes are NOT part** of the endomembrane system (functions not coordinated with the above).

### 8.1 Endoplasmic reticulum (ER)
- Network of tiny **tubular structures** scattered in the cytoplasm.
- Divides intracellular space into two compartments: **luminal** (inside ER) and **extra-luminal** (cytoplasm).

| Feature | Rough ER (RER) | Smooth ER (SER) |
|---|---|---|
| Ribosomes | **Attached** on outer surface | Absent |
| Found in | Cells actively involved in **protein synthesis and secretion** | – |
| Continuity | Extensive and **continuous with the outer nuclear membrane** | – |
| Main function | Protein synthesis | **Major site for lipid synthesis**; in animal cells, **steroidal hormones** synthesised here |

### 8.2 Golgi apparatus
- First observed by **Camillo Golgi (1898)** as densely stained reticular structures near the nucleus.
- Many flat, disc-shaped sacs – **cisternae** – of **0.5 µm to 1.0 µm** diameter, stacked parallel to each other.
- Cisternae arranged **concentrically near the nucleus** with:
  - **Cis face** (forming face) – **convex**
  - **Trans face** (maturing face) – **concave**
- Cis and trans faces are entirely different but interconnected.
- Functions:
  - Principally **packaging materials**, delivered to intracellular targets or **secreted** outside the cell.
  - Vesicles from ER **fuse with the cis face** and move towards the maturing (trans) face – hence Golgi remains closely associated with ER.
  - Proteins synthesised on ER are **modified** in the cisternae before release from the trans face.
  - Important site of formation of **glycoproteins and glycolipids**.

### 8.3 Lysosomes
- Membrane-bound **vesicular** structures formed by **packaging in the Golgi apparatus**.
- Very rich in almost all types of **hydrolytic enzymes (hydrolases – lipases, proteases, carbohydrases)**, optimally active at **acidic pH**.
- Can digest **carbohydrates, proteins, lipids and nucleic acids**.

### 8.4 Vacuoles
- Membrane-bound space in the cytoplasm containing **water, sap, excretory products** and other materials not useful for the cell.
- Bound by a **single membrane** called **tonoplast**.
- In plant cells, vacuoles can occupy up to **90% of the cell volume**.
- In plants, the tonoplast transports ions and other materials **against concentration gradients** into the vacuole – so their concentration is higher in the vacuole than in the cytoplasm.
- **Contractile vacuole** in *Amoeba* – **osmoregulation and excretion**.
- **Food vacuoles** – formed by engulfing food particles (in protists).

> **Remember:** Endomembrane system = ER + Golgi + Lysosome + Vacuole ("E-G-L-V"). Golgi: cis = convex = forming face; trans = concave = maturing face.

## 9. Mitochondria

- Not easily visible unless **specifically stained**.
- Number per cell depends on **physiological activity**.
- Typically **sausage-shaped or cylindrical**; diameter **0.2–1.0 µm (average 0.5 µm)**; length **1.0–4.1 µm**.
- **Double membrane-bound**: outer and inner membranes divide the lumen into two aqueous compartments – **outer compartment** and **inner compartment**.
- Inner compartment filled with dense homogeneous **matrix**.
- **Outer membrane** – continuous limiting boundary.
- **Inner membrane** – forms infoldings called **cristae** towards the matrix; **cristae increase surface area**.
- Both membranes have their own specific enzymes.
- **Sites of aerobic respiration**; produce **ATP** – "**power houses**" of the cell.
- Matrix has a **single circular DNA**, a few RNA molecules, **ribosomes (70S)** and components for protein synthesis (semi-autonomous).
- Divide by **fission**.

## 10. Plastids

- Found in **all plant cells** and in **euglenoids**. Large; easily observed.
- Classified by pigments:

| Plastid | Pigment / content | Colour / function |
|---|---|---|
| **Chloroplasts** | **Chlorophyll and carotenoids** | Trap light energy for photosynthesis |
| **Chromoplasts** | Fat-soluble carotenoids – **carotene, xanthophylls** | Yellow, orange or red colour |
| **Leucoplasts** | Colourless; store nutrients | See below |

**Types of leucoplasts:**
- **Amyloplasts** – store **carbohydrates (starch)**, e.g., **potato**
- **Elaioplasts** – store **oils and fats**
- **Aleuroplasts** – store **proteins**

### 10.1 Chloroplast structure
- Mostly in **mesophyll cells** of leaves.
- **Lens-shaped, oval, spherical, discoid or ribbon-like**; length **5–10 µm**, width **2–4 µm**.
- Number: **1 per cell in *Chlamydomonas*** to **20–40 per cell in mesophyll**.
- **Double membrane-bound**; the **inner membrane is relatively less permeable**.
- **Stroma** – space bounded by the inner membrane.
- **Thylakoids** – organised flattened membranous sacs in the stroma, arranged in stacks like piles of coins = **grana** (singular granum) or intergranal thylakoids.
- **Stroma lamellae** – flat membranous tubules connecting thylakoids of different grana.
- **Lumen** – space enclosed by thylakoid membrane.
- **Stroma** contains enzymes for synthesis of **carbohydrates and proteins**; also small, **double-stranded circular DNA** and ribosomes.
- **Chlorophyll pigments are in the thylakoids**.
- Chloroplast ribosomes are **70S** (smaller than cytoplasmic **80S**).
- Grana = site of **light reactions**; stroma = site of **dark reactions** (NCERT summary).

> **Key Point:** Mitochondria and chloroplasts are both **double membrane-bound**, have their **own circular DNA** and **70S ribosomes**. This is why they are called semi-autonomous organelles.

## 11. Ribosomes

- First observed under the electron microscope as dense particles by **George Palade (1953)**.
- Composed of **RNA and proteins**; **not surrounded by any membrane**.

| Type | Found in | Subunits |
|---|---|---|
| **80S** | Eukaryotic cytoplasm | **60S + 40S** |
| **70S** | Prokaryotes; mitochondria and chloroplasts | **50S + 30S** |

- **S = Svedberg's unit** = **sedimentation coefficient**; indirectly a measure of **density and size**.

## 12. Cytoskeleton

- Elaborate network of filamentous **proteinaceous** structures: **microtubules, microfilaments and intermediate filaments**.
- Functions: **mechanical support, motility, maintenance of cell shape**.

## 13. Cilia and Flagella

- Hair-like outgrowths of the cell membrane.
- **Cilia** – small; work like **oars**, moving either the cell or the surrounding fluid.
- **Flagella** – longer; responsible for **cell movement**.
- Prokaryotic flagella are **structurally different** from eukaryotic flagella.

### 13.1 Ultrastructure (9+2 array)
- Covered with **plasma membrane**.
- Core = **axoneme**, with microtubules running parallel to the long axis.
- **Nine doublets** of radially arranged peripheral microtubules + **a pair of centrally located microtubules** = **9+2 array**.
- Central tubules connected by **bridges** and enclosed by a **central sheath**.
- Central sheath connected to one tubule of each peripheral doublet by a **radial spoke** → **nine radial spokes**.
- Peripheral doublets interconnected by **linkers** (interdoublet bridges).
- Both cilium and flagellum emerge from centriole-like structures called **basal bodies**.

## 14. Centrosome and Centrioles

- **Centrosome** – organelle usually containing **two cylindrical centrioles**, surrounded by amorphous **pericentriolar material**.
- The two centrioles lie **perpendicular** to each other; each has a **cartwheel** organisation.
- Made up of **nine evenly spaced peripheral fibrils of tubulin** protein; **each peripheral fibril is a triplet**; adjacent triplets are linked.
- Central part of the proximal region = **hub** (proteinaceous), connected to the peripheral triplets by **radial spokes** made of protein.
- Functions:
  - Form the **basal body of cilia or flagella**
  - Form **spindle fibres** that give rise to the **spindle apparatus** during cell division in **animal cells**

> **Exam Tip:** Cilia/flagella axoneme = **9 doublets + 2 central** (9+2). Centriole/basal body = **9 triplets, no central microtubules** (9+0, cartwheel with hub). This comparison is a very frequent NEET question.

## 15. Nucleus

### 15.1 History
- First described by **Robert Brown (1831)**.
- Material of the nucleus stained by **basic dyes** was named **chromatin** by **Flemming**.

### 15.2 Structure of interphase nucleus
- Highly extended nucleoprotein fibres = **chromatin**, **nuclear matrix**, and one or more spherical **nucleoli**.
- **Nuclear envelope** – **two parallel membranes** with a space between (**10 to 50 nm**) called the **perinuclear space**; barrier between nucleus and cytoplasm.
- **Outer membrane** is usually **continuous with the ER** and bears **ribosomes**.
- **Nuclear pores** – formed by **fusion of the two membranes**; passages for movement of **RNA and protein** molecules in **both directions**.
- Usually one nucleus per cell; variations are common (multinucleate cells).
- Some mature cells **lack a nucleus**: **erythrocytes of many mammals** and **sieve tube cells** of vascular plants.

### 15.3 Nucleolus
- Spherical structures in the nucleoplasm; **not membrane-bound** (content continuous with nucleoplasm).
- Site for active **ribosomal RNA (rRNA) synthesis**.
- **Larger and more numerous nucleoli** in cells actively carrying out **protein synthesis**.

### 15.4 Chromatin and chromosomes
- Chromatin contains **DNA**, basic proteins called **histones**, some **non-histone proteins** and **RNA**.
- A single human cell has about **2 metres of DNA** distributed among its **46 chromosomes (23 pairs)**.
- Chromosomes are visible only in dividing cells.
- Every chromosome has a **primary constriction (centromere)**, on the sides of which are disc-shaped **kinetochores**. The centromere holds the **two chromatids**.

### 15.5 Types of chromosomes (position of centromere)

| Type | Centromere position | Arms |
|---|---|---|
| **Metacentric** | **Middle** | Two **equal** arms |
| **Sub-metacentric** | Slightly away from the middle | One shorter, one longer arm |
| **Acrocentric** | **Close to the end** | One extremely short, one very long arm |
| **Telocentric** | **Terminal** | Only one arm |

- **Satellite** – some chromosomes have **non-staining secondary constrictions** at a constant location, giving the appearance of a small fragment called the satellite.

## 16. Microbodies

- Many **membrane-bound minute vesicles** containing various **enzymes**; present in both **plant and animal cells**. (Examples: peroxisomes, glyoxysomes.)

### 16.1 Organelles – membrane summary

| Membrane | Organelles |
|---|---|
| **Double membrane** | Nucleus (nuclear envelope), mitochondria, chloroplasts (plastids) |
| **Single membrane** | ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vacuoles, microbodies (peroxisomes) |
| **No membrane** | Ribosomes, centrosome/centrioles, nucleolus, cytoskeleton |

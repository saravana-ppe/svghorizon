## 1. Cell Cycle

### 1.1 Definition
- The sequence of events by which a cell **duplicates its genome, synthesises other constituents and eventually divides** into two daughter cells.
- Cell growth (cytoplasmic increase) is **continuous**, but **DNA synthesis occurs only in one specific stage**.
- Events are under **genetic control**.
- **Human cells in culture** divide about **once every 24 hours**. **Yeast** can complete the cycle in about **90 minutes**.

### 1.2 Phases of the cell cycle
- Two basic phases: **Interphase** and **M phase (mitosis phase)**.
- In a 24-hour human cell cycle, cell division proper lasts only **about an hour**. **Interphase lasts more than 95%** of the cycle.
- **M phase** starts with nuclear division (**karyokinesis**) and usually ends with division of cytoplasm (**cytokinesis**).
- **Interphase** – called the "resting phase", but the cell is preparing for division by **cell growth and DNA replication**.

| Phase | Events |
|---|---|
| **G<sub>1</sub> (Gap 1)** | Interval between mitosis and initiation of DNA replication; cell is **metabolically active and grows**, but **does not replicate DNA**. (Most organelle duplication also occurs in this phase – NCERT summary) |
| **S (Synthesis)** | **DNA replication**; DNA amount **doubles from 2C to 4C**; **chromosome number stays 2n**. In animal cells, **centriole duplicates in the cytoplasm** |
| **G<sub>2</sub> (Gap 2)** | **Proteins synthesised** in preparation for mitosis; cell growth continues |
| **G<sub>0</sub> (Quiescent stage)** | Cells that do not divide further exit G<sub>1</sub> and enter G<sub>0</sub>. **Metabolically active** but **no longer proliferate** unless called on. Example: **heart cells** |

- In animals, mitosis is usually seen only in **diploid somatic cells**. Exception: **male honey bees** – haploid cells divide by mitosis.
- **Plants** show mitosis in **both haploid and diploid** cells.

> **Exam Tip (DNA content vs chromosome number):** Onion root tip cell has 16 chromosomes. G<sub>1</sub>: 16 chromosomes, 2C DNA. After S: 16 chromosomes, 4C DNA. G<sub>2</sub>: 16 chromosomes, 4C. After M: 16 chromosomes each daughter cell, 2C. DNA doubles in S phase, but chromosome number does not change.

## 2. M Phase – Mitosis

- Most dramatic period; major reorganisation of virtually all components of the cell.
- Parent and progeny have the same number of chromosomes → **equational division**.
- Karyokinesis has four stages: **prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase**. It is a **progressive process** – no clear-cut lines between stages.

### 2.1 Prophase
- Follows S and G<sub>2</sub> (new DNA molecules are intertwined, not distinct).
- **Initiation of condensation** of chromosomal material; material becomes untangled.
- Key events:
  - Chromosomal material **condenses** to form compact mitotic chromosomes, each with **two chromatids** attached at the **centromere**.
  - **Centrosome** (duplicated during S phase) begins to move towards **opposite poles**. Each centrosome radiates out microtubules called **asters**. The two asters with spindle fibres form the **mitotic apparatus**.
- At the end of prophase, the cell **does not show Golgi complexes, ER, nucleolus and the nuclear envelope**.

### 2.2 Metaphase
- Starts with **complete disintegration of the nuclear envelope**; chromosomes spread through the cytoplasm.
- Chromosome condensation is **complete** – **best stage to study chromosome morphology**.
- Each chromosome = two sister chromatids held at the centromere.
- **Kinetochores** – small disc-shaped structures at the surface of centromeres; **sites of attachment of spindle fibres**.
- All chromosomes come to lie at the **equator**; one chromatid attached by its kinetochore to fibres from one pole and the sister chromatid to fibres from the opposite pole.
- Plane of alignment = **metaphase plate**.
- Key features:
  - Spindle fibres attach to kinetochores.
  - Chromosomes move to the spindle equator and align along the metaphase plate.

### 2.3 Anaphase
- Each chromosome at the metaphase plate **splits simultaneously**; the two daughter chromatids (now **daughter chromosomes**) migrate to opposite poles.
- The **centromere leads** (directed towards the pole) with arms trailing behind.
- Key events:
  - **Centromeres split and chromatids separate.**
  - Chromatids move to opposite poles.

### 2.4 Telophase
- Chromosomes at the poles **decondense** and lose individuality.
- Key events:
  - Chromosomes cluster at opposite poles; identity lost.
  - **Nuclear envelope** develops around each cluster → two daughter nuclei.
  - **Nucleolus, Golgi complex and ER reform**.

### 2.5 Cytokinesis
- Division of cytoplasm into two daughter cells.

| Animal cells | Plant cells |
|---|---|
| A **furrow** appears in the plasma membrane, deepens and joins in the centre (cleavage) | Enclosed by inextensible cell wall. **Wall formation starts in the centre** and grows **outward** to meet the lateral walls. Begins with a precursor called the **cell plate**, which represents the **middle lamella** |

- Organelles like mitochondria and plastids get distributed between daughter cells.
- **Syncytium** – when karyokinesis is **not followed by cytokinesis**, a **multinucleate** condition arises. Example: **liquid endosperm in coconut**.

## 3. Significance of Mitosis

- Usually restricted to **diploid cells**; but in some **lower plants** and some **social insects**, haploid cells divide by mitosis.
- Produces **diploid daughter cells with identical genetic complement**.
- **Growth** of multicellular organisms.
- **Restores the nucleo-cytoplasmic ratio** (disturbed by cell growth).
- **Cell repair** – cells of upper epidermis, lining of gut and blood cells are constantly replaced.
- Mitosis in meristems (**apical and lateral cambium**) → **continuous growth of plants** throughout life.

## 4. Meiosis

- Specialised division that **reduces chromosome number by half**, producing **haploid** daughter cells (gametes are formed from specialised diploid cells).
- Meiosis ensures the haploid phase; **fertilisation restores the diploid phase**.
- Occurs during **gametogenesis** in plants and animals.

### 4.1 Key features
- **Two sequential cycles** of nuclear and cell division (**meiosis I and meiosis II**) but **only a single cycle of DNA replication**.
- Meiosis I starts after parental chromosomes have replicated (identical sister chromatids at S phase).
- Involves **pairing of homologous chromosomes** and **recombination between non-sister chromatids**.
- **Four haploid cells** at the end of meiosis II.

### 4.2 Meiosis I – Prophase I (five sub-stages)

| Sub-stage | Key events |
|---|---|
| **Leptotene** | Chromosomes become gradually **visible** under the light microscope; compaction continues |
| **Zygotene** | Chromosomes start **pairing** = **synapsis**; paired chromosomes = homologous chromosomes. Synapsis accompanied by formation of **synaptonemal complex**. Complex of a pair of synapsed homologues = **bivalent or tetrad** (more clearly visible at next stage) |
| **Pachytene** | **Longest** of the first three (leptotene and zygotene are short-lived). Four chromatids of each bivalent become distinct → **tetrads**. **Recombination nodules** appear – sites of **crossing over** between **non-sister chromatids** of homologous chromosomes. Crossing over is **enzyme-mediated – recombinase**. Recombination completed by end of pachytene; chromosomes linked at crossover sites |
| **Diplotene** | **Dissolution of the synaptonemal complex**; recombined homologues tend to separate **except at crossover sites**. These X-shaped structures = **chiasmata**. In **oocytes of some vertebrates**, diplotene can last **months or years** |
| **Diakinesis** | **Terminalisation of chiasmata**; chromosomes **fully condensed**; **meiotic spindle assembled**. By the end, **nucleolus disappears** and **nuclear envelope breaks down**. Represents **transition to metaphase** |

> **Mnemonic for prophase I:** "**L**azy **Z**ebras **P**lay **D**uring **D**ay" – Leptotene, Zygotene, Pachytene, Diplotene, Diakinesis.

### 4.3 Rest of meiosis I
- **Metaphase I** – **bivalents** align on the equatorial plate; microtubules from opposite poles attach to kinetochores of **homologous chromosomes**.
- **Anaphase I** – **homologous chromosomes separate**, while **sister chromatids remain associated at their centromeres**.
- **Telophase I** – nuclear membrane and nucleolus reappear; cytokinesis follows → **dyad of cells**. Chromosomes do not reach the fully extended interphase state.
- **Interkinesis** – stage between the two meiotic divisions; **short-lived**; **no DNA replication**.

### 4.4 Meiosis II (resembles normal mitosis)
- **Prophase II** – begins immediately after cytokinesis, usually before chromosomes fully elongate; nuclear membrane disappears by the end; chromosomes become compact again.
- **Metaphase II** – chromosomes align at the equator; microtubules attach to kinetochores of **sister chromatids**.
- **Anaphase II** – **simultaneous splitting of the centromere** of each chromosome; sister chromatids move to opposite poles by **shortening of microtubules** attached to kinetochores.
- **Telophase II** – two groups of chromosomes enclosed by nuclear envelopes; cytokinesis → **tetrad of cells (four haploid daughter cells)**.

## 5. Significance of Meiosis

- **Conservation of specific chromosome number** of each species across generations in sexually reproducing organisms (even though it paradoxically halves the chromosome number).
- **Increases genetic variability** in the population from one generation to the next.
- **Variations are very important for evolution**.

## 6. Mitosis vs Meiosis – Comparison

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Type of division | Equational | Reductional (meiosis I) + equational (meiosis II) |
| Occurs in | Somatic cells (usually diploid) | Diploid cells destined to form gametes |
| Number of divisions | One | Two (with one DNA replication) |
| Daughter cells | 2, diploid, genetically identical | 4, haploid, genetically varied |
| Prophase | Short, simple | Prophase I long and complex (5 sub-stages) |
| Pairing of homologues (synapsis) | Absent | Present (zygotene) |
| Crossing over | Absent | Present (pachytene) |
| Metaphase alignment | Individual chromosomes | Bivalents (metaphase I) |
| Anaphase | Centromeres split; sister chromatids separate | Anaphase I: homologues separate (centromeres do not split). Anaphase II: sister chromatids separate |
| Significance | Growth, repair, restoring nucleo-cytoplasmic ratio | Conserves chromosome number; creates variation |

> **PYQ Trend:** Frequently asked:

> - Best stage to study chromosome morphology (**metaphase**).
> - Anaphase of mitosis vs anaphase I (centromere splits in mitosis and anaphase II, not in anaphase I).
> - Cells in G<sub>0</sub> (heart cells), duration of cell cycle (24 h human, 90 min yeast).
> - Syncytium example – liquid endosperm of coconut.

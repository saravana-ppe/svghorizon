## 1. Five Kingdom Classification

### 1.1 History of classification systems
- **Aristotle** – earliest scientific attempt. Classified plants into **trees, shrubs and herbs** (simple morphological characters). Animals into two groups: those with **red blood** and those without.
- **Linnaeus – Two Kingdom system:** Plantae and Animalia.
- **Drawbacks of the two kingdom system:** it did not distinguish between
  - eukaryotes and prokaryotes,
  - unicellular and multicellular organisms,
  - photosynthetic (green algae) and non-photosynthetic (fungi) organisms.
- Earlier systems put bacteria, blue-green algae, fungi, mosses, ferns, gymnosperms and angiosperms together under "Plants" only because they all had a **cell wall**.
- **R.H. Whittaker (1969)** proposed the **Five Kingdom Classification**: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia.
- **Criteria used by Whittaker:** cell structure, body organisation, mode of nutrition, reproduction and phylogenetic relationships.
- A **three-domain system** has also been proposed. It divides Monera into two domains, leaving the eukaryotic kingdoms in the third domain – giving a **six kingdom** classification.

> **Key Point:** Why fungi were separated: fungi are heterotrophic with **chitin** walls, while green plants are autotrophic with **cellulose** walls. Kingdom Protista brought together *Chlamydomonas* and *Chlorella* (earlier algae/plants, with cell walls) with *Paramoecium* and *Amoeba* (earlier animals, without cell walls).

### 1.2 Table 2.1 – Characteristics of the Five Kingdoms (most important table of the unit)

| Character | Monera | Protista | Fungi | Plantae | Animalia |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell type | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic | Eukaryotic | Eukaryotic | Eukaryotic |
| Cell wall | Present, non-cellulosic (polysaccharide + amino acid) | Present in some | Present, with chitin | Present (cellulose) | Absent |
| Nuclear membrane | Absent | Present | Present | Present | Present |
| Body organisation | Cellular | Cellular | Multicellular / loose tissue | Tissue / organ | Tissue / organ / organ system |
| Mode of nutrition | Autotrophic (chemosynthetic and photosynthetic) and heterotrophic (saprophytic / parasitic) | Autotrophic (photosynthetic) and heterotrophic | Heterotrophic (saprophytic / parasitic) | Autotrophic (photosynthetic) | Heterotrophic (holozoic / saprophytic etc.) |

## 2. Kingdom Monera

- **Bacteria** are the sole members of Kingdom Monera.
- Most abundant micro-organisms; found almost everywhere – hundreds in a handful of soil; also in extreme habitats like hot springs, deserts, snow and deep oceans. Many are parasites.

### 2.1 Shapes of bacteria

| Shape | Name (singular / plural) |
|---|---|
| Spherical | Coccus / cocci |
| Rod-shaped | Bacillus / bacilli |
| Comma-shaped | Vibrium / vibrio |
| Spiral | Spirillum / spirilla |

### 2.2 Nutrition
- Bacteria as a group show the **most extensive metabolic diversity**.
- **Autotrophic** – make food from inorganic substrates: photosynthetic autotrophs or chemosynthetic autotrophs.
- **Heterotrophic** – vast majority; depend on other organisms or dead organic matter.

### 2.3 Archaebacteria
- Live in the most harsh habitats:
  - **Halophiles** – extreme salty areas
  - **Thermoacidophiles** – hot springs
  - **Methanogens** – marshy areas
- They differ from other bacteria in having a **different cell wall structure**. This feature is responsible for their survival in extreme conditions.
- **Methanogens** are present in the **gut of ruminants** (cows, buffaloes) and produce **methane (biogas)** from the dung of these animals.

### 2.4 Eubacteria ("true bacteria")
- Have a **rigid cell wall** and, if motile, a **flagellum**.

**(a) Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae)**
- Have **chlorophyll a** similar to green plants; photosynthetic autotrophs.
- Unicellular, colonial or filamentous; freshwater, marine or terrestrial.
- Colonies are surrounded by a **gelatinous sheath**.
- Often form **blooms** in polluted water bodies.
- Some fix atmospheric nitrogen in specialised cells called **heterocysts**, e.g., ***Nostoc*** and ***Anabaena***.

**(b) Chemosynthetic autotrophic bacteria**
- Oxidise inorganic substances such as nitrates, nitrites and ammonia and use the released energy for ATP production.
- Important in **recycling nutrients** like nitrogen, phosphorus, iron and sulphur.

**(c) Heterotrophic bacteria**
- **Most abundant** in nature. Majority are important **decomposers**.
- Useful: making **curd** from milk, production of **antibiotics**, **nitrogen fixation** in legume roots.
- Harmful: pathogens causing **cholera, typhoid, tetanus, citrus canker**.

### 2.5 Reproduction in bacteria
- Mainly by **fission**.
- Under unfavourable conditions, they produce **spores**.
- A sort of sexual reproduction by a primitive type of **DNA transfer** from one bacterium to another.

### 2.6 Mycoplasma
- **Completely lack a cell wall**.
- **Smallest living cells** known.
- Can survive **without oxygen**.
- Many are pathogenic in animals and plants.

> **Common Mistake:** Cyanobacteria are called "blue-green algae" but they are NOT algae. They are prokaryotes in Kingdom Monera. Similarly, heterocysts are the nitrogen-fixing cells of cyanobacteria (*Nostoc*, *Anabaena*), not of true algae.

## 3. Kingdom Protista

- All **single-celled eukaryotes** are placed under Protista. Boundaries are not well defined.
- NCERT includes five groups: **Chrysophytes, Dinoflagellates, Euglenoids, Slime moulds and Protozoans**.
- Primarily **aquatic**. Forms a link with plants, animals and fungi.
- Being eukaryotes, they have a well-defined nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. Some have flagella or cilia.
- Reproduce **asexually and sexually** (by cell fusion and zygote formation).

### 3.1 Chrysophytes
- Include **diatoms** and **golden algae (desmids)**.
- Freshwater and marine; microscopic; float passively in water currents (**plankton**). Most are photosynthetic.
- **Diatoms:** cell walls form two thin **overlapping shells** that fit together like a **soap box**. Walls are embedded with **silica**, so they are indestructible.
- Accumulated diatom walls over billions of years = **diatomaceous earth**. Being gritty, it is used in **polishing, filtration of oils and syrups**.
- Diatoms are the **chief producers in the oceans**.

### 3.2 Dinoflagellates
- Mostly **marine** and photosynthetic.
- Appear yellow, green, brown, blue or red depending on main pigments.
- Cell wall has **stiff cellulose plates** on the outer surface.
- Most have **two flagella** – one **longitudinal**, the other **transverse** in a furrow between the wall plates.
- Red dinoflagellates (e.g., ***Gonyaulax***) multiply rapidly and make the sea appear red – **red tides**. Toxins released may kill fishes and other marine animals.

### 3.3 Euglenoids
- Mostly **freshwater**, found in **stagnant water**.
- **No cell wall**; instead have a **protein-rich layer called pellicle** which makes the body flexible.
- Two flagella – **one short and one long**.
- **Photosynthetic in sunlight**; when deprived of sunlight they behave as **heterotrophs** by predating on smaller organisms (mixotrophic).
- Their pigments are **identical to those of higher plants**.
- Example: ***Euglena***.

### 3.4 Slime moulds
- **Saprophytic** protists. The body moves along decaying twigs and leaves, engulfing organic matter.
- Under suitable conditions they form an aggregation called **plasmodium**, which may spread over several feet.
- Under unfavourable conditions, the plasmodium forms **fruiting bodies** bearing spores at their tips.
- Spores possess **true walls**, are extremely resistant, survive for many years, and are dispersed by **air currents**.

### 3.5 Protozoans
- All are **heterotrophs** – predators or parasites. Believed to be **primitive relatives of animals**.

| Group | Locomotion / key feature | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Amoeboid** | Pseudopodia (false feet) to move and capture prey. Live in fresh water, sea water or moist soil. Marine forms have **silica shells**. | *Amoeba*; parasitic *Entamoeba* |
| **Flagellated** | Flagella; free-living or parasitic. Parasites cause **sleeping sickness**. | *Trypanosoma* |
| **Ciliated** | Thousands of **cilia**; a cavity called **gullet** opens to the outside. Coordinated cilia movement steers food-laden water into the gullet. | *Paramoecium* |
| **Sporozoans** | Have an **infectious spore-like stage** in the life cycle. | *Plasmodium* (malarial parasite) |

> **Remember – protist one-liners:** Soap-box silica wall = diatoms. Red tide = *Gonyaulax*. Pellicle = *Euglena*. Plasmodium (aggregation) = slime moulds. Gullet = *Paramoecium*. Sleeping sickness = *Trypanosoma*. Do not confuse "plasmodium" (slime mould stage) with "*Plasmodium*" (malarial parasite).

## 4. Kingdom Fungi

### 4.1 General features
- Unique kingdom of **heterotrophic** organisms; great diversity in morphology and habitat.
- Seen on moist bread and rotten fruits; mushrooms and toadstools are fungi.
- **White spots on mustard leaves** – parasitic fungus (*Albugo*).
- **Yeast** (unicellular) – used to make bread and beer.
- ***Puccinia*** – causes **wheat rust**.
- ***Penicillium*** – source of antibiotics.
- Cosmopolitan; prefer **warm and humid** places (hence refrigeration prevents spoilage).
- Except **yeasts (unicellular)**, fungi are **filamentous**.
- Body: long, slender thread-like **hyphae**; network of hyphae = **mycelium**.
  - **Coenocytic hyphae** – continuous tubes with multinucleated cytoplasm (no cross walls).
  - **Septate hyphae** – with septae or cross walls.
- Cell wall made of **chitin and polysaccharides**.
- Nutrition:
  - **Saprophytes** – absorb soluble organic matter from dead substrates (most fungi)
  - **Parasites** – depend on living plants and animals
  - **Symbionts** – with algae as **lichens**; with roots of higher plants as **mycorrhiza**

### 4.2 Reproduction in fungi
- **Vegetative:** fragmentation, fission and budding.
- **Asexual:** spores called **conidia**, **sporangiospores** or **zoospores**.
- **Sexual:** **oospores, ascospores and basidiospores**. Spores are produced in structures called **fruiting bodies**.
- **Three steps of the sexual cycle:**
  1. **Plasmogamy** – fusion of protoplasms of two motile or non-motile gametes.
  2. **Karyogamy** – fusion of two nuclei.
  3. **Meiosis** in the zygote → haploid spores.
- Two haploid hyphae of **compatible mating types** fuse.
- In some fungi, fusion immediately gives diploid cells (2n).
- In **ascomycetes and basidiomycetes**, an intervening **dikaryotic stage (n + n, two nuclei per cell)** occurs. This condition is a **dikaryon** and the phase is the **dikaryophase**. Later the nuclei fuse and cells become diploid.
- Basis of dividing fungi into classes: **morphology of mycelium, mode of spore formation and fruiting bodies**.

### 4.3 Classes of fungi (very important comparison)

| Feature | Phycomycetes | Ascomycetes (sac fungi) | Basidiomycetes | Deuteromycetes (imperfect fungi) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Habitat | Aquatic; decaying wood in moist places; obligate parasites on plants | Saprophytic, decomposers, parasitic or **coprophilous** (on dung) | Soil, logs, tree stumps; parasites in living plants (rusts, smuts) | Saprophytes, parasites; many decompose litter and help mineral cycling |
| Mycelium | **Aseptate and coenocytic** | Branched and septate | Branched and septate | Septate and branched |
| Asexual spores | **Zoospores** (motile) or **aplanospores** (non-motile), produced **endogenously in sporangium** | **Conidia**, produced **exogenously** on **conidiophores** | Generally **not found**; fragmentation common | **Only asexual – conidia** |
| Sexual reproduction | **Zygospore** by fusion of two gametes – isogamous, anisogamous or oogamous | **Ascospores**, produced **endogenously** in sac-like **asci**; asci arranged in **ascocarps** | **Sex organs absent**; plasmogamy by fusion of two vegetative/somatic cells of different strains. Dikaryotic → **basidium**; karyogamy + meiosis in basidium → **4 basidiospores** produced **exogenously**; fruiting body = **basidiocarp** | **Not known** (hence "imperfect") |
| Examples | *Mucor*, *Rhizopus* (bread mould), *Albugo* (parasite on mustard) | *Penicillium*, yeast (*Saccharomyces*, unicellular), *Aspergillus*, *Claviceps*, *Neurospora*; morels and truffles (edible delicacies) | *Agaricus* (mushroom), *Ustilago* (smut), *Puccinia* (rust); bracket fungi, puffballs | *Alternaria*, *Colletotrichum*, *Trichoderma* |

- *Neurospora* is used extensively in **biochemical and genetic work**.
- **Deuteromycetes:** When the sexual (perfect) stages are discovered, members are moved to the correct class – often to ascomycetes or basidiomycetes.

> **PYQ Trend:** NEET frequently asks: (1) which fungus has aseptate coenocytic mycelium (Phycomycetes – *Mucor*, *Rhizopus*, *Albugo*); (2) exogenous vs endogenous spores (conidia and basidiospores are exogenous; ascospores and sporangiospores are endogenous); (3) matching *Puccinia*–rust, *Ustilago*–smut, *Albugo*–white rust of mustard; (4) "imperfect fungi" with examples *Alternaria*, *Colletotrichum*, *Trichoderma*.

## 5. Kingdom Plantae and Kingdom Animalia (overview)

**Kingdom Plantae**
- All **eukaryotic chlorophyll-containing** organisms (plants).
- A few are **partially heterotrophic**: insectivorous plants – **Bladderwort** and **Venus fly trap**; parasite – ***Cuscuta***.
- Cells have prominent chloroplasts and a **cellulose** cell wall.
- Includes algae, bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms and angiosperms.
- Life cycle has two phases – **diploid sporophyte** and **haploid gametophyte** – that alternate. This is called **alternation of generations**.

**Kingdom Animalia**
- **Heterotrophic, eukaryotic, multicellular**; cells **lack cell walls**.
- Depend directly or indirectly on plants for food.
- Digest food in an internal cavity; store food as **glycogen or fat**.
- Nutrition is **holozoic** (by ingestion).
- Definite growth pattern; adults have a definite shape and size.
- Higher forms show elaborate sensory and neuromotor mechanisms; most are capable of locomotion.
- Sexual reproduction by copulation of male and female, followed by embryological development.

## 6. Viruses, Viroids, Prions and Lichens

These are **not included** in Whittaker's five kingdom classification.

### 6.1 Viruses
- Not considered truly "living" if living means having a cell structure. They are **non-cellular** and have an **inert crystalline structure outside the living cell**.
- Once inside a cell, they take over the host machinery to replicate, killing the host.
- "Virus" means **venom or poisonous fluid**.

| Scientist | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Dmitri Ivanowsky (1892)** | Recognised microbes causing **mosaic disease of tobacco**; found them smaller than bacteria (passed through bacteria-proof filters) |
| **M.W. Beijerinck (1898)** | Showed extract of infected tobacco could infect healthy plants; named the pathogen "**virus**" and the fluid ***Contagium vivum fluidum*** (infectious living fluid) |
| **W.M. Stanley (1935)** | Showed viruses could be **crystallised**; crystals consist largely of **proteins** |

- Viruses are **obligate parasites**; inert outside the specific host cell.
- Contain **protein + genetic material (RNA or DNA)**. **No virus contains both RNA and DNA.**
- A virus is a **nucleoprotein**; the **genetic material is infectious**.

| Host | Usual genetic material |
|---|---|
| Plant viruses | **Single-stranded RNA** |
| Animal viruses | Single- or double-stranded RNA, or double-stranded DNA |
| Bacteriophages (bacterial viruses) | Usually **double-stranded DNA** |

- The protein coat is the **capsid**, made of small subunits called **capsomeres**, arranged in **helical or polyhedral** forms. It protects the nucleic acid.
- Viral diseases in humans: **mumps, small pox, herpes, influenza, AIDS**.
- Symptoms in plants: **mosaic formation, leaf rolling and curling, yellowing and vein clearing, dwarfing and stunted growth**.
- Examples in figures: **TMV (Tobacco Mosaic Virus)** – RNA virus; **Bacteriophage** – head, collar, sheath, tail fibres.

### 6.2 Viroids
- Discovered by **T.O. Diener (1971)**.
- Infectious agent **smaller than viruses**; caused **potato spindle tuber disease**.
- A **free RNA** lacking the protein coat (hence "viroid"). The RNA has **low molecular weight**.

### 6.3 Prions
- Infectious agents made of **abnormally folded protein**; similar in size to viruses.
- Cause infectious neurological diseases:
  - **Bovine spongiform encephalopathy (BSE)** – "mad cow disease" in cattle
  - **Creutzfeldt–Jacob disease (CJD)** – analogous variant in humans

### 6.4 Lichens
- **Symbiotic (mutually useful) associations between algae and fungi**.
- Algal partner = **phycobiont** (autotrophic); fungal partner = **mycobiont** (heterotrophic).
- Algae prepare food for fungi; fungi provide shelter and absorb mineral nutrients and water.
- Lichens are **very good pollution indicators** – they **do not grow in polluted areas** (sensitive to SO<sub>2</sub>).

> **Exam Tip:** Virus = protein + nucleic acid (RNA or DNA). Viroid = only RNA (no protein coat). Prion = only protein (no nucleic acid). This three-way comparison is a favourite NEET question.

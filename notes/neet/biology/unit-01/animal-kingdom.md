## 1. Animal Kingdom – Basis of Classification

Over a million species of animals have been described. Common fundamental features used for classification: arrangement of cells, body symmetry, nature of coelom, patterns of digestive, circulatory or reproductive systems.

### 1.1 Levels of organisation

| Level | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Cellular** | Cells arranged as loose aggregates; some division of labour | Sponges (Porifera) |
| **Tissue** | Cells performing the same function arranged into tissues | Coelenterates (and Ctenophores) |
| **Organ** | Tissues grouped to form organs | Platyhelminthes |
| **Organ system** | Organs form functional systems | Annelids, Arthropods, Molluscs, Echinoderms, Chordates (and Aschelminthes) |

- **Digestive system:** **Incomplete** – single opening serving as both mouth and anus (Platyhelminthes). **Complete** – two openings, mouth and anus.
- **Circulatory system:**
  - **Open type** – blood pumped out of heart; cells and tissues directly bathed in it.
  - **Closed type** – blood circulates through vessels of varying diameters (arteries, veins, capillaries).

### 1.2 Symmetry
- **Asymmetrical** – no plane through the centre divides into equal halves (most **sponges**).
- **Radial** – any plane through the central axis divides into two identical halves (**coelenterates, ctenophores, echinoderms**).
- **Bilateral** – body divided into identical left and right halves in **only one plane** (annelids, arthropods, etc.).

### 1.3 Diploblastic and triploblastic
- **Diploblastic:** two germ layers – outer **ectoderm** and inner **endoderm**, with an undifferentiated **mesoglea** in between. Example: **coelenterates** (and ctenophores).
- **Triploblastic:** third germ layer **mesoderm** between ectoderm and endoderm. **Platyhelminthes to chordates**.

### 1.4 Coelom
- **Coelom** = body cavity between body wall and gut wall, **lined by mesoderm**.
- **Coelomates:** annelids, molluscs, arthropods, echinoderms, hemichordates and chordates.
- **Pseudocoelomates:** body cavity not lined by mesoderm; mesoderm present as scattered pouches between ectoderm and endoderm. Example: **Aschelminthes**.
- **Acoelomates:** body cavity absent. Example: **Platyhelminthes**.

### 1.5 Segmentation
- Body externally and internally divided into segments with serial repetition of at least some organs = **metameric segmentation (metamerism)**. Example: **earthworm**. First seen in **Annelida**.

### 1.6 Notochord
- **Mesodermally derived rod-like structure** formed on the **dorsal side** during embryonic development.
- Animals with notochord = **chordates**; without = **non-chordates** (Porifera to Echinodermata, plus Hemichordata).

## 2. Non-chordate Phyla

### 2.1 Phylum Porifera (sponges)
- Generally **marine**, mostly **asymmetrical**. Primitive multicellular animals with **cellular level** of organisation.
- **Water transport / canal system:** water enters through minute pores **ostia** → central cavity **spongocoel** → exits through **osculum**. Helps in food gathering, respiratory exchange and removal of waste.
- **Choanocytes (collar cells)** line the spongocoel and canals.
- Digestion is **intracellular**.
- Skeleton of **spicules** or **spongin fibres**.
- **Hermaphrodite** (eggs and sperms by same individual).
- Asexual: fragmentation; sexual: gametes. **Fertilisation internal**; **development indirect** (larval stage distinct from adult).
- Examples: ***Sycon* (*Scypha*)**, ***Spongilla*** (freshwater sponge), ***Euspongia*** (bath sponge).

### 2.2 Phylum Coelenterata (Cnidaria)
- Aquatic, mostly marine; sessile or free-swimming; **radially symmetrical**.
- Name from **cnidoblasts/cnidocytes** containing stinging capsules **nematocysts**, on tentacles and body. Used for **anchorage, defence and capture of prey**.
- **Tissue level**, **diploblastic**.
- Central **gastro-vascular cavity** with a single opening – mouth on **hypostome**.
- Digestion **extracellular and intracellular**.
- Some, e.g., **corals**, have a skeleton of **calcium carbonate**.
- Two body forms:
  - **Polyp** – sessile, cylindrical (e.g., *Hydra*, *Adamsia*)
  - **Medusa** – umbrella-shaped, free-swimming (e.g., *Aurelia*, jellyfish)
- Those with both forms show **alternation of generations (metagenesis)**: **polyps produce medusae asexually**, **medusae form polyps sexually** (e.g., ***Obelia***).
- Examples: ***Physalia*** (Portuguese man-of-war), ***Adamsia*** (sea anemone), ***Pennatula*** (sea-pen), ***Gorgonia*** (sea-fan), ***Meandrina*** (brain coral).

### 2.3 Phylum Ctenophora
- Commonly **sea walnuts or comb jellies**; **exclusively marine**, radially symmetrical, diploblastic, tissue level.
- **Eight external rows of ciliated comb plates** for locomotion.
- Digestion extracellular and intracellular.
- **Bioluminescence** (emitting light) is well-marked.
- Sexes not separate (hermaphrodite). **Only sexual reproduction**. **External fertilisation**, indirect development.
- Examples: ***Pleurobrachia***, ***Ctenoplana***.

### 2.4 Phylum Platyhelminthes (flatworms)
- **Dorso-ventrally flattened** body.
- Mostly **endoparasites** in animals including humans.
- **Bilateral**, **triploblastic**, **acoelomate**, **organ level**.
- **Hooks and suckers** in parasitic forms. Some absorb nutrients directly through the body surface.
- **Flame cells** – osmoregulation and excretion.
- Sexes not separate. Internal fertilisation; development through **many larval stages**.
- ***Planaria*** has **high regeneration capacity**.
- Examples: ***Taenia*** (tapeworm), ***Fasciola*** (liver fluke).

### 2.5 Phylum Aschelminthes (roundworms)
- Body **circular in cross-section**.
- Free-living (aquatic, terrestrial) or parasitic in plants and animals.
- **Organ-system level**, bilateral, triploblastic, **pseudocoelomate**.
- **Complete alimentary canal** with a well-developed **muscular pharynx**.
- **Excretory tube** removes wastes through the **excretory pore**.
- **Sexes separate (dioecious)**; **females often longer than males** (sexual dimorphism).
- Internal fertilisation; development direct or indirect.
- Examples: ***Ascaris*** (roundworm), ***Wuchereria*** (filaria worm), ***Ancylostoma*** (hookworm).

### 2.6 Phylum Annelida
- Aquatic (marine, freshwater) or terrestrial; free-living, sometimes parasitic.
- Organ-system level, bilateral, triploblastic, **metamerically segmented**, **coelomate**. (Latin *annulus* = little ring.)
- **Longitudinal and circular muscles** for locomotion.
- Aquatic ***Nereis*** has lateral appendages **parapodia** for swimming.
- **Closed circulatory system**.
- **Nephridia** – osmoregulation and excretion.
- Neural system: paired ganglia connected by lateral nerves to a **double ventral nerve cord**.
- *Nereis* is **dioecious**; **earthworms and leeches are monoecious**. Reproduction sexual.
- Examples: ***Nereis***, ***Pheretima*** (earthworm), ***Hirudinaria*** (blood-sucking leech).

### 2.7 Phylum Arthropoda
- **Largest phylum** of Animalia; includes insects. **Over two-thirds of all named species** on Earth are arthropods.
- Organ-system, bilateral, triploblastic, segmented, coelomate.
- **Chitinous exoskeleton**. Body: head, thorax, abdomen.
- **Jointed appendages** (*arthros* = joint, *poda* = appendages).
- Respiratory organs: **gills, book gills, book lungs or tracheal system**.
- **Open circulatory system**.
- Sense organs: **antennae, eyes (compound and simple), statocysts** (balancing organs).
- Excretion through **Malpighian tubules**.
- Mostly dioecious; fertilisation usually internal; mostly **oviparous**; development direct or indirect.

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Economically important insects | ***Apis*** (honey bee), ***Bombyx*** (silkworm), ***Laccifer*** (lac insect) |
| Vectors | ***Anopheles, Culex, Aedes*** (mosquitoes) |
| Gregarious pest | ***Locusta*** (locust) |
| Living fossil | ***Limulus*** (king crab) |

### 2.8 Phylum Mollusca
- **Second largest** animal phylum.
- Terrestrial or aquatic; organ-system, bilateral, triploblastic, coelomate.
- Body covered by a **calcareous shell**; **unsegmented**, with distinct **head, muscular foot and visceral hump**.
- **Mantle** – soft spongy skin layer over the visceral hump. Space between hump and mantle = **mantle cavity**, containing **feather-like gills** (respiratory and excretory).
- Head has **sensory tentacles**.
- Mouth has a file-like rasping feeding organ – **radula**.
- Usually **dioecious**, **oviparous**, **indirect development**.
- Examples: ***Pila*** (apple snail), ***Pinctada*** (pearl oyster), ***Sepia*** (cuttlefish), ***Loligo*** (squid), ***Octopus*** (devil fish), ***Aplysia*** (sea hare), ***Dentalium*** (tusk shell), ***Chaetopleura*** (chiton).

> **Common Mistake:** Cuttlefish (*Sepia*), devil fish (*Octopus*), silverfish and starfish are NOT fishes. Cuttlefish and devil fish are molluscs; starfish is an echinoderm.

### 2.9 Phylum Echinodermata
- **Endoskeleton of calcareous ossicles** – "spiny bodied".
- **All marine**; organ-system level.
- **Adults radially symmetrical**, **larvae bilaterally symmetrical**.
- Triploblastic, coelomate.
- Complete digestive system: **mouth on lower (ventral) side**, **anus on upper (dorsal) side**.
- Most distinctive feature: **water vascular system** – helps in **locomotion, capture and transport of food and respiration**.
- **Excretory system absent**.
- Sexes separate; sexual reproduction; fertilisation usually **external**; development **indirect** with **free-swimming larva**.
- Examples: ***Asterias*** (star fish), ***Echinus*** (sea urchin), ***Antedon*** (sea lily), ***Cucumaria*** (sea cucumber), ***Ophiura*** (brittle star).

### 2.10 Phylum Hemichordata
- Earlier a sub-phylum of Chordata; **now a separate phylum under non-chordata**.
- Have a rudimentary structure in the collar region called **stomochord** (similar to notochord).
- Small group of **worm-like marine** animals; organ-system, bilateral, triploblastic, coelomate.
- Body cylindrical: **proboscis, collar and trunk**.
- **Open circulatory system**; respiration through **gills**; excretory organ = **proboscis gland**.
- Sexes separate; **external fertilisation**; indirect development.
- Examples: ***Balanoglossus***, ***Saccoglossus***.

## 3. Phylum Chordata

### 3.1 Fundamental characters
1. **Notochord**
2. **Dorsal hollow nerve cord**
3. **Paired pharyngeal gill slits**
- Also: bilateral, triploblastic, coelomate, organ-system level, **post-anal tail**, **closed circulatory system**.

### 3.2 Table 4.1 – Chordates vs Non-chordates

| No. | Chordates | Non-chordates |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Notochord present | Notochord absent |
| 2 | Central nervous system **dorsal, hollow and single** | CNS **ventral, solid and double** |
| 3 | Pharynx perforated by gill slits | Gill slits absent |
| 4 | Heart is **ventral** | Heart is **dorsal** (if present) |
| 5 | Post-anal part (tail) present | Post-anal tail absent |

### 3.3 Subphyla of Chordata
- **Urochordata (Tunicata)** and **Cephalochordata** = **protochordates**; exclusively **marine**.
  - **Urochordata:** notochord **only in larval tail**. Examples: ***Ascidia, Salpa, Doliolum***.
  - **Cephalochordata:** notochord extends **from head to tail**, **persistent throughout life**. Example: ***Branchiostoma*** (Amphioxus or lancelet).
- **Vertebrata:** notochord present during the **embryonic period**, replaced by a **cartilaginous or bony vertebral column** in adults.
  - **All vertebrates are chordates, but all chordates are not vertebrates.**
  - Vertebrates also have a **ventral muscular heart** with 2, 3 or 4 chambers, **kidneys** for excretion and osmoregulation, and **paired appendages** (fins or limbs).

### 3.4 Classification of Vertebrata

| Division | Super class | Classes |
|---|---|---|
| **Agnatha** (lacks jaw) | – | Cyclostomata |
| **Gnathostomata** (bears jaw) | **Pisces** (bear fins) | Chondrichthyes, Osteichthyes |
| **Gnathostomata** (bears jaw) | **Tetrapoda** (bear limbs) | Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves, Mammalia |

## 4. Classes of Vertebrates

### 4.1 Class Cyclostomata
- All living members are **ectoparasites on some fishes**.
- Elongated body with **6–15 pairs of gill slits**.
- **Sucking and circular mouth without jaws**.
- Body **devoid of scales and paired fins**.
- Cranium and vertebral column **cartilaginous**. Closed circulation.
- **Marine but migrate to fresh water for spawning**; die within a few days after spawning. Larvae, after metamorphosis, return to the ocean.
- Examples: ***Petromyzon*** (lamprey), ***Myxine*** (hagfish).

### 4.2 Class Chondrichthyes (cartilaginous fishes)
- **Marine**, streamlined body, **cartilaginous endoskeleton**.
- **Mouth ventral**. **Notochord persistent throughout life**.
- Gill slits **separate, without operculum**.
- Tough skin with minute **placoid scales**. **Teeth are modified placoid scales**, backwardly directed. Powerful jaws; predaceous.
- **No air bladder** → must swim constantly to avoid sinking.
- **Two-chambered heart** (1 auricle, 1 ventricle).
- **Electric organs** in ***Torpedo***; **poison sting** in ***Trygon***.
- **Cold-blooded (poikilothermous)**.
- Sexes separate; males have **claspers on pelvic fins**. **Internal fertilisation**; many are **viviparous**.
- Examples: ***Scoliodon*** (dog fish), ***Pristis*** (saw fish), ***Carcharodon*** (great white shark), ***Trygon*** (sting ray).

### 4.3 Class Osteichthyes (bony fishes)
- Marine and freshwater; **bony endoskeleton**; streamlined body.
- **Mouth mostly terminal**.
- **Four pairs of gills** covered by an **operculum** on each side.
- Skin with **cycloid/ctenoid scales**.
- **Air bladder present** – regulates **buoyancy**.
- Two-chambered heart; cold-blooded.
- Sexes separate; fertilisation usually **external**; mostly **oviparous**; development **direct**.
- Examples:
  - Marine: ***Exocoetus*** (flying fish), ***Hippocampus*** (sea horse)
  - Freshwater: ***Labeo*** (rohu), ***Catla*** (katla), ***Clarias*** (magur)
  - Aquarium: ***Betta*** (fighting fish), ***Pterophyllum*** (angel fish)

### 4.4 Class Amphibia
- *Amphi* = dual, *bios* = life. Live in **aquatic and terrestrial** habitats.
- Most have **two pairs of limbs**. Body = head + trunk; tail in some.
- Skin **moist, without scales**. Eyes have **eyelids**. **Tympanum** represents the ear.
- Alimentary canal, urinary and reproductive tracts open into a common chamber, **cloaca**.
- Respiration by **gills, lungs and skin**.
- **Three-chambered heart** (2 auricles, 1 ventricle). Cold-blooded.
- Sexes separate; **external fertilisation**; oviparous; **indirect development**.
- Examples: ***Bufo*** (toad), ***Rana*** (frog), ***Hyla*** (tree frog), ***Salamandra*** (salamander), ***Ichthyophis*** (limbless amphibian).

### 4.5 Class Reptilia
- Latin *repere* = to creep or crawl. Mostly **terrestrial**.
- Body covered by **dry and cornified skin, epidermal scales or scutes**.
- **No external ear openings**; tympanum represents ear.
- Limbs, when present, two pairs.
- Heart **usually three-chambered**, but **four-chambered in crocodiles**.
- Poikilotherms. Snakes and lizards shed scales as **skin cast**.
- Sexes separate; **internal fertilisation**; oviparous; **direct development**.
- Examples: ***Chelone*** (turtle), ***Testudo*** (tortoise), ***Chameleon*** (tree lizard), ***Calotes*** (garden lizard), ***Crocodilus*** (crocodile), ***Alligator***, ***Hemidactylus*** (wall lizard); poisonous snakes ***Naja*** (cobra), ***Bangarus*** (krait), ***Vipera*** (viper).

### 4.6 Class Aves (birds)
- Presence of **feathers**; most can fly except **flightless birds** (e.g., ostrich). Possess a **beak**.
- **Forelimbs modified into wings**. Hind limbs have scales; adapted for walking, swimming or clasping branches.
- Skin **dry, without glands** except the **oil gland at the base of the tail**.
- Endoskeleton fully **ossified**; long bones **hollow with air cavities (pneumatic)**.
- Digestive tract has additional chambers – **crop and gizzard**.
- Heart **completely four-chambered**.
- **Warm-blooded (homoiothermous)**.
- Respiration by lungs; **air sacs** connected to lungs supplement respiration.
- Sexes separate; internal fertilisation; oviparous; direct development.
- Examples: ***Corvus*** (crow), ***Columba*** (pigeon), ***Psittacula*** (parrot), ***Struthio*** (ostrich), ***Pavo*** (peacock), ***Aptenodytes*** (penguin), ***Neophron*** (vulture).

### 4.7 Class Mammalia
- Found in all habitats – polar ice caps, deserts, mountains, forests, grasslands, dark caves. Some fly or live in water.
- **Most unique feature: mammary glands** (milk-producing) to nourish the young.
- Two pairs of limbs adapted for walking, running, climbing, burrowing, swimming or flying.
- Skin has **hair**. **External ears (pinnae)** present.
- **Different types of teeth** in the jaw (heterodont).
- Four-chambered heart; homoiothermous; lungs.
- Sexes separate; internal fertilisation; **viviparous with few exceptions**; direct development.
- Examples:
  - **Oviparous:** ***Ornithorhynchus*** (platypus)
  - **Viviparous:** ***Macropus*** (kangaroo), ***Pteropus*** (flying fox), ***Camelus*** (camel), ***Macaca*** (monkey), ***Rattus*** (rat), ***Canis*** (dog), ***Felis*** (cat), ***Elephas*** (elephant), ***Equus*** (horse), ***Delphinus*** (common dolphin), ***Balaenoptera*** (blue whale), ***Panthera tigris*** (tiger), ***Panthera leo*** (lion)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Habitat | Marine (spawn in fresh water) | Marine | Marine and freshwater | Water and land | Mostly terrestrial | Air/land | All habitats |
| Skin covering | No scales | Placoid scales | Cycloid/ctenoid scales | Moist, no scales | Dry, cornified scales/scutes | Feathers | Hair |
| Endoskeleton | Cartilaginous | Cartilaginous | Bony | Bony | Bony | Bony, pneumatic | Bony |
| Heart chambers | – | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 (4 in crocodile) | 4 | 4 |

| Special feature | Jawless, circular mouth; 6–15 pairs gill slits | No air bladder; claspers; no operculum | Air bladder; operculum; 4 pairs of gills | Cloaca; tympanum; 3 modes of respiration | No external ear; skin cast | Air sacs, crop, gizzard, oil gland | Mammary glands, pinnae |

> **PYQ Trend:** Frequently asked from Chapter 4:
> - Match the animal to its phylum or class (e.g., *Pteropus* – mammal; *Hippocampus* – bony fish; *Ichthyophis* – limbless amphibian; *Limulus* – living fossil arthropod; *Balanoglossus* – Hemichordata).
> - Features: flame cells (Platyhelminthes), nephridia (Annelida), Malpighian tubules (Arthropoda), water vascular system (Echinodermata), radula (Mollusca), comb plates and bioluminescence (Ctenophora), choanocytes (Porifera), cnidoblasts (Coelenterata), proboscis gland (Hemichordata).
> - Exceptions: four-chambered heart in crocodile; oviparous mammal *Ornithorhynchus*; excretory system absent in echinoderms; air bladder absent in cartilaginous fishes.
> - Metagenesis in *Obelia*; sexual dimorphism in *Ascaris*; bilateral larva but radial adult in echinoderms.

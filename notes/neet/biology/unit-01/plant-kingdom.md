## 1. Plant Kingdom – Classification Systems

| System | Basis | Given by / Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Artificial** | Gross superficial morphology: habit, colour, number and shape of leaves; mainly vegetative characters or **androecium structure** | **Linnaeus**. Separated closely related species; gave equal weight to vegetative and sexual characters (not acceptable, since vegetative characters are easily affected by environment) |
| **Natural** | Natural affinities; external **and internal** features – ultrastructure, anatomy, embryology, phytochemistry | **George Bentham and Joseph Dalton Hooker** (for flowering plants) |
| **Phylogenetic** | Evolutionary relationships; assumes members of a taxon have a common ancestor | Currently accepted |

**Other modern approaches:**
- **Numerical taxonomy** – uses computers; based on **all observable characteristics**; numbers and codes assigned to characters; **each character given equal importance**; hundreds of characters considered.
- **Cytotaxonomy** – based on cytological information: **chromosome number, structure, behaviour**.
- **Chemotaxonomy** – uses the **chemical constituents** of the plant.

> **Note:** Fungi and the members of Monera and Protista that have cell walls are now excluded from Plantae. So cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) are not "algae" any more.

## 2. Algae

### 2.1 General features
- Chlorophyll-bearing, simple, **thalloid**, autotrophic, largely **aquatic** (fresh water and marine).
- Also found on moist stones, soils and wood; with fungi (**lichen**) and with animals (e.g., on **sloth bear**).
- Form and size vary: colonial (***Volvox***), filamentous (***Ulothrix***, ***Spirogyra***); marine **kelps** form massive plant bodies.

### 2.2 Reproduction in algae
- **Vegetative:** fragmentation – each fragment develops into a thallus.
- **Asexual:** spores, most commonly **zoospores** (flagellated, motile).
- **Sexual:** fusion of two gametes.

| Type | Gametes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Isogamous** | Similar in size; flagellated | *Ulothrix* |
| **Isogamous** | Similar in size; **non-flagellated (non-motile)** | *Spirogyra* |
| **Anisogamous** | Dissimilar in size | *Eudorina* |
| **Oogamous** | Large non-motile female gamete + small motile male gamete | *Volvox*, *Fucus* |

### 2.3 Economic importance of algae
- At least **half of the total CO<sub>2</sub> fixation** on Earth is by algae through photosynthesis.
- Increase dissolved oxygen in their environment.
- **Primary producers** – basis of food cycles of all aquatic animals.
- **Food:** 70 species of marine algae used as food, e.g., *Porphyra*, *Laminaria*, *Sargassum*.
- **Hydrocolloids** (water-holding substances): **algin** (from brown algae) and **carrageen** (from red algae).
- **Agar** – from ***Gelidium*** and ***Gracilaria*** (red algae); used to grow microbes and in ice-creams and jellies.
- ***Chlorella*** – unicellular alga rich in **proteins**; used as food supplement even by **space travellers**.

### 2.4 Classes of algae – Table 3.1 (the most asked table in plant kingdom)

| Feature | Chlorophyceae | Phaeophyceae | Rhodophyceae |
|---|---|---|---|
| Common name | Green algae | Brown algae | Red algae |
| Major pigments | Chlorophyll **a, b** | Chlorophyll **a, c**, **fucoxanthin** | Chlorophyll **a, d**, **phycoerythrin** |
| Stored food | **Starch** | **Mannitol, laminarin** | **Floridean starch** |
| Cell wall | Cellulose | **Cellulose and algin** | Cellulose, pectin and **polysulphate esters** |
| Flagella – number and position | **2–8, equal, apical** | **2, unequal, lateral** | **Absent** |
| Habitat | Fresh water, brackish, salt water | Fresh water (rare), brackish, salt water | Fresh water (some), brackish, salt water (most) |

### 2.5 Chlorophyceae (green algae)
- Plant body unicellular, colonial or filamentous.
- Grass green due to dominance of chlorophyll a and b, localised in definite chloroplasts.
- **Chloroplast shapes:** discoid, plate-like, reticulate, **cup-shaped** (*Chlamydomonas*), **spiral** (*Spirogyra*), ribbon-shaped.
- **Pyrenoids** – storage bodies in chloroplasts; contain **protein besides starch**. Some store oil droplets.
- Cell wall: **inner layer cellulose**, **outer layer pectose**.
- Reproduction: fragmentation; asexual by **flagellated zoospores** in zoosporangia; sexual is isogamous, anisogamous or oogamous.
- Examples: ***Chlamydomonas, Volvox, Ulothrix, Spirogyra, Chara***.

### 2.6 Phaeophyceae (brown algae)
- Primarily **marine**.
- Range from simple branched filaments (***Ectocarpus***) to profusely branched **kelps** (may reach **100 metres**).
- Pigments: chlorophyll a, c, carotenoids and xanthophylls. Colour olive green to brown depending on the xanthophyll **fucoxanthin**.
- Food stored as complex carbohydrates – **laminarin or mannitol**.
- Cellulosic wall covered on the outside by gelatinous **algin**.
- Protoplast has plastids, a centrally located vacuole and nucleus.
- Plant body: **holdfast** (attachment), **stipe** (stalk) and **frond** (leaf-like photosynthetic organ).
- Asexual reproduction by **biflagellate zoospores** that are **pear-shaped** with **two unequal laterally attached flagella**.
- Sexual: isogamous, anisogamous or oogamous. Union of gametes in water or within the **oogonium** (oogamous species). Gametes are **pyriform** with two lateral flagella.
- Examples: ***Ectocarpus, Dictyota, Laminaria, Sargassum, Fucus***.

### 2.7 Rhodophyceae (red algae)
- Red due to **r-phycoerythrin**.
- Mostly **marine**, more in **warmer** areas. Found both near the surface and at **great depths** where little light penetrates.
- Thalli mostly multicellular; some have complex organisation.
- Food: **floridean starch** – very similar to **amylopectin and glycogen** in structure.
- Vegetative: fragmentation; asexual: **non-motile spores**; sexual: **non-motile gametes**, **oogamous**, with **complex post-fertilisation developments**.
- Examples: ***Polysiphonia, Porphyra, Gracilaria, Gelidium***.

> **Remember – algae examples by class:**
> - Green: *Chlamydomonas, Volvox, Ulothrix, Spirogyra, Chara*
> - Brown: *Ectocarpus, Dictyota, Laminaria, Sargassum, Fucus*
> - Red: *Polysiphonia, Porphyra, Gracilaria, Gelidium*
> - *Porphyra*, *Laminaria*, *Sargassum* = food. Algin = brown. Carrageen and agar = red.

## 3. Bryophytes

### 3.1 General features
- Include **mosses and liverworts**; grow in **moist shaded areas in the hills**.
- Called **"amphibians of the plant kingdom"** – live on soil but **depend on water for sexual reproduction**.
- Play an important role in **plant succession on bare rocks/soil**.
- Plant body more differentiated than algae; thallus-like, prostrate or erect; attached by **unicellular or multicellular rhizoids**.
- **Lack true roots, stem and leaves** (have root-like, stem-like, leaf-like structures).
- **Main plant body is haploid** and produces gametes → it is the **gametophyte** (dominant phase).
- Sex organs are **multicellular**:
  - Male: **antheridium** → **biflagellate antherozoids**
  - Female: **archegonium** – **flask-shaped**, produces a **single egg**
- Antherozoids are released into **water** and reach the archegonium; fusion with egg → **zygote**.
- Zygote does **not undergo meiosis immediately**. It forms a multicellular **sporophyte**.
- The sporophyte is **not free-living** – it is **attached to and nourished by the photosynthetic gametophyte**.
- Some cells of the sporophyte undergo **meiosis** → haploid **spores** → germinate into gametophytes.

### 3.2 Economic and ecological importance
- Some mosses are food for herbaceous mammals, birds and other animals.
- ***Sphagnum*** (moss) provides **peat** – used as **fuel** and as **packing material** for trans-shipment of living material (due to its **water-holding capacity**).
- Mosses with lichens are the **first organisms to colonise rocks** (pioneers); they decompose rocks and make the substrate suitable for higher plants.
- Mosses form dense mats on soil – reduce the impact of falling rain and **prevent soil erosion**.

### 3.3 Liverworts vs Mosses

| Feature | Liverworts | Mosses |
|---|---|---|
| Habitat | Moist shady places – banks of streams, marshy ground, damp soil, tree bark, deep woods | Moist shaded areas |
| Gametophyte | **Thalloid**, **dorsiventral**, closely appressed to substrate (e.g., *Marchantia*). Leafy members have tiny leaf-like appendages in **two rows** | Two stages: (1) **Protonema** – creeping, green, branched, filamentous, develops directly from spore; (2) **Leafy stage** – from **secondary protonema** as lateral bud; upright slender axes with **spirally arranged leaves**; multicellular branched rhizoids; bears sex organs |
| Asexual / vegetative reproduction | Fragmentation or **gemmae** – green, multicellular asexual buds in **gemma cups** on thalli | Fragmentation and **budding in secondary protonema** |
| Sex organs | On same or different thalli | Antheridia and archegonia at the **apex of leafy shoots** |
| Sporophyte | Foot, seta and capsule | Foot, seta and capsule – **more elaborate** than liverworts; elaborate mechanism of spore dispersal |
| Examples | *Marchantia* | *Funaria, Polytrichum, Sphagnum* |

> **Common Mistake:** In bryophytes the **gametophyte is dominant and independent**; the sporophyte is dependent on it. In pteridophytes and seed plants, the **sporophyte is dominant**. Gemmae are asexual (not spores, not gametes).

## 4. Pteridophytes

### 4.1 General features
- Include **horsetails and ferns**. Used as medicine, soil-binders and ornamentals.
- **First terrestrial plants to possess vascular tissues – xylem and phloem.**
- Found in cool, damp, shady places; some in sandy soil.
- **Main plant body is the sporophyte** – differentiated into **true root, stem and leaves** with well-differentiated vascular tissues.
- Leaves: **microphylls** (small, e.g., ***Selaginella***) or **macrophylls** (large, e.g., **ferns**).
- Sporophytes bear **sporangia** subtended by leaf-like appendages called **sporophylls**.
- Sporophylls may form compact structures called **strobili or cones** (***Selaginella***, ***Equisetum***).
- Spores are produced by **meiosis** in spore mother cells.
- Spores germinate into small, multicellular, free-living, mostly photosynthetic, thalloid gametophytes called **prothallus**. These need cool, damp, shady places.
- Because of this restricted requirement and need for **water for fertilisation**, living pteridophytes are limited to **narrow geographical regions**.
- Gametophytes bear **antheridia and archegonia**. Water carries **antherozoids** to the mouth of the archegonium. Fusion with egg → zygote → multicellular sporophyte (dominant phase).

### 4.2 Homospory and heterospory
- **Homosporous:** all spores of one kind (majority of pteridophytes).
- **Heterosporous:** two kinds of spores – **megaspores (large)** and **microspores (small)**. Examples: ***Selaginella*** and ***Salvinia***.
  - Megaspores → female gametophyte; microspores → male gametophyte.
  - Female gametophyte is **retained on the parent sporophyte** for variable periods.
  - Zygote develops into a young embryo **within the female gametophyte**.
  - This is a **precursor to the seed habit** – an important step in evolution.

### 4.3 Classes of pteridophytes

| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| Psilopsida | *Psilotum* |
| Lycopsida | *Selaginella*, *Lycopodium* |
| Sphenopsida | *Equisetum* (horsetail) |
| Pteropsida | *Dryopteris, Pteris, Adiantum* (ferns) |

> **PYQ Trend:** Heterospory in *Selaginella* and *Salvinia* and its role as the "precursor of seed habit" is one of the most repeated NEET questions from this chapter.

## 5. Gymnosperms

### 5.1 General features
- *Gymnos* = naked, *sperma* = seeds. **Ovules are not enclosed by any ovary wall** and remain exposed before and after fertilisation. Seeds are naked.
- Medium or tall trees and shrubs. Giant redwood ***Sequoia*** is one of the **tallest tree species**.
- Roots are generally **tap roots**.
  - ***Pinus*** – fungal association as **mycorrhiza**.
  - ***Cycas*** – small specialised **coralloid roots** associated with **N<sub>2</sub>-fixing cyanobacteria**.
- Stems: unbranched (***Cycas***) or branched (***Pinus***, ***Cedrus***).
- Leaves simple or compound; in *Cycas* **pinnate leaves persist for a few years**.
- Leaves adapted to extremes of temperature, humidity and wind. **Conifers** have **needle-like leaves** (reduce surface area), **thick cuticle** and **sunken stomata** (reduce water loss).

### 5.2 Reproduction
- **Heterosporous** – haploid microspores and megaspores, produced in sporangia on **sporophylls** arranged spirally to form **strobili or cones**.
- **Male strobili** (microsporangiate) bear microsporophylls and microsporangia. Microspores → **highly reduced male gametophyte** of a few cells = **pollen grain**, formed within microsporangia.
- **Female strobili** (macrosporangiate) bear megasporophylls with **ovules** (megasporangia).
- Male and female cones on the **same tree in *Pinus*** (monoecious); in ***Cycas*** male cones and megasporophylls are on **different trees** (dioecious).
- **Ovule** = nucellus + protective envelopes (integuments). **Megaspore mother cell** differentiates from one cell of the **nucellus**, divides meiotically → 4 megaspores. One develops into a **multicellular female gametophyte** bearing **two or more archegonia**. It is retained within the megasporangium.
- Unlike bryophytes and pteridophytes, **male and female gametophytes have no independent free-living existence** in gymnosperms.
- **Pollination by air currents** (wind). The **pollen tube** carries male gametes to the archegonia. After fertilisation, zygote → embryo; ovule → **seed** (not covered).

> **Key Point:** Examples in NCERT figures: *Cycas*, *Pinus*, *Ginkgo*. Other names in text: *Sequoia*, *Cedrus*. Gymnosperms do NOT need water for fertilisation – the pollen tube delivers the male gametes.

## 6. Angiosperms (brief)
- Pollen grains and ovules develop in specialised structures called **flowers**; **seeds are enclosed in fruits**.
- Very large group, wide range of habitats.
- Size range: smallest ***Wolffia*** to tall ***Eucalyptus*** trees (over **100 metres**).
- Provide food, fodder, fuel, medicines and commercial products.
- Two classes: **dicotyledons** and **monocotyledons**.

### 6.1 Comparison across plant groups (quick revision)

| Feature | Bryophytes | Pteridophytes | Gymnosperms | Angiosperms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dominant phase | Gametophyte (n) | Sporophyte (2n) | Sporophyte (2n) | Sporophyte (2n) |
| Vascular tissue | Absent | Present (first) | Present | Present |
| True roots, stem, leaves | Absent | Present | Present | Present |
| Water for fertilisation | Needed | Needed | Not needed (pollen tube) | Not needed (pollen tube) |
| Seeds | No | No (heterospory is precursor) | Naked seeds | Seeds enclosed in fruit |
| Gametophyte | Free-living, dominant | Free-living prothallus | Dependent, reduced | Highly reduced, dependent |

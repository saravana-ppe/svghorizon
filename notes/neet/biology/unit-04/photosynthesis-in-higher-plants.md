## 1. Photosynthesis – Introduction

- Green plants synthesise their food through photosynthesis and are called **autotrophs**. All organisms that depend on green plants are **heterotrophs**.
- **Photosynthesis** – a **physico-chemical process** by which plants use **light energy** to drive the synthesis of organic compounds.
- Importance:
  1. **Primary source of all food** on earth.
  2. Responsible for **release of oxygen** into the atmosphere.
- Simple experiments show that **chlorophyll, light and CO<sub>2</sub>** are required:
  - **Variegated leaf / partially covered leaf** – starch forms only in **green parts** exposed to **light**.
  - **Half-leaf experiment with KOH** – the part of the leaf enclosed in a tube with **KOH-soaked cotton (absorbs CO<sub>2</sub>)** tests **negative for starch**; the exposed part tests positive → **CO<sub>2</sub> is required**.

## 2. Early Experiments (scientists and discoveries)

| Scientist | Experiment / contribution |
|---|---|
| **Joseph Priestley (1770)** | Candle in a closed bell jar gets extinguished; mouse suffocates. With a **mint plant** in the bell jar, the mouse stayed alive and the candle burned. Hypothesis: **plants restore to the air whatever breathing animals and burning candles remove**. (He discovered **oxygen in 1774**) |
| **Jan Ingenhousz (1730–1799)** | Same setup in dark and in sunlight → **sunlight is essential**. With an **aquatic plant**: bubbles (later identified as **oxygen**) formed around **green parts** in bright sunlight, not in dark → **only green parts release oxygen** |
| **Julius von Sachs (1854)** | Evidence for **production of glucose** when plants grow; glucose usually stored as **starch**. Green substance (chlorophyll) located in special bodies (**chloroplasts**) |
| **T.W. Engelmann (1843–1909)** | Split light with a **prism**; illuminated green alga ***Cladophora*** in a suspension of **aerobic bacteria**. Bacteria accumulated mainly in **blue and red light** regions. Described the **first action spectrum of photosynthesis**; resembles absorption spectra of chlorophyll a and b |
| **Cornelius van Niel (1897–1985)** | Studied **purple and green bacteria**. Showed photosynthesis is a light-dependent reaction in which **hydrogen from an oxidisable compound reduces CO<sub>2</sub>**: 2H<sub>2</sub>A + CO<sub>2</sub> → 2A + CH<sub>2</sub>O + H<sub>2</sub>O. In green plants H<sub>2</sub>O is the hydrogen donor → **O<sub>2</sub> evolved comes from water, not CO<sub>2</sub>** (later proved by radioisotopes). In purple and green sulphur bacteria, **H<sub>2</sub>S** is the donor and the product is sulphur/sulphate, not O<sub>2</sub> |
| **Melvin Calvin** (with J.A. Bassham) | Used radioactive **<sup>14</sup>C** in algal photosynthesis; first CO<sub>2</sub> fixation product = **3-carbon PGA**; worked out the **Calvin cycle**. **Nobel Prize 1961** |

**Equations:**
- Empirical (mid-19th century): CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O → [CH<sub>2</sub>O] + O<sub>2</sub> (in light)
- **Correct overall equation:** **6CO<sub>2</sub> + 12H<sub>2</sub>O → C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub> + 6H<sub>2</sub>O + 6O<sub>2</sub>** (in light)
- 12 molecules of water are used because all **12 O atoms of 6O<sub>2</sub> come from water**. It is a multistep process, not a single reaction.

> **PYQ Trend:** "Who showed that O<sub>2</sub> comes from water?" – van Niel (inference; confirmed by radioisotopes). "First action spectrum?" – Engelmann with *Cladophora*. "Plants restore air?" – Priestley with mint.

## 3. Site of Photosynthesis

- Takes place in **green leaves** and also in **other green parts** of the plant.
- **Mesophyll cells** have a large number of **chloroplasts**; chloroplasts align along the walls of mesophyll cells to get optimum light.
- Chloroplast: membranous system of **grana, stroma lamellae** and the matrix **stroma**.
- **Division of labour** within the chloroplast:

| Part | Reactions | Name |
|---|---|---|
| **Membrane system** (grana, thylakoids) | **Trapping light energy**; synthesis of **ATP and NADPH** | **Light reactions (photochemical reactions)** |
| **Stroma** | **Enzymatic reactions** synthesise sugar, which forms starch | **Dark reactions (carbon reactions / biosynthetic phase)** – not directly light-driven but depend on ATP and NADPH |

> **Common Mistake:** "Dark reaction" does NOT mean it happens in darkness or is light-independent. It depends on the products of light reactions. After light is removed, the biosynthetic process continues for some time and then stops – so the name is a misnomer.

## 4. Photosynthetic Pigments

- **Paper chromatography** of leaf pigments shows **four pigments**:

| Pigment | Colour in chromatogram |
|---|---|
| **Chlorophyll a** | **Bright or blue green** |
| **Chlorophyll b** | **Yellow green** |
| **Xanthophylls** | **Yellow** |
| **Carotenoids** | **Yellow to yellow-orange** |

- **Pigments** – substances that absorb light at specific wavelengths.
- **Chlorophyll a** shows maximum absorption in the **blue and red** regions; these also show higher rates of photosynthesis → **chlorophyll a is the chief pigment** associated with photosynthesis (and the most abundant plant pigment in the world).
- **Absorption spectrum** of chlorophyll a and **action spectrum** of photosynthesis overlap closely but **not one-to-one**.
- Most photosynthesis occurs in **blue and red** regions; some also at other wavelengths.
- **Accessory pigments** – **chlorophyll b, xanthophylls and carotenoids**:
  - Absorb light and **transfer energy to chlorophyll a**
  - Enable a **wider range of wavelengths** to be used
  - **Protect chlorophyll a from photo-oxidation**

## 5. Light Reaction (Photochemical Phase)

- Includes: **light absorption, water splitting, oxygen release, formation of ATP and NADPH**.
- Pigments are organised into two **light harvesting complexes (LHC)** within **Photosystem I (PS I)** and **Photosystem II (PS II)**.
- Photosystems are named **in the sequence of their discovery**, **not in the sequence in which they function**.
- LHC = hundreds of pigment molecules bound to proteins.
- Each photosystem has all pigments (except **one molecule of chlorophyll a**) forming a light harvesting system called **antennae**. These absorb different wavelengths, making photosynthesis efficient.
- The single chlorophyll a molecule forms the **reaction centre**.

| Photosystem | Reaction centre | Absorption peak |
|---|---|---|
| **PS I** | **P700** | **700 nm** |
| **PS II** | **P680** | **680 nm** |

## 6. Electron Transport – The Z Scheme

1. In **PS II**, reaction centre chlorophyll a absorbs **680 nm red light**; electrons get **excited** and jump to an orbit farther from the nucleus.
2. Electrons are picked up by an **electron acceptor**, which passes them to an **electron transport system of cytochromes**. This movement is **downhill** in terms of redox potential.
3. Electrons are **not used up**; they are passed on to the pigments of **PS I**.
4. Simultaneously, electrons in the **PS I** reaction centre are excited by **700 nm red light** and transferred to another acceptor with greater redox potential.
5. These electrons move **downhill** again to **NADP<sup>+</sup>**, reducing it to **NADPH + H<sup>+</sup>**.
- The whole scheme (PS II → uphill to acceptor → down ETC to PS I → excitation → acceptor → downhill to NADP<sup>+</sup>) is called the **Z scheme** because of its shape when carriers are placed on a **redox potential scale**.

### 6.1 Splitting of water (photolysis)
- Electrons removed from PS II are **replaced by electrons from water splitting**.
- Water splitting is **associated with PS II**.
- **2H<sub>2</sub>O → 4H<sup>+</sup> + O<sub>2</sub> + 4e<sup>−</sup>**
- This creates **oxygen** – one of the net products of photosynthesis.
- Electrons needed to replace those removed from PS I are provided by PS II.
- The **water-splitting complex** is associated with PS II, which is physically located on the **inner side of the thylakoid membrane** → protons and O<sub>2</sub> are released **into the lumen**.

### 6.2 Cyclic and non-cyclic photophosphorylation
- **Phosphorylation** – process by which ATP is synthesised (in mitochondria and chloroplasts).
- **Photophosphorylation** – synthesis of ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate **in the presence of light**.

| Feature | Non-cyclic | Cyclic |
|---|---|---|
| Photosystems | **PS II and PS I in series** (first PS II, then PS I) | **Only PS I** |
| Electron flow | One direction, from water to NADP<sup>+</sup> (Z scheme) | Electron **cycled back to PS I** through the ETC |
| Products | **ATP + NADPH + H<sup>+</sup>** (and O<sub>2</sub> from water splitting) | **Only ATP** (no NADPH, no O<sub>2</sub>) |
| Location | **Grana lamellae** (have both PS I and PS II) | Possibly **stroma lamellae** – which **lack PS II and NADP reductase** |
| When | Normal conditions | Also occurs when only light of wavelengths **beyond 680 nm** is available |

> **PYQ Trend:** Stroma lamellae lack PS II and NADP reductase → cyclic flow → only ATP. Water splitting is linked to PS II. P680 = PS II, P700 = PS I. These are among the most repeated photosynthesis questions.

## 7. Chemiosmotic Hypothesis (ATP synthesis in chloroplast)

- ATP synthesis is linked to development of a **proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane**.
- **Difference from respiration:** in chloroplasts, protons accumulate **inside the thylakoid (lumen)**; in mitochondria, protons accumulate in the **intermembrane space**.

### 7.1 Three causes of the proton gradient
1. **Splitting of water** occurs on the **inner side** of the membrane → protons accumulate in the **lumen**.
2. As electrons move through the photosystems, the **primary acceptor** (on the **outer side** of the membrane) transfers its electron to an **H carrier** (not an electron carrier). It removes a **proton from the stroma** while transporting an electron; when it passes the electron to a carrier on the inner side, the proton is **released into the lumen**.
3. **NADP reductase** is on the **stroma side**; reduction of NADP<sup>+</sup> to NADPH + H<sup>+</sup> uses protons **from the stroma**.
- Result: **protons decrease in stroma, accumulate in lumen** → proton gradient + measurable **decrease in pH in the lumen**.

### 7.2 ATP synthase
- The **breakdown of the gradient** leads to ATP synthesis.
- Protons move back from lumen to stroma through the **transmembrane channel CF<sub>0</sub>** of ATP synthase.

| Part | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **CF<sub>0</sub>** | **Embedded** in thylakoid membrane | Transmembrane channel; **facilitated diffusion of protons** |
| **CF<sub>1</sub>** | Protrudes on the **outer surface** facing the **stroma** | Energy from gradient breakdown causes a **conformational change** in CF<sub>1</sub> → synthesises ATP |

- **Chemiosmosis requires:** a **membrane**, a **proton pump**, a **proton gradient** and **ATP synthase**.
- ATP and NADPH are used immediately in the **stroma** for CO<sub>2</sub> fixation and sugar synthesis.

## 8. Biosynthetic Phase – Where are ATP and NADPH Used?

- Products of light reaction: **ATP, NADPH and O<sub>2</sub>**. O<sub>2</sub> diffuses out; ATP and NADPH drive sugar synthesis.
- **Calvin's use of <sup>14</sup>C** showed the **first CO<sub>2</sub> fixation product** is a **3-carbon acid – 3-phosphoglyceric acid (PGA)**.
- In another group of plants, the first stable product is a **4-carbon acid – oxaloacetic acid (OAA)**.
  - **C<sub>3</sub> pathway** – first product PGA (C<sub>3</sub> acid)
  - **C<sub>4</sub> pathway** – first product OAA (C<sub>4</sub> acid)

### 8.1 Primary acceptor of CO<sub>2</sub>
- Scientists expected a 2-carbon acceptor (since product was C<sub>3</sub>), and spent years searching.
- The acceptor is a **5-carbon ketose sugar – ribulose bisphosphate (RuBP)**.

### 8.2 The Calvin cycle
- Operates in a **cyclic** manner; RuBP is regenerated.
- **Occurs in all photosynthetic plants** – C<sub>3</sub>, C<sub>4</sub> or any other.
- Three stages:

| Stage | Events |
|---|---|
| **1. Carboxylation** | **Fixation of CO<sub>2</sub>** into a stable organic intermediate – **most crucial step**. CO<sub>2</sub> + RuBP → **2 molecules of 3-PGA**, catalysed by **RuBP carboxylase**. Since it also has oxygenation activity → **RuBP carboxylase-oxygenase (RuBisCO)** |
| **2. Reduction** | Series of reactions leading to glucose. Uses **2 ATP** (phosphorylation) and **2 NADPH** (reduction) **per CO<sub>2</sub> fixed**. **6 CO<sub>2</sub> and 6 turns** needed for **one glucose** |
| **3. Regeneration** | Regeneration of CO<sub>2</sub> acceptor **RuBP**; needs **1 ATP** per CO<sub>2</sub> for phosphorylation to form RuBP |

- **Per CO<sub>2</sub> fixed: 3 ATP + 2 NADPH**. The extra ATP need is probably met by **cyclic photophosphorylation**.

| In | Out |
|---|---|
| **6 CO<sub>2</sub>** | **1 glucose** |
| **18 ATP** | 18 ADP |
| **12 NADPH** | 12 NADP |

> **Exam Tip:** For one glucose: 6 turns × 3 ATP = 18 ATP; 6 × 2 NADPH = 12 NADPH. For one CO<sub>2</sub>: 3 ATP + 2 NADPH. NEET asks both versions.

## 9. The C<sub>4</sub> Pathway (Hatch and Slack Pathway)

### 9.1 Features of C<sub>4</sub> plants
- Adapted to **dry tropical regions**.
- First CO<sub>2</sub> fixation product is **OAA (C<sub>4</sub>)**, but they still use the **Calvin cycle** as the main biosynthetic pathway.
- Special because they:
  - Have a special leaf anatomy – **Kranz anatomy**
  - **Tolerate higher temperatures**
  - Respond to **high light intensities**
  - **Lack photorespiration**
  - Have **greater productivity of biomass**

### 9.2 Kranz anatomy
- "Kranz" = **wreath** (arrangement of cells).
- Particularly large cells around the vascular bundles = **bundle sheath cells**.
- Bundle sheath cells: may form several layers; have **large number of chloroplasts**, **thick walls impervious to gaseous exchange**, **no intercellular spaces**.
- Examples of C<sub>4</sub> plants: **maize, sorghum**.

### 9.3 Steps of the Hatch and Slack pathway
1. Primary CO<sub>2</sub> acceptor = **3-carbon phosphoenol pyruvate (PEP)**, in **mesophyll cells**.
2. Enzyme = **PEP carboxylase (PEPcase)**. **Mesophyll cells lack RuBisCO**.
3. **OAA (C<sub>4</sub>)** formed in mesophyll cells.
4. OAA forms other 4-carbon compounds – **malic acid or aspartic acid** – in mesophyll, which are transported to **bundle sheath cells**.
5. In bundle sheath cells, C<sub>4</sub> acids are broken down to release **CO<sub>2</sub> and a 3-carbon molecule**.
6. The 3-carbon molecule returns to mesophyll and is converted to **PEP** again – cycle complete.
7. CO<sub>2</sub> released in bundle sheath enters the **Calvin cycle**. Bundle sheath cells are **rich in RuBisCO but lack PEPcase**.

- In **C<sub>3</sub> plants**, the Calvin pathway occurs in **all mesophyll cells**. In **C<sub>4</sub> plants**, it occurs **only in bundle sheath cells**.

## 10. Photorespiration

- RuBisCO is the **most abundant enzyme in the world**. Its active site can bind **both CO<sub>2</sub> and O<sub>2</sub>** (hence the name).
- RuBisCO has **much greater affinity for CO<sub>2</sub>** when CO<sub>2</sub>:O<sub>2</sub> is nearly equal. **Binding is competitive**; relative concentration determines which binds.
- In **C<sub>3</sub> plants**, some O<sub>2</sub> binds RuBisCO → CO<sub>2</sub> fixation decreases.
- RuBP + O<sub>2</sub> → **one molecule of phosphoglycerate + phosphoglycolate (2C)** – this pathway is **photorespiration**.
- In photorespiration:
  - **No synthesis of sugars, ATP or NADPH**
  - **Release of CO<sub>2</sub> with utilisation of ATP**
  - **Biological function is not known yet** (wasteful process)
- **C<sub>4</sub> plants lack photorespiration** because they **increase CO<sub>2</sub> concentration at the enzyme site** (C<sub>4</sub> acid broken down in bundle sheath releases CO<sub>2</sub>). RuBisCO then acts as a **carboxylase**, minimising oxygenase activity.
- Hence C<sub>4</sub> plants have **better productivity and yields** and tolerate **higher temperatures**.

### 10.1 C<sub>3</sub> vs C<sub>4</sub> plants (completed NCERT Table 11.1)

| Characteristic | C<sub>3</sub> plants | C<sub>4</sub> plants |
|---|---|---|
| Cell type where Calvin cycle occurs | Mesophyll | **Bundle sheath** |
| Cell type of initial carboxylation | Mesophyll | Mesophyll |
| Number of cell types that fix CO<sub>2</sub> | One: mesophyll | **Two: bundle sheath and mesophyll** |
| Primary CO<sub>2</sub> acceptor | **RuBP** | **PEP** |
| Carbons in primary acceptor | **5** | **3** |
| Primary CO<sub>2</sub> fixation product | **PGA** | **OAA** |
| Carbons in primary product | **3** | **4** |
| RuBisCO present? | Yes | Yes |
| PEPcase present? | Not always (no, for carboxylation) | Yes |
| Which cells have RuBisCO | Mesophyll | **Bundle sheath** |
| CO<sub>2</sub> fixation rate under high light | Low/medium | **High** |
| Photorespiration at low light | Negligible | Negligible |
| Photorespiration at high light | **High** | **Negligible** |
| Photorespiration at low CO<sub>2</sub> | **High** | Negligible |
| Photorespiration at high CO<sub>2</sub> | Negligible | Negligible |
| Temperature optimum | **20–25°C** | **30–40°C** |
| Examples | Wheat, rice, most plants | **Maize, sorghum**, sugarcane |

## 11. Factors Affecting Photosynthesis

- **Internal (plant) factors:** number, size, age and orientation of leaves; mesophyll cells and chloroplasts; internal CO<sub>2</sub> concentration; amount of chlorophyll.
- **External factors:** sunlight, temperature, CO<sub>2</sub> concentration and water.

### 11.1 Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors (1905)
> **Key Point:** "If a chemical process is affected by more than one factor, then its rate will be determined by the factor which is **nearest to its minimal value**: it is the factor which directly affects the process if its quantity is changed."
> - Example: a green leaf with optimal light and CO<sub>2</sub> may not photosynthesise if **temperature is very low**; given optimal temperature, it starts.

### 11.2 Light
- Consider **quality, intensity and duration** of light.
- **Linear relationship** between incident light and CO<sub>2</sub> fixation at **low light intensities**. At higher intensities, rate stops increasing as other factors become limiting.
- **Light saturation occurs at 10% of full sunlight**. Hence, except for plants in shade or dense forests, **light is rarely a limiting factor** in nature.
- Excess light causes **breakdown of chlorophyll** and decrease in photosynthesis.

### 11.3 Carbon dioxide concentration
- **CO<sub>2</sub> is the major limiting factor** for photosynthesis.
- Atmospheric CO<sub>2</sub> is very low: **0.03–0.04%**.
- Increase up to **0.05%** can increase fixation rates; beyond this, damaging over longer periods.
- At **low light**, neither C<sub>3</sub> nor C<sub>4</sub> respond to high CO<sub>2</sub>. At **high light**, both show increased rates.
- **C<sub>4</sub> plants saturate at about 360 µL L<sup>−1</sup>**; **C<sub>3</sub> plants saturate only beyond 450 µL L<sup>−1</sup>**. So **current CO<sub>2</sub> levels limit C<sub>3</sub> plants**.
- **Greenhouse crops** like **tomatoes and bell pepper** are grown in **CO<sub>2</sub>-enriched atmosphere** for higher yields.

### 11.4 Temperature
- **Dark reactions** (enzymatic) are temperature-controlled; light reactions are affected much less.
- C<sub>4</sub> plants respond to higher temperatures; **C<sub>3</sub> plants have lower temperature optimum**.
- **Tropical plants** have higher temperature optimum than temperate plants.

### 11.5 Water
- Effect is more **through its effect on the plant** than directly on photosynthesis.
- **Water stress closes stomata** → reduces CO<sub>2</sub> availability.
- Water stress makes leaves **wilt** → reduces leaf surface area and metabolic activity.

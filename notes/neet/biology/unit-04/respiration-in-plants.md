## 1. Respiration in Plants – Introduction

- All energy for life processes comes from **oxidation of macromolecules (food)**.
- Green plants and cyanobacteria make food by photosynthesis. In green plants, **only chloroplast-containing cells** photosynthesise; all non-green parts need food translocated to them.
- **Respiration** – breaking of **C–C bonds** of complex compounds through **oxidation** within cells, releasing considerable energy.
- **Respiratory substrates** – compounds oxidised; usually **carbohydrates**, but **proteins, fats and organic acids** can also be used.
- Energy is released in **slow, step-wise enzyme-controlled reactions** and trapped as **ATP** – the **energy currency of the cell**.
- The **carbon skeleton** produced during respiration is used as **precursors for biosynthesis** of other molecules.
- Photosynthesis occurs in chloroplasts; breakdown of molecules occurs in the **cytoplasm and mitochondria** (in eukaryotes).

## 2. Do Plants Breathe? (Exchange of Gases)

- Plants need O<sub>2</sub> and give out CO<sub>2</sub>, but have **no specialised respiratory organs**. They use **stomata and lenticels**.
- **Reasons plants can manage without respiratory organs:**
  1. **Each plant part takes care of its own gas-exchange needs**; very little transport of gases between parts.
  2. Plants **do not have great demands for gas exchange** – roots, stems and leaves respire at far lower rates than animals. Large volumes of gases are exchanged only during photosynthesis, and O<sub>2</sub> is released within the cell then.
  3. **Diffusion distances are small** – each living cell is close to the surface. In stems, living cells are in thin layers inside and beneath the **bark**, with **lenticels**; interior cells are dead (mechanical support). Loose packing of **parenchyma** provides interconnected air spaces.
- **Complete combustion of glucose**: C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub> + 6O<sub>2</sub> → 6CO<sub>2</sub> + 6H<sub>2</sub>O + Energy (mostly heat). Cells oxidise glucose in **many small steps** so energy can be coupled to ATP synthesis.
- Organisms may be **facultative** or **obligate anaerobes**. All organisms retain machinery to **partially oxidise glucose without oxygen** = **glycolysis**. The first cells probably lived in an atmosphere **lacking oxygen**.

## 3. Glycolysis (EMP Pathway)

- Greek *glycos* = sugar, *lysis* = splitting.
- Scheme given by **Gustav Embden, Otto Meyerhof and J. Parnas** → **EMP pathway**.
- In **anaerobic organisms**, it is the **only process** in respiration.
- Occurs in the **cytoplasm**; present in **all living organisms**.
- **Glucose is partially oxidised to two molecules of pyruvic acid**.
- In plants, glucose comes from **sucrose** (end product of photosynthesis) or storage carbohydrates. **Invertase** converts sucrose into **glucose and fructose**.
- **Ten reactions**, each under a different enzyme.

### 3.1 Steps of glycolysis
1. **Glucose → glucose-6-phosphate** by **hexokinase** (**ATP used**).
2. Glucose-6-phosphate → **fructose-6-phosphate** (isomerisation).
3. **Fructose-6-phosphate → fructose-1,6-bisphosphate** (**ATP used**).
4. Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate splits into **dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP)** and **3-phosphoglyceraldehyde (PGAL)** – two triose phosphates (3C each).
5. **PGAL → 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate (BPGA)**: PGAL is **oxidised** (two redox equivalents removed as hydrogen atoms and given to **NAD<sup>+</sup> → NADH + H<sup>+</sup>**) and combines with inorganic phosphate.
6. **BPGA → 3-phosphoglyceric acid (PGA)** – energy-yielding; **ATP formed** (substrate-level phosphorylation).
7. PGA → 2-phosphoglycerate.
8. 2-phosphoglycerate → **phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP)** (H<sub>2</sub>O removed).
9. **PEP → pyruvic acid** – **ATP formed**.

### 3.2 Energy account of glycolysis (per glucose)

| Item | Number |
|---|---|
| ATP used | **2** (steps 1 and 3) |
| ATP formed directly | **4** (2 at BPGA → PGA, 2 at PEP → pyruvate, since there are 2 trioses) |
| **Net ATP (direct)** | **2** |
| NADH + H<sup>+</sup> formed | **2** |
| Pyruvic acid | **2** |

> **Common Mistake:** ATP is **used** at two steps (glucose → G6P; F6P → F1,6BP) and **formed** at two steps (BPGA → PGA; PEP → pyruvate). NADH is formed at only one step: **PGAL → BPGA**.

### 3.3 Fate of pyruvic acid
Depends on cellular need. Three major ways:
1. **Lactic acid fermentation**
2. **Alcoholic fermentation**
3. **Aerobic respiration** (Krebs' cycle; needs O<sub>2</sub>)

## 4. Fermentation (Anaerobic Respiration)

| Type | Organism | Reaction | Enzymes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alcoholic fermentation** | **Yeast** | Pyruvic acid → **CO<sub>2</sub> + ethanol** | **Pyruvic acid decarboxylase** and **alcohol dehydrogenase** |
| **Lactic acid fermentation** | Some **bacteria**; **animal muscles during exercise** when O<sub>2</sub> is inadequate | Pyruvic acid → **lactic acid** | **Lactate dehydrogenase** |

- **NADH + H<sup>+</sup>** is the reducing agent, reoxidised to **NAD<sup>+</sup>** in both.
- **Less than 7%** of the energy in glucose is released, and not all is trapped as ATP.
- Processes are **hazardous** – acid or alcohol is produced.
- **Net gain of only 2 ATP** per glucose (from glycolysis).
- **Yeasts poison themselves to death** when alcohol reaches about **13%**. Beverages with higher alcohol content are made by **distillation**.
- Fermentation occurs in many **prokaryotes, unicellular eukaryotes and germinating seeds**.

### 4.1 Fermentation vs aerobic respiration

| Fermentation | Aerobic respiration |
|---|---|
| **Partial** breakdown of glucose | **Complete** degradation to CO<sub>2</sub> and H<sub>2</sub>O |
| Net **2 ATP** per glucose | Many more ATP (**38** theoretical) |
| NADH oxidised to NAD<sup>+</sup> **slowly** | Oxidation is **very vigorous** |
| No O<sub>2</sub> needed | O<sub>2</sub> needed (final hydrogen acceptor) |

## 5. Aerobic Respiration

- **Pyruvate** (from glycolysis in cytoplasm) is transported into the **mitochondria**.
- Crucial events:
  1. **Complete oxidation of pyruvate** by stepwise removal of all hydrogen atoms, leaving **three molecules of CO<sub>2</sub>** – in the **matrix**.
  2. **Passing of electrons** (removed as part of hydrogen atoms) to **molecular O<sub>2</sub>** with simultaneous synthesis of ATP – on the **inner mitochondrial membrane**.

### 5.1 Link reaction – oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate
- In the **mitochondrial matrix**, catalysed by **pyruvic dehydrogenase** (pyruvate dehydrogenase complex).
- Requires coenzymes **NAD<sup>+</sup> and coenzyme A**; **Mg<sup>2+</sup>** as cofactor.
- **Pyruvic acid + CoA + NAD<sup>+</sup> → Acetyl CoA + CO<sub>2</sub> + NADH + H<sup>+</sup>**
- From one glucose (2 pyruvates): **2 NADH** produced.

### 5.2 Tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle / Krebs' cycle / Citric acid cycle
- Named after **Hans Krebs**. Occurs in the **mitochondrial matrix**.
- Steps:
  1. **Acetyl group (2C) + oxaloacetic acid (OAA, 4C) + water → citric acid (6C)**, catalysed by **citrate synthase**; CoA released.
  2. Citrate **isomerised** to **isocitrate**.
  3. Two successive **decarboxylations** → **α-ketoglutaric acid (5C)** → **succinyl-CoA**.
  4. Succinyl-CoA → **succinic acid**: **one GTP** synthesised (**substrate-level phosphorylation**). GTP → GDP with simultaneous ADP → **ATP** (coupled reaction).
  5. Succinic acid → **fumaric acid** → **malic acid (4C)** → **OAA**, allowing the cycle to continue.
- **Three points** where **NAD<sup>+</sup> → NADH + H<sup>+</sup>**; **one point** where **FAD<sup>+</sup> → FADH<sub>2</sub>** (at succinate oxidation).
- Continued oxidation needs continuous replenishment of **OAA** and regeneration of **NAD<sup>+</sup> and FAD<sup>+</sup>**.
- **Summary equation (per pyruvate, including link reaction):**
  - Pyruvic acid + 4NAD<sup>+</sup> + FAD<sup>+</sup> + 2H<sub>2</sub>O + ADP + Pi → **3CO<sub>2</sub> + 4NADH + 4H<sup>+</sup> + FADH<sub>2</sub> + ATP**
- Per glucose, after glycolysis + link + Krebs: **8 NADH + H<sup>+</sup>** (in mitochondria), **2 FADH<sub>2</sub>**, and **2 ATP** from TCA cycle.

> **Remember – carbon count in Krebs' cycle:** Acetyl CoA (2C) + OAA (4C) → Citric acid (6C) → α-ketoglutaric acid (5C) → Succinyl CoA/Succinic acid (4C) → Malic acid (4C) → OAA (4C). Two CO<sub>2</sub> are released in the cycle per acetyl CoA; one more in the link reaction.

### 5.3 Electron Transport System (ETS) and oxidative phosphorylation
- ETS is located in the **inner mitochondrial membrane**.
- Releases and uses energy stored in NADH + H<sup>+</sup> and FADH<sub>2</sub>; electrons passed to **O<sub>2</sub> → H<sub>2</sub>O**.

| Complex | Name / function |
|---|---|
| **Complex I** | **NADH dehydrogenase** – oxidises NADH (from matrix); electrons → **ubiquinone** (within inner membrane) |
| **Complex II** | Receives reducing equivalents via **FADH<sub>2</sub>** (from oxidation of **succinate**) → ubiquinone |
| **Complex III** | **Cytochrome bc<sub>1</sub> complex** – reduced ubiquinone (**ubiquinol**) is oxidised; electrons → **cytochrome c** |
| **Cytochrome c** | Small protein attached to the **outer surface of the inner membrane**; **mobile carrier** between complex III and IV |
| **Complex IV** | **Cytochrome c oxidase** – contains **cytochromes a and a<sub>3</sub> and two copper centres**; passes electrons to O<sub>2</sub> |
| **Complex V** | **ATP synthase** – produces ATP from ADP + Pi |

- **1 NADH → 3 ATP**; **1 FADH<sub>2</sub> → 2 ATP**.
- **Role of oxygen:** limited to the **terminal stage**, but vital – it **drives the whole process by removing hydrogen**. **Oxygen is the final hydrogen acceptor.**
- **Oxidative phosphorylation** – energy of **oxidation-reduction** is used to create the proton gradient (unlike photophosphorylation, where light energy is used).

### 5.4 ATP synthase in mitochondria
- Two major components:
  - **F<sub>1</sub> headpiece** – **peripheral** membrane protein complex; contains the **site for ATP synthesis** from ADP and Pi.
  - **F<sub>0</sub>** – **integral** membrane protein complex forming the **channel** through which protons cross the inner membrane.
- Passage of protons through F<sub>0</sub> is coupled to the catalytic site of F<sub>1</sub>.
- **For each ATP produced, 4H<sup>+</sup> pass through F<sub>0</sub>** from the **intermembrane space to the matrix** down the electrochemical gradient.

## 6. The Respiratory Balance Sheet

**Assumptions (not really valid in a living system):**
- A sequential, orderly pathway: glycolysis → TCA → ETS, one after another.
- **NADH from glycolysis is transferred into mitochondria** and undergoes oxidative phosphorylation.
- **None of the intermediates** are used to synthesise other compounds.
- **Only glucose** is respired; no alternative substrates enter at intermediate stages.

In reality, all pathways work simultaneously; substrates enter and leave as needed; ATP is used when needed; enzyme rates are controlled.

**Net gain = 38 ATP per glucose** during aerobic respiration (theoretical).

| Stage | Direct ATP | NADH | FADH<sub>2</sub> | ATP via ETS | Total ATP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glycolysis | 2 (net) | 2 | – | 2 × 3 = 6 | 8 |
| Link reaction (2 pyruvate) | – | 2 | – | 2 × 3 = 6 | 6 |
| Krebs' cycle (2 turns) | 2 (via GTP) | 6 | 2 | 6 × 3 + 2 × 2 = 22 | 24 |
| **Total** | **4** | **10** | **2** | **34** | **38** |

## 7. Amphibolic Pathway

- **Glucose** is the favoured substrate; other carbohydrates are first converted to glucose.
- **Entry of other substrates into respiration:**

| Substrate | Broken into | Enters pathway as |
|---|---|---|
| **Fats** | Glycerol + fatty acids | **Fatty acids → acetyl CoA**; **glycerol → PGAL** |
| **Proteins** | Amino acids (by **proteases**), then **deamination** | At some stage in **Krebs' cycle**, or as **pyruvate or acetyl CoA** (depending on structure) |

- These same compounds are **withdrawn** from the pathway for **synthesis**: e.g., acetyl CoA is withdrawn to make fatty acids.
- **Catabolism** = breaking down; **anabolism** = synthesis.
- Since the respiratory pathway is involved in **both anabolism and catabolism**, it is better called an **amphibolic pathway** rather than a catabolic one.

## 8. Respiratory Quotient (RQ)

- **RQ = Volume of CO<sub>2</sub> evolved / Volume of O<sub>2</sub> consumed** (also called respiratory ratio).
- Depends on the **type of respiratory substrate**.

| Substrate | Equation | RQ |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbohydrates** (complete oxidation) | C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub> + 6O<sub>2</sub> → 6CO<sub>2</sub> + 6H<sub>2</sub>O + Energy | 6/6 = **1.0** |
| **Fats** (e.g., tripalmitin) | 2(C<sub>51</sub>H<sub>98</sub>O<sub>6</sub>) + 145O<sub>2</sub> → 102CO<sub>2</sub> + 98H<sub>2</sub>O + energy | 102/145 = **0.7** |
| **Proteins** | – | About **0.9** |

- In living organisms, respiratory substrates are often **more than one**; **pure proteins or fats are never used**.

> **Exam Tip [beyond NCERT text, frequently asked]:** Organic acids (e.g., malic acid) give RQ **more than 1** (e.g., about 1.33). Anaerobic respiration (no O<sub>2</sub> consumed) gives RQ = **infinity**.

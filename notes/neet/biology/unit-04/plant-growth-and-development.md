## 1. Plant Growth and Development – Seed Germination

- Development of a mature plant from a zygote follows a precise, highly ordered succession of events, producing roots, leaves, branches, flowers, fruits and seeds, and eventually death.
- **The first step in plant growth is seed germination.**
- A seed germinates when **favourable conditions for growth** exist in the environment.
- In the absence of favourable conditions, seeds **do not germinate** and go into a period of **suspended growth or rest** (dormancy). When favourable conditions return, seeds resume metabolic activities and growth.
- NCERT figure shows **germination and seedling development in bean** – parts: seed coat, cotyledons, **hypocotyl**, **epicotyl**, **epicotyl hook**, soil line.
- **Development** is controlled by **intrinsic (internal)** and **extrinsic (external)** factors.

## 2. Growth

### 2.1 Definition
- **Growth** = an **irreversible permanent increase in size** of an organ, its parts or even an individual cell.
- Accompanied by **metabolic processes** (anabolic and catabolic) at the **expense of energy**.
- Example: expansion of a leaf is growth. (Swelling of wood in water is **not** growth – it is reversible and not metabolic.)

### 2.2 Plant growth is generally indeterminate
- Plants retain the capacity for **unlimited growth throughout life** due to **meristems**.
- Meristem cells divide and **self-perpetuate**; products lose the capacity to divide and form the plant body.
- New cells always being added by meristems = **open form of growth**.
- **Primary growth** – by **root and shoot apical meristems**; elongation along the axis.
- **Secondary growth** – by lateral meristems (**vascular cambium and cork cambium**) that appear later in **dicots and gymnosperms**; increase in **girth**.

### 2.3 Growth is measurable
- At cellular level, growth = increase in **protoplasm**, which is hard to measure directly. So we measure proportional quantities: **fresh weight, dry weight, length, area, volume, cell number**.

| Example | Growth measured as |
|---|---|
| **Maize root apical meristem** – more than **17,500 new cells per hour** | Increase in **cell number** |
| **Watermelon** cells – increase in size up to **3,50,000 times** | Increase in **cell size** |
| **Pollen tube** | Increase in **length** |
| **Dorsiventral leaf** | Increase in **surface area** |

### 2.4 Phases of growth
- Three phases: **meristematic, elongation and maturation** (seen in root tips; detected by the **parallel line technique** – zones immediately behind the apex elongate most).

| Phase | Location | Cell features |
|---|---|---|
| **Meristematic** | Root apex and shoot apex (constantly dividing) | **Rich in protoplasm**, **large conspicuous nuclei**; **primary cell walls** – thin, cellulosic, abundant **plasmodesmatal** connections |
| **Elongation** | Just proximal to meristematic zone | **Increased vacuolation**, cell enlargement, **new cell wall deposition** |
| **Maturation** | Further away (proximal to elongation zone) | Cells attain **maximal size** in wall thickening and protoplasmic modifications |

### 2.5 Growth rates
- **Growth rate** = increased growth per unit time.

| Feature | Arithmetic growth | Geometric growth |
|---|---|---|
| Cell division | After mitosis, **only one daughter cell continues to divide**; the other differentiates | **Both progeny cells retain ability to divide** |
| Example | **Root elongating at a constant rate** | Most systems; cells in culture |
| Curve | **Linear curve** (length vs time) | **Sigmoid (S-shaped) curve** – lag, log (exponential), stationary phases |
| Equation | **L<sub>t</sub> = L<sub>0</sub> + rt** | **W<sub>1</sub> = W<sub>0</sub> e<sup>rt</sup>** |

- In arithmetic growth: L<sub>t</sub> = length at time t; L<sub>0</sub> = length at time zero; r = growth rate (elongation per unit time).
- In geometric growth: W<sub>1</sub> = final size; W<sub>0</sub> = initial size; r = **relative growth rate**; t = time; e = base of natural logarithms.
- **r** is also the measure of the ability of the plant to produce new plant material = **efficiency index**. The final size W<sub>1</sub> depends on the initial size W<sub>0</sub>.
- **Sigmoid curve** – initial slow growth (**lag phase**), rapid exponential growth (**log/exponential phase**), then slowing due to limited nutrients (**stationary phase**). Characteristic of living organisms growing in a natural environment; typical for **all cells, tissues and organs** of a plant.

**Absolute vs relative growth rate:**
- **Absolute growth rate** – total growth per unit time.
- **Relative growth rate** – growth per unit time expressed on a common basis, e.g., **per unit initial parameter**.
- NCERT example: leaves A (small) and B (large) both increase area by **5 cm<sup>2</sup>** in the same time. Absolute growth is equal, but the **smaller leaf A has a much higher relative growth rate**.

### 2.6 Conditions for growth
- **Water** – cell enlargement needs water; **turgidity helps extension growth**; medium for enzymatic activities.
- **Oxygen** – releases metabolic energy.
- **Nutrients** (macro and micro elements) – for synthesis of protoplasm and as energy source.
- **Optimum temperature** range.
- **Environmental signals** – **light and gravity** affect certain phases.

## 3. Differentiation, Dedifferentiation and Redifferentiation

| Process | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Differentiation** | Cells derived from apical meristems and cambium **mature to perform specific functions**; undergo structural changes in walls and protoplasm | **Tracheary element** – cells **lose protoplasm** and develop strong, elastic, **lignocellulosic secondary walls** to carry water under extreme tension |
| **Dedifferentiation** | Living differentiated cells that had lost the capacity to divide **regain the capacity of division** | Formation of **interfascicular cambium and cork cambium** from fully differentiated **parenchyma** cells |
| **Redifferentiation** | Cells produced by dedifferentiated tissue again **lose capacity to divide** and **mature** for specific functions | Secondary xylem, secondary phloem, cork (products in woody dicots) |

- **Differentiation in plants is open** – cells from the same meristem have different structures at maturity; the final structure depends on the **location** of the cell. E.g., cells positioned away from root apical meristem become **root-cap cells**; those pushed to the periphery become **epidermis**.
- Parenchyma cells made to divide in tissue culture form a **callus** (dedifferentiation).

## 4. Development

- **Development** includes all changes that an organism goes through during its life cycle **from germination of the seed to senescence**.
- **Development = growth + differentiation**.

### 4.1 Sequence of developmental process in a plant cell
**Meristematic cell → cell division → plasmatic growth → expansion (elongation) → differentiation → maturation → mature cell → senescence → death**

### 4.2 Plasticity
- Plants follow **different pathways** in response to environment or phases of life to form different structures = **plasticity**.
- Example: **heterophylly** (different leaf shapes on the same plant):
  - **Due to phases of life (juvenile vs mature):** **cotton, coriander, larkspur** – juvenile leaves differ in shape from mature leaves.
  - **Due to environment:** **buttercup** – leaves produced in **air** differ from those produced in **water**.
- Development is controlled by:
  - **Intrinsic factors** – **intracellular (genetic)** or **intercellular** (chemicals such as PGRs)
  - **Extrinsic factors** – light, temperature, water, oxygen, nutrition

## 5. Plant Growth Regulators (PGRs)

### 5.1 Characteristics
- Small, simple molecules of diverse chemical composition. Also called **plant growth substances, plant hormones or phytohormones**.

| Chemical nature | PGR |
|---|---|
| **Indole compounds** | Indole-3-acetic acid (**IAA**) |
| **Adenine derivatives** | N<sup>6</sup>-furfurylamino purine (**kinetin**) |
| **Derivatives of carotenoids** | **Abscisic acid (ABA)** |
| **Terpenes** | **Gibberellic acid (GA<sub>3</sub>)** |
| **Gases** | **Ethylene (C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub>)** |

- Two groups by function:
  - **Plant growth promoters** – cell division, cell enlargement, pattern formation, tropic growth, flowering, fruiting, seed formation: **auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins**.
  - **Plant growth inhibitors** – responses to wounds and stresses (biotic and abiotic); dormancy and abscission: **abscisic acid**.
  - **Ethylene** could fit either group, but is **largely an inhibitor**.

### 5.2 Discovery of PGRs (all accidental)

| PGR | Discovery |
|---|---|
| **Auxin** | **Charles Darwin and Francis Darwin** – coleoptiles of **canary grass** bent towards unilateral light (**phototropism**); **tip of coleoptile** is the site of the transmittable influence. **F.W. Went** isolated auxin from **tips of coleoptiles of oat seedlings** |
| **Gibberellin** | **'Bakanae' (foolish seedling) disease of rice** caused by fungus ***Gibberella fujikuroi***. **E. Kurosawa (1926)** reproduced symptoms using sterile fungal filtrates. Active substance: **gibberellic acid** |
| **Cytokinin** | **F. Skoog** and co-workers: callus from **tobacco stem internodal segments** proliferated only if, besides auxin, the medium had extracts of vascular tissues, yeast extract, **coconut milk** or DNA. **Miller et al. (1955)** identified and crystallised the cytokinesis-promoting substance – **kinetin** |
| **Abscisic acid** | Mid-1960s: three inhibitors – **inhibitor-B, abscission II and dormin** – proved chemically identical → named **abscisic acid (ABA)** |
| **Ethylene** | **H.H. Cousins (1910)** – volatile substance from **ripened oranges** hastened ripening of stored **unripened bananas**; identified as **ethylene** |

## 6. Physiological Effects of PGRs

### 6.1 Auxins
- Greek *auxein* = to grow. First isolated from **human urine**.
- Term applied to **IAA** and other natural and synthetic compounds with growth-regulating properties.
- Produced by **growing apices of stems and roots**, then migrate to regions of action.
- **Natural:** **IAA** and **IBA (indole butyric acid)**. **Synthetic:** **NAA (naphthalene acetic acid)** and **2,4-D (2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid)**.
- **Functions and uses:**
  - **Initiate rooting in stem cuttings** (plant propagation)
  - **Promote flowering**, e.g., in **pineapples**
  - **Prevent fruit and leaf drop** at early stages; **promote abscission of older mature leaves and fruits**
  - **Apical dominance** – growing apical bud inhibits growth of lateral (axillary) buds. **Decapitation** (removing shoot tips) results in growth of lateral buds – applied in **tea plantations and hedge-making**
  - Induce **parthenocarpy**, e.g., in **tomatoes**
  - **Herbicides** – **2,4-D** kills **dicot weeds** but does not affect mature monocots; used for **weed-free lawns**
  - Control **xylem differentiation** and help in **cell division**

### 6.2 Gibberellins
- **Promotory** PGR. **More than 100 gibberellins** reported from fungi and higher plants: GA<sub>1</sub>, GA<sub>2</sub>, GA<sub>3</sub>...
- **GA<sub>3</sub>** – one of the first discovered; most intensively studied.
- **All GAs are acidic**.
- **Functions and uses:**
  - Increase **length of axis** – used to increase length of **grape stalks**
  - Cause fruits like **apple to elongate** and improve shape
  - **Delay senescence** – fruits left on tree longer, extending market period
  - **GA<sub>3</sub> speeds up malting** in the **brewing industry**
  - **Sugarcane** – spraying GA increases stem length, increasing yield by as much as **20 tonnes per acre**
  - Spraying **juvenile conifers** hastens maturity → **early seed production**
  - Promote **bolting** (internode elongation just prior to flowering) in **beet, cabbages** and rosette plants

### 6.3 Cytokinins
- Specific effects on **cytokinesis**.
- Discovered as **kinetin** (a modified form of **adenine**, a purine) from **autoclaved herring sperm DNA**. **Kinetin does not occur naturally in plants.**
- Natural cytokinin **zeatin** isolated from **corn kernels and coconut milk**.
- Natural cytokinins synthesised where **rapid cell division** occurs – **root apices, developing shoot buds, young fruits**.
- **Functions:**
  - Help produce **new leaves**, **chloroplasts in leaves**, **lateral shoot growth** and **adventitious shoot formation**
  - **Overcome apical dominance**
  - Promote **nutrient mobilisation**, which **delays leaf senescence**

### 6.4 Ethylene
- Simple **gaseous** PGR. Synthesised in large amounts by tissues undergoing **senescence** and by **ripening fruits**.
- **Effects:**
  - **Horizontal growth of seedlings**, **swelling of the axis** and **apical hook formation** in dicot seedlings
  - Promotes **senescence and abscission** of leaves and flowers
  - Highly effective in **fruit ripening**; enhances respiration rate during ripening = **respiratory climacteric**
  - **Breaks seed and bud dormancy**; initiates germination in **peanut seeds**; sprouting of **potato tubers**
  - Promotes rapid internode/petiole elongation in **deep water rice** (keeps leaves above water)
  - Promotes **root growth and root hair formation** (increases absorption surface)
  - Initiates flowering and **synchronises fruit-set in pineapples**; induces **flowering in mango**
- **Ethephon** – most widely used source of ethylene; absorbed and transported in aqueous solution, releases ethylene slowly.
  - Hastens fruit ripening in **tomatoes and apples**
  - Accelerates abscission in flowers and fruits (**thinning of cotton, cherry, walnut**)
  - Promotes **female flowers in cucumbers** → higher yield
- One of the **most widely used PGRs in agriculture**.

### 6.5 Abscisic acid (ABA)
- Discovered for its role in regulating **abscission and dormancy**.
- **General plant growth inhibitor** and inhibitor of plant metabolism.
- **Inhibits seed germination**.
- **Stimulates closure of stomata**; increases tolerance to stresses → called the **stress hormone**.
- Important role in **seed development, maturation and dormancy**; by inducing dormancy, helps seeds withstand **desiccation**.
- In most situations, ABA is an **antagonist to GAs**.

### 6.6 PGR interactions
- Roles may be **complementary or antagonistic**, **individualistic or synergistic**.
- Many events involve more than one PGR: **dormancy in seeds/buds, abscission, senescence, apical dominance**.
- Extrinsic factors like temperature and light often act **via PGRs** (vernalisation, flowering, dormancy, germination, plant movements).

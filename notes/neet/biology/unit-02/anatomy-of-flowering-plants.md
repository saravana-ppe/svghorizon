## 1. Plant Tissues [Beyond current NCERT]

A **tissue** is a group of cells having a common origin and usually performing a common function. Plant tissues are of two main types: **meristematic** and **permanent**.

### 1.1 Meristematic tissues
Growth in plants is restricted to specialised regions of active cell division called **meristems**.

| Meristem | Location | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Apical meristem** | Tips of roots (root apical meristem) and shoots (shoot apical meristem) | Produce **primary tissues**; increase **length** |
| **Intercalary meristem** | Between mature tissues, e.g., at the base of internodes in **grasses** | Regenerate parts removed by grazing herbivores |
| **Lateral meristem** | Occur in mature regions of roots and shoots; cylindrical | Produce **secondary tissues**; increase **girth**. Examples: **fascicular vascular cambium, interfascicular cambium, cork cambium** |

- Apical and intercalary meristems are **primary meristems** (appear early in life and form the primary plant body).
- Lateral meristems (cambium) are **secondary meristems** (appear later).
- As the shoot apical meristem forms leaves, some cells are left behind as **axillary buds**, which can form a branch or a flower.

### 1.2 Permanent tissues
Cells that have become structurally and functionally specialised and lost the ability to divide.
- **Simple tissues** – made of one type of cell.
- **Complex tissues** – made of more than one type of cell, working together as a unit.

### 1.3 Simple permanent tissues

| Tissue | Cell features | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parenchyma** | Isodiametric (spherical, oval, round, polygonal, elongated); thin cellulose walls; closely packed or with small intercellular spaces | Major component within organs | Photosynthesis, storage, secretion |
| **Collenchyma** | Cells thickened at the **corners** due to deposition of **cellulose, hemicellulose and pectin**; may contain chloroplasts; no intercellular spaces at corners | In layers below the epidermis in **dicot** plants (hypodermis of dicot stem); **absent in monocots**; also in petioles | **Mechanical support** to growing parts (young stem, petiole) |
| **Sclerenchyma** | Long narrow cells, thick **lignified** walls with few or numerous pits; usually **dead**, without protoplasts | Fibres – in various parts. **Sclereids** – fruit walls of **nuts**, pulp of **guava, pear, sapota**, seed coats of **legumes**, leaves of **tea** | Mechanical support |

- **Fibres** – thick-walled, elongated, pointed cells, usually in groups.
- **Sclereids** – spherical, oval or cylindrical, highly thickened dead cells with very narrow cavities (lumen).

### 1.4 Complex permanent tissues

**Xylem** – conducts water and minerals from roots to stem and leaves; also gives mechanical strength.

| Element | Features |
|---|---|
| **Tracheids** | Elongated, tube-like cells with thick lignified walls and tapering ends; **dead**, without protoplasm; inner wall thickenings in various patterns |
| **Vessels** | Long cylindrical tube-like structures made of many cells called **vessel members**, each with lignified walls and a large central cavity; dead; members interconnected through **perforations** in their common walls. **Presence of vessels is a characteristic of angiosperms** |
| **Xylem fibres** | Highly thickened walls and obliterated central lumen; septate or aseptate |
| **Xylem parenchyma** | **Living**, thin-walled; walls of cellulose; store food as starch or fat, and tannins. Radial conduction of water by **ray parenchymatous cells** |

- **Gymnosperms lack vessels** in their xylem (they have tracheids).
- **Protoxylem** – first-formed primary xylem. **Metaxylem** – later-formed primary xylem.
- **Endarch** – protoxylem towards the centre (pith), metaxylem towards periphery – in **stems**.
- **Exarch** – protoxylem towards the periphery, metaxylem towards the centre – in **roots**.

**Phloem** – transports food materials, usually from leaves to other parts.

| Element | Features |
|---|---|
| **Sieve tube elements** | Long tube-like structures arranged longitudinally, associated with companion cells. End walls perforated in a sieve-like manner = **sieve plates**. Mature sieve element has peripheral cytoplasm and a large vacuole but **lacks a nucleus**. Functions controlled by the nucleus of companion cells |
| **Companion cells** | Specialised **parenchymatous** cells closely associated with sieve tube elements (connected by **pit fields**); help maintain **pressure gradient** in sieve tubes |
| **Phloem parenchyma** | Elongated, tapering cylindrical cells with dense cytoplasm and nucleus; stores food, resins, latex, mucilage. **Absent in most monocots** |
| **Phloem fibres (bast fibres)** | Sclerenchymatous, elongated, unbranched, pointed; thick walls; **generally absent in primary phloem**, found in secondary phloem. Commercially used fibres of **jute, flax and hemp** |

- **Gymnosperms** have **albuminous cells and sieve cells** instead; they **lack sieve tubes and companion cells**.
- **Protophloem** has narrow sieve tubes; **metaphloem** has bigger sieve tubes.

> **PYQ Trend:** Living vs dead elements are a favourite: in xylem only **xylem parenchyma** is living; in phloem only **phloem fibres** are dead. Vessels are characteristic of angiosperms; companion cells are absent in gymnosperms.

## 2. The Tissue Systems (NCERT)

Based on structure and location there are **three tissue systems**: **epidermal**, **ground (fundamental)** and **vascular (conducting)**.

### 2.1 Epidermal tissue system
- Outermost covering of the whole plant body: **epidermal cells, stomata and epidermal appendages (trichomes and hairs)**.
- **Epidermis** – outermost layer of the primary plant body; elongated, compactly arranged cells forming a continuous layer; usually **single-layered**; parenchymatous with a small amount of cytoplasm lining the wall and a large vacuole.
- **Cuticle** – waxy thick layer on the outside of epidermis that **prevents water loss**. **Cuticle is absent in roots.**
- **Stomata** – in the epidermis of leaves; regulate **transpiration and gaseous exchange**.
  - Each stoma has two **bean-shaped guard cells** enclosing the stomatal pore. In **grasses**, guard cells are **dumb-bell shaped**.
  - **Outer walls** of guard cells (away from pore) are **thin**; **inner walls** (towards pore) are **highly thickened**.
  - Guard cells possess **chloroplasts** and regulate opening and closing.
  - **Subsidiary cells** – specialised epidermal cells near the guard cells.
  - **Stomatal apparatus** = stomatal aperture + guard cells + subsidiary cells.
- **Root hairs** – **unicellular** elongations of epidermal cells; absorb water and minerals.
- **Trichomes** – epidermal hairs on the stem; usually **multicellular**; branched or unbranched, soft or stiff, may be **secretory**; help **prevent water loss** due to transpiration.

### 2.2 Ground tissue system
- **All tissues except epidermis and vascular bundles**.
- Consists of simple tissues: parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma.
- Parenchyma is present in **cortex, pericycle, pith and medullary rays** in primary stems and roots.
- In leaves, ground tissue = thin-walled chloroplast-containing cells = **mesophyll**.

### 2.3 Vascular tissue system
- Complex tissues **xylem and phloem** together form **vascular bundles**.

| Type of vascular bundle | Description | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Open** | **Cambium present** between xylem and phloem; can form secondary xylem and phloem | **Dicot stems** |
| **Closed** | **No cambium**; do not form secondary tissues | **Monocots** |
| **Radial** | Xylem and phloem arranged **alternately on different radii** | **Roots** |
| **Conjoint** | Xylem and phloem on the **same radius**; phloem usually on the outer side of xylem | **Stems and leaves** |

## 3. Anatomy of Dicot and Monocot Plants

### 3.1 Dicot root (e.g., sunflower root)
1. **Epiblema** – outermost layer; many cells protrude as **unicellular root hairs**.
2. **Cortex** – several layers of thin-walled **parenchyma** with intercellular spaces.
3. **Endodermis** – innermost layer of cortex; single layer of **barrel-shaped** cells **without intercellular spaces**. Tangential and radial walls have deposits of water-impermeable, waxy **suberin** as **casparian strips**.
4. **Pericycle** – a few layers of thick-walled parenchyma next to endodermis. **Initiation of lateral roots and vascular cambium** (during secondary growth) takes place here.
5. **Pith** – small or inconspicuous.
6. **Conjunctive tissue** – parenchyma lying between xylem and phloem.
7. Usually **two to four** xylem and phloem patches (**diarch to tetrarch**). Later a cambium ring develops between xylem and phloem.
- **Stele** = all tissues inside the endodermis: **pericycle, vascular bundles and pith**.

### 3.2 Monocot root
- Similar to dicot root: epidermis, cortex, endodermis, pericycle, vascular bundles and pith.
- **More than six (polyarch)** xylem bundles.
- **Pith is large and well developed**.
- **No secondary growth**.

### 3.3 Dicot stem (young)
1. **Epidermis** – outermost protective layer; thin cuticle; may bear trichomes and a few stomata.
2. **Cortex** – three sub-zones:
   - **Hypodermis** – a few layers of **collenchyma** just below the epidermis; gives **mechanical strength** to young stem.
   - Cortical layers – rounded, thin-walled **parenchyma** with conspicuous intercellular spaces.
   - **Endodermis** – innermost cortical layer; cells rich in **starch grains** = **starch sheath**.
3. **Pericycle** – inner to endodermis and above the phloem as **semi-lunar patches of sclerenchyma**.
4. **Medullary rays** – a few layers of radially placed parenchyma between vascular bundles.
5. **Vascular bundles** – many, arranged in a **ring** (characteristic of dicot stem); each is **conjoint, open, with endarch protoxylem**.
6. **Pith** – large number of rounded parenchyma cells with large intercellular spaces in the centre.

### 3.4 Monocot stem
- **Sclerenchymatous hypodermis**.
- Large number of **scattered vascular bundles**, each surrounded by a **sclerenchymatous bundle sheath**.
- Large, conspicuous **parenchymatous ground tissue** (no distinction into cortex, endodermis, pericycle, pith).
- Vascular bundles **conjoint and closed**.
- **Peripheral bundles smaller** than central ones.
- **Phloem parenchyma absent**.
- **Water-containing cavities** present within vascular bundles.

### 3.5 Dorsiventral (dicot) leaf
- Vertical section shows **epidermis, mesophyll and vascular system**.
- Epidermis covers **upper (adaxial)** and **lower (abaxial)** surfaces with a conspicuous cuticle.
- **Abaxial epidermis bears more stomata**; adaxial may even lack stomata.
- **Mesophyll** (parenchyma with chloroplasts; carries out photosynthesis) has two types of cells:
  - **Palisade parenchyma** – **adaxially** placed; **elongated** cells arranged **vertically and parallel** to each other.
  - **Spongy parenchyma** – oval or round, **loosely arranged**, below the palisade, extending to lower epidermis; numerous large spaces and air cavities.
- **Vascular bundles** in veins and midrib; size depends on vein size (veins vary in thickness in reticulate venation). Surrounded by thick-walled **bundle sheath cells**.

### 3.6 Isobilateral (monocot) leaf
- Similar to dorsiventral leaf, with these differences:
  - **Stomata on both surfaces** of epidermis.
  - Mesophyll **not differentiated** into palisade and spongy parenchyma.
- **Bulliform cells** – in grasses, certain **adaxial** epidermal cells along the veins become large, empty, colourless cells. When **turgid**, the leaf surface is exposed; when **flaccid** (water stress), they make the leaves **curl inwards** to minimise water loss.
- Parallel venation is reflected in the **near similar sizes of vascular bundles** (except in main veins).

### 3.7 Dicot vs monocot – master comparison

| Feature | Dicot root | Monocot root | Dicot stem | Monocot stem |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Xylem bundles | 2–4 (diarch–tetrarch) | More than 6 (polyarch) | Many, in a ring | Many, scattered |
| Vascular bundle type | Radial | Radial | Conjoint, open, endarch | Conjoint, closed |
| Pith | Small / inconspicuous | Large, well developed | Large | Not distinct (ground tissue) |
| Hypodermis | – | – | Collenchymatous | Sclerenchymatous |
| Bundle sheath | – | – | Absent | Sclerenchymatous |
| Secondary growth | Yes | No | Yes | No |
| Special | Casparian strips in endodermis | Casparian strips in endodermis | Starch sheath; semi-lunar sclerenchyma patches in pericycle | Water cavities; phloem parenchyma absent |

> **PYQ Trend:** NEET often gives a description and asks you to identify the section: "vascular bundles conjoint, scattered, surrounded by sclerenchymatous bundle sheath, phloem parenchyma absent" = **monocot stem**. "Polyarch xylem, large pith" = **monocot root**. "Conjoint, open, endarch, ring arrangement" = **dicot stem**.

**NEET UG 2026 Biology – Notes from NCERT**

**NCERT chapters covered:** Class XI – Chapter 5 (Morphology of Flowering Plants), Chapter 6 (Anatomy of Flowering Plants), Chapter 7 (Structural Organisation in Animals)

**NEET syllabus topics in this unit:**

1. Morphology and modifications; Tissues; Anatomy and functions of different parts of flowering plants: root, stem, leaf, inflorescence (cymose and racemose), flower, fruit and seed
2. Families: Malvaceae, Cruciferae, Leguminosae, Compositae, Graminae
3. Animal tissues; Morphology, anatomy and functions of different systems (digestive, circulatory, respiratory, nervous and reproductive) of Frog (brief account only)

> **Note: Content beyond the current NCERT reprint**
> The 2026-27 NCERT reprint has dropped some sections that the NEET 2026 syllabus still lists: modifications of root, stem and leaf; plant tissues (meristematic and permanent); animal tissues; and the five plant families. These are marked **[Beyond current NCERT]** in these notes and are written from the earlier NCERT edition, so students do not miss them.

> **NEET Focus: How this unit is tested**
> - Morphology gives direct example-based questions: phyllotaxy, aestivation, placentation, position of ovary, types of stamens, fruit and seed parts. NEET loves "Which pair is correctly matched?" questions from these lists.
> - Floral formula of families (especially Solanaceae, Fabaceae, Brassicaceae) is asked almost every few years.
> - Anatomy: differences between dicot and monocot root, stem and leaf; types of vascular bundles; endodermis and casparian strips; bulliform cells.
> - Frog: sexual dimorphism, excretion (ureotelic), heart (3 chambers, sinus venosus, conus arteriosus), portal systems, cranial nerves, Bidder's canal.

## Quick Revision – High-Yield One-liners

- Tap root – mustard; fibrous – wheat; adventitious – grass, *Monstera*, banyan.
- Root functions include **synthesis of plant growth regulators**.
- Growth in length of root – **region of elongation**. Root hairs – **region of maturation**.
- Stem develops from **plumule**; root from **radicle**.
- Pulvinus – swollen leaf base in legumes.
- Bud absent in axil of leaflets of compound leaf.
- Pinnate compound – neem; palmate compound – silk cotton.
- Alternate phyllotaxy – china rose, mustard, sunflower; opposite – *Calotropis*, guava; whorled – *Alstonia*.
- Racemose – acropetal; cymose – basipetal.
- Perianth – lily. Asymmetric flower – *Canna*.
- Actinomorphic – mustard, *Datura*, chilli; zygomorphic – pea, gulmohur, bean, *Cassia*.
- Superior ovary – mustard, china rose, brinjal; half-inferior – plum, rose, peach; inferior – guava, cucumber, ray florets of sunflower.
- Valvate – *Calotropis*; twisted – china rose, lady's finger, cotton; imbricate – *Cassia*, gulmohur; vexillary – pea, bean.
- Epipetalous – brinjal; epiphyllous – lily.
- Monoadelphous – china rose; diadelphous – pea; polyadelphous – *Citrus*.
- Apocarpous – lotus, rose; syncarpous – mustard, tomato.
- Parthenocarpic fruit – formed without fertilisation.
- Drupe – mango (edible mesocarp), coconut (fibrous mesocarp).
- Endospermic dicot seed – castor; non-endospermic – bean, gram, pea; non-endospermic monocot – orchids.
- Aleurone layer – proteinous; scutellum – cotyledon of maize.
- Solanaceae – obliquely placed bicarpellary ovary, axile placentation, persistent calyx; berry.
- Guard cells dumb-bell shaped in grasses; inner walls thick.
- Cuticle absent in roots.
- Casparian strips – suberin, in endodermis.
- Lateral roots arise from **pericycle** (endogenous).
- Starch sheath – endodermis of dicot stem.
- Bulliform cells – grasses; leaf curling.
- Vessels absent in gymnosperms; companion cells absent in gymnosperms.
- Tendons – muscle to bone; ligaments – bone to bone.
- Frog – ureotelic, 10 pairs cranial nerves, nucleated RBCs, 3-chambered heart.

## Practice Questions (NEET pattern)

1. Which one of the following is a modified stem?
   - (a) Sweet potato
   - (b) Potato
   - (c) Carrot
   - (d) Turnip
2. Vexillary aestivation is characteristic of:
   - (a) China rose
   - (b) *Calotropis*
   - (c) Pea
   - (d) *Cassia*
3. Which pair shows correctly matched placentation?
   - (a) Mustard – axile
   - (b) Sunflower – free central
   - (c) Pea – marginal
   - (d) *Dianthus* – basal
4. Perigynous flowers with half-inferior ovary are found in:
   - (a) Plum
   - (b) Mustard
   - (c) Guava
   - (d) Cucumber
5. The floral formula ⊕ ⚥ K<sub>(5)</sub> C<sub>(5)</sub> A<sub>5</sub> G<sub>(2)</sub> with epipetalous stamens and obliquely placed ovary belongs to:
   - (a) Solanaceae
   - (b) Fabaceae
   - (c) Brassicaceae
   - (d) Liliaceae
6. Tetradynamous stamens and parietal placentation are features of:
   - (a) Malvaceae
   - (b) Asteraceae
   - (c) Poaceae
   - (d) Brassicaceae
7. Casparian strips are found in:
   - (a) Pericycle
   - (b) Epidermis
   - (c) Endodermis
   - (d) Cortex
8. Conjoint, closed vascular bundles scattered in ground tissue with sclerenchymatous bundle sheath indicate:
   - (a) Dicot root
   - (b) Monocot root
   - (c) Monocot stem
   - (d) Dicot stem
9. Which structure helps grass leaves to roll during water stress?
   - (a) Guard cells
   - (b) Subsidiary cells
   - (c) Bulliform cells
   - (d) Palisade cells
10. Which tissue connects bone to bone?
    - (a) Tendon
    - (b) Areolar tissue
    - (c) Ligament
    - (d) Cartilage
11. In male frog, the vasa efferentia open into:
    - (a) Cloaca directly
    - (b) Bidder's canal
    - (c) Urinary bladder
    - (d) Oviduct
12. Which feature is found only in male frogs?
    - (a) Nictitating membrane
    - (b) Tympanum
    - (c) Webbed hind limbs
    - (d) Copulatory pad on first digit of forelimb

**Answers:** 1–b, 2–c, 3–c, 4–a, 5–a, 6–d, 7–c, 8–c, 9–c, 10–c, 11–b, 12–d

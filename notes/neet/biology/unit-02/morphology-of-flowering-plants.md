## 1. Morphology of Flowering Plants – Introduction

- Angiosperms are characterised by the presence of **roots, stems, leaves, flowers and fruits**.
- The underground part is the **root system**; the part above ground is the **shoot system**.
- Morphology = study of external form. We need standard technical terms and definitions to classify and understand plants, and to understand adaptations (for protection, climbing, storage, etc.).

## 2. The Root

### 2.1 Types of root systems

| Type | Origin | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Tap root system** | Direct elongation of the **radicle** forms the primary root, which bears lateral roots (secondary, tertiary). Primary root + branches = tap root system. Common in **dicots**. | **Mustard** |
| **Fibrous root system** | In **monocots**, the primary root is short-lived and replaced by many roots arising from the **base of the stem**. | **Wheat** |
| **Adventitious roots** | Roots arising from parts of the plant **other than the radicle**. | **Grass, *Monstera*, banyan tree** |

### 2.2 Functions of roots
- Absorption of water and minerals from the soil
- Proper anchorage to the plant
- Storing reserve food material
- **Synthesis of plant growth regulators**

### 2.3 Regions of the root tip (from apex upwards)
1. **Root cap** – thimble-like structure covering the apex; protects the tender apex as it moves through soil.
2. **Region of meristematic activity** – a few mm above the root cap; cells very small, thin-walled, dense protoplasm; divide repeatedly.
3. **Region of elongation** – cells undergo rapid elongation and enlargement; responsible for **growth in length**.
4. **Region of maturation** – cells differentiate and mature. Some epidermal cells here form **root hairs**, which absorb water and minerals.

### 2.4 Modifications of root [Beyond current NCERT]

| Modification | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Storage (tap root) | Store food | **Carrot, turnip** |
| Storage (adventitious) | Store food | **Sweet potato** |
| **Prop roots** | Support; hanging roots from branches grow into soil | **Banyan tree** |
| **Stilt roots** | Support; arise from lower nodes of stem | **Maize, sugarcane** |
| **Pneumatophores** | Respiration – roots grow vertically upwards to get oxygen in swampy (mangrove) areas | ***Rhizophora*** |

> **Exam Tip:** Sweet potato is a root (adventitious root modified for storage). Potato is a stem (tuber). This pair is a classic NEET trap.

## 3. The Stem

### 3.1 Features
- Ascending part of the axis bearing branches, leaves, flowers and fruits.
- Develops from the **plumule** of the embryo.
- Has **nodes** (where leaves are borne) and **internodes** (portions between nodes).
- Bears **buds** – terminal or axillary.
- Green when young, later often woody and dark brown.
- Features that distinguish stem from root: nodes and internodes, multicellular hairs and **positively phototropic** nature.

### 3.2 Functions
- Spreading out branches bearing leaves, flowers and fruits.
- Conducting water, minerals and photosynthates.
- Some stems: storage of food, support, protection, vegetative propagation.

### 3.3 Modifications of stem [Beyond current NCERT]

| Modification | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Underground storage stems | Store food; perennation (survive unfavourable conditions) | **Potato** (tuber), **ginger** (rhizome), **turmeric** (rhizome), **zaminkand** (corm), ***Colocasia*** (corm) |
| **Stem tendrils** (from axillary buds) | Climbing; slender and spirally coiled | **Gourds (cucumber, pumpkin, watermelon), grapevine** |
| **Thorns** (axillary buds) | Protection from browsing animals; woody, straight, pointed | ***Citrus*, *Bougainvillea*** |
| **Flattened stem (phylloclade)** | Photosynthesis in arid regions (leaves reduced to spines) | ***Opuntia*** |
| **Fleshy cylindrical stem** | Photosynthesis, stores water | ***Euphorbia*** |
| Sub-aerial – **runner** | Vegetative propagation; lateral branch spreads to new niches, roots touch soil | **Grass, strawberry** |
| Sub-aerial – **stolon** | Slender lateral branch arising from base of main axis; arches down to touch ground | **Mint, jasmine** |
| Sub-aerial – **offset** | Short lateral branch with one internode, each node bearing a rosette of leaves and a tuft of roots; aquatic plants | ***Pistia*, *Eichhornia*** |
| Sub-aerial – **sucker** | Lateral branch from basal and underground portion of main stem; grows horizontally under soil then comes up obliquely | **Banana, pineapple, *Chrysanthemum*** |

> **Common Mistake:** Thorn (stem modification, arises from axillary bud, has vascular connection) vs spine (leaf modification, e.g., cactus) vs prickle (epidermal outgrowth, e.g., rose). NEET tests which one arises from an axillary bud.

## 4. The Leaf

### 4.1 Structure
- Lateral, generally flattened structure borne on the stem; develops at the **node** and bears a **bud in its axil**. The axillary bud later develops into a branch.
- Leaves originate from **shoot apical meristems** and are arranged in **acropetal order**.
- Most important vegetative organs for **photosynthesis**.
- Three main parts:
  - **Leaf base** – attaches leaf to stem; may bear two lateral small leaf-like **stipules**. In monocots the leaf base expands into a **sheath** covering the stem. In some leguminous plants the leaf base becomes swollen = **pulvinus**.
  - **Petiole** – holds the blade to light. Long thin flexible petioles let blades flutter in wind, **cooling the leaf** and bringing fresh air to the surface.
  - **Lamina (leaf blade)** – green expanded part with veins and veinlets; middle prominent vein = **midrib**. Veins give rigidity and act as channels of transport for water, minerals and food.

### 4.2 Venation
- Arrangement of veins and veinlets in the lamina.
- **Reticulate** – veinlets form a network; generally in **dicots**.
- **Parallel** – veins run parallel; characteristic of most **monocots**.

### 4.3 Simple and compound leaves
- **Simple leaf:** lamina entire, or incisions do not touch the midrib.
- **Compound leaf:** incisions reach the midrib, breaking it into **leaflets**.
- A **bud is present in the axil of the petiole** in both simple and compound leaves, **but not in the axil of leaflets** (this distinguishes a compound leaf from a branch with leaves).
- **Pinnately compound:** leaflets on a common axis, the **rachis** (represents the midrib), e.g., **neem**.
- **Palmately compound:** leaflets attached at a common point (tip of petiole), e.g., **silk cotton**.

### 4.4 Phyllotaxy (arrangement of leaves on stem)

| Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Alternate** | Single leaf at each node in alternate manner | **China rose, mustard, sunflower** |
| **Opposite** | A pair of leaves at each node, opposite each other | ***Calotropis*, guava** |
| **Whorled** | More than two leaves at a node forming a whorl | ***Alstonia*** |

### 4.5 Modifications of leaf [Beyond current NCERT]

| Modification | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Leaf tendrils** | Climbing | **Pea** |
| **Spines** | Defence; reduce water loss | **Cacti** |
| **Fleshy leaves** | Store food | **Onion, garlic** |
| **Phyllode** | Petiole expands, becomes green and photosynthesises; leaves are small and short-lived | **Australian *Acacia*** |
| **Insectivorous (pitcher / trap)** | Trap insects (nitrogen supply) | **Pitcher plant, Venus fly trap** |

## 5. The Inflorescence

- A **flower is a modified shoot**: the shoot apical meristem changes into a **floral meristem**; internodes do not elongate, the axis gets condensed, and the apex produces floral appendages at successive nodes instead of leaves.
- When a shoot tip transforms into a flower, it is always **solitary**.
- **Inflorescence** = arrangement of flowers on the floral axis.

| Feature | Racemose | Cymose |
|---|---|---|
| Main axis | **Continues to grow** (indefinite) | **Terminates in a flower** (limited growth) |
| Order of flowers | **Acropetal succession** – older flowers at base, younger at top | **Basipetal order** – older flower at top |
| Examples [Beyond current NCERT] | Mustard, radish (raceme); wheat (spike); sunflower (head/capitulum) | *Solanum* (cyme), jasmine, *Calotropis* |

> **Remember:** Racemose = Acropetal (think "R-A"). Cymose = basipetal. The main axis of cymose ends in a flower.

## 6. The Flower

### 6.1 General
- The **reproductive unit** in angiosperms; meant for sexual reproduction.
- Four whorls on the swollen end of the stalk (**pedicel**), called the **thalamus or receptacle**: **calyx, corolla, androecium and gynoecium**.
- Calyx and corolla = **accessory organs**; androecium and gynoecium = **reproductive organs**.
- In some flowers like **lily**, calyx and corolla are not distinct and are called **perianth**.
- **Bisexual** – has both androecium and gynoecium. **Unisexual** – only stamens or only carpels.
- **Trimerous, tetramerous, pentamerous** – floral appendages in multiples of 3, 4 or 5.
- **Bracteate** – flowers with bracts (reduced leaf at base of pedicel); **ebracteate** – without bracts.

### 6.2 Symmetry of flower

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Actinomorphic** (radial symmetry) | Divisible into two equal radial halves in any radial plane through the centre | **Mustard, *Datura*, chilli** |
| **Zygomorphic** (bilateral symmetry) | Divisible into two similar halves in only one particular vertical plane | **Pea, gulmohur, bean, *Cassia*** |
| **Asymmetric** (irregular) | Cannot be divided into two similar halves by any vertical plane | ***Canna*** |

### 6.3 Position of floral parts on thalamus

| Type | Position | Ovary | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hypogynous** | Gynoecium at the highest position; other parts below it | **Superior** | **Mustard, china rose, brinjal** |
| **Perigynous** | Gynoecium in the centre; other parts on the rim of the thalamus almost at the same level | **Half inferior** | **Plum, rose, peach** |
| **Epigynous** | Margin of thalamus grows upward, encloses and fuses with the ovary; other parts arise above the ovary | **Inferior** | **Guava, cucumber, ray florets of sunflower** |

> **PYQ Trend:** "Which of the following has an inferior ovary?" – Guava, cucumber, sunflower (ray florets). "Half-inferior ovary" – plum, rose, peach. This list is repeatedly asked.

## 7. Parts of a Flower

### 7.1 Calyx
- Outermost whorl; members are **sepals**. Generally green, leaf-like; **protect the flower in the bud stage**.
- **Gamosepalous** (sepals united) or **polysepalous** (sepals free).

### 7.2 Corolla
- Composed of **petals**; usually brightly coloured to **attract insects** for pollination.
- **Gamopetalous** (united) or **polypetalous** (free).
- Shapes: tubular, bell-shaped, funnel-shaped, wheel-shaped.

### 7.3 Aestivation
The mode of arrangement of sepals or petals in the floral bud with respect to other members of the same whorl.

| Type | Arrangement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Valvate** | Sepals/petals just **touch** one another at the margin **without overlapping** | ***Calotropis*** |
| **Twisted** | One margin of each appendage overlaps the next one, and so on (in one direction) | **China rose, lady's finger, cotton** |
| **Imbricate** | Margins overlap one another but **not in any particular direction** | ***Cassia*, gulmohur** |
| **Vexillary (papilionaceous)** | Five petals: the largest **standard** (vexillum) overlaps two lateral **wings**, which overlap the two smallest anterior petals, the **keel** | **Pea, bean** |

### 7.4 Androecium
- Composed of **stamens** (male reproductive organs). Each stamen = **filament** (stalk) + **anther**.
- Each anther is usually **bilobed**; each lobe has two chambers, the **pollen sacs** (so 4 pollen sacs per anther). Pollen grains are produced in pollen sacs.
- **Staminode** = a sterile stamen.

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Epipetalous** | Stamens attached to petals | **Brinjal** |
| **Epiphyllous** | Stamens attached to perianth | **Lily** |
| **Polyandrous** | Stamens free | – |
| **Monoadelphous** | United into one bundle | **China rose** |
| **Diadelphous** | Two bundles | **Pea** |
| **Polyadelphous** | More than two bundles | ***Citrus*** |
| Variation in filament length | Filaments of different lengths in the same flower | ***Salvia*, mustard** |

### 7.5 Gynoecium
- Female reproductive part; made of one or more **carpels**.
- Carpel = **stigma, style and ovary**.
  - **Ovary** – enlarged basal part; bears one or more **ovules** attached to a flattened cushion-like **placenta**.
  - **Style** – elongated tube connecting ovary to stigma.
  - **Stigma** – usually at the tip of style; **receptive surface for pollen grains**.
- **Apocarpous** – carpels free (**lotus, rose**).
- **Syncarpous** – carpels fused (**mustard, tomato**).
- After fertilisation, ovules → **seeds**; ovary → **fruit**.

### 7.6 Placentation (arrangement of ovules within the ovary)

| Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Marginal** | Placenta forms a ridge along the **ventral suture**; ovules in two rows | **Pea** |
| **Axile** | Placenta axial; ovules attached to it in a **multilocular ovary** | **China rose, tomato, lemon** |
| **Parietal** | Ovules on the **inner wall / periphery** of the ovary; one-chambered ovary becomes two-chambered by a **false septum** | **Mustard, *Argemone*** |
| **Free central** | Ovules on a **central axis**; **septa absent** | ***Dianthus*, *Primrose*** |
| **Basal** | Placenta at the **base** of ovary; **single ovule** attached | **Sunflower, marigold** |

> **Remember – placentation examples:** Marginal – Pea. Axile – China rose, Tomato, Lemon. Parietal – Mustard, Argemone. Free central – Dianthus, Primrose. Basal – Sunflower, Marigold.

## 8. The Fruit

- Characteristic of flowering plants; a **mature or ripened ovary** developed after fertilisation.
- **Parthenocarpic fruit** – formed **without fertilisation** of the ovary.
- Fruit = wall (**pericarp**) + seeds. Pericarp may be dry or fleshy.
- When thick and fleshy, pericarp is differentiated into **epicarp** (outer), **mesocarp** (middle) and **endocarp** (inner).

### 8.1 Drupe
- Examples: **mango and coconut**.
- Develop from **monocarpellary superior ovaries**; **one-seeded**.

| Fruit | Epicarp | Mesocarp | Endocarp |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mango | Outer thin | **Fleshy, edible** | Stony, hard |
| Coconut | – | **Fibrous** | Hard |

> **Exam Tip:** Edible part of mango = mesocarp. Coconut mesocarp is fibrous (coir). Both are drupes from a monocarpellary superior ovary.

## 9. The Seed

- Ovules after fertilisation develop into seeds. Seed = **seed coat + embryo**.
- Embryo = **radicle**, **embryonal axis**, and **one cotyledon** (wheat, maize) or **two cotyledons** (gram, pea).

### 9.1 Dicotyledonous seed (e.g., gram, pea, bean)
- **Seed coat** – two layers: outer **testa**, inner **tegmen**.
- **Hilum** – scar through which the developing seed was attached to the fruit.
- **Micropyle** – small pore **above the hilum**.
- **Embryo** – embryonal axis + two cotyledons (often fleshy, full of reserve food). **Radicle** and **plumule** at the two ends of the axis.
- **Endospermic seeds** – endosperm (formed by double fertilisation) stores food, e.g., **castor**.
- **Non-endospermous seeds** – endosperm absent in mature seed, e.g., **bean, gram, pea**.

### 9.2 Monocotyledonous seed (e.g., maize)
- Generally **endospermic**; but **orchids are non-endospermic**.
- In cereals such as maize, the **seed coat is membranous and fused with the fruit wall**.
- **Endosperm** is bulky and stores food.
- **Aleurone layer** – **proteinous layer** on the outer covering of the endosperm, separating it from the embryo.
- Embryo is small, in a groove at one end of the endosperm. It has one large, **shield-shaped cotyledon** called **scutellum**, and a short axis with plumule and radicle.
- **Coleoptile** – sheath enclosing the **plumule**. **Coleorhiza** – sheath enclosing the **radicle**.

> **Common Mistake:** Coleoptile covers the plumule (shoot); coleorhiza covers the radicle (root). "Ptile" = shoot side. The aleurone layer is proteinous, not starchy.

## 10. Semi-technical Description, Floral Formula and Floral Diagram

- A flowering plant is described in sequence: **habit → vegetative characters (root, stem, leaves) → floral characters (inflorescence, flower parts)** → floral diagram and floral formula.

### 10.1 Symbols used in floral formula

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Br | Bracteate |
| K | Calyx |
| C | Corolla |
| P | Perianth |
| A | Androecium |
| G | Gynoecium |
| G with a line **below** the number | Superior ovary |
| G with a line **above** the number | Inferior ovary |
| ♂ / ♀ / ⚥ | Male / female / bisexual |
| ⊕ | Actinomorphic |
| % | Zygomorphic |
| Number in brackets, e.g., (5) | **Fusion** (cohesion) of members |
| Line drawn above symbols of two whorls | **Adhesion** (e.g., epipetalous stamens) |

- **Floral diagram** – shows the number of parts, their arrangement and relation to one another. The **mother axis** is shown by a **dot on top**. Calyx is outermost; gynoecium in the centre.
- The NCERT floral diagram example is of **mustard (family Brassicaceae)**: ⊕ ⚥ K<sub>2+2</sub> C<sub>4</sub> A<sub>2+4</sub> G<sub>(2)</sub> (superior).

## 11. Family Solanaceae (NCERT)

- Large family, commonly called the **"potato family"**. Distributed in tropics, subtropics and even temperate zones.
- Example in NCERT figure: ***Solanum nigrum*** (makoi).

| Character | Description |
|---|---|
| Habit | Mostly herbs, shrubs, rarely small trees |
| Stem | Herbaceous, rarely woody; aerial, erect, cylindrical, branched, solid or hollow, hairy or glabrous; **underground stem in potato (*Solanum tuberosum*)** |
| Leaves | Alternate, simple, rarely pinnately compound, **exstipulate**; reticulate venation |
| Inflorescence | Solitary, axillary or **cymose** (as in *Solanum*) |
| Flower | Bisexual, actinomorphic |
| Calyx | Sepals 5, united, **persistent**, valvate |
| Corolla | Petals 5, united; valvate |
| Androecium | Stamens 5, **epipetalous** |
| Gynoecium | **Bicarpellary, obliquely placed**, syncarpous; ovary **superior**, **bilocular**; placenta swollen with many ovules; **axile** |
| Fruit | **Berry or capsule** |
| Seeds | Many, **endospermous** |
| Floral formula | ⊕ ⚥ K<sub>(5)</sub> C<sub>(5)</sub> A<sub>5</sub> (epipetalous) G<sub>(2)</sub> superior |

**Economic importance:** Food – **tomato, brinjal, potato**; spice – **chilli**; medicine – **belladonna, ashwagandha**; fumigatory – **tobacco**; ornamental – **petunia**.

> **PYQ Trend:** Obliquely placed ovary, persistent calyx and epipetalous stamens are the favourite identifying features of Solanaceae in NEET questions.

## 12. Other NEET Families [Beyond current NCERT]

> **Note:** The NEET 2026 syllabus names Malvaceae, Cruciferae (Brassicaceae), Leguminosae (Fabaceae), Compositae (Asteraceae) and Graminae (Poaceae). The current NCERT describes only Solanaceae in detail. The notes below cover the key features students need, including floral formulae.

### 12.1 Fabaceae (Leguminosae, sub-family Papilionoideae) – Pea family

| Character | Description |
|---|---|
| Habit | Trees, shrubs, herbs |
| Root | Tap root with **root nodules** (nitrogen-fixing bacteria) |
| Leaves | Alternate, pinnately compound or simple; leaf base **pulvinate**; **stipulate**; reticulate venation |
| Inflorescence | **Racemose** |
| Flower | Bisexual, **zygomorphic** |
| Calyx | Sepals 5, gamosepalous; imbricate |
| Corolla | Petals 5, polypetalous, **papilionaceous** – 1 standard (posterior), 2 wings (lateral), 2 keel (anterior, fused); **vexillary** aestivation |
| Androecium | 10 stamens, **diadelphous (9)+1**; anthers dithecous |
| Gynoecium | Ovary superior, **monocarpellary**, unilocular, many ovules; single style; **marginal** placentation |
| Fruit | **Legume (pod)** |
| Seed | One to many, **non-endospermic** |
| Floral formula | % ⚥ K<sub>(5)</sub> C<sub>1+2+(2)</sub> A<sub>(9)+1</sub> G<sub>1</sub> superior |

**Economic importance:** Pulses – gram, arhar, sem, moong, soybean; edible oil – soybean, groundnut; dye – *Indigofera*; fibre – sunhemp; fodder – *Sesbania*, *Trifolium*; ornamentals – lupin, sweet pea; medicine – muliathi.

### 12.2 Brassicaceae (Cruciferae) – Mustard family

| Character | Description |
|---|---|
| Habit | Mostly herbs, with pungent juice |
| Root | Tap root; sometimes modified for storage (radish, turnip) |
| Leaves | Alternate, simple, exstipulate |
| Inflorescence | **Racemose** (raceme / corymbose raceme) |
| Flower | Bisexual, actinomorphic, **tetramerous**, hypogynous, ebracteate |
| Calyx | 4 sepals in two whorls (2+2), polysepalous |
| Corolla | 4 petals, free, arranged in the form of a cross – **cruciform** |
| Androecium | 6 stamens, **tetradynamous** – 2 short (outer) + 4 long (inner) |
| Gynoecium | **Bicarpellary**, syncarpous, ovary superior; unilocular but becomes **bilocular due to a false septum (replum)**; **parietal** placentation |
| Fruit | **Siliqua** (long) or silicula (short) |
| Floral formula | ⊕ ⚥ K<sub>2+2</sub> C<sub>4</sub> A<sub>2+4</sub> G<sub>(2)</sub> superior (same as NCERT mustard floral diagram) |

**Economic importance:** Mustard oil; vegetables – cabbage, cauliflower, radish, turnip; ornamentals – candytuft (*Iberis*); *Arabidopsis thaliana* is a model plant in research.

### 12.3 Malvaceae – Cotton family

| Character | Description |
|---|---|
| Habit | Herbs, shrubs, trees; mucilage present; often **stellate (star-shaped) hairs** |
| Leaves | Simple, alternate, **stipulate**; palmately reticulate venation |
| Inflorescence | Solitary axillary |
| Flower | Bisexual, actinomorphic, hypogynous; **epicalyx** (whorl of bracteoles below calyx) present |
| Calyx | Sepals 5, gamosepalous, valvate |
| Corolla | Petals 5, polypetalous but fused at base with staminal tube; **twisted** aestivation |
| Androecium | Numerous stamens, **monadelphous** (filaments fused into a **staminal tube** around the style); anthers **monothecous** (one lobe), kidney-shaped |
| Gynoecium | Pentacarpellary (or more), syncarpous, ovary superior; **axile** placentation |
| Fruit | Capsule or schizocarp |
| Floral formula | ⊕ ⚥ Epicalyx K<sub>(5)</sub> C<sub>5</sub> A<sub>(∞)</sub> G<sub>(5)</sub> superior |

**Economic importance:** Fibre – **cotton (*Gossypium*)**; vegetable – **lady's finger/okra (*Abelmoschus esculentus*)**; ornamentals – **China rose (*Hibiscus*)**, hollyhock.

> **Remember:** Malvaceae = monadelphous stamens + monothecous anthers + twisted aestivation + epicalyx + axile placentation. China rose shows all of these, and all are separately listed as NCERT examples (monoadelphous – china rose; twisted – china rose, cotton, lady's finger; axile – china rose).

### 12.4 Asteraceae (Compositae) – Sunflower family

| Character | Description |
|---|---|
| Habit | Mostly herbs, some shrubs |
| Leaves | Alternate or opposite, simple, exstipulate |
| Inflorescence | **Capitulum (head)** – many small flowers (florets) on a flat receptacle, surrounded by an **involucre of bracts** |
| Florets | **Ray florets** (marginal) – ligulate (strap-shaped), zygomorphic, female or neuter. **Disc florets** (central) – tubular, actinomorphic, bisexual |
| Calyx | Modified into **pappus** (hairs or scales) – helps in fruit dispersal |
| Corolla | 5 petals, gamopetalous |
| Androecium | 5 stamens, **syngenesious** (anthers fused, filaments free), epipetalous |
| Gynoecium | **Bicarpellary**, syncarpous, **inferior ovary**, unilocular, one ovule, **basal** placentation |
| Fruit | **Cypsela** |
| Floral formula (disc floret) | ⊕ ⚥ K<sub>pappus</sub> C<sub>(5)</sub> A<sub>(5)</sub> (syngenesious, epipetalous) G<sub>(2)</sub> inferior |

**Economic importance:** Oil – sunflower, safflower; vegetable – lettuce; ornamentals – marigold, *Dahlia*, *Chrysanthemum*, *Zinnia*; medicine – *Artemisia*; weed – *Parthenium* (congress grass).

> **Exam Tip:** Links with NCERT text: epigynous flower (inferior ovary) – "ray florets of sunflower"; basal placentation – "sunflower, marigold"; racemose inflorescence type – head/capitulum.

### 12.5 Poaceae (Gramineae) – Grass family

| Character | Description |
|---|---|
| Habit | Mostly herbs; some woody (bamboo) |
| Root | **Fibrous**; stilt roots in maize, sugarcane |
| Stem | **Culm** – cylindrical, with solid **nodes** and usually hollow (**fistular**) internodes |
| Leaves | Alternate, **distichous** (in two rows), simple; **sheathing leaf base**; a small outgrowth called **ligule** at the junction of sheath and blade; **parallel** venation |
| Inflorescence | Spike of **spikelets** (compound spike) or panicle |
| Spikelet / floret | Spikelet bears two sterile bracts, **glumes**; each floret enclosed by **lemma** and **palea** |
| Perianth | Represented by 2–3 small scales called **lodicules** |
| Androecium | Usually **3 stamens**; anthers **versatile** (attached at the middle, swing freely); long filaments – adaptation for **wind pollination** |
| Gynoecium | Ovary superior, unilocular, **one ovule**, **basal** placentation; **two feathery stigmas** (wind pollination) |
| Fruit | **Caryopsis** – seed coat fused with fruit wall (as described in NCERT for maize) |
| Floral formula | % ⚥ P<sub>(lodicules 2)</sub> A<sub>3</sub> G<sub>1</sub> superior |

**Economic importance:** Cereals – **wheat, rice, maize**, millets (bajra, jowar, ragi); **sugarcane**; **bamboo** (paper, construction); fodder grasses; lemongrass (oil).

### 12.6 Family comparison – quick table

| Feature | Fabaceae | Brassicaceae | Malvaceae | Asteraceae | Poaceae | Solanaceae |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

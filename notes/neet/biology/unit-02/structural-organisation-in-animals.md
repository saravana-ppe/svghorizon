## 1. Animal Tissues [Beyond current NCERT]

- A **tissue** is a group of similar cells along with intercellular substances performing a specific function.
- All complex animals consist of only **four basic types of tissues**: **epithelial, connective, muscular and neural**.
- **Organs** (stomach, lung, heart, kidney) are made of these tissues in a specific proportion; the **heart has all four types**.
- Two or more organs performing a common function form an **organ system** (division of labour).

### 1.1 Epithelial tissue
- Has a **free surface** facing a body fluid or the outside environment. Provides a covering or lining.
- Cells are compactly packed with **little intercellular matrix**.
- Two types: **simple epithelium** (single layer; lining of cavities, ducts, tubes) and **compound epithelium** (two or more layers; protective).

| Type | Structure | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Squamous** | Single thin layer of flattened cells with irregular boundaries | Walls of **blood vessels**, **air sacs of lungs** | Forming diffusion boundary |
| **Cuboidal** | Single layer of cube-like cells | **Ducts of glands**, tubular parts of **nephrons** (with microvilli = **brush border** in PCT) | Secretion and absorption |
| **Columnar** | Single layer of tall, slender cells; nuclei at base; free surface may have **microvilli** | Lining of **stomach and intestine** | Secretion and absorption |
| **Ciliated** (columnar or cuboidal with cilia) | Cilia move particles or mucus in a specific direction | Inner surface of hollow organs like **bronchioles and fallopian tubes** | Moving particles/mucus |
| **Glandular** | Specialised columnar or cuboidal cells for secretion | Unicellular – **goblet cells** of alimentary canal; multicellular – **salivary gland** | Secretion |
| **Compound** | Multi-layered | Dry surface of **skin**, moist surface of **buccal cavity, pharynx**, inner lining of **ducts of salivary glands and pancreatic ducts** | Protection against chemical and mechanical stresses; limited role in secretion and absorption |

- **Exocrine glands** – secrete mucus, saliva, earwax, oil, milk, digestive enzymes; products released **through ducts**.
- **Endocrine glands** – **no ducts**; products (**hormones**) secreted directly into the fluid bathing the gland.

**Cell junctions** (structural and functional links between cells):
- **Tight junctions** – stop substances from **leaking** across a tissue.
- **Adhering junctions** – perform **cementing** to keep neighbouring cells together.
- **Gap junctions** – facilitate cells to **communicate** by connecting the cytoplasm of adjoining cells, for rapid transfer of ions, small molecules and sometimes big molecules.

### 1.2 Connective tissue
- **Most abundant and widely distributed** tissue; links and supports other tissues/organs.
- Range from soft connective tissues to cartilage, bone, adipose and blood.
- In all except **blood**, cells secrete fibres of structural proteins – **collagen or elastin** – giving strength, elasticity and flexibility. Cells also secrete modified polysaccharides forming the **matrix (ground substance)**.

| Category | Type | Features / Location |
|---|---|---|
| **Loose connective** | **Areolar** | Present **beneath the skin**; support framework for epithelium; contains **fibroblasts** (produce fibres), **macrophages** and **mast cells** |
| | **Adipose** | Located mainly **beneath the skin**; cells specialised to **store fats**; excess nutrients stored as fat |
| **Dense connective** | **Dense regular** | Collagen fibres in **rows between parallel bundles**. **Tendons** attach **skeletal muscle to bone**; **ligaments** attach **bone to bone** |
| | **Dense irregular** | Fibroblasts and fibres (mostly collagen) oriented differently; present in the **skin** |
| **Specialised connective** | **Cartilage** | Intercellular material solid and pliable, resists compression; cells (**chondrocytes**) enclosed in small cavities in the matrix. Most cartilage in vertebrate embryos is replaced by bone in adults. In adults: **tip of nose, outer ear joints, between adjacent bones of vertebral column, limbs and hands** |
| | **Bone** | Hard, non-pliable ground substance rich in **calcium salts and collagen fibres**; main tissue that provides **structural frame**; supports and protects softer tissues. Limb bones (long bones) are weight-bearing; interact with skeletal muscles. **Bone marrow** in some bones is the site of **blood cell production**. Bone cells = **osteocytes**, present in spaces called **lacunae** |
| | **Blood** | **Fluid connective tissue** containing plasma, RBCs, WBCs and platelets; main circulating fluid that helps transport substances |

### 1.3 Muscle tissue
- Made of long, cylindrical fibres arranged in parallel arrays; fibres are composed of fine fibrils (**myofibrils**).
- Fibres **contract** (shorten) in response to stimulation, then **relax** (lengthen) in a coordinated fashion.

| Type | Features | Location | Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Skeletal** | Closely attached to skeletal bones; **striated** (striped); fibres bundled together in parallel; sheath of tough connective tissue encloses bundles | e.g., **biceps** | **Voluntary** |
| **Smooth** | Fibres **taper at both ends (fusiform)**; **no striations**; cell junctions hold them together; bundled in a connective tissue sheath | Wall of **internal organs** – blood vessels, stomach, intestine | **Involuntary** |
| **Cardiac** | Contractile tissue present **only in the heart**; striated; cell junctions fuse plasma membranes and make them stick together; communication junctions (**intercalated discs**) at some fusion points let cells contract as a unit | **Heart** | **Involuntary** |

### 1.4 Neural tissue
- Exerts the **greatest control** over the body's responsiveness to changing conditions.
- **Neurons** – unit of the neural system; **excitable cells**.
- **Neuroglial cells** – make up **more than one-half the volume** of neural tissue; protect and support neurons.
- When a neuron is suitably stimulated, an **electrical disturbance** is generated which swiftly travels along its plasma membrane; arrival at the nerve ending triggers stimulation or inhibition of adjacent neurons and other cells.

> **Remember:** Tendon = muscle to bone. Ligament = bone to bone. Both are dense regular connective tissue. Blood is a connective tissue (fluid). Intercalated discs are only in cardiac muscle.

## 2. Frog (*Rana tigrina*) – Morphology

### 2.1 General features
- Frogs live **both on land and in freshwater**; class **Amphibia**, phylum **Chordata**.
- Most common species in India: ***Rana tigrina*** (Indian bullfrog).
- **Cold-blooded (poikilotherms)** – body temperature varies with the environment.
- **Change colour** to hide from enemies (camouflage) – this protective coloration is called **mimicry**.
- **Aestivation** – summer sleep; **hibernation** – winter sleep. They shelter in deep burrows during peak summer and winter.

### 2.2 External features
- **Skin** smooth, slippery due to **mucus**; always moist.
- **Dorsal side** – olive green with dark irregular spots; **ventral side** – uniformly pale yellow.
- The frog **never drinks water** but **absorbs it through the skin**.
- Body divisible into **head and trunk**; **neck and tail absent**.
- A pair of **nostrils** above the mouth.
- Eyes bulged, covered by a **nictitating membrane** (protects eyes in water).
- **Tympanum** (membranous ear) on either side of the eyes receives sound signals.
- **Forelimbs** end in **four digits**; **hind limbs** end in **five digits**, larger and more muscular. Feet have **webbed digits** for swimming.
- **Sexual dimorphism** – males have **sound-producing vocal sacs** and a **copulatory pad on the first digit of the forelimbs**; absent in females.

## 3. Frog – Anatomy (Organ Systems)

### 3.1 Digestive system
- Alimentary canal + digestive glands.
- Alimentary canal is **short** because frogs are **carnivores** (reduced intestine length).
- Pathway: **mouth → buccal cavity → pharynx → oesophagus (short tube) → stomach → intestine → rectum → cloaca** (opens outside).
- Food captured by the **bilobed tongue**.
- **Liver** secretes **bile**, stored in the **gall bladder**. **Pancreas** produces pancreatic juice with digestive enzymes.
- Digestion in stomach by **HCl and gastric juices**. Partially digested food = **chyme**, passed to the **duodenum**.
- Duodenum receives bile and pancreatic juice through a **common bile duct**. Bile **emulsifies fat**; pancreatic juice digests carbohydrates and proteins.
- Final digestion in the intestine; absorption by finger-like **villi and microvilli**.
- Undigested waste → rectum → out through **cloaca**.

### 3.2 Respiratory system

| Situation | Organs | Type |
|---|---|---|
| In water | **Skin** | **Cutaneous respiration** (dissolved O<sub>2</sub> exchanged by diffusion) |
| On land | **Buccal cavity, skin and lungs** | Lungs = **pulmonary respiration** |
| During aestivation and hibernation | **Skin** only | Cutaneous |

- Lungs: a pair of **elongated, pink, sac-like** structures in the upper trunk (thorax). Air enters: nostrils → buccal cavity → lungs.

### 3.3 Circulatory system
- Well-developed **closed** type. Frogs also have a **lymphatic system** (lymph, lymph channels, lymph nodes).
- **Heart** – muscular, in the upper part of body cavity; **three chambers – two atria and one ventricle**; covered by **pericardium**.
- **Sinus venosus** – triangular structure joining the **right atrium**; receives blood through major veins **vena cava**.
- **Conus arteriosus** – sac-like structure into which the ventricle opens, on the **ventral** side of the heart.
- Arteries carry blood from heart (arterial system); veins bring it back (venous system).
- Special venous connections:
  - **Hepatic portal system** – between **liver and intestine**
  - **Renal portal system** – between **kidney and lower parts of the body**
- **Blood** = plasma + cells: RBCs (erythrocytes), WBCs (leucocytes) and platelets. **Frog RBCs are nucleated** and contain **haemoglobin**.
- **Lymph** lacks a few proteins and RBCs.
- Frog has **single circulation** (as stated in NCERT summary).

### 3.4 Excretory system
- A pair of **kidneys**, **ureters**, **cloaca** and **urinary bladder**.
- Kidneys are compact, **dark red, bean-like**, a little posterior in the body cavity on both sides of the vertebral column.
- Each kidney has many **uriniferous tubules or nephrons**.
- **In males**, the ureters act as **urinogenital ducts** opening into the cloaca.
- **In females**, **ureters and oviducts open separately** into the cloaca.
- Thin-walled **urinary bladder** is ventral to the rectum and opens into the cloaca.
- Frog excretes **urea** → **ureotelic** animal.

### 3.5 Control and coordination
- **Endocrine glands:** pituitary, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus, pineal body, pancreatic islets, adrenals and gonads.
- **Nervous system:**
  - **Central (CNS)** – brain and spinal cord
  - **Peripheral (PNS)** – cranial and spinal nerves
  - **Autonomic (ANS)** – sympathetic and parasympathetic
- **Ten pairs of cranial nerves**.
- Brain enclosed in a bony **brain box (cranium)**.
- Brain divisions:
  - **Fore-brain** – olfactory lobes, paired cerebral hemispheres, unpaired diencephalon
  - **Mid-brain** – a pair of **optic lobes**
  - **Hind-brain** – cerebellum and medulla oblongata
- **Medulla oblongata** passes out through the **foramen magnum** and continues into the **spinal cord** (enclosed in the vertebral column).

### 3.6 Sense organs
- Touch – **sensory papillae**; taste – **taste buds**; smell – **nasal epithelium**; vision – **eyes**; hearing – **tympanum with internal ears**.
- **Eyes and internal ears are well-organised structures**; the rest are cellular aggregations around nerve endings.
- Eyes: a pair of spherical **simple eyes** (one unit each) in the orbit.
- **External ear absent**; only the tympanum is visible. The ear is an organ of **hearing and balancing (equilibrium)**.

### 3.7 Reproductive system

**Male**
- A pair of **yellowish ovoid testes**, adhered to the upper part of kidneys by a double fold of peritoneum = **mesorchium**.
- **Vasa efferentia – 10 to 12**, arise from testes, enter the kidneys and open into **Bidder's canal**.
- Bidder's canal communicates with the **urinogenital duct**, which comes out of the kidney and opens into the cloaca.
- **Cloaca** – small median chamber for passing **faecal matter, urine and sperms**.

**Female**
- A pair of **ovaries** near the kidneys, with **no functional connection with the kidneys**.
- A pair of **oviducts** opens **separately** into the cloaca.
- A mature female lays **2500 to 3000 ova** at a time.
- **Fertilisation external** (in water). Development involves a larval stage, the **tadpole**, which undergoes **metamorphosis** into the adult.

### 3.8 Importance of frogs
- Eat insects and **protect crops**.
- Maintain **ecological balance** – important link in food chains and food webs.
- Muscular legs used as food in some countries.
